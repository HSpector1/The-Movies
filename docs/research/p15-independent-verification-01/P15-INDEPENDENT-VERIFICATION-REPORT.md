Every Owner direction from D to K is successor design, never parity.
6. **Four weeks is a sound market window.** Against the accepted roster and Prima's release cadence, a rival same-genre release lands inside 4 weeks 12–27 % of the time (same week 3–8 %, 8 weeks 23–47 %) — often enough to teach a scheduling habit, rare enough to be a signal. Refine the shape, not the length: a tapered decay (1.00/0.55/0.20) inside the window plus a separate bounded 26-week genre-saturation stock, contribution scaled by public reach. Run-overlap as the definition couples P15 to a flat P07 constant and mis-models eras.
7. **Keep Power Ranking, Book Net Worth and Estimated Studio Value as three distinct objects.** Recommended presentation **Model D**: three creative lanes from trailing frozen facts (film outcomes, recognition, delivery), competition tie rule to match the shipped charts, and a **Financial Standing band** (Thriving/Stable/Leveraged/Strained/Distressed) in the same quarterly row that can never move the rank and is never ordered. A hot leveraged studio ranks #1 and is the poorest; a wealthy cold studio ranks last and is the richest — both visible, neither merged. Rivals show the band only; the player sees exact figures.
8. **Book Net Worth is buildable today from ledger truth** (cash + facility ledger-capex × 0.5 + set capex × 0.35 − loan principal − accrued interest; a liquidation-basis book by construction), with Guaranteed Obligations kept separate (existing REQ-016 law) and a "Wind-Down Position" stress line. **Estimated Studio Value** should ship only as a labelled range (≈ 3–6 × three-year trailing operating surplus + cash − debt) that no mechanic consumes. A profitable debt-funded studio can have negative book equity and a solidly positive value — show both, never one.
9. **One shared failure ladder for player and rivals, differing only at presentation and selection:** Healthy → Warning → Distress → Severe Distress (Event of Default: 4 missed weekly loan instalments in a rolling 13-week window with capitalization and a catch-up cure — **or** Distress sustained ≥ 26 of 32 weeks with no loan, so that never borrowing is not immunity) → Insolvency (one bounded 13-week rescue window with a visible clock) → Bankruptcy → Settlement/Archive. Every stage test is a rolling-window majority, never a consecutive count (a lucky week must not reset it); a stage never moves on the sign of cash or on **net worth alone** (a conservatively-valued but current borrower must not be punished for borrowing); scheduled receipts in flight count before a miss; Warning and Distress have no clock; every counter starts at zero on migration.
10. **Player terminal experience: C → B.** The Insolvency window is the one bounded rescue; if uncured, the terminal event settles the studio under the same rules as a rival and **immediately opens the Legacy/postmortem dossier using the same P15C reducer with an early trigger** — not a hard game-over (the Hollywood Animal pattern players call a trap), not a bare postmortem. Capitalism Lab's "continue as employee" is an unshipped DLC preview and is not a precedent.
11. **Loans: two products and one rule.** A single fixed-term amortizing **Studio Loan** (5/10 yr; era base + credit-grade spread; weekly automatic repayment; penalty-free early payoff; cap = the tighter of 3× trailing surplus, 3× interest coverage and 60 % LTV on book-after-salvage) plus a distress-only **Rescue loan** offered by the ladder as a typed remedy; **distress interest on negative cash after 8 sustained weeks** (OpenTTD's lesson, softened) closes the "overdraft beats borrowing" exploit without a revolver. Coverage binds before leverage at current tuning. Refinancing only from Stable with an escalating fee; cross-default; **acceleration is the only finance event that moves a stage**. P11 owns the math; P15B consumes; rivals borrow under the identical law through P12 policy.
12. **Whether rivals borrow is the dial that decides whether consolidation happens at all.** If they never borrow, they never fail — a floor by omission. If they borrow under the same law, failure emerges and is already differentiated by the authored policy spread (`reserveWeeks` 12–20, `negativeScale` 0.94–1.20). Calibration check, not target: exactly nine rivals are ever minted and none replaced, so an annual terminal hazard ≥ 2 % very likely empties Hollywood before 2040; 0.2–1 % leaves 3–8 survivors.

The one severe risk is not head-count but the **talent-market collapse** from Directions G and H compounding (each closure dumps a roster into free agency *and* removes a bidder; at 4 → 2 studios demand is 12 roles against ~48 freed people). Least-intrusive answers: an explicit "this industry may consolidate" disclosure at new game; symmetric rival borrowing; and **rival post-entry capacity growth** (new P12 scope) as the emergent sink for freed talent. Recommend **against** a GearCity-style monopoly lawsuit as a general mechanic — it is the mirror image of the rejected floor; a dominance guard, if wanted, belongs only in acquisition eligibility.

## 3. Shared market / release-window recommendation

**Owner direction (settled):** genre + release-window competition; ≈4-week primary window; exact window/curve open tuning; no screen market without major evidence.

**Verdict: four weeks is a sensible abstraction; keep it, and refine the shape inside it rather than the length.** The arithmetic, run against the accepted roster, is the reason:

- Release density (PROVISIONAL assumptions: 9 rivals × 2 films/year — Prima's "10 in five years" cap used as the illustrative rate; genre drawn from the authored affinity weights in `hollywoodStartingData.ts`): expected rival releases/year by genre ≈ romance 4.1, horror 3.9, comedy 3.6, adventure 3.1, drama 1.7, crime 1.7. The authored roster gives drama/crime only ~40 % of romance's rate — a legibility fact the tuning pass must expect (the window will feel busier in some genres from day one), not a formula bug.
- Probability that a rival same-genre release lands inside a window (Poisson): **same week 3–8 %; 2 weeks 6–15 %; 4 weeks 12–27 %; 8 weeks 23–47 %; 6-week run-overlap 18–38 %.** Same-week/2-week fire so rarely the mechanic reads as absent; 8 weeks fires so often in busy genres that pressure becomes ambient and the INFO-tier "pressure changed" notice stops informing; 4 weeks is the shortest window that is common enough to teach a scheduling habit and rare enough to be a signal when it fires.
- Run-overlap is conceptually attractive (a rival's film is literally still playing) but couples P15's versioned formula to `THEATRICAL_WEEKS: 6`, a flat P07 constant that P15 does not own and that silently mis-models era differences if run length ever varies by era. Reject as the *window definition*.
- Computational cost is asymptotically identical for 2/4/8 weeks (O(active exposures + due events), aggregate-once/subtract-self per batch); run-overlap adds a second source of truth per batch.

**Recommended bounded model (parameters PROVISIONAL):** reuse the P15 package's frozen same-week batch with explicit self-exclusion (§12.1) unchanged; two lanes on one contribution number:

| Lane | Definition | Provisional values |
|---|---|---|
| Temporal overlap (the "4-week window") | `exposureWindowWeeks` with a **tapered** `decaySchedule` (bounded ordered stages, already an Annex D.1 field): peak weight 1.00 in release week R, 0.55 for [R+1, R+3), 0.20 for [R+3, R+4), archived at R+4 | 4 weeks; 1.00/0.55/0.20 |
| Genre-saturation stock | A bounded stock per genre, +contribution per real (batch-frozen) release, exponential decay, clamped [0,100]; separate typed reason code | 26-week window, ~13-week half-life (deliberately not 13 or 52, to avoid accidental coupling to chart/ranking cadences) |
| Contribution | `contributionBounds` [0.05, 1.00] **scaled by disclosed public reach/opening-exposure band**, never a flat per-film unit | resolves Open Question 2 in the direction that closes the cheap-film spam vector |

Why taper rather than cliff: a hard cliff creates a boundary-dodge exploit (release one day past the envelope for full immunity); a taper **reduces the cliff from 1.00 to 0.20** (it does not eliminate it — R+4 is still an exact boundary; a continuously decaying tail past R+4 is optional if fully cliff-free behaviour is wanted). Why two lanes: Prima itself names "time and saturation" as two factors; "a rival just opened here" and "this genre has been busy for months" need separate reasons to pass the Annex F.3 explainability test.

Exploit review (with the verifiers' additions): release-date dodging is the intended decision, not an exploit; announce-and-cancel is already closed by state law (pressure exists only from batch-frozen releases; previews are non-authoritative and refresh in place); spamming cheap films is closed for rivals by reach-scaled contribution plus their structural cap (one production in flight; the true steady-state ceiling is ≈ 9 weeks/film ≈ 5.8/yr, above Prima's 2/yr — an upside risk to expect in tuning), but **nothing caps how many well-marketed same-genre films a capitalized player releases into one window** — add a per-(studio, genre, window) contribution clamp before the shared aggregate (law-8/9-compliant, applies to everyone); boundary splitting is reduced by the taper. Two rules the direction implies but the prior text never stated: **the formula must be well-defined at zero or one competitors** (no competitors ⇒ zero competitive pressure — a reachable end state under Direction G), and **pre-P15 saves cold-start every exposure and stock at zero from a `recordedFromWeek` boundary** (law 7, no retroactive fiction). One question only the Owner can answer: deliberately timing high-marketing releases into a fragile rival's anchor genre to hasten its failure is legal under one shared law — **is player-engineered rival bankruptcy sanctioned emergent strategy or an exploit to bound?** (If bounded, cap the market-pressure term's contribution to any studio's failure conditions; see §10.)

Explanation grammar (word-based direction, counts, window, sources): "Comedy pressure: rising — 2 known releases within 4 weeks"; "Comedy pressure: recovering — clears in 2 weeks"; "Comedy fatigue: elevated — 7 comedies released in the past 26 weeks". Cancelled/delayed announcements unwind with no special-case code: nothing was ever written for an unreleased film; only the live preview count changes.

**Screens:** the direction's rider ("no physical theater/screen allocation market unless evidence shows a major benefit") stands — the original had no exhibition model; the only comparator with theater competition (Hollywood Animal) is Early Access with no verifiable benefit evidence; nothing found meets the bar. **Ownership:** P15A owns both lanes and the batch law; the genre taxonomy's era stability is a P13 question; an era-conditioned window is a legitimate later P13/P15 refinement, not P15A.1 scope. **Remaining Owner tuning:** confirm 4 weeks; taper shape (three stages vs continuous exponential); Open Question 2 (reach-scaled contribution, recommended); saturation window/half-life; confirm era-window out of scope for P15A.1. **Structural problems with the direction: none found.**

## 4. Power Ranking vs Net Worth vs Valuation

**Owner direction (settled):** quarterly Power Ranking; transparent multi-factor; distinct from P08 Standing and Legacy; financial strength represented "somewhere meaningful"; do not assume wealthiest = most powerful; net worth/value calculated and displayed; book ≠ enterprise value.

### 4.1 They are three different objects — keep all three distinct

| Number | Question it answers | What it is made of |
|---|---|---|
| **Power Ranking** | "Who is strongest/hottest in Hollywood right now?" | trailing-window momentum from frozen facts: film outcomes, recognition, delivery — capped lanes, published |
| **Book Net Worth** | "What does the studio own at recorded value, minus what it owes?" | cash + facility/set book value − loan principal − accrued interest; a record of the past |
| **Estimated Studio Value** | "What might the business be worth to a buyer?" | a labelled **range** from trailing earning power, plus cash, minus debt; a bet about the future |

The worked example that settles it (PROVISIONAL): a "hot, leveraged" studio (cash $1.5M, book assets $9.5M, loan $8M → net worth $3M; trailing revenue $22M) ranks **#1** on the three creative lanes and is the **poorest** of four by net worth; a "wealthy, cold" studio (cash $30M, no debt, trailing revenue $2M) ranks **last** and is by far the richest. Any model that sums money into the rank drags the first down or the second up for reasons unrelated to "hottest right now"; any model that hides finance leaves the player unable to see that the #1 studio is one bad quarter from trouble.

### 4.2 Presentation models compared

| | A: two separate leaderboards | B: fourth financial lane inside the rank | C: rank stays creative; Valuation is the M&A number | **D (recommended): rank stays creative; a Financial Standing band rides beside it** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Money inside the rank's math | No | **Yes** | No | No |
| Finance visible in the quarterly table | Yes (ordered by wealth) | Yes (inside the order) | No | Yes — a 5-label band, never summed, never ordered |
| "Wealthiest = most powerful" misread risk | Medium | **High** | Low | **Lowest** |
| New rival disclosure required | near-exact dollars | banded lane value | one valuation number | **band only, no dollars** |
| Degrades to 2–3 studios | a 3-row wealth board reads as private-ledger exposure | same | fine | fine — a band needs no cohort |
| Original-game parity? | none (Capital was cash-in-bank inside the rating) | closest to the original's Capital 24 % — a departure either way | none | none |

**Model D:** the quarterly table shows the three creative lanes, their sum (optional; the Owner may keep per-lane ranks primary), rank, movement, **and one more column — Financial Standing: Thriving / Stable / Leveraged / Strained / Distressed** — shown in the same row but mathematically incapable of moving the rank. Band inputs (PROVISIONAL, all three published): debt ÷ book assets, runway in weeks of fixed costs, and the sign of Book Net Worth — e.g., Thriving = debt/assets < 20 % **and** runway ≥ 26 weeks; Stable = < 40 % and ≥ 13 weeks; Leveraged = 40–70 % or runway 8–13 weeks; Strained = > 70 % or runway < 8 weeks; Distressed = Book Net Worth ≤ 0 or an active distress stage. **Compute the band on a trailing 13-week average, not the snapshot week** — otherwise a studio (deterministic rivals included) can pay down debt the week before the quarter and re-borrow after (window dressing). Exact Book Net Worth and Estimated Studio Value live on each studio's detail page: full precision for the player's own studio, **band-only for rivals**; a new debt field defaults to zero on migration. This satisfies "represented somewhere meaningful" in the same glance as the rank, cannot structurally produce "richest wins", and needs the smallest new disclosure surface of any financially-aware model.

### 4.3 Specifying P15A.2 against the shipped surface (a correction to the prior research)

At 592e926 a **quarterly, per-lane comparison already ships**: `HollywoodChartSnapshot` every 13 weeks (`hollywoodTick.ts:306-313`), and `bridge/industry.ts:66-84` computes per-lane rank/priorRank/movement over `audienceAwareness`, `industryPrestige`, `commercialConfidence`, `output` with a **competition tie rule (1-1-3)**, page-titled "Studio Charts", with the notice "there is no combined Power score" (`bridge/industry.ts:110`). It is a Standing-ordered chart, query-time and unpersisted beyond two snapshots — not a momentum Power Ranking — but it occupies the UI real estate. Consequences:

1. **Extend, don't fork:** Power Ranking should be a distinct dated snapshot *view* over the same quarterly cadence, and the shipped per-lane Studio Charts should remain the non-ranked browse view (Simutrans pattern: one window, many lane choices). The notice sentence must change the day Power Ranking ships.
2. **Tie rule:** the P15A.2 candidate proposed dense ties; the shipped surface uses competition ranking. **Recommend keeping competition ranking** (one tie rule across adjacent screens).
3. **Lane sources:** P15 law 3 ("Standing is not rank") and P12A SAF-012 argue that Power Ranking lanes come from **trailing-52-week frozen facts** (P07 film outcomes capped at the strongest four; P08 honors; P12 disclosed delivery), not from copies of Standing values. But Awards do not exist at 592e926 (stub only; P08B blocked), so a "prestige/honors" lane has no data yet. In Endless play the ranking continues on its own dated record set and never touches the frozen 2040 dossier.

### 4.4 Net worth and value — what Project: Studio can legitimately compute

What exists at 592e926 (CODE): `studio.cash`; a full signed ledger reconciling to cash; facility purchase price only as the `constructionCapex` ledger row (no persisted cost on `PlacedFacility`); flat refund fractions 0.5 (facility) / 0.35 (set) computed from *today's* blueprint; land `ownedFromStart: true` with no value; founding plant with no value and no Opex; contracts = `annualSalary × remaining weeks` guaranteed (`guaranteedComp`), exit at 50 %; locked future run revenue (`pipelineRunRevenue`); rivals: `RivalAccount` cash + annual category periods; **no** loans, interest, depreciation, land value, or insolvency.

**Book Net Worth (recommend, P11 read model):**
`Cash + Σ facility ledger-capex × 0.5 + Σ set ledger-capex × 0.35 + $0 (land/founding plant) − loan principal − accrued unpaid interest.`
Basis = **recorded ledger capex, never today's catalog price** (stable across eras; the live refund function's catalog lookup is fine for a liquidation offer today but dishonest for a book record). Multiplier = the existing refund fractions, so the asset side is honestly a **liquidation-basis** book ("what you'd get if you sold it all today"); it upgrades for free when an age curve ships. **Guaranteed contract remainder stays a separate "Guaranteed Obligations" figure** (existing REQ-016 law: shown beside cash, never subtracted), with a third, clearly secondary **"Wind-Down Position" = Book Net Worth − 50 % × Obligations** as a stress line. Library/IP contributes nothing until a P16 rights system exists.

**Estimated Studio Value (recommend exposing, as a range only):** `[3, 6] × (3-year trailing operating surplus, floored at 0) + Cash − loan principal − accrued interest`, **floored at Book Net Worth** (labelled "liquidation-bound" when the floor binds — otherwise a rich-but-declining studio reads as "worth more dead than alive", an arbitrage signature any later acquisition system would inherit); purely financial — no Standing adjustment (that would read the same persistent-reputation channel the "Standing is not rank" law protects); tooltip: "A rough range from your recent earning power … Not a real offer. Nobody is bidding on your studio." Display-only, never consumed by any mechanic; never a single point number. (The 3–6× band is general small-business EBITDA practice surfaced during verification — PROVISIONAL, not project evidence.) Render Book Net Worth, Obligations and Wind-Down in one panel by default (burying Wind-Down behind a tab reopens the misreading it exists to prevent), and note at purchase time that capex is marked to book at the refund fraction (otherwise a headline net worth that drops the moment you build reads as a bug). Rivals have no itemized placements — their book value is a coarser proxy from `RivalAccount`'s lump `capacity` movement — and any cross-population ratio display needs a minimum comparable cohort (≥ 4 non-player studios), else it discloses the lone rival's figure by subtraction.

Worked examples (PROVISIONAL): a mid-game studio (cash $6.5M; 3 facilities; 4 sets; $5M loan; 8 contracts) has Book Net Worth **$4.25M**, Obligations $10.8M, Wind-Down **−$1.1M**, Value **$6.75–12.15M**; a hot debt-funded studio (cash $2M; heavy assets; $9M loan; $3.2M/yr surplus) has Book Net Worth **−$1.03M** but Value **$2.3–11.9M** — the "profitable company, negative book equity" case that proves both numbers must be shown and never merged.

Era scaling: state every limit as a multiple of a trailing observed quantity; rates as base (authored per era band or fixed real rate) + spread; never store a dollar constant in a covenant; book values carry the `costScale` of their build year unless an explicit revaluation event fires.

**Disclosure (new law, needs sign-off):** the player's own studio sees exact figures; every rival shows **band only**, never dollars — the same "public is a projection rule" boundary the game already applies to contract terms and rival cash. Computation symmetry is a build dependency: any loan/net-worth law is written once and applied identically to `RivalAccount` and the player ledger, as `updateStanding` already is.

**Ownership:** P11 owns Book Net Worth, Obligations, Wind-Down and loan math as read models over ledger truth (new P11 requirement; not blocked by REQ-041/042, which name loans/bankruptcy); Estimated Studio Value and the Financial Standing band are display definitions consumed by P15A.2 (and later by the disposal package); Valuation feeding an acquisition price is P15D/P16+ scope. **Prior text "valuation → P16+" is SUPERSEDED BY OWNER DIRECTION (C)**; the constraint that survives is literal P11 accounting and hidden rival cash.

## 5. Rival bankruptcy / failure model

**Owner direction (settled):** rivals can fail; healthy → warning → distress → severe distress → insolvency/bankruptcy → settlement/auction/closure; one bad movie must not bankrupt; failure from sustained inability to meet real obligations under the same law; identity never disappears; history accessible forever.

### 5.1 Why this is a new system — what exists today

Rivals never miss a payment: `moveRivalMoney` has no floor; payroll/overhead/opex debit unconditionally; below `operatingReserve` (12–20 weeks of cost, per authored `reserveWeeks`) a rival simply stops signing/commissioning/greenlighting and can run negative forever — a de-facto zombie with an indirect public signal (contracts expiring) but no state, event, or exit. The player side is the same law (`canAfford` refuses all positive voluntary commitments at cash < 0; nothing else happens; the D-16 lab measured the state as "mechanically absorbing"). **Prerequisites for any ladder:** a "missed due obligation" fact (P11), loans (so a real default can exist), and a status field on studio identity (a versioned root — `StudioIdentity` is exact-key validated).

### 5.2 Canonical trigger hierarchy (shared by player and rivals; presentation/selection differ)

| Stage | Trigger (PROVISIONAL constants) | Cure | Visible to player |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Healthy** | standard and typical-recent film affordable; cash not shrinking (`RecoveryPosition = healthy`, reusing the shipped classifier generalized to a `RivalAccount` projection) | — | — |
| **Warning** | `constrained` in **≥ 4 of the last 6 weeks** (rolling majority, never "consecutive" — a single lucky opening week must not reset it), OR cash < 8 weeks of fixed costs (payroll + overhead + Opex + debt service) in ≥ 4 of the last 6 weeks, OR a covenant (leverage/coverage/minimum-cash) breach (opens its 8-week cure) | condition clears over the same rolling window | rival: public stage + sourced reasons after disclosure threshold; player: ATTENTION notice with causes, runway, due obligations, remedies (the shipped `cashNegative` advance-stop is repurposed as this notice) |
| **Distress** | `severe`-equivalent in **≥ 13 of the last 16 weeks** | sustained recovery over the window | remedy menu opens (≥ 2 typed remedy families, equal eligibility/cost/timing for player and rival): release talent (50 %), delay/cancel an uncommitted plan, complete a conserved release, dormancy (suspend new commitments), Rescue loan |
| **Severe distress / Event of Default** | **(a) 4 missed weekly loan instalments within a rolling 13-week window** — only loan instalments are ever "missed" (payroll/overhead/Opex debit unconditionally); a missed instalment **capitalizes** onto the balance and the loan re-amortizes (otherwise missing 3 of every 13 for ever is a free 23 % discount); cure = catch up **every** currently-missed instalment; **or (b) the debt-free path: Distress sustained ≥ 26 of the last 32 weeks with no improving trend** — without (b) a studio that never borrows can never fail (the D-16 "absorbing" trap by another route) | as stated | acceleration: full balance due, default interest, no new borrowing |
| **Insolvency (bounded rescue window)** | automatic on an uncured Event of Default; **13 weeks**; visible clock; one window per distress episode (Owner choice: once per campaign, or re-armed only after ≥ 260 consecutive Healthy weeks — recommended) | return to at least a cured Distress | imposed remedy menu; Rescue loan offered if the studio qualifies; rivals accept deterministically whenever eligible and post-draw coverage improves (a P12 policy rule — without it recovery route 2 is player-only) |
| **Bankruptcy (terminal)** | rescue window expires uncured (a player may also **voluntarily declare** from Distress+ — Capitalism Lab's "declare" precedent — with no better terms than the automatic path) | — | settlement fires the same week; the studio's own loan balance is marked `writtenOff`, never deleted |
| **Settlement → Auction → Archived** | automatic, atomic within one tick, idempotent (a duplicate request returns the existing receipt) | — | see §8 and §9 |

Design rules the scenarios and the verifiers forced: (i) **a stage change needs an unpaid due obligation, a breached published covenant, or a sustained rolling-window condition — never a sign on cash and never Book Net Worth alone** (a conservatively-valued, debt-funded but current studio can carry negative book equity for years; letting that escalate would punish exactly the borrowing Direction F encourages — the player-failure lane's "net worth negative 26 weeks" disjunct was **dropped** in reconciliation; this exclusion is the analysis's recommendation, not Owner text, and is listed as a decision); (ii) **scheduled receipts in flight count** before a miss is declared (`pipelineRunRevenue`/`financeUpcoming` already exist) — bridging as a cooldown ("at least 13 weeks between uses"), bounded in practice by the 6-week run, so "one week before my release" never reads as an unearned loss; (iii) **cures are sustained** (MGT2's one-good-month reset is the exploit to avoid — hence rolling-majority tests everywhere, not consecutive counts); (iv) **clocks are visible** (GearCity's hidden non-resetting counter is the unfairness to avoid); (v) Warning and Distress have **no clock** — recovery remains possible indefinitely before an Event of Default; only the rescue window is bounded; (vi) **migration: every counter and window starts at zero at the first tick after upgrade, never backfilled** — an in-progress save already years in the red must still walk the whole ladder (law 7; otherwise patch day is a mass bankruptcy); (vii) all counters are pure selectors over persisted ledger facts (replay-deterministic), and single-player save-scumming of the rescue window is accepted as an ordinary sandbox caveat unless the Owner wants rescue outcomes seeded at window-open.

### 5.3 Paper scenarios (hypothetical, PROVISIONAL; code scale: $20M opening cash, ~$40–60k/week fixed burn + payroll, films $1–5M, 6-week runs at 52 %)

### 5.4 Settlement of a closed rival (game-legible rule)

- **Employment ends at the settlement week**: intervals already due end with the existing `expiry` reason; earlier-ending intervals end with a new reason `employerClosed`; every freed `PersonId` enters `freeAgents` that week. Unpaid payroll to the settlement week is paid first from the estate; the remaining guarantee is written down (no severance cash beyond that — a legible abstraction of wage priority; an explicit severance abstraction is an Owner option).
- **Conserved work in flight completes under an estate label**: a greenlit production (frozen participants, fully prepaid) finishes on schedule and releases; a theatrical run completes and settles — cancelling either would manufacture fiction against P07's frozen-result law and strand earned credits. **Undeveloped screenplays are cancelled** (`projectCancelled` receipt; concept/script IDs persist as a cancelled fact).
- **Assets**: facilities (abstract capacity for rivals) and cash go to creditors/settlement; the film library never transfers (no rights model); the studio name may become a dormant label only if a disposal slice exists.
- **History persists**: `StudioId`, films, people, credits, receipts and chart rows remain forever; the studio leaves the live cohort and gains a public `closed` state; a `studioClosed` receipt carries a fixed settlement summary (contract count, productions completing under estate, projects cancelled) and an origin cause ("missed loan instalments" / "sustained distress, no loan" / "failed rescue"), not a tautological "uncured distress". Rival rosters are architecturally single-digit, so the summary can be a fixed-size receipt; a player-scale closure needs the Annex's chunked manifest. **The load-bearing code seam the lane missed:** `advanceHollywoodWeek` iterates every business unconditionally — `staff()`/`decide()` must be gated on the operating status (a "closed" rival would otherwise hire a fresh roster and greenlight films for ever), while `operateStage`/run payouts stay unconditional for estate work and overhead/Opex stop once no production or run remains; the status root is keyed only to entered identities. P10 is a required settlement co-signer alongside P11/P12/P14 (P15 package §12.3, law 16); the D-11.9 "a writer mid-task cannot be released" guard is a solvent-employer courtesy that does not bind involuntary closure.
- **Dormancy**: pre-terminal distress never touches existing contracts (the estate keeps paying everyone on schedule; only new commitments are blocked — the existing reserve gate); a bounded **suspend-and-return** posture (Blockbuster Inc. precedent: rival pauses a year, then returns) is a legitimate remedy family and the natural posture during the Insolvency window. Whether a named public "dormant" state exists between Insolvency and closure is an Owner decision.

### 5.5 What makes bankruptcy feel earned — and the survivorship constraint

Earned = a visible chain of sourced causes (missed instalments, breached covenants, sustained shortfalls) with real remedy attempts on record; never a random headline, never one film. **Population math is the constraint no comparator faces:** exactly nine rival identities are ever minted, the last in 1969, with 71 more years to 2040 and no replacements. An illustrative constant annual terminal hazard (`9 × (1 − p)^120`) gives expected survivors by 2040 of ≈ 8.0 (0.1 %), 7.1 (0.2 %), 4.9 (0.5 %), 2.7 (1 %), 0.8 (2 %), 0.02 (5 %). **A hazard near 2 %/year or above very likely empties Hollywood before 2040 — a severe failure by the Owner's own standard; a 0.2–1 % band leaves a 3–8 survivor field consistent with "consolidation as emergent history."** This is not a target to hit by fiat (that would be the forbidden count-tuning); it is the acceptance band the trigger constants must be checked against in playtest, and it means no comparator's default (GearCity's 6-negative-months, Software Inc.'s same-day $0) may be copied at face value.

**Ownership:** P11 (missed-instalment fact, covenant read model, loan ledger); P15B (trigger hierarchy, clocks, remedy orchestration, terminal event, notice tiering); P12 (rival policy choosing among the same remedy families; registry commits the status transition inside a P15B-requested candidate; `employerClosed`/`studioClosed`/`projectCancelled` receipts); auction shape → the disposal package (§9).

## 6. Player bankruptcy / failure model

**Owner direction (settled, revising protected continuity):** the player's studio can ultimately fail with meaningful warning and recovery first; not "negative cash = game over".

**Governance prerequisite:** `docs/OWNER-RULINGS-HOLLYWOOD-HORIZON.md §3` (2026-08-18) states in writing that the player prohibition on "financing, loans, bailouts, restructuring, hard bankruptcy, failure ladder" is unchanged, and P11-REQ-041/042 are OWNER-BLOCKED. Direction E/F is exactly the "separate Owner gate" those rows ask for — but it exists only as this research pass's instruction. **A dated documentation-only ruling superseding §3 and re-dispositioning REQ-041/042 (plus D-16 R10's loan clause and D-17B §4) would need to be recorded before any charter could cite this direction.** Not a code change; not something this research may do.

1. **Insolvency vs ordinary negative cash.** Ordinary negative cash = the current state: voluntary commitments refused, unavoidable debits continue, nothing else (the original's build lockout, stricter). Insolvency = the uncured Event of Default state of §5.2: loan instalments missed 4× in a quarter, a covenant breach uncured, or Distress sustained ≥ 26 of 32 weeks with no loan — after Warning and Distress. The sign of cash never moves a stage; **sustained** negative cash (≥ 8 weeks) starts distress interest and, at 13 of 16 weeks, can sustain Distress. Fixed dollar thresholds (Two Point Hospital's −150k warn / −300k fail) were considered and rejected: a dollar constant cannot survive `era.costScale` across 1920–2040.
2. **Trigger** = the same hierarchy as rivals. The stage names are Direction D's own words, not an analyst proposal; only the thresholds and the two-stage reading of "insolvency/bankruptcy" are open. The shipped `classifyRecovery` (`healthy/constrained/severe`) is the reusable seed; `adapter.ts`'s existing `cashNegative` advance-stop is repurposed as the Warning-stage notice, not a gate.
3. **Warning quantity** (PROVISIONAL): ≥ 4 of 6 weeks in Warning before Distress can begin; ≥ 13 of 16 weeks of Distress before an Event of Default is even possible; 13-week rescue window with a visible clock; every stage shows causes, runway, due obligations and the remedy menu. Illustrative path for a 40-employee studio (fixed costs = payroll ≈ $57k + overhead $75k + Opex ≈ $23k + mortgage $14k ≈ $169k/week; 8-week liquidity line ≈ $1.35M): Healthy (wk 0, $20M) → Warning (~wk 180) → Distress (~wk 230) → Event of Default (~wk 243, 4th missed mortgage instalment) → Insolvency window → Bankruptcy (~wk 256) — roughly 76 weeks of visible warning from first notice to terminal (the lane's original timeline understated the burn by omitting payroll and Opex; the shape, not the dollars, is the point).

## 7. Loan / debt system recommendation

**Owner direction (settled):** studios can borrow; interest; must repay; too much debt can contribute to bankruptcy; simple; P11 likely owns the math.

### 7.1 Product comparison

| Product | Understanding | Strategic value | Exploitability | Rival compatibility | Bankruptcy interaction | UI burden | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed-term amortizing | HIGH (one weekly number, visible schedule) | HIGH (rate/term trade-off) | LOW (principal retires weekly) | HIGH (pure formula) | clean: a missed instalment is a discrete fact | LOW | **adopt (primary)** |
| Interest-only / bullet | MEDIUM (small payment, balloon ignored) | MEDIUM (bridge only) | HIGH (rollover chains) | HIGH | balloon is legible but chained rollovers hide exposure | LOW | reject as a standing product; allowed only inside the Rescue tier |
| Revolving line | MEDIUM (moves weekly; "max it day one") | LOW here | HIGH (OpenTTD/RCT max-loan snowball) | needs a second live balance | rarely reaches default | MEDIUM | reject as a product; **adopt its lesson** (interest must apply to sustained negative cash) |
| Several standardized tiers (A–D) | MEDIUM | HIGH | MEDIUM | HIGH | four default paths | HIGH | reject the four-tier menu; collapse to two |
| Comparator abstractions | HA researched ladder (gating good; 365-day cooldown = the strongest "trap" complaint); CS1/TPH fixed tiers (legible); GearCity line+loans+bonds+IPO (reject: finance sim); Blockbuster Inc. loans while negative (precedent for a rescue instrument) | | | | | | adopt gating-by-scale and fixed shape; reject cooldowns and bond/IPO stacks |

### 7.2 Recommended design — two products, one rule change (all numbers PROVISIONAL)

- **Studio Loan** (the only loan a healthy studio sees): fixed-term amortizing; term 5 or 10 years (260/520 weeks); rate = era base + spread by credit grade; weekly automatic unconditional repayment (same debit pattern as payroll); early repayment penalty-free; eligibility ≥ 52 weeks of history, no current default, positive trailing surplus; security = the studio's own facility/set book **at book-after-salvage** (the existing 0.5/0.35 fractions), never at cost. Purpose-agnostic (cash is fungible in the code; do not pretend to track use of proceeds).
- **Rescue loan** (Distress+ only, not player-selectable on demand): offered by the P15B condition machine as one of the typed remedy families (GearCity's inline Cut Funding/Cut Production is the precedent; Blockbuster Inc. shows borrowing while negative is shipped practice); small, ≤ 3 years, heaviest spread; identical mechanics; identical for rivals.
- **Rule change — interest on sustained negative cash:** after cash has been below zero for 8 sustained weeks, distress interest accrues weekly on the negative balance at the plain base rate (no spread, no punitive multiple), debited like overhead. This closes OpenTTD's "overdraft beats borrowing" hole without a second product, and gives the ladder a second, independent finance input. It does not make the sign of cash a trigger.
- **Borrowing limit:** total debt ≤ **3× trailing-52-week operating surplus** AND interest coverage ≥ **3×** AND minimum cash ≥ **8 weeks of fixed costs** (the liquidity covenant the finance lane recommended and the loan lane dropped — without it a studio borrows to the ceiling and spends to zero), whichever is most restrictive, with collateral at book-after-salvage as a hard ceiling. The arithmetic shows coverage binds first at 8 %/10 yr (a $1.5M-surplus studio reaches 3× coverage at ≈ 2.3× leverage, ≈ $3.44M of debt) — present the rule as "the tightest cap governs", not as if 3× leverage is usually reachable.
- **Rates:** base authored per era band (e.g., 5–6 % 1920s, 2–3 % ~1950, 10–20 % ~1980, 3–7 % 2010–25, authored 2026–40) or one fixed real rate with `costScale` carrying prices — an Owner decision; spread by a deterministic three-band credit grade (Good +2 / Watch +4 / Impaired +8, Rescue only) from history, capacity and collateral — no dice.
- **Default rule:** a missed instalment is a recorded fact whose **principal and interest capitalize onto the balance** (the loan re-amortizes over its remaining term — otherwise skipped instalments are free); **Event of Default = 4 missed weekly instalments within a rolling 13-week window; cure = catch up every currently-missed instalment**; covenant breach → Warning with an 8-week cure; cross-default across both products; **acceleration is the only finance event that may originate Severe Distress**, and only a covenant breach or the 8-week sustained-negative-cash fact may originate a finance-sourced Warning.
- **Refinancing:** only from Stable, only into a tier the studio qualifies for, fee 1–2 % rising with each successive refinance of the same obligation — where "refinance" is **functional**: any new loan whose proceeds retire ≥ 50 % of an existing balance within 4 weeks counts against the original obligation's correlation id, so penalty-free early repayment cannot be used to dodge the escalating fee.
- **Rivals:** draw a Studio Loan preventively when projected weeks-to-reserve-exhaustion ≤ N, trailing surplus is positive and the caps allow; **accept an offered Rescue whenever eligible and post-draw coverage improves** — a deterministic P12 rule without which recovery route 2 is player-only. Correlated covenant breaches across genre-concentrated rivals in one bad quarter (a failure wave) are a real tuning risk to pressure-test, not a single-studio question.
- **Labelling the prior text precisely:** P15 §16's sentence "Loans, bailouts, investors, forced sales, or acquisition are not implied" is SUPERSEDED for *loans* (F) and *acquisition* (I) only; bailouts, investors and forced sales remain excluded exactly as written. The Rescue loan is not a bailout: it is priced, interest-bearing, repayable, and available to rivals on identical terms.

## 8. Failed-studio talent settlement

**Owner direction (settled):** talent becomes free agency, not lost with the corporation; large industry notice; no duplicate PersonIds; P14/P12 employment truth settles lawfully.

**Code ground truth:** `IndustryEmployment` rows end via `endedWeek` + a closed reason vocabulary (rival reasons are `entry/renewal/replacement`; `termination` is player-only and validator-forbidden for rivals — `hollywoodValidation.ts:165, 357-358, 399`); expiry unions people into `state.freeAgents`; one-employer exclusivity is enforced at hire and at validation; `PersonId` is minted once and never re-minted; contracts carry only salary/bonus/term (no guarantees or buyouts); rival productions have frozen participants; conserved projects must resolve explicitly. **Nothing exists for an employer that disappears** — there is no orphaning transition, and a closure that ended rival contracts early would today fail validation at three points.

**Recommended game-legible rule (one settlement week; two clocks):**

| Question | Rule |
|---|---|
| Do all contracts terminate immediately? | Every open interval at the closed studio ends **at the settlement week** — no wind-down employment. Intervals already due end with the existing `expiry` reason; earlier-ending intervals end with a **new reason `employerClosed`**. Nobody's job is ever at risk from a pre-terminal stage; only the terminal event ends contracts (the estate keeps paying in full until then; the existing reserve gate merely blocks new commitments). |
| Guarantees / severance? | Unpaid payroll to the settlement week is paid first from the estate (legible wage-priority abstraction); the remaining guarantee is **written down** — no cash to the person beyond that. A bounded severance abstraction (e.g., N weeks) is an Owner option, not a recommendation; the original had none. |
| Do active productions delay release? | **Conserved, fully prepaid work completes under an estate label** (greenlit productions with frozen participants finish and release; runs in progress complete and settle) — cancelling would fabricate a result against P07's frozen-result law and strand earned credits; its people are still freed at the settlement week (the estate does not employ them; the credit is already frozen). **Undeveloped screenplays (no `productionId`) are cancelled** with a `projectCancelled` receipt; concept/script IDs persist as a cancelled historical fact. |
| Does bankruptcy override expiration? | Yes, for intervals ending after the settlement week (`employerClosed`); no retroactive change to intervals already due. |
| How may others sign them? | Freed people enter `freeAgents` at the settlement week under the ordinary hiring law (P14 competitive-market rules when P14 exists); no first refusal for an asset buyer unless the disposal package says so. |
| Residual obligations of the failed employer? | None after settlement; the archive keeps the history. |

Worked example (rival with six staggered contracts, one production at tick 4/8, one run at week 3/6, two screenplays): four contracts end `employerClosed`, two end `expiry` on their natural week, all six PersonIds enter free agency at week 1000; the production completes at week 1004 and releases; the run pays weeks 4–6 and settles; both screenplays cancel; one `studioClosed` receipt carries the settlement summary; every ID persists. Rival rosters are structurally single-digit (fixed capacity), so a fixed-size receipt suffices; a player-scale closure must use the Annex's ≤ 100-row chunked manifest.

**Additive roots / validator changes:** new `IndustryReceipt` kinds (`studioClosed`, `projectCancelled`, and a closure-settlement family) — the union is closed and the validator throws on unknown kinds; employment end-reason vocabulary gains `employerClosed`; a public operating status (`active | dormant | closed`) lives on a **versioned root**, not by widening exact-key `StudioIdentity`; `policy.version` is pinned, so any rival distress-posture field is also versioned. **Ownership:** P12 commits the roster/employer/interval dispositions inside a P15B-requested transition; P14 inherits the free-agency market once implemented (an explicit "P14 absent" disposition is needed until then).

## 9. Bankruptcy auction and M&A recommendation

**Owner direction (settled):** eventual ability to buy other studios; package boundary and shape open; do not assume all of M&A belongs in core P15. Acquisition is successor design — never parity (pre-release 2002 only).

### 9.1 One price basis, four price mechanics, two post-sale states

**Frozen Reference Price (FRP)** = Σ facility refund value (ledger-recorded capex × 0.5; rivals' abstract capacity priced the same way from the lump `capacity` movement) + Σ set refund value (× 0.35) + cash − outstanding obligations (loan principal; termination cost only where contracts are terminated rather than transferred). Using the *recorded* capex rather than today's catalog price is a **new design choice** (the live demolition refund prices off today's blueprint; the P11 handoff sentence often cited as law governs finance-history rows and is an inference by analogy here). **Excludes the film library** (no rights/chain-of-title model; historical creator/owner facts are immutable). Frozen at the moment of offer, never recomputed per bidder (OpenTTD's `bankrupt_value` discipline). The six requested shapes collapse to four price mechanics × two post-sale states:

| Shape | Price | Debt | Contracts | Projects | Identity afterwards |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Asset-only liquidation | piecemeal refund value | paid first from proceeds | terminated at 50 % | struck/cancelled | closed |
| 2 Whole-studio auction | FRP | extinguished before sale | transfer with consent or run to expiry | transfer | buyer's choice |
| 3 Assumption of debt | assets + cash, buyer takes the loan | transfers | with consent | transfer | buyer's choice — **economically degenerate whenever debt > assets** (a rational buyer needs an inducement); never present as free equity |
| 4 Clean purchase after creditors settle | max(0, FRP); creditor recovers pro-rata | extinguished (creditor haircut) | with consent | transfer | buyer's choice — **the only sound "buy it" path for an insolvent studio** |
| 5 Subsidiary / label continuation | FRP × a going-concern premium | assumed | continue unchanged | continue | **label persists** with its own Standing and its own releases |
| 6 Absorption | ≈ shape 2 | extinguished | with consent, then absorbed | fold or cancel | closes as an operator; history persists |

### 9.2 Paper scenarios (PROVISIONAL)

- **A. Small failed studio** (2 facilities, 5 employees, 3 films, little debt): FRP small and positive; shapes 1/2/4 all trivial; the only interesting question is whether the label survives (shape 5) — cheap because it reuses the existing rival AI policy.
- **B. Valuable distressed studio** (strong Standing, 6 facilities, 12 contracts, 40-film library, $30M debt vs $22M assets): FRP = **−$8M**. Liquidation returns creditors 73 cents on the dollar; a clean whole-studio sale is infeasible without funding the shortfall; debt assumption is a structurally negative price; **clean purchase after settlement at $0–1 nominal** (creditor haircut $8M) is the only sound path; label continuation is only possible **before** insolvency (in Warning/Distress), at a price that must include a brand premium book value cannot capture. Structural note: **shape-5 pricing cannot be built before the Direction-C valuation read model exists** — the premium must come from the same Financial Standing / Estimated Studio Value numbers the player already sees, not a bespoke M&A engine; shapes 1/2/4 need nothing beyond FRP.
- **C. Large failed rival** (the lane's "20 employees, 3 active projects" exceeds a rival's shipped capacity — one stage, single-digit roster; read it as a player-scale or capacity-grown studio): per-project buyer choice (continue as going concern or liquidate at 35 % + 50 % termination); contracts only with consent; debt priced in or extinguished, never silently transferred (OpenTTD's acquirer does *not* inherit the loan — the wiki text describing share trading is legacy); the set-refund arithmetic in scenario B is $2.135M at 35 %, not $2.15M.

### 9.3 Bidding, transfers, identity, guardrails

- **Eligibility filter (always on):** a **post-acquisition leverage cap on the buyer's own balance sheet** (total loan principal after the purchase ≤ the loan caps of §7 on combined surplus — "cash-only" is unenforceable because cash is fungible), and **no bidding while in Warning or worse** (Football Manager's embargo-under-administration precedent).
- **Order rule (Owner choice):** **sealed simultaneous offers** recommended as default (fairest, no first-mover advantage, identical for player and AI), collected during the settlement stage of the ladder (a minimum-length bidding window, never same-tick, so a human can act) and **resolved deterministically at window close in one tick** with an explicit tie-break (founding-order seniority) — identical bids from deterministic rivals are the normal case, not an edge case; pending bids need a small additive root or the window resolves synchronously. Leader-first (OpenTTD asks the best-performing company first) is viable only with a dominance guard and best matches "consolidation as emergent history"; worst-first is not shipped anywhere; player-last/player-premium (GearCity charges the player more "to fix player exploits") is a variant to keep open. A bid that would leave **zero independent active studios** is blocked (or surfaces Direction G's severe-failure notice) — an Owner choice.
- **What transfers:** facilities as abstract capacity (rival buyer — a player lot collapses to a capability tally if a rival ever acquires it) or physical placements (player buyer — capped at buildable footprint, excess auto-refunded at 50 %); contracts only with consent or to expiry; projects by buyer choice; debt per shape; **films/library never** until P16 rights exist; other employees → free agency. `StudioIdentity.role` is a fixed `'player' | 'rival'` literal today: whether it transitions on ownership change (a rival-controlled ex-player studio) must be stated by the charter.
- **StudioId permanence:** an acquisition is one more append-only event on the same identity; a label needs (i) an ownership edge on a **versioned root** (`GameStateV20 = GameStateV19 & { ownership }` pattern; pre-existing saves default to `ownedBy: null`), (ii) per-label Standing (already per-business), (iii) a greenlight policy — **autonomous** (label keeps its rival AI policy; owner collects cash flows: Railway Empire "keep it operating", Offworld's AI-run subsidiary) reuses existing code end to end; **player-directed** greenlight is a second decision loop and the single largest scope item. Later resale and rival-to-rival acquisitions fall out for free once the edge and FRP exist as an N-party mechanism.
- **Anti-snowball:** the leverage cap; a per-buyer cooldown (RT2 once-a-year) **scoped to the ultimate controlling `StudioId`** (follow `ownedBy` to the root — otherwise each freshly bought label carries a fresh clock); no bidding while distressed; acquired debt transfers or is priced, never erased; optional dominance guard. The biggest snowball risk is not any single price formula but **repeated near-zero clean-shell purchases with leader-first order and no cooldown** — a buyer that rolls up the ecosystem is the *cause* of the severe consolidation failure Direction G asks to be reported; a single settlement week also dumps a whole roster into free agency at once, favouring whichever studio has the most idle cash.

## 10. Consolidation / no-replacement analysis

**Owner direction (settled):** no automatic replacements; authored arrivals preserved; no artificial floor; report a severe failure rather than floor silently.

**Conclusion: consolidation is acceptable as selected — say so plainly.** Every mechanical surface degrades gracefully toward one or zero rivals: the shared-market law is symmetric and self-excluding per studio (fewer studios = less saturation = easier releases, an earned emergent benefit); the tick loop is a plain iteration; Power Ranking's `rank = 1 + count(greater)` and `sameCohort` work for any cohort size; performance improves as the field thins (closed studios must leave the weekly loop once a status exists). The "minimum three active AI rivals" was a P12 **design/benchmark recommendation that was never Owner law** (P12A register SIM-015: "not Owner law") and is **SUPERSEDED BY OWNER DIRECTION (G)**; no comparator praises a synthetic floor; the closest analogue to authored-window entrants (GearCity) ships without one; player complaints about empty late games are about **unopposed dominance and AI passivity**, not head-count.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Does market competition still work at 4/2/1? | Yes — pressure simply falls; the survivor's own releases face less friction (legitimate, earned). Drama/crime are thin from day one regardless (roster authoring). |
| Does the talent market become too easy? | **This is the one severe risk.** Directions G and H compound in one direction: each closure dumps a roster into free agency *and* removes a bidder. Illustrative (PROVISIONAL): 10 → 8 studios: demand 48 roles, ~12 freed; 8 → 4: demand 24, ~36 freed; 4 → 2: demand 12, ~48 freed; 2 → 1: the player is the only employer. Salary pressure collapses; a growing pool of named, never-rehired people sits idle for decades — a legibility problem as much as a difficulty one. |
| Does genre saturation vanish? | It shrinks, never vanishes (the player's own releases still saturate); the saturation stock is per genre, not per population. |
| Does Power Ranking become silly with 3 rows? | No — "#1 of 3" is still informative; show "3 active, 7 closed" and keep closed studios as permanent historical rows; the minimum eligible-cohort size for publishing is an open UX decision (the `insufficient-history` state already exists). A wealth leaderboard of 3 rows would read as private-ledger exposure — another point for Model D's band. |
| Does M&A create irreversible snowballing? | Only without the §9 guards (post-acquisition leverage cap, root-scoped cooldown, no bidding while distressed, sealed offers). |
| Is two AI rivals enough? Is one? | Mechanically yes to both; experientially an emergent, late, rare tail of a 120-year run is a different thing from Transport Fever 2's authored zero-AI design. The risk is only real if it becomes *common* — which is why the failure rate (below) matters more than any head-count rule. |
| Should mechanics make survivors stronger? | Only emergently, under the same law and at the same cost: free agents gravitating to the remaining bidders (already true); lower per-release friction (already true); **rival post-entry capacity growth** (a survivor spends capex to add a stage/dev slots, raising its cadence and headcount — the same law the player has; **not implemented for rivals today**, entry-time capacity only). Forbidden: any multiplier keyed to rival count; free capacity/staff granted on a closure. |
| Do the triggers make consolidation likely or rare? | **Decisive, and undecided by the direction:** if rivals never borrow and hold their authored reserves they may never cross into insolvency — a floor by omission; if rivals borrow under the same law, failure becomes possible and **differentiated by the already-authored policy spread** (`reserveWeeks` 12–20, `negativeScale` 0.94–1.20, `marketingRatio` 0.14–0.24 — Bright Meridian is authored as the most exposed, Night Orchard the most conservative). Recommend symmetric rival borrowing and letting the rate emerge from that spread; the calibration check is the §5.5 hazard band (≈ 0.2–1 %/year), never a target count. |

Four risks the adversarial pass added that the lane itself missed: (1) **patch-day mass failure** — rivals can already be years in the red; every distress clock must start at zero on the first tick after migration (rule vi in §5.2), or the new law reads old history as "sustained"; (2) **genre-anchor clustering** — three of nine rivals anchor romance and three anchor horror, so a bad run in one genre can remove all its defenders in a burst; track the per-genre active-anchor count as its own signal; (3) **Endless Sandbox makes full consolidation a certainty** — a death process with no births converges to zero rivals with probability one given unbounded time; say so in the new-game disclosure and the Endless mode marker; (4) **the mirror case** — the player fails first while rivals continue is the same law running the other way (settlement + postmortem; §6). Passive hoarding as a path to dominance is bounded by the same fixed costs and the shared market, not by any rule.

**Least-intrusive safeguards, ranked:** (1) an Owner-visible "this industry may consolidate; no studio is ever replaced" disclosure at new game — adopt, zero mechanic cost; (2) the player's own closure under the same law (already decided — the real counterweight to a lone survivor); (3) dormancy-with-return as a remedy family (already required by "recovery first"; Blockbuster Inc. precedent); (4) **rival post-entry capacity growth** — recommended as the actual fix for the talent-market risk, but it is new P12 scope; (5) an opt-in authored late-entrant pack at world creation (non-synthetic in fiction; real production cost; NBA 2K MyNBA is a user setting, not simulated consolidation); (6) a GearCity-style monopoly lawsuit as a general mechanic — **recommended against** (it is the mirror image of the rejected floor: an artificial downward force on earned dominance); if the Owner wants a dominance guard, make it an **acquisition-eligibility rule** in P15D, not a penalty on organic success; the 1948 divestiture is finale narrative material, not a mechanic.

## 11. Finale and Endless Sandbox

**Owner direction (settled):** ceremony + interactive Legacy dossier; no single score; multiple archetypes; every claim traces to evidence; post-2040 Endless Sandbox that never rewrites the frozen 1920–2040 Legacy.

### 11.1 Ceremony
The ceremony is the **trigger and celebration**; the dossier is the **record**. Freeze `legacyFinaleSnapshot` (source manifest, ≤ 8 archetypes with ≤ 12 qualifying + ≤ 12 contrary refs each, ≤ 12 lens summaries, completeness notices, interpretation/presentation version) at the governed week **before** anything is presented; the ceremony reads from the manifest and never computes. ### 11.3 Endless Sandbox
- **One explicit, irreversible mode event** presented on the finale screen, recorded as `postFinaleMode`, with two real choices: "End the campaign here" (permanently forecloses Endless; archive stays browsable) or "Continue in Endless Sandbox" — a separate "browse the archive" button is a view, not a third state. Comparators: Civ IV/V ("victory no longer a factor"), Civ VII (One More Turn with an infinity marker and a named exit — retained through 1.4.0, which replaced Legacy Paths with Triumphs; do not cite the 1.2.0 Legacy-Path wording as current), CK3's pre-set rule, Frostpunk's named modes; Vic3's "Switch Country" workaround is the confusion to avoid. "End Endless play" is a resumable pause, not a second freeze.
- **Frozen forever:** the 2040 manifest, archetype set, completeness notices, interpretation/presentation version, the mode event. Nothing in Endless play may append to, re-rank or supersede them (Civ IV: "further accomplishments will not be recorded"; OpenRCT2 sets the completion value once). **Enforce it at the engine, not the UI:** the frozen record carries a marker inside its existing opaque `checkpointJson` that the load/tick path refuses to advance (no schema bump); a "never offer the button" convention cannot satisfy Direction K's "must not rewrite". The `eligible → source-frozen` transition commits atomically within one tick/save write; a migrated save already past the 2040 week evaluates eligibility on its ordinary tick and freezes the span where it evaluates true, never backdated. Post-2040 evidence IDs continue the same identifier sequences — only the presentation record is second, not the identifier space. Add one more honest degrade beside "not recorded before week N": **"too few comparable rivals to characterize a dominance period"** — decades of 90 %+ share against one zombie rival must not read as an implicit score.
- **Continues:** the simulation; Standing; Power Ranking and awards ceremonies if the Owner wants them (Stellaris: victory "of little concern", play continues); rival distress and failure; **the player can still fail post-2040** under the same law — a consequence-free tail would be its own unlabelled boundary; the no-replacement rule continues unchanged (no Endless-only floor).
- **Endless gets its own dated record set** ("Endless Sandbox record, post-2040-W##"), never interleaved with the 1920–2040 chronology without a hard visual boundary; a permanent HUD marker ("Endless Sandbox — legacy frozen 2040-W##") and a named exit.
- **Era/technology content policy (labelled menu; P13 not built yet):** default **freeze at the last authored era** (OpenTTD "never expire" semantics; the original's own 1999–2005 tail, but labelled this time); a **repeatable generic stub** only where a system needs a sink (Stellaris repeatables / Civ Future Tech), never with fabricated names or dates; a **procedural future** only if separately authorized and shipped with a persistent disclaimer (GearCity "random history mode": "not much post-2020 testing"), never exposed as recorded history. **No fabricated future history** is a hard rule regardless of policy. P13 should plan its catalogue with an explicit "last authored era" marker and a post-catalogue policy field from the start.
- **Save As support:** Save As already yields a frozen-by-copy branch with the original record preserved; the read-only "2040 archive" record uses the in-checkpoint frozen marker above (a library-format flag is the heavier alternative). Never hardcode the horizon week in presentation (OpenTTD issue #8625). The dossier's 16-domain manifest cap counts domains, not sub-views; the lens table above uses eleven domains.

**Reconciliation note:** the 2026-08-18 Horizon ruling already permits continuation beyond 2040 and "plausible alternate-future progression" (c2-planning 00A); the P13–P15 rulings say Endless "remains undecided … P15 may not silently authorize it". Direction K decides it explicitly — record it, and note that "Finale and stop" is now the option not taken.

### 12.3 Rankings, value presentation, consolidation, events

- **Transparent multi-factor ranking:** OpenTTD's Company League Table is nine published, capped, linear components (delivered cargo 40 %; cash 5 %; loan 5 %; no "company value" component — verifier correction), shown in-game as `(value/needed)` per row with tooltips; **quarterly**; the Company Value graph is a **separate** window; the rating has diegetic consequences (HQ growth, order of bankruptcy sale offers). Simutrans ranks by **one sortable criterion at a time** (Revenue, Profit, Cash, Net Wealth, …) — no composite. Capitalism Lab publishes separate revenue / profit / market-cap rankings plus a personal Billionaires list. GearCity has separate Company Directory, Market Cap and **Networth** reports. Football Manager's club reputation is opaque (0–10000) and players want it inspectable. MGT2's studio rating "has no impact" (dev, 2021) — an inert comparative number is dismissed.
- **Book vs sale value:** OpenTTD computes three different numbers — company value (assets − loan + cash, graphed), bankruptcy price (assets + cash), hostile price (assets + loan + 2 years profit) — and shows the sale prices **only in the offer dialog/headline**. GearCity shows Evaluation (assets at cost less debt) and market cap separately and prices control as a third number. Software Inc.'s undocumented stock-implied value is the cautionary case.
- **Consolidation:** GearCity uses authored founding years, lets the field thin through the economy, has no synthetic respawn, and caps dominance with lawsuits; OpenTTD refills to a setting on a game-tick timer (hidden rule; the entrant reads as synthetic; no player praises it); Capitalism Lab players say bankrupting everyone "takes the challenge out" but attribute it to **AI passivity**, not count; Transport Fever 2 ships no AI and one thread calls that "a deal breaker … no competitive objective" (community, low). The recurring complaint is **unopposed dominance**, not the absence of a floor.
- **Events:** OpenTTD's staged big-font newspaper chain (trouble → sale → bankrupt/merger → new company) is non-modal; sale price disclosed, hostile price "undisclosed"; per-category news volume Off/Summary/Full. Capitalism Lab pauses only on accepting an offer. GearCity added a "Wait" button after a bare bankruptcy-auction memo caused blind purchases — **decision-bearing events need disclosure and a pause affordance; plain news must not pause**.

### 12.4 Finale and continuation

- Every inspected long-run title writes its official record **once** at the trigger and never re-scores it (Civ IV manual: further accomplishments "will not be recorded"; OpenRCT2 sets the completion company value once; Civ VI GS Hall of Fame written at the victory trigger; OpenTTD 2050 high score). Continuation is either explicitly allowed and labelled (Civ IV/V "victory no longer a factor"; Civ VII 1.2.0 "Just One More Turn" with an infinity marker and a named exit — retained through 1.4.0, which replaced Legacy Paths with Triumphs), a pre-set rule (CK3 1453 / No End Date; OpenTTD ending year), or a UI workaround that confuses players within days (Victoria 3 "Switch Country"; EU4 non-ironman).
- Post-horizon content policies observed: freeze the last era via a toggle (OpenTTD "never expire vehicles"), a repeatable generic stub (Stellaris repeatables, Civ Future Tech), or a procedural mode shipped with a disclaimer that it is less tested (GearCity "random history mode"). **Nobody authors fake future history.**
- Finale presentation: features players trust are typed, dated logs (Civ VI Timeline / Historic Moments with world-first vs your-first; Dwarf Fortress Legends; CK2 Chronicle); interpretation layers (CK3 Legends) carry their own status tiers and are partly authored; score-to-title ladders (OpenTTD Engineer→Tycoon) read as a single score however dressed; removing previously visible history is punished (FM26 club history; Civ VI launch without replay/HoF). Civ VII launched without One More Turn or a post-game summary and patched both in within ten weeks — shipping a finale without an epilogue/continue path is a product risk. Firaxis's own framing for Legends is "Make Losing Fun"; a small CivFanatics thread asks for "where you struggled" graphs, a designated golden age, runner-up recognition and a replayable chronology (illustrative, not a demand measure).
- The original's own pattern — tally at a fixed ceremony, then **unlabelled** continuation with nothing new — is the weakest version of "frozen result + endless tail"; the successor must label what the original left implicit.
