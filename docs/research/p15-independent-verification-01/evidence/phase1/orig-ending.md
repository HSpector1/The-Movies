# Original *The Movies* (2005): Campaign Ending, Post-Ending Continuation, Lifetime Honors, Sandbox, Pre-Release Promises

Evidence reader report for Project: Studio P15 (Corporate Hollywood, Shared Market & Studio Legacy). READ-ONLY research; nothing under any repository was modified. Written 2026-09-11.

## 0. Scope, method, and source tiers

Method: (1) grep the plain-text extractions of the manual, Prima guide, two GameFAQs guides and the gamepressure page; (2) confirm exact page numbers against the PDFs with `pdftotext -f N -l N` and visual `Read` of the key Prima and manual pages; (3) recover pre-release and contemporary web coverage through Wayback Machine captures (live GameSpot/Eurogamer/Fandom/TV Tropes pages returned 403/402/Cloudflare challenges); (4) check the compiled mechanics bible only to locate underlying sources, never as a source itself; (5) grep the accepted-code snapshot for any existing finale/lifetime/sandbox implementation.

Page conventions used below:
- **Prima** = *The Movies: Prima Official Game Guide* (Greg Kramer, Prima Games, 2005). Page numbers are the printed page numbers; the PDF page index is printed+1 (verified: printed p.80 = PDF page 81).
- **Manual** = *The Movies* PC manual (Activision/Lionhead, QXP dated 9/27/05). The PDF is a 22-page two-up spread; printed pp.4–5 = PDF page 3, pp.6–7 = PDF page 4, pp.22–23 = PDF page 12 (verified visually for PDF page 3).

Source tiers (as required): **RETAIL SHIPPED MECHANIC** (manual/Prima/retail walkthrough), **PRIMA/MANUAL EVIDENCE**, **CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL** (retail-era reviews), **OFFICIAL DEVELOPER STATEMENT** (Lionhead's own site, pre-ship but retained post-ship), **PRE-RELEASE PROMISE** (previews 2002–May 2005), **COMMUNITY INFERENCE** (wikis, forums, retrospectives).

## 1. Campaign ending: what happened at and after the 2005 awards ceremony

### 1.1 The horizon is 2005, and play continues past it

| # | Claim | Source / location | Tier | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The game is playable past 2005, but "reward-wise" it ends there; the 85 years of studio history are "tallied up in your Lifetime Honor". | Prima p.80 (PDF 81), "Lifetime Honors" opening paragraph (visually confirmed). | PRIMA (developer-reviewed; Awards chapter credits Tim Spencer of Lionhead, p.81) | HIGH |
| 2 | Lionhead's own FAQ answer to "Is there an ending in the game?": starts early 1920s, goes on until 2005, "won't stop there", keep playing "for as long as you like with all of the game's technology unlocked". Same answer in the "game modes" entry: Story Mode runs "from the early 1920s till 2005 and beyond". | Lionhead official site FAQ, `lionhead.com/themovies/faq.html`, Wayback captures 2005-05-13 and 2005-11-26 (identical text pre- and post-ship). | OFFICIAL DEVELOPER STATEMENT (written pre-ship, retained post-ship) | HIGH that this was Lionhead's stated design; MEDIUM as retail proof on its own (corroborated by Prima row 1) |
| 3 | Reward content is front-loaded well before 2005: the research-pack schedule's last automatic unlock is 1999 ("Late 1990s" costumes); nothing in the Prima pack table unlocks in 2000–2005. | Prima p.87 (PDF 88), research pack unlock table. | PRIMA | HIGH |
| 4 | Award categories stop being added after 1975 (one new award every ceremony 1925–1975); the ceremony recurs every five years from 1925. | Prima p.77–78 (PDF 78–79); Manual p.22 ("Every five years an awards ceremony takes place"). | RETAIL SHIPPED MECHANIC | HIGH |
| 5 | Rival studios stop arriving after the 1967–1971 window (nine named rivals; the last, Booboo & Dingo Films, appears 1967–1971). | Prima p.51 (PDF 52), "Rival Studios" table. | PRIMA | HIGH |
| 6 | "You can't actually lose": IGN's retail review says it is "nearly impossible to actually lose, though it's not guaranteed that you'll actually win". | IGN review, Dan Adams, 8/10, Nov 2005 (`ign.com/articles/the-movies`). | CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL | HIGH |
| 7 | Community claim that the timeline is "1920–2020" (a "100-year career") or that "technologies tail off in 2020". | forceforgood.co.uk retrospective (Rik, 8 Dec 2012); Codex Gamicus wiki (Wikipedia-derived). | COMMUNITY INFERENCE | LOW; contradicted by Prima rows 1 and 3 (85 years to 2005; last unlock 1999) |

**What no inspected source establishes:** whether the five-yearly awards ceremony *event* keeps firing after 2005 (with no new honors at stake) or stops. Prima says only that rewards end; the Lionhead FAQ says only that play continues with all technology unlocked. Prima's Platinum walkthrough ends with "After the 2005 ceremony, make a big film with the Diner set" (p.84, PDF 85), which confirms post-2005 play but says nothing about further ceremonies. Treat "ceremonies continue past 2005" as **UNVERIFIED**.

### 1.2 Lifetime Honors (the terminal evaluation)

Prima p.80 (PDF 81) is the only detailed retail source. Structure:

| Tier | Requirement (evaluated by/at the 2005 ceremony) | Reward | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| (none) | Complete the 2005 ceremony without all nine Achievement Awards: "you get, well, nothing". | nothing | Prima p.80 |
| **Gold Lifetime Honor** | All nine Achievement Awards earned by the end of the 2005 ceremony. | Sci-Fi: Starship Bridge 3 set, usable in the current studio and in all Sandbox games. | Prima p.80; set entry "Available: Gold Lifetime Achievement Honor", Prima p.23 (PDF 24), cost $56,618. |
| **Platinum Lifetime Honor** | All nine Achievement Awards AND each of the 13 ceremony awards won a prescribed number of times. | Suburban: Diner set + a special bonus credits sequence ("go to the game's main menu and select Credits"). | Prima p.80–81; set entry "Available: Platinum Lifetime Honor", Prima p.24 (PDF 25), cost $58,804. |

Platinum per-award win counts (Prima p.81, PDF 82): Best Acting Performance 5; Best Direction 5; Best Employer 5; Highest Charting Movie 5; Highest Charting Newcomer 2; Highest Charting Star 5; Highest Charting Studio 5; Highest Climbing Star 2; Highest Climbing Studio 2; Most Prestigious Studio Lot 13; Most Prolific Star 3; Most Prolific Studio 2; Movie Quality Output 2. Prima adds that "the earliest you can hope to nab this award is the 1990 awards ceremony" (p.81), i.e. the Platinum checklist can be completed before 2005 but is only paid out as a Lifetime Honor.

The nine Achievement Awards (Prima p.79–80, PDF 80–81) are the campaign's reward ladder; Prima says they "must be completed in order" and each unlocks a facility or set the moment its requirements are met (Custom Script Office, Rural: Forest, Proficient Script Office, Publicity Office, Urban: Wall Section, First-Class Script Office, Palatial Trailer, Urban: Municipal Building, Suburban: School Library). The manual (p.22, "Movie Mogul Ranks") calls the same ladder "nine official ranks of studio owner", starting at "Greenhorn", and says every prize "unlocked in New Game mode will also become available for you to use in Sandbox mode".

**Source conflict inside Prima (new finding):** the Facilities chapter, Prima p.14 (PDF 15), says you "may be eligible for one of the three Lifetime Achievement awards", each unlocking "a coveted set", granted for "getting a studio to 2005", for all nine Achievement Awards by 2005, or for all nine plus multiple ceremony wins. The Awards chapter (p.80) says explicitly there is *nothing* for merely reaching 2005, and the set catalogue lists only two Lifetime-gated sets (Starship Bridge 3, Diner). The GameFAQs Maxx FAQ (v0.24, PDF p.8) repeats the three-tier wording ("running a studio into 2005, winning all nine ... or winning all nine ... and winning each reward ceremony more than once"), so the three-tier phrasing may derive from in-game or pre-final text. Verdict: two rewarded tiers is the better-supported reading (HIGH for Gold/Platinum existing; MEDIUM that there is no third "reached 2005" reward). No inspected source shows a Bronze/Silver Lifetime reward object.

### 1.3 Was there a final score, ending screen, comparison of studios, or retrospective?

- **No inspected source describes an ending screen, a final numeric score, a studio-versus-studio end comparison, or a history/retrospective presentation at 2005.** Prima's only terminal presentation is the Lifetime Honor itself (a set unlock; for Platinum also the bonus credits reel, whose stills on p.80 show Lionhead staff as movie characters). Prima p.80 does not describe a certificate screen for the Lifetime Honor; the "certificate" language in Prima (p.77) belongs to the nine Achievement Awards ("Award certificates display the requirements ... and the reward").
- What the game *does* keep continuously, which a player could read as a running score: (a) the Studio Charts rank shown in the HUD (Manual p.6, "Studio Ranking"), computed from a single composite Studio Rating with headline weights Movies 24 / Stars 24 / Capital 24 / Lot Prestige 14 / Awards 14 (Prima p.46, PDF 47, "How Studios Are Rated", corroborated by the Maxx FAQ pp.27–28); (b) the Awards screen's "View Tally" button showing the "studio's lifetime record of wins" (Prima p.77); (c) the Finance screen "trends and recent history of your studio's earnings" (Prima p.46, PDF 47, "How Studios Are Rated"). None of these is framed as a finale.
- The only comparison of studios at the 2005 ceremony is the normal ceremony: each award goes to the best studio/star/movie by its criterion (Prima p.78 Award Directory), exactly as at every ceremony since 1925.
- Contemporary reviewers describe the retail game as having no win/lose framing: GameSpot (Ryan Davis, 8 Nov 2005) calls the campaign "less of a challenge and more of a suggestion" with "no 'wrong' or a 'right' way"; IGN (above) says the "trick is not to care that your studio isn't at the top of the charts"; Eurogamer (Kieron Gillen, 16 Nov 2005, 8/10) praises "the regular Oscars-styled ceremony where all the different companies compete" but wishes for "a harder business management game (to get the sense of deal-making)". None mentions an ending.

Conclusion for P15: the original's terminal evaluation was a **binary/tiered checklist evaluated once at the 2005 ceremony**, not a blended score and not a narrative retrospective (HIGH).

## 2. Sandbox mode

### 2.1 Options (retail)

| Option | Manual wording (pp.4–5, PDF page 3) | Corroboration |
|---|---|---|
| Game Start | "Choose which decade you want to begin playing in"; in Sandbox "you can only choose decades 30 years beyond what you have played in the New Game section". | Maxx FAQ p.2 (same 30-year rule); IGN review mentions choosing "to start your game in the year 2000"; Lionhead FAQ: choose a decade, technology/sets "available up to the year that your Sandbox game begins" unlocked. |
| Starting Money | "shoestring budget or a pile of cash". | Maxx FAQ ("I of course selected the highest amount"); IGN ("set as much money as they want"); TV Tropes claims a range of $100,000 to $100 million (COMMUNITY, LOW). |
| Instant Movie-making | films "shot instantly", but still must be cast and staffed. | Maxx; IGN ("in sandbox mode you can have movies made instantly"); Codex Gamicus notes instant shoots get an "awful" crew rating (COMMUNITY, LOW). |
| Instantly Constructed Buildings | toggle. | Maxx; IGN. |
| Stars Don't Misbehave | "Toggle whether Stars will behave or whether their moods will change." | Maxx; IGN ("keep stars from misbehaving"). |
| Buildings Don't Decay | toggle. | Maxx; IGN ("turn building decay off"). |
| Start with a Ready Built Studio Lot | "empty lot, or have a basic one ready". | IGN ("even start with a ready-built lot"). |

Additional retail rules: the Custom Scriptwriting Office is available immediately in Sandbox (Prima p.98, PDF 99); any asset unlocked in any saved simulation game is available in Sandbox regardless of date, and assets whose scheduled year has arrived unlock in either mode (Prima p.98); to have "the entire selection of sets, costumes, etc." in Sandbox you must have a simulation save "played through to 2005" with the Platinum Lifetime Honor (Prima p.98). The manual frames Sandbox as letting you "focus on making movies without worrying about competing for awards" (p.4); Mark_E_1990's FAQ calls it play "without any computer set objectives". A discrepancy: IGN says you must still research technology "even if you select to start your game in the year 2000", whereas the Lionhead FAQ says technology up to the chosen year is unlocked; Prima's rule (scheduled-year assets unlock in either mode) supports the FAQ for scheduled packs, so IGN may be describing award-gated or research-only items. UNRESOLVED.

### 2.2 Did Sandbox have rivals and charts?

No inspected source states that rival studios, the three Charts, or the five-yearly ceremony are removed in Sandbox. IGN describes Sandbox as "the same tycoon style play, but with extra options to keep the difficulty down"; the manual's toggles list contains nothing about rivals or awards. The manual's "without worrying about competing for awards" is a framing sentence, not a feature toggle. Verdict: rivals/charts/ceremonies in Sandbox = **UNVERIFIED, leaning present** (MEDIUM-LOW). The Sandbox screenshot on manual p.4 shows an options panel whose entries are illegible in the scan.

## 5. Current accepted code (592e926) note

A grep of `src/`, `bridge/` and `ui/src/engine/` in the accepted snapshot finds no implementation of a campaign finale, Lifetime Honor ladder, Sandbox mode, or post-horizon mode; "2040" appears only in comments (`src/core/productionIdentity.ts:49`, `src/core/rng.ts:61`, `src/core/screenplay.ts:10-11`), and `src/core/tuning.ts:1219-1236` references the 2005 set catalogue only to justify set selection. ## 6. Open uncertainties

1. Whether the awards ceremony event recurs after 2005 in retail (no source either way).
2. Whether a third, lowest Lifetime tier for "reaching 2005" existed in the shipped build (Prima p.14 and Maxx say three tiers; Prima p.80 says nothing is awarded; the set catalogue shows only two Lifetime-gated sets).
3. Whether rival studios, Charts and ceremonies are present in Sandbox mode (no source states removal; IGN implies the full sim persists).
4. Sandbox starting-money bounds ($100k–$100M is TV Tropes only) and whether the technology-unlock rule in Sandbox matches the Lionhead FAQ or IGN's contrary statement.
5. Whether any era-decency penalties or "first onscreen kiss" bonuses shipped (repeated in 2003–2004 previews; silent in retail sources).
6. The original 2002 E3 First Look was GameSpot Complete paywalled at the time; the text used is the 2005 republished capture (same author/date stamp), which I treat as faithful.
