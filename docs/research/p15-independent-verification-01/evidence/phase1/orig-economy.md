## 1. Loans, debt, and what "in the red" actually did

### 1.1 Borrowing: none

No inspected source — manual, Prima, either GameFAQs guide, gamepressure, GameSpot walkthrough/review, Eurogamer review — describes a loan, bank, credit line, investor, interest charge, or debt ceiling. Prima's only uses of "interest" are audience genre interest; its only "bank" hits are the Wild West/Urban bank *sets*. **No inspected source establishes borrowing existed; none establishes an interest charge; none establishes a debt floor/ceiling.** (Confidence HIGH that retail had no *player-facing* loan mechanic; absence of a hidden floor cannot be proven from documents.)

### 1.2 What happens when cash goes negative — RETAIL SHIPPED

The manual is explicit (p.6, "Cash Balance", left column of the "Page 6" spread; verified in the PDF): the balance *can* go into the red, and while in debt the player "won't be able to build new sets or add certain facilities and lot ornamentation." Nothing else is stated: no game-over, no interest, no forced sales, no staff loss tied to debt. Confidence HIGH.

Prima (p.14, "Building in Debt") turns "certain facilities" into an exact list. Paraphrase: most facilities and sets cannot be built in debt, with exactly these exceptions — Casting Office, Crew Facility, Production Office, Basic Script Office, Stage School, Stage Set, Star & Script Selling Facility. Every asset profile in Prima's Buildings chapter (pp.15–27) carries a `Build in Debt: Yes/No` line. Counting the extracted text: **71 profiles, 7 "Yes", 64 "No"**, and the 7 "Yes" entries are exactly the p.14 exception list. Confidence HIGH. The Maxx GameFAQs FAQ (Dec 2005) repeats the identical seven-item list (COMMUNITY, corroborating).

Pre-release corroboration of the same design (not needed for parity, but useful for dating): GameSpot's Updated Impressions of 2005-08-19 already said that going into debt leaves you "very limited in what you can do until you get out of debt" — i.e., the lockout design was in place three months before ship.

### 1.4 Build-in-Debt — sets (Prima pp.22–27)

44 set profiles (Rural ×6, Sci-Fi ×7, Stage, Suburban ×8, Traveling Vehicle: Automobile, Tropical: Beach, Urban ×13, War ×2, Wild West ×5). **Only "Stage" ($2,000, 1920, Practice Genre Comedy) is `Build in Debt: Yes`**; all 43 others are `No` (Prima p.23; Maxx FAQ notes the same single exception). Confidence HIGH.

### 1.5 Design reading

The seven debt-legal assets are precisely the minimum production loop (hire cast/crew → write a one-star script → cast → shoot on a bare Stage → release) plus the one liquidation building. In other words, retail debt is a **soft lockout with a guaranteed recovery path**, not a terminal state: a studio in the red can always keep producing cheap pictures and can always liquidate Stars/scripts for cash. (INFERENCE from the list; not stated in any source as design intent.)

### 1.6 Recovery mechanics

- Box-office income keeps flowing while in debt (no source says otherwise; the manual's release mechanics are unconditional on cash).
- Star/script sale at the Star & Script Selling Facility (buildable in debt) — manual p.17, Prima p.20.
- Selling (demolishing) buildings for a depreciated refund — Prima p.10 (see §3.1).
- The "Half Price" ceremony bonus halves star payroll for the interval (Prima p.78) — not a debt mechanic, but a payroll relief.
- No inspected source describes interest, penalties, warnings, or a debt collector. Prima's only "debt" advice is the p.82 Platinum walkthrough: release enough films "to keep you in positive financial territory" — treating debt as an avoidable nuisance, not a crisis.

## 3. Selling assets: Stars, scripts, buildings; the "Market Value" figure

### 3.1 What could be sold — RETAIL SHIPPED

- **Stars and scripts** via the Star & Script Selling Facility ($3,000, 1920, buildable in debt). Manual p.17: sell Stars or scripts "to rival studios to make some quick money" by dropping them into the building. Prima p.20: selling beats firing because a fired Star walks to a rival and you "get nothing." Mark FAQ: hovering the Star/script over the building shows an *estimated selling price* (UI detail, COMMUNITY, MEDIUM). Maxx FAQ: they are "auctioned off" for an amount based on rating (COMMUNITY wording; Prima does not use "auction").
- **Buildings and sets** via a Builder on the Sell Building ("bomb") icon: Prima p.10 — permanently demolishes and recoups "a depreciated portion of its original purchase price"; leaves debris that depresses Attractiveness. Maxx FAQ corroborates (COMMUNITY). Prima p.19 tip literally recommends selling five Small Restrooms per Large Restroom built. Exact depreciation curve: **not documented anywhere** (LOW).
- **Movies:** not sellable; they earn while "in release" then are archived (manual p.7; Prima p.44-45). No library sale, no rights transfer.
- **Not sellable / not transferable:** staff other than Stars (no source), facilities to rivals (no source), the studio itself (no source).

### 3.2 Star Market Value — PRIMA (developer-reviewed), HIGH

- Definition (Prima p.60-61): market value is "the price you could get in the Star & Script Selling Facility"; it is a function of **Star rating and actual age**; it climbs until **age 55** then declines yearly toward mandatory retirement at **70**.
- Tenure rule (Prima p.20-21 and p.61): a Star must be with the studio **eight years** before fetching full market value; until then only "a rising fraction." Applies equally to Stars poached from rivals.
- Relevance (Prima p.61): market value "is only important if you intend to make cash by selling a Star… Otherwise, it's not important." It feeds neither Studio Rating nor Star rating.
- Script market value (Prima p.21; Maxx): depends on script star rating only.
- UI confirmation: the evidence-folder Star-card screenshot (Jan 1953, cash $2,644,803) shows a Director card reading "50 years old / Annual Salary: $50,000 / Market Value: $63,073" — market value is a per-person card line separate from salary. (SCREENSHOT, provenance web image; MEDIUM.)
- Formula: **not recovered** by any source (LOW). Mark FAQ adds "image" as an input (COMMUNITY, uncorroborated by Prima).
- Scale of the money: GameSpot walkthrough (CONTEMPORARY) calls SSSF cash "relatively minor" next to film revenue; Mark FAQ calls script sales "a small cash boost."

### 3.3 Where sold Stars go
Manual p.17 says sold "to rival studios"; Prima p.20 "to another studio." No source describes the buyer selection, the buyer's payment, or any later consequence beyond the Star leaving. Rival Stars in turn appear in the player's Stage School queue in proportion to Studio Rating (Prima p.46).

## 4. Studio valuation / net worth — did the game display one?

**Finding: no.** No inspected source shows a studio net worth, company value, enterprise value, share price, or asset valuation. The manual's only "value of your studio lot" phrase (p.21) is about landscaping attractiveness, not money. The economic HUD is exactly: **Cash Balance** (top right; click → Finance screen with balance/income/expenditure graphs, F5; Salary screen, F6 — manual p.6, p.14, p.37; Maxx FAQ) plus the **Studio Ranking** rosette. Confidence HIGH.

### 4.1 What "Capital" meant inside Studio Rating — PRIMA p.46-47, HIGH

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Capital | 24% |
| Movies | 24% |
| Stars | 24% |
| Lot Prestige | 14% |
| Awards | 14% |

- **Capital = cash in the bank at the moment**, nothing else: "a measure of your studio's wealth based on the amount of money your studio has in the bank at any given time" (Prima p.46). No assets, no library, no liabilities. It moves whenever the balance moves.
- **Scale:** $50,000 (lowest score) to $1,600,000 (maximum); the 50%-score point is "about one-fifth of maximum ($300,000)", and above $300,000 "it takes progressively more to gain points." Prima prints the arithmetic midpoint as "$775,000" — a printing/arithmetic slip; (50k+1.6M)/2 = $825,000. To max the factor, "amass and keep more than $1,600,000 in the bank at all times."
- **Below $50,000 (including negative):** Prima gives the floor as the *lowest rating*; no source states whether negative cash scores below the floor or simply clamps to it (LOW).
- **Conflicting community figure:** a GameFAQs-tier community guide (bible §Studio Rating; not re-fetched here, GameFAQs blocks fetch) claims "5 million seems to be the limit." Prima's $1.6M is developer-reviewed and should govern; the $5M claim is COMMUNITY, LOW.
- Manual p.6 and p.21 corroborate qualitatively that "cash balance"/"finances" feed the ranking; the Mark FAQ says Capital is "Rated out of five stars" (COMMUNITY).
- The Achievement Award ladder separately uses cash thresholds ("Amass at least $X in funds"), but source figures disagree (Prima p.79: Promising Studio Manager $100,000 vs Prima p.42 text $500,000 vs Mark/GameSpot $1,000,000; Big Fish $6M Prima/GameSpot vs $8M Mark). Not adjudicated here; flagged.

### 4.2 Interpretation for P15
Capital was a **cash-hoarding incentive**, a comparative snapshot, not a valuation. gamepressure (COMMUNITY) describes the resulting tension: keeping Capital high "will not allow us to make some serious investments early on." Prima's p.82 Platinum route exploits the composite by deliberately *not* releasing films to stay low in the charts before 1935 — evidence the rating was gameable and rank-relative rather than a wealth measure.

## 5. Starting cash, Sandbox cash, and how pressure actually worked

### 5.1 Starting cash (campaign)
- No official document prints the campaign starting balance (manual, Prima: none). Bible Q042 remains open.
- SCREENSHOT evidence (evidence folder, a captioned video frame of the tutorial): Jan 1920, HUD cash **$145,000** with the Staff Office present and a Stage School under construction. Consistent with a $150,000 start minus the $5,000 Stage School, or a $145,000 start with the school gifted — cannot be distinguished (MEDIUM for "≈$145–150k", LOW for the exact constant).
- Pre-release: GameSpot 2004-02-23 — "a small studio, a limited budget, and some restrictions" (no figure).

### 5.2 Sandbox cash
- Manual p.4 (OFFICIAL): Sandbox "Starting Money — Choose to start with a shoestring budget or a pile of cash!" No amounts.
- Maxx FAQ (COMMUNITY): "Choose how much money you want to start off with. I of course selected the highest amount."
- Search-engine synthesis of a Neoseeker/fandom FAQ (source pages block fetch): options **$100,000 / $1,000,000 / $10,000,000 / $100,000,000**. Two fandom wikis fetched directly say only that Sandbox allows "budgets of over $100 million." Treat the four-step list as COMMUNITY, LOW-MEDIUM; the "$100M top" as COMMUNITY, MEDIUM.
- Prima's Sandbox passages (p.98) discuss unlock inheritance only; no cash figures.

### 5.3 Running costs that create pressure (rules-as-written)
| Cost | Figure | Source/tier |
|---|---|---|
| Staff salary | $1,000/yr each (builders, janitors, crew, writers, scientists) | Maxx FAQ (COMMUNITY); Prima p.76 "$1,000 per year" for personal assistants |
| Extras | $3,000/yr | Maxx FAQ (COMMUNITY) |
| Stars | start $6,000/yr, player-set; rating credit caps at $100,000 | Prima p.61 (HIGH); Maxx |
| Marketing spend | None $0 / Small $50k / Average $100k / Large $150k / Gigantic $200k | Prima p.45 (HIGH) |
| Facilities/sets | see §1.3–1.4 | Prima (HIGH) |
| Paving | Tarmac $5, Path $20, Grass $10, Sand $5 | Mark FAQ; tutorial screenshot tooltip (MEDIUM) |
| Building decay/repair | builders needed; a building in disrepair cannot be used until repaired | manual p.18; Maxx |

Pressure points the manual names: debt lockout (p.6), Star walk-outs taking "all the time and money you invested" (p.15), marketing mis-spend on a "box office dud" (p.39), paving distance costs (p.20).

### 5.4 Lived experience — the recorded disagreement
- **Forgiving:** forceforgood.co.uk (Rik, 2012-12-08, COMMUNITY retrospective): "capital never really seems to be an issue"; "it's pretty hard to screw things up badly"; "you're not likely to lose money on a movie." oldpcgaming.net (2018 retrospective): making money is just keeping "a steady stream of cheap and crappy films in the pipeline."
- **Punishing:** IMDb user review (COMMUNITY): lost sleep "trying to come up with strategies to save my studio from financial ruin"; studio "a century" in multi-million debt with "only three tiny sets."
- **Reconciliation (INFERENCE):** both are consistent with the documented rules. Revenue scales with Success rating and never goes negative per film (no source documents a per-film loss; bible Q037 open), so a studio that keeps releasing cheap films cannot spiral; but a studio that stops releasing (or over-builds) can sit in debt indefinitely because nothing forces a resolution and most builds are locked — exactly the "three tiny sets for a century" trap. The economy is **non-terminal in both directions**: neither ruin nor forced success.

## 6. Retail vs. current accepted code (context only, not a parity ruling)

Read-only observations against `accepted-592e926`:
- `src/core/employment.ts:78-86` — D-12 solvency gate: any *voluntary* commitment must leave cash ≥ 0; unavoidable weekly debits may push cash negative; no bankruptcy. Structurally the same family as the original's debt lockout, but stricter (blocks *all* voluntary spend, vs. the original's seven debt-legal essentials) and applied at the commitment rather than at the build menu.
- `src/core/tuning.ts:70` — `INITIAL_CASH: 20_000_000` vs. the original's ≈$145k in 1920 (different scale; not a defect, noted for calibration).
- `src/core/tuning.ts:812-816` — facility demolition refund 0.5, set strike refund 0.35: a concrete instance of the original's undocumented "depreciated portion."
- No studio valuation, loan, or market-value-per-person concept found in `src/core` (grep for valuation/netWorth/marketValue: only `market.baseMarketValue` in `agents.ts`, a labor-market base, not a sale price).
