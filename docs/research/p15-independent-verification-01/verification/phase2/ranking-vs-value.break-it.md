## 4. A genuinely unverifiable term in the memo's own model: "strong reserve"

The Financial Standing band definitions (§4) are: *"Thriving <20% debt/assets & strong reserve · Stable <40% · Leveraged 40–70% or thin reserve · Strained >70% · Distressed net worth ≤ 0."* Recompute against the worked table: **Your Studio is at 0% debt/assets** (well under the 20% Thriving threshold) yet is banded **Stable**, while **Bellwether**, also at 0% debt/assets, is banded **Thriving**. The only stated variable that could explain the split is "strong reserve" — but no reserve figure, formula, or threshold appears anywhere in the memo, and the worked example doesn't give the inputs (weekly overhead depends on employee count, which isn't stated for any of the four studios) needed to compute one. A player asking "why is Bellwether Thriving and I'm only Stable when we're both debt-free?" has no answer the game (or this memo) currently provides — this is precisely the "can the player explain the number/stage in one sentence" failure the assigned lens asks me to hunt for, and it's failing inside the memo's own illustrative example, not a hypothetical. **Smallest fix:** add a reserve column (e.g., weeks-of-runway = cash ÷ weekly burn) to the §4 band table with the same transparency the debt/assets ratio already gets, and make the Thriving/Leveraged reserve thresholds explicit numbers rather than a bare adjective.

## 5. Exploit not addressed: quarter-end balance-sheet window dressing

Both Power Ranking and the recommended Financial Standing band are **point-in-time snapshots taken every 13 weeks** (`week%13===0`), with no trailing average. Nothing in the memo's disclosure or banding rule prevents a studio — player or, more importantly, a **deterministic rival whose decision code has full self-account access** (memo's own §7.2) — from repaying a large loan chunk in the week immediately before the snapshot to jump a band (e.g., Leveraged→Stable), then re-borrowing immediately after, exactly the real-world "quarter-end window dressing" pattern the economist lens is meant to catch. This is cheaper and faster to execute for money (a single transaction) than the equivalent gaming of Standing (which drifts continuously and can't be moved in one tick), so the existing "Standing is already point-in-time-snapshotted and that's accepted" precedent the memo could lean on doesn't fully cover it. The memo never raises this. **Smallest fix:** either (a) compute the band from a trailing average (e.g., mean debt/assets ratio over the preceding 13 weeks) instead of the instant at the snapshot tick, or (b) explicitly accept the exploit as in-scope-and-harmless because the band never affects Power Ranking or player-facing stakes beyond information (a legitimate answer, but it should be a stated decision, not a silent gap) — flag as a P15D research question either way.
