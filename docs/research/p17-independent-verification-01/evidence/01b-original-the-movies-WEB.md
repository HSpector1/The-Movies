## 1. Did The Movies ever get a sequel? (shipped)

**No. There has never been a sequel, and multiple independent sources from 2005 through November 2025 all agree on this.**

- MobyGames "Related Games" / "The Movies series" group for both the base game and the expansion lists only: *The Movies* (2005), *The Movies: Stunts & Effects* (2006, add-on), *The Movies: Complete Pack* (2006, bundle of the two), and *The Movies (Premiere Edition)* (2005, SKU variant) — no numbered sequel, no "The Movies 2." Source: `evidence/web/moby_tm.txt` and `evidence/web/moby_se.txt` (MobyGames game pages, Moby ID 20117 / 22721). What it proves: the canonical game-database "family" for the title contains no sequel entry. Confidence HIGH. Tier: **CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL SOURCE** (structured database, continuously maintained).
- The Movies Wiki (Fandom, `themoviesgame.fandom.com`) full page-title list (63 titles, MediaWiki `allpages` API result with no `continue` token, i.e. the complete namespace-0 list at capture time) contains no page for "Sequel," "Franchise," "Series," "Prequel," "Reboot," or "Remake." Source: `evidence/web/tm_allpages.json`. What it proves: the fan wiki never treated sequel/franchise mechanics as a documentable feature of the shipped game — an absence-of-evidence signal, not proof of non-existence, but a meaningful one given the wiki actively documents minor mechanics (e.g. individual award pages, DJ characters). Confidence MEDIUM-HIGH. Tier: **COMMUNITY INFERENCE**.
- Lionhead co-founder Peter Molyneux, asked directly in 2006 whether he had "plans to produce a sequel for some of your most successful games," named *Syndicate*, *Populous*, and *Dungeon Keeper* as games he'd love to revisit — he did **not** mention *The Movies*, one year after its release. Source: GameSpot, "Q&A: Lionhead's Molyneux roars," Aug 14, 2006 (`evidence/web/gs_qanda_roars.txt`, line ~554): *"PM: Aside from the licensing complications, some sort of next-gen online version of Syndicate would certainly be popular with gamers...I'd love to reprise games like Syndicate, Populous, and Dungeon Keeper..."* What it proves: as of mid-2006, the designer's own public sequel wish-list for his catalogue did not include The Movies. Confidence MEDIUM (absence-by-omission, not a denial). Tier: **CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL SOURCE**.
- Lionhead Studios was dissolved by Microsoft on 29 April 2016 (sources differ on the exact date: the Guardian says "31 April" [sic, likely a copyediting error for the 29th] — see `evidence/web/guardian.txt` line 125), foreclosing any first-party sequel permanently. Source: The Guardian, Rich Stanton, "Lionhead: the rise and fall of a British video game legend," Fri 20 May 2016 (`evidence/web/guardian.txt`). Confidence HIGH. Tier: **CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL SOURCE**.
- A 20th-anniversary retrospective explicitly frames this as an open wish rather than a fact: "20 Years Have Passed, It's Time For A Sequel To Peter Molyneux's The Movies," TheGamer, Andrew King, May 17, 2025 (fetched live via WebSearch/WebFetch, not in local cache). What it proves: as recently as 2025, professional games journalism still describes a sequel as something that has *never happened* and is only being wished for. Confidence HIGH. Tier: **CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL SOURCE**.
- A 2011 Quarter To Three forum thread ("Re-visiting The Movies") revived itself organically across 2011 → 2024 → November 2025 (the 20-year anniversary), and every post treats the game as a closed, unrepeated one-off; a 2025 post links to a *different* Eurogamer retrospective headlined "Lionhead's secret best game, and one of the greatest ever abandonware games" (Alex Donaldson, ~Nov 8 2025) — again framing it as history, not an active franchise. Source: `evidence/web/qt3.json` (Discourse API dump of topic posts). Confidence HIGH for "no sequel exists," confidence LOW on exact Eurogamer article text since it wasn't independently re-fetched (only the QT3 poster's block-quote of its headline/teaser was captured). Tier: **COMMUNITY INFERENCE** (forum) citing a **CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL SOURCE** (Eurogamer) at one remove.
- Wikipedia's "The Movies (video game)" article (live-fetched) confirms the only follow-on product ever shipped was the Stunts & Effects expansion, and names a 2024 game, *Blockbuster Inc.*, as an unrelated third-party "spiritual successor" — i.e., the closest thing to a sequel is a different developer's homage, not a Lionhead/Activision continuation. What it proves: no first-party or licensed sequel exists as of the article's last edit. Confidence HIGH. Tier: **CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL SOURCE** (tertiary/encyclopedic, itself sourced).

**Conclusion for Q1:** Zero shipped sequels, in any of the P17 taxonomy's five continuation types (direct sequel, prequel, film spin-off, remake, reboot). The only shipped continuation of any kind is the *Stunts & Effects* expansion pack (2006) — an add-on to the same base game/same campaign, not a new game with its own release and not a "franchise installment" in the P17 sense. This makes Q2–Q11 (sequel performance ties, timing, recognition persistence across installments, remake/prequel/spin-off/reboot mechanics, recurring-cast benefits *across installments*, title reuse across installments) **moot for shipped retail** — there is no second installment for any of these mechanics to have operated on. What web sources *do* show is (a) a promised **in-game** sequel-generation feature that was never built (Section 2 below, critical), and (b) a promised **remake** capability for movies-within-the-game that also appears to have shipped only in reduced form if at all (Section 5).

## 2. Sequel-performance-tied-to-original & sequel timing — PRE-RELEASE PROMISE, and the single most important finding in this file

This is a genuine smoking gun for the exact question the P17 package is trying to answer, and it comes from two independent, non-community sources years apart.

- **GameSpot, "Shedding light on The Movies,"** Curt Feldman & Justin Calvert interviewing Peter Molyneux, Sept 23, 2003 (`evidence/web/gs_shedding.txt`, lines 393–394):
  > **GS: How will sequels be dealt with in The Movies? Do you see them as a positive or a negative side of the movie industry?**
  > **PM: Sequels have had mixed fortunes in the history of the movie industry. There have been times when they have been frowned upon and times when there seems to be nothing else released—with sequels outperforming the original in both box office receipts and critical acclaim. So, in the game, the success of a sequel will depend on when you decide to make it, and, secondly, how it compares to the original. It's up to the gamer whether they make a Terminator 3 or a Highlander 2.**

  What it proves: **more than two years before release**, Molyneux described an intended in-game sequel system with exactly the two axes the P17 package cares about — timing ("when you decide to make it") and quality-relative-to-original ("how it compares to the original"). This maps almost one-to-one onto P17 direction items B/C/D (Recognition/Momentum/Fatigue, timing-sensitive, no automatic bonus). Confidence HIGH that this was said; confidence LOW-to-MEDIUM that it describes what actually shipped (see next bullet). Tier: **PRE-RELEASE PROMISE**.

- **Kotaku, "The Man Who Promised Too Much,"** Jason Schreier, March 11, 2014 (`evidence/web/kotaku.txt`, lines 91–95) — a retrospective feature built on direct interviews, including one with then-Lionhead studio head **Gary Carr**, about a 2006 press event in Barcelona (timed to the Stunts & Effects rollout) where Molyneux and Carr demoed The Movies to journalists:
  > *"There was one feature Peter wanted—he wanted the ability to make sequels in the game," Carr recalled. "It was something I think I pushed back on because it meant a whole bunch of new work that we just didn't have time for."*
  > *"But Molyneux was attached to the idea. So as they demoed The Movies to a room full of journalists, Molyneux started describing how a sequel could be generated with the game's script-writing software, and he asked Carr to show it off—**even though there was no such system in the game**."*
  > *"He threw me under the bus, there," Carr said, laughing, "and I remember reading a piece that said 'the demoer'—uh, thanks for that, I was the head of the studio—'didn't seem to know how to play the game.'"*

  What it proves, with HIGH confidence because it is a direct, on-the-record quote from the studio head who was physically present and personally embarrassed by the incident: **(1)** an explicit "make a sequel" feature, generated via the script-writing tool, was discussed and wanted by the lead designer; **(2)** it was **rejected internally for scope/time reasons by the person running the studio**; **(3)** it was **never built** — "there was no such system in the game," stated flatly by the journalist based on Carr's account; **(4)** Molyneux nevertheless **publicly claimed the feature existed** in a live press demo, and the resulting confusion when Carr couldn't produce it made trade press coverage of that demo describe the presenter as not knowing how to play his own game. This is a textbook "Molyneux-ism" (over-promise that outpaces engineering), explicitly named as such by the article's own title and thesis. Tier: **PRE-RELEASE PROMISE that demonstrably did not ship**, evidenced via **CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL SOURCE** (2014 investigative journalism quoting a primary participant).

  Caveat on dating: the Kotaku piece places this Barcelona demo "In 2006, as the team at Lionhead prepared to release... The Movies" — but The Movies itself shipped Nov 2005; a 2006 Barcelona event more likely relates to the Stunts & Effects press push (Stunts & Effects was previewed/announced Feb–March 2006 and shipped June 2006, see Section 15). Either the article is imprecise about which product was being demoed, or this was a later, separate showing of the base game. This does not weaken the core fact (no in-game sequel-generation system ever existed) but is flagged as a minor source-fidelity uncertainty. Confidence on the underlying fact: HIGH. Confidence on the exact 2006/product framing: LOW-MEDIUM.

**Conclusion for Q2/Q3:** The developers **wanted and publicly discussed** a mechanic where an in-game sequel's success would depend on release timing and comparison to the original — precisely the shape of P17's Recognition/Momentum/Fatigue design. It was **rejected for being too much engineering work** and **never implemented**, and the shipped retail game (per every review, guide, and wiki page in this corpus — none of which mention a "make a sequel" button or a franchise/sequel data field) contains no such system. Nothing stopped a player from manually reusing the same script, cast, and title-suffix to *simulate* a sequel (the Custom Scriptwriting Office lets you "edit other scripts... and tweak scenes to re-shoot them," per the Fandom wiki, `evidence/web/tm_pages.txt`), but there is no mechanical linkage, no recognition carry-over, and no timing/quality-comparison scoring tied to it. This is a DESIGN INFERENCE bridging the Custom Scriptwriting Office fact (COMMUNITY INFERENCE, wiki, citing "IGN Guide" as its source) with the absence of any sequel-scoring feature in every review/preview read for this file.

## 3. Persistent recognition across releases (franchise-level, not single-movie)

No web source (fandom wiki, MobyGames, previews, reviews, retrospectives) describes any studio-level, cross-movie "recognition" stat that persists and is keyed to a story property/IP the way P17 packages it. What web sources *do* confirm as shipped, single-entity mechanics that are adjacent but not the same thing:
- **Per-star popularity/ranking** that follows an individual actor across their whole career and affects box office: "Once the star has been hired, they'll acquire a popularity rating amongst all other actors in the world. The more popular the star, the better the box office returns." — IGN preview, Dan Adams, April 4, 2005 (`evidence/web/ign_601281.txt`). Tier: **CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL SOURCE**.
- **Per-studio prestige/awards** tracked across the whole campaign via a five-yearly awards ceremony giving "bonus which lasts until the next ceremony" (Wikipedia-mirrored infobox text, `evidence/web/lh_api.json`). Tier: **OFFICIAL/DEVELOPER** (Wikipedia-sourced).
- **The Movies Online (community site)** kept persistent cross-player charts — Top Movies, Top Studios, Top Genres, Hall of Fame, monthly "Lionhead Motion Picture Awards" snapshots that granted importable in-game credit bonuses (`evidence/web/tmo_home.txt`, `evidence/web/tmo_faq.txt` line 147: *"Every month, an award ceremony takes place wherein a snapshot of all the online charts is stored for posterity. The top 25 movies overall, and in each genre, the top 25 studios, and the top 25 stars all receive significant credit bonuses."*). This is recognition at the *studio/star* level, persisting indefinitely on a public leaderboard — but again, not a *StoryProperty/franchise* recognition object. Tier: **OFFICIAL/DEVELOPER** (official community-site FAQ, archived).

None of this constitutes evidence *for or against* franchise-level recognition as P17 defines it — it's simply absent from every web source found. Absence noted explicitly per task instructions.

## 4. Remakes — a specific, unusually strong PRE-RELEASE PROMISE that appears not to have shipped as advertised

- **Eurogamer preview**, Kristan Reed, 5 August 2003 (`evidence/web/eg_p_themovies_pc.txt`, line 72):
  > *"Molyneux even boldly claims that you'll be able to remake your favourite movies ('even Star Wars'), choosing the exact same cast - all at the same stage in their movie career - and replicate given scenes 'down to the haircut'. 'You could, theoretically, put Keanu Reeves in his real life movies throughout his career, upload him to the website and auction him off.'"*

  What it proves: an explicit, specific PRE-RELEASE PROMISE of a remake feature that would let a player recreate a real film with the "same" cast pinned to a specific career stage. Confidence HIGH that it was said; the same Eurogamer piece immediately hedges — *"Despite our nagging doubts about being able to remake Memento, The Movies is shaping up to be..."* (line 77) — signalling contemporary journalist skepticism even at the time. No review, guide, or wiki page in this corpus describes an actual "remake mode," a "same cast, same career stage" lock, or a Keanu-Reeves-style real-actor recreation feature as shipped. The shipped feature that *does* exist — reusing a script/cast via the Custom Scriptwriting Office (Fandom wiki) — is a generic re-edit tool, not a purpose-built "remake" system with the promised fidelity. Tier: **PRE-RELEASE PROMISE**, likely **not shipped as described** (DESIGN INFERENCE from the absence of any shipped-feature description matching it).
- The same preview promises a "Director's Cut" **add-on pack** giving "control over every aspect" of a movie, and floats hypothetical movie **tie-in packs** ("imagine a Terminator 3 add-on pack with all the real sets, etc") — see Section 7 (spin-offs) and Section 15 (promises that didn't ship): **only one add-on ever shipped, and it was Stunts & Effects, not a Director's Cut pack or any movie-tie-in pack.**
- A **community-made** (not official) "Director's Cut" mod/modpack exists on the surviving fan site TheMovies3D.com — `https://www.themovies3d.com/en/downloads/download/12-all-other-mods/1068-tm-directors-cut-standard-version` (found via WebFetch of a 2018 TheMovies3D.com forum thread, "Director's Cut conflict with another mod"). This is very likely fans filling the gap left by Molyneux's unshipped official "Director's Cut" concept, but the forum thread itself does not confirm lineage/intent, so this is **speculative** — flagged as such. Confidence LOW-MEDIUM on the naming connection; Tier: **COMMUNITY INFERENCE**.

## 8. Recurring cast benefits — genre experience, relationships/chemistry, image

This is one of the better-evidenced questions from web sources, and the picture is mixed: the *design intent* (genre specialization, actor-director chemistry) is confirmed as shipped, but multiple contemporary reviewers independently reported the chemistry/relationship half of it barely functioned in practice.

- **Genre specialization ("typecasting") shipped**: *"Managing a star's career can be tricky though. Most actors and directors have a specialty of some sort, so attaching them to the right script is a big plus."* — IGN preview, Dan Adams, April 4, 2005 (`evidence/web/ign_601281.txt`). Tier: **CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL SOURCE**, confidence HIGH this is a real, shipped stat (it's described as an existing preview build behavior, not a promise).
- **Actor/director relationship-building ("chemistry") shipped but reportedly barely worked**:
  - 1UP review, Garnett Lee, Nov 8 2005 (`evidence/web/1up_review.txt`): *"It takes repeatedly putting a couple together to create the chemistry that will be invaluable when they work together on the set."* — describes it as functioning, if grindy. Tier: **CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL SOURCE**.
  - IGN review, Dan Adams, Nov 8 2005, page 2 (`evidence/web/ign_665338_p2.txt`): *"After nearly 20 hours of playing the tycoon portion of The Movies, I have yet to have any relationships increase over the acquaintance level... I think they gain relationship when you have stars practice or perform in movies together, but it's negligible if it does."* Tier: **CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL SOURCE**.
  - Eurogamer review, Kieron Gillen, 16/11/2005, score 8/10 (`evidence/web/eg_review.txt`): *"Relationships are improved by having stars talk to each other... Keep this long enough up, and they'll become increasingly close friends, but oddities abound... I'd only managed to have a couple of significant friendships cultivated by 1970, with everyone else refusing to talk to each other except in public on the streets... it takes forever."* Tier: **CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL SOURCE**.
  - Reading across all three: the mechanic **shipped** (three independent reviewers all describe the same drag-two-stars-together-repeatedly interaction), but it was **slow/tedious/perceived-as-negligible** by at least two of three reviewers — a genuine "shipped but underwhelming" finding, not a promise-vs-ship gap. Confidence HIGH (three independent contemporary sources agree on the mechanic's existence and its friction).
- **"Image"/publicity effects of stars**: IGN preview (`evidence/web/ign_601281.txt`) also describes a PR-management angle distinct from genre/chemistry: *"if there are a couple of stars with flagging careers, a shrewd movie mogul might decide to intentionally get them in trouble of some sort in order to put their faces back in the news and increase their popularity. Even negative press can be good for business sometimes."* Tier: **CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL SOURCE**.
- **No web source ties any of this specifically to *sequel* casting** (i.e., "the original lead returns for the sequel and gets a bonus") — because, per Section 1, there is no shipped sequel mechanic for it to attach to. This is a genuine gap: recurring-cast-across-installments benefits (P17 item F) cannot be evidenced from web sources for this game because installments never happened.

## 9. Audience fatigue / genre popularity cycles

Shipped, and described in detail pre-release by Molyneux himself:

- GameSpot preview, April 27, 2004 (`evidence/web/gs_preview.txt`, lines 190–191): *"GS: We know that although players will need to conserve their funds by reusing sets, costumes, and actors, they will also benefit from innovating—by breaking new ground with their movies. Could you explain how this system will work...? PM: If you have a rough knowledge of the history of the movies, then that should give you an idea of when there have been new technology developments... if you notice a spate of horror films has been released over the past few months, you may decide that the time has come to change direction... as you play though the game, you'll get bonuses for mixing genres and setting up film action in ways that other players do not."* Tier: **PRE-RELEASE PROMISE**, but corroborated as shipped by:
- Wikipedia infobox/body text (mirrored in `evidence/web/lh_api.json`): a historical timeline runs across the whole campaign and "movie-making fads" and "major historical events" affect what audiences want — general confirmation of the era/trend system, without web-level detail on the exact genre-saturation formula. Tier: **OFFICIAL/DEVELOPER**.
- No web source uses the words "fatigue" or "overexposure" specifically, and none describe genre popularity cycling as tied to *franchise* fatigue (P17's distinct Fatigue factor) — this is a **general genre-trend system** (real-world-history-driven, e.g., 1920s Depression → comedy demand, per the Fandom wiki's unread-here award pages) rather than a repeated-mediocre-output-raises-fatigue-faster-than-excellent-output curve. **Gap noted**: no web evidence of a franchise-specific fatigue curve, because no franchise mechanic exists to have fatigue on.

## 11. Max principal cast per movie / script tiers / Advanced Movie Maker limits

Web sources confirm the **studio-wide employee/star roster cap** clearly, but do **not** give a clean number for *per-movie* principal (lead/antagonist/support-equivalent) cast slots — that level of numeric detail (if it exists) is more likely to live in the official manual/Prima guide, which is the sibling corpus agent's territory. What web sources do establish:

- **Studio-wide star roster practical ceiling**: *"While you really wouldn't want to hire more than 12 stars (they're a huge pain in the ass to manage)... The biggest of these impediments is on the number of employees that you can hire."* — IGN review, Dan Adams, Nov 8 2005 (`evidence/web/ign_665338.txt`). This is a *soft, reviewer-observed* practical limit on total contracted stars across the whole studio, not a hard per-movie lead-role cap. Tier: **CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL SOURCE**, confidence HIGH as a reviewer's lived-experience number, MEDIUM as a hard game-coded limit (phrased as "wouldn't want to," i.e., a management-burden observation, not a stated engine cap).
- **Employee categories distinct from "stars"**: extras, maintenance crews, film crews, writers, scientists, entourage are separate hireable pools with their own limits (GameSpy review, `evidence/web/gamespy_review.txt`; IGN review, `evidence/web/ign_665338.txt`; QT3 forum poster Nikolaj, 2011: *"everybody wanted an evergrowing entourage, and you could only hire a very limited number of people,"* `evidence/web/qt3.json`). This confirms a general "hard employee caps annoyed people" pattern across the whole product life (2005 review → 2011 forum nostalgia) but not per-movie script-tier lead-role counts.
- **Advanced Movie Making mode** is named and described as receiving an upgrade in Stunts & Effects ("an upgraded Advanced Movie Making mode offering greater filmmaking control," IGN, David Adams, March 15 2006, `evidence/web/ign_696097.txt`), confirming the mode's existence and that S&E specifically expanded its filmmaking controls (FreeCam, stunts) — but no web source enumerates a lead-role-count limit within it.
- **Gap, stated explicitly**: none of the previews, reviews, or wiki pages read for this file give a number for "maximum principal/lead cast members per script tier." This appears to be a manual-level mechanical detail outside what preview/review journalism would normally report. Recommend cross-checking the sibling 01a corpus file (manual/Prima) for the authoritative number; do not treat this file's silence as evidence the number doesn't exist.

## 14. Pre-release promises that did NOT ship (master list)

This is the richest vein in the web corpus. Consolidated below, each already sourced above where applicable.

1. **Console versions (Xbox, PlayStation 2, GameCube).** Promised repeatedly and specifically from the earliest previews through mid-2005, then quietly narrowed, then formally cancelled:
   - GameSpot E3 2004 preview (`evidence/web/gs_e3_2004.txt`, line 83): *"...the fact that it's going to ship for the PC, GameCube, Xbox, and PlayStation 2 will guarantee broad interest for the game."* PRE-RELEASE PROMISE.
   - Eurogamer preview, Aug 2003 (`evidence/web/eg_p_themovies_pc.txt`): *"Expect the PC version first, with the GameCube, PS2 and Xbox versions 'as closely together as we can'."* PRE-RELEASE PROMISE, direct Molyneux quote.
   - GameSpot Q&A, Sept 2003 (`evidence/web/gs_shedding.txt`, line 383): confirms console development was actively underway alongside PC. PRE-RELEASE PROMISE.
   - The Movies Online official FAQ (archived; `evidence/web/tmo_faq.txt` line 68): *"The Movies is available on PC and will be release on Xbox, PS2 and GameCube in 2006."* — an **official, on-the-record** promise, not just a journalist's paraphrase. Tier: **OFFICIAL/DEVELOPER**.

Tier: **CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL SOURCE**.
   - Cancellation confirmed independently by three outlets: IGN news, Feb 8 2006 (`evidence/web/ign_686776.txt`): *"During an earnings conference call earlier this week, Activision revealed it had canceled the console versions of The Movies... after slow sales of the PC version."*; Eurogamer's own cross-linked headline "The Movies console canned" (referenced from `evidence/web/eg_review.txt` and `evidence/web/eg_p_themovies_pc.txt` "Related content" sidebars, though the article itself 404s on the Wayback Machine — see Open Gaps); MobyGames trivia (`evidence/web/moby_tm.txt`): *"A console version was announced, but later cancelled after the disappointing sales of the PC release."* A 2025 retrospective (TheGamer, Andrew King, May 17 2025) reconfirms: *"the ports were quietly canceled — likely due to the PC game stalling out commercially."* **This is the single best-corroborated unshipped promise in the whole file** (5+ independent sources spanning 2003–2025). Confidence VERY HIGH.
2. **In-game "make a sequel" feature**, generated from the script-writing tool, with success tied to timing and comparison to the original. Promised by Molyneux (GameSpot 2003) and again claimed live on-stage in front of press (per Kotaku 2014/Gary Carr), but per Carr's direct account, "there was no such system in the game," ever. See Section 2. Confidence HIGH.
3. **Full remake fidelity** — "remake your favourite movies... even Star Wars," same cast pinned to the same real-world career stage, "down to the haircut" (Eurogamer 2003). No shipped feature description in any review/guide matches this level of fidelity; the closest shipped analogue (Custom Scriptwriting Office script re-editing) is generic, not a purpose-built remake tool. See Section 4. Confidence MEDIUM-HIGH that the advertised fidelity did not ship as described.
4. **"Director's Cut" add-on pack**, promised to give "control over every aspect" of a production (Eurogamer 2003). Never released officially; only Stunts & Effects (2006) ever shipped as an official add-on (confirmed repeatedly: Activision PR `evidence/web/act_pr_2006.txt`, MobyGames `evidence/web/moby_se.txt` "Related Games," and a live WebSearch synthesis: *"On 6 June 2006, the first, and to date, only expansion pack was released, The Movies: Stunts and Effects."*). A community-made mod of the same name exists on TheMovies3D.com, unofficial. Confidence HIGH the official pack never shipped.
5. **Movie tie-in add-on packs** (Molyneux's example: "a Terminator 3 add-on pack with all the real sets"). Never mentioned again in any later preview, press release, or review; never shipped. Confidence HIGH (silence across 2+ years of subsequent coverage is strong evidence here, though technically an absence claim).
6. **Online console movie-sharing** (Xbox Live / PS2 broadband movie exchange) — GameSpot April 2004 preview (`evidence/web/gs_preview.txt` line 242): *"We had heard about tentative plans to support online movie exchanges via Xbox Live or PS2 broadband. Any word on either of these? PM: Not at the moment."* Already hedged as "tentative" pre-release and mooted entirely once console versions were cancelled. Confidence HIGH this never shipped (no console version ever shipped at all).

Minor but real promise/ship gap. Confidence HIGH.
8. **Adult/uncensored user content** — Lionhead explicitly said in 2003 they would keep hardcore content out ("We thought if we don't do it, the hackers will," Eurogamer 2003) — this one was a promise that was *kept* (not a broken promise), included here only because it's adjacent; not itself an unshipped-feature finding.

## Bottom line for the P17 authors

The strongest, best-corroborated fact in this entire file is **Section 2**: Lionhead's own designer wanted an in-game sequel-generation mechanic whose success would depend on timing and comparison-to-original — almost exactly what P17 is building — and the studio head **personally rejected it as too much engineering work**, it was **never built**, and Molyneux **publicly overclaimed it existed** in front of press, to Carr's on-record embarrassment. Pair this with the near-universal, multi-decade-spanning corroboration that **no sequel of any kind ever shipped or was seriously greenlit** (Section 1), and the equally strong evidence that **console ports were promised for three years and then explicitly cancelled** (Section 15.1) and that a **remake feature with unrealistic fidelity was promised and never matched by anything reviewers or wiki editors describe as shipped** (Section 4). The recurring-cast chemistry/genre-specialty mechanics **did** ship, but multiple contemporary reviewers independently found the chemistry half barely functional in practice — worth remembering when P17 calibrates how "rewarding" a working version of this system should feel relative to the original's reputation for over-promising.
