# Comparator Atlas — Hollywood Animal (Weappy Studio, Early Access 2025–present)

**Scope note:** Hollywood Animal is the single closest comparator to Project: Studio's P17 scope — a Hollywood-studio management sim with sequels, IP/rights language, actor fame, contracts, and era progression, currently in Steam Early Access. All evidence below is drawn from the cached corpus at `evidence/ha-raw/` (Steam guides, Steam news/patch-note dump, Steam Discussions threads, Steam reviews export, developer roadmap images, an official developer YouTube transcript, a GamesPress press release, and a save-file editing guide that exposes real internal tech/perk IDs). Two live-web sources (Reddit search API, TVTropes) were attempted by the prior pass and returned empty/blocked (`reddit-franchise.json` / `reddit-sequel.json` = literal `"Not Found"`; `tvtropes.html` = a Cloudflare JS challenge page) — these are recorded as **open gaps**, not silently dropped.

Game identity: Weappy Studio (Poland; also made *This Is the Police* 1 & 2), Steam AppID 2680550, announced Nov 2023, demo cycles through 2024, Early Access launch **April 10, 2025**, still in EA as of the most recent captured patch (0.8.72EA, Aug 3 2026). Structured in "Acts" covering different Hollywood eras; Act 1 (roughly 1929–~1959) is the only content live at time of capture; Act 2 (up to 1965) is in active dev-diary/pre-announcement stage as of April 2026.

## 1. Sequel mechanics

- Source: `threads-sequels-1.txt`, thread "How to make sequel film?" ### 1d. Penalties for too-fast sequels / cooldown
- Source: cross-referenced across `threads-sequels-1.txt`, `threads-sequels-2.txt`, and `raw/thread-6sequels.html`: no post in the corpus describes any cooldown, wait timer, or fast-sequel penalty. Confidence HIGH (absence-of-evidence across an extensive, achievement/exploit-focused set of threads is reasonably strong here); source-tier COMMUNITY INFERENCE (a documented absence, not a dev statement).

## 3. Fatigue / overexposure

- Source: `steam-news-all.txt`, "'The Stuff That Dreams Are Made Of'" dev diary, 2025-05-07 (cited above in §1b); what it proves: fatigue was designed at the **story-element level**, not the franchise level — individual plot/setting/character tags "wear out" from repeated use across the whole game (any studio's movies, not just the player's), reducing their audience-attraction while "worn," and this rule applies to AI rivals identically; confidence HIGH; source-tier DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL (pre-patch commitment).
- Source: `threads-suggestions.txt`, thread "Suggestion - First time bonus" (…/594033220191057784/), OP Rooky, 6 Sep 2025; quote: *"With the Beta introducing the fresh and rotten mechanic I feel it would be nice to have a buff if you are the first studio to do something... As a balance to the harsh Stale mechanic."*; what it proves: the shipped (beta, later live) implementation is community-named **"Fresh / Rotten / Stale"** for elements, confirmed as live and "harsh" enough that players are requesting a compensating "first-mover" bonus; confidence HIGH (direct player description of a shipped beta feature, positively received: 3 replies, all agreeing); source-tier COMMUNITY INFERENCE, corroborating the dev-diary promise as shipped.
- Source: `steam-news-all.txt`, "Hollywood Animal's First Act Gets a Major Overhaul," 2025-09-29 (steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/…/1811772772373428); what it proves: this is the update that shipped "a reworked economy" and the beta-tested Act 1 overhaul referenced by the Fresh/Rotten/Stale thread above went live to all players on this date; confidence HIGH; source-tier DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL (patch announcement).
- What this means for P17: Hollywood Animal's fatigue system is **element-level and studio-agnostic** (a "shootout" tag gets stale for everyone once overused industry-wide), not **franchise-level** (there is no "this specific franchise is overexposed" meter — the closest thing is that a sequel reusing the same retained story elements will itself suffer from those elements being Stale). This is a structurally different design from Owner Direction C's three-factor, per-franchise Recognition/Momentum/Fatigue model. It is a useful negative case: HA shows what "fatigue with no franchise object underneath it" looks like in practice — diffuse, hard for players to reason about ("Does picking actors with matching traits affect only box office or also quality? We don't know" — see §9 quote below, symptomatic of the same underlying legibility problem), and not owner-attributable the way P17's Direction M (a lightweight persistent Franchise identity object) is designed to be legible.

  - `steam-reviews.json` id 194554424 (positive, 85.3 hrs), quote: "hoping it expands into the 70's so I can have my own MASH **reboot**" — a wishlist comment about future eras, not a mechanic description.
  - `steam-reviews.json` id 192548712 (positive, 118.2 hrs), quote: "This is kinda like if Paradox **rebooted** The Movies?" — again describing the game itself, not an in-game feature.
  - `steam-reviews.json` id 192545227 (positive, 3.8 hrs), quote: "I can't wait to **remake** every humphrey bogart film ever" — a stated player *desire*, evidence of demand for a remake-of-real/existing-work mechanic, not evidence one exists.
- What it proves: confidence HIGH that Remake and Reboot, as distinct mechanics (Owner Direction I), are **absent** from the shipped game; confidence HIGH also that there is organic player *demand* for something remake-shaped (redoing a specific earlier property, ideally a real-world one). Source-tier: COMMUNITY INFERENCE for the absence (documented, extensive non-mention across a large corpus purpose-built to surface exactly this); player reviews for the demand signal.
- Design implication for P17: this is a case where the comparator offers **no downside risk to flag**, because it never attempted the mechanic — Owner Direction I's remake-vs-reboot distinction is untested territory for this genre, not a territory where HA already hit a wall. Nothing here argues for reopening the Owner's choice; it simply means P17 cannot lean on HA for implementation patterns here and should treat this sub-system as green-field.

## 8. Early sequel production (before predecessor release)

- Source: `raw/thread-6sequels.html`, OP text (Voice From Beyond, 26 Apr 2025): *"A film must have been shown to start a sequel and only 1 sequel can be in production at a time..."*; what it proves: Hollywood Animal **requires the predecessor to have already released (been "shown")** before its sequel can even enter script-writing, and only one sequel-in-flight is allowed per lineage at any time; confidence HIGH (stated plainly, uncontested across the whole "6 sequels" thread which is *entirely* organized around working within this constraint); source-tier COMMUNITY INFERENCE, strongly corroborated (every subsequent poster in the thread designs their strategy around exactly this rule without disputing it).
- Design implication: this is a **direct structural contrast with Owner Direction G** (early continuation greenlight before predecessor release, "intentionally risky," rivals make equivalent bets). HA's shipped design is the more conservative, release-gated approach — and the corpus shows real player frustration at the resulting rigidity (§1a's Gh0ast complaint: "Most of the time the wait on research is worth it, not this time" — i.e., the *combination* of a research-tech gate *and* a release-gate *and* a forced-cast-carryover rule stacked together is what generates the specific complaint, not any single constraint alone). This is not an argument to reopen the Owner's chosen Direction G (early-greenlight is explicitly authorized and should stay); it *is* a concrete, evidenced illustration of the failure mode Direction G is designed to avoid — a same-genre comparator that gates too conservatively and visibly frustrates players for it.

## 9. Cast/talent continuity ("same team/actors" bonus, recasting)

- Source: `yt-shameless-sequel-transcript.txt` (cited above), 2:47–3:44: the dev-authored showcase narrates that *"Audiences don't like it too much when you replace an actor. We need to get a legend. There's only one guy for the part..."* — followed by a comedic bit about coercing a retired legend back on-set; what it proves: HA's design intent explicitly models **audience sensitivity to actor replacement** on a per-franchise/character basis, and treats securing a specific, narratively "correct" actor as a distinct, effortful goal (not a generic same-cast bonus, but tied to *that* character); confidence HIGH for design intent; source-tier PRE-RELEASE PROMISE, but structurally consistent with the shipped talent/negotiation systems described elsewhere in the corpus (contracts, poaching, loyalty).
- Source: `threads-sequels-1.txt`, "HUD for sequels" thread (…/594022439299642616/), OP WdeWorde and reply greifental, May 2025; what it proves: in the shipped game, "All available actors and staff members are preselected for their respective roles in preproduction and postproduction already" when starting a sequel — i.e. **default cast continuity is automatic**, not opt-in, and the player's complaint is only about the UI not showing *who* is preselected when they're busy elsewhere ("I've resorted to taking photos of the previous films so I can put the right people in the right places"); confidence HIGH; source-tier COMMUNITY INFERENCE, corroborating automatic-continuity-by-default as shipped behavior.

## Per-mechanic decision/fun/tedium/ADOPT-ADAPT-REJECT analysis

### Mechanic: Continuity/Freshness score at sequel-creation time
1. **Decision it creates:** which story elements to keep (audience recognizes it, "safe") vs. which to swap in fresh (novelty risk/reward), weighted against two independent meters shown live while designing the sequel script.
2. **What's fun:** it's a legible, immediate, single-screen trade-off — the player gets to *feel* clever balancing "enough same, enough new" rather than guessing; the dev's own showcase video frames it as a darkly comic, very "Hollywood" tension (do the same thing, pretend it's new).
3. **What becomes tedious/exploitable:** because the *quality bonus is inherent* to being a sequel (§1b) rather than earned, and because the score is calculated at design-time only (not decaying over the game's later life), the system rewards min-maxing the two meters once, then coasting — it does not need to be revisited, which is part of why long-session players describe converging on formulaic "smash the extend-release button" sequel farming (§1c, id 192527024).
4. **Hard-timer optimization?** Not on this specific sub-mechanic in isolation (it's a one-time design-time score), but it *composes* with the production-time constraints below to create one.
5. **Sequel spam?** Indirectly yes — because the score floor is forgiving (an "inherent bonus" applies regardless), there is little quality-based disincentive against low-effort sequels, which is exactly what the Trash King achievement exploits.
6. **ADOPT / ADAPT / REJECT for Project: Studio:**
   - **ADAPT:** the idea of a legible, two-axis, *design-time* preview meter (retained-vs-new) is genuinely good UX and is compatible with Owner Direction C's three-factor model — P17 could show a similar "how much of this continuation is carried vs. new" preview at StoryProperty/SubProperty authoring time, *without* granting an automatic quality bonus for high retention (which HA does and Owner Direction B explicitly forbids). Keep the transparency, drop the automatic-bonus behavior it currently drives.
   - **REJECT:** the "inherent audience bonus" for being a sequel *regardless of quality* — this directly conflicts with Owner Direction B (no automatic sequel quality bonus) and is exactly the mechanic HA's own long-tenure reviewers describe exploiting into a degenerate farm loop.

### Mechanic: One-sequel-in-flight-per-lineage + release-gated ("must have been shown") continuation
1. **Decision it creates:** sequencing — a studio can only ever be advancing one branch of one franchise's timeline at a time, forcing a queue/prioritization decision across multiple properties.
2. **What's fun:** it keeps each franchise's story state unambiguous (no risk of two divergent, uncommitted sequels to the same film existing at once), which is simple to reason about for both player and (per the corpus) apparently for the AI.
3. **What becomes tedious/exploitable:** exploitable in exactly the direction the achievement rewards — chaining minimal-effort sequels back-to-back as fast as possible is the *dominant* strategy once a player notices the achievement, and the mandatory-carryover-of-dead-or-awkward-characters rule (Gh0ast's complaint) makes the "quality" path of *not* immediately sequelizing feel penalized relative to the "just chain it" path.
4. **Hard-timer optimization?** **Yes, explicitly** — the "6 sequels in a year" achievement (§1c) is a textbook hard-timer optimization target, and the corpus documents a whole community sub-genre of theorycrafting around it (minimum shoot-day settings, Space location for its 2–3 day shoot, secondary-crew-only staffing).
5. **Sequel spam?** **Yes**, by design, for one entire named studio-policy playstyle (Trash King) — this is the strongest "sequel spam is rewarded" evidence found for any comparator in this genre.
6. **ADOPT / ADAPT / REJECT for Project: Studio:**
   - **REJECT the release-gate for early greenlighting specifically** — Owner Direction G already authorizes early continuation greenlight before the predecessor releases; HA's stricter release-gate is the opposite choice and demonstrably frustrates players who want to plan ahead (Gh0ast: "Most of the time the wait on research is worth it, not this time"). No need to reopen G; this is confirmatory, not a reason to change course.
   - **REJECT the un-gated, achievement-incentivized speed-chain pattern** as a model to imitate. Direction D's "no hard cooldown" is not the same thing as "actively reward the fastest possible chain via an achievement" — HA conflates the two, and the result is the single clearest sequel-spam failure mode in this entire comparator. P17 should keep "no hard cooldown" (per D) while making sure Fatigue (per C) actually bites hard enough, hard enough *specifically on repeated near-identical low-quality output*, that a HA-style speedrun chain would visibly tank Recognition/raise Fatigue rather than being achievement-rewarded. This is the paper's central structural-risk flag for this comparator.
   - **ADAPT** the single-slot-per-lineage constraint itself (only one *active, uncommitted* continuation per branch) as a light, sensible production-capacity constraint (consistent with Owner Direction D's "production capacity/opportunity cost is real"), decoupled from any hard-timer reward.

### Mechanic: Automatic, mandatory cast/character carryover into sequels (no recast choice, dead characters still auto-inserted)
1. **Decision it creates:** almost none — carryover is default and largely non-negotiable, which removes a decision point players evidently wanted (Gh0ast; the id-197507522 feature request for actor↔character "known for" tracking implies players want *more* agency around character identity, not less).
2. **What's fun:** the automatic preselection does save real friction for players who *do* want continuity (WdeWorde/greifental thread) — "it just works" is genuinely convenient when the default is what you wanted anyway.
3. **What becomes tedious/exploitable:** tedious when it isn't what you wanted — forced-in dead antagonists, no role-swap/promotion, no way to retire a character gracefully into a new role. Not obviously exploitable (it's a rigidity problem, not a min-max problem).
4. **Hard-timer optimization?** No.
5. **Sequel spam?** Not directly, though the rigidity plausibly *pushes* players toward starting wholly new IP rather than working within an awkward existing one, which is an indirect anti-franchise-depth pressure worth noting.
6. **ADOPT / ADAPT / REJECT for Project: Studio:**
   - **ADOPT** the convenience default (preselecting the prior film's cast into the obvious slots of a direct sequel) as a UX pattern — it is good, low-friction default behavior.
   - **REJECT** the *mandatory, non-overridable* part. This is the clearest structural-risk exposure for Owner Direction F/J's boundary: whatever P16/P17 build for "recurring character ↔ actor" association must let the player override or retire a character's continuity without the system forcing a narratively dead/awkward character back into existence. This validates (not reopens) Direction F's explicit goal of "prevent mandatory cast lock-in" — HA is a live example of the *story-level* sibling of that exact failure mode.

### Mechanic: Franchises/licensed-IP system
1–6. Not evaluable on the fun/tedium/spam axes — **it has not shipped** (§2). The only assessable "decision" is the meta-decision the *developer* faced and, per the evidence, appears to have deprioritized: whether to build a full cross-film franchise/rights layer at all, given the scope of Act 1's own rebalancing and Act 2's contract/distribution overhaul consuming the team's stated roadmap bandwidth for over a year. **ADOPT/ADAPT/REJECT is not applicable here in the technical sense** — there is nothing implemented to adopt, adapt, or reject. The actionable lesson is scope-management, not mechanics: a comparator studio with a comparable ambition explicitly promised this exact feature set and, 21 months later, still has not shipped it, while shipping substantial adjacent systems (contracts, distributors, international offices, TV licensing) instead. Project: Studio's P16/P17 split (rights/ownership as its own package, separate from continuation-state) is, if anything, corroborated by this: HA's promise bundled "Franchises" + "licensed external IP" + timeline/spin-off UI as one deliverable, and that bundling may be part of why it has stalled — supporting (not reopening) the Owner's package-boundary choices (P16 vs P17 vs P18) as a scope-management safeguard.
