## 0. Headline

Both comparators ship a **single, hard, code-level threshold** where the Owner explicitly rejected one (Owner-direction D: "No hard sequel cooldown… timing alone is not bad"): GDT's decompiled Review Algorithm applies a flat **−0.4 quality-factor penalty** to any sequel/expansion released **less than 40 weeks** after its predecessor, and its Sales Algorithm **independently** converts what would have been a fan *gain* into a forced fan *loss* under the same 40-week condition — two separate systems keyed to the identical cliff-edge, confirmed by community decompilation of the shipped executable. GDS gates continuation on a **binary Hall-of-Fame pass/fail** (≥32/40) with a **permanent, non-recoverable chain-death** if a sequel misses the bar — the single clearest shipped counter-example to Owner-direction P ("franchises never permanently unusable"). Neither game models remakes, reboots, prequels, spin-offs, licensed/rented IP, cast/talent continuity, or AI rivals making franchise decisions at all — both are single-player economies with no simulated competitor agency, so **neither comparator offers any evidence for Owner-direction G/K (rivals making equivalent early-continuation bets under the same rules)**. That is a real, load-bearing gap for Project: Studio to note, not solve by analogy to these two games.

## 1. Game Dev Tycoon (Greenheart Games)

### 1.1 Basic facts
- Greenheart Games is an Australian indie studio founded July 2012 by brothers Patrick and Daniel Klug; Game Dev Tycoon released 10 Dec 2012. (Wikipedia "Greenheart Games" / "Game Dev Tycoon", via WebSearch synthesis, 12 Sep 2026; HIGH; CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL SOURCE)
- The Sequel feature exists from very early in the game's patch history: v1.1.1 (19 Feb 2013) shipped a fix for "Game crashes when trying to develop a sequel from a multi-genre game," and v1.0.3 (24 Dec 2012, i.e. 14 days after launch) already had Expansion Pack fixes ("Disabled expansion packs if no appropriate games are available"; "Numerous issues due to invalid expansion pack"). (Greenheart Games official changelog, https://www.greenheartgames.com/game-dev-tycoon-changelog/ ; via WebFetch, 12 Sep 2026; HIGH; DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL) — proves Sequel/Expansion Pack are core, day-one-adjacent systems, not a late feature bolt-on.

### 1.2 Sequel mechanics — how a sequel is created, gates, and what it inherits

**Feature unlock (a fixed calendar gate on the whole SYSTEM, not a per-title cooldown):**
- The "Sequels" special research becomes researchable at a fixed point in every playthrough: **Year 8, Month 6**. This is listed alongside other special researches (Target Audience Y3M1W1, Casual Games Y3M11W1, Marketing Y4M5W2, Multi Genre Y12M8, Expansion Pack "MMO researched and MMO Game is on Sale", Multi-Platform Y17M2W4). (Game Dev Tycoon Wiki, "Unlocks" page, https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Unlocks , pulled via MediaWiki API 12 Sep 2026; HIGH; COMMUNITY INFERENCE — but a structured, internally-consistent table, cross-confirmed below)
- Independent Steam-thread confirmation: a player asks "you can only develop sequels at like, year 8 month 7. Why is that?" and another player ("Alan Dracula") confirms a mod ("Starting Sequels") is needed to unlock sequels earlier, "after your first game (as long as it's not terrible)," adding "it honestly completely changes the dynamic of the game." (Steam discussion "Sequels", https://steamcommunity.com/app/239820/discussions/0/135509758528979794/ ; player "Mighty Idea" and "Alan Dracula"; via WebFetch 12 Sep 2026; HIGH; COMMUNITY INFERENCE) — **this is a structural fact worth flagging for Project: Studio**: GDT's own players regard the fixed-calendar sequel gate as an arbitrary, un-fun restriction severe enough to need a community mod to remove, which is independent evidence against any hard universal unlock-date gate (reinforces Owner-direction D/G, that early/flexible continuation should be a player decision, not a calendar unlock).
- A sequel cannot be made while the originating game is under an external Publisher contract; it must be self-published. Steam players describe the workaround as building fans through publisher deals first, then self-publishing sequels once independent: "Just do the sequels yourself once you have enough fans… sequels should usually only be done once you have a new graphics update" (Dr.Acula); "IMO best thing to do is make new games with publishers until you get enough fans then pump out" [sequels] (Kotli); "Unfortunately, no, there is no way to do a true sequel while dealing with a publisher" (Balveda_Vex). (Steam discussion "Sequels + Publisher", https://steamcommunity.com/app/239820/discussions/0/458605613390537055/ ; via WebFetch 12 Sep 2026; MEDIUM-HIGH; COMMUNITY INFERENCE)

**What a sequel requires and inherits, and the exact "too soon" penalty (community decompilation of shipped code, `Review Algorithm` wiki page):**
- Verbatim, from the "Various checks" section of the reverse-engineered Review Algorithm (this page documents the game's code path step-by-step and is treated as authoritative by the GDT community; pulled via the Fandom API, redirect target `Review Algorithm/1.4.4`, https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Review_Algorithm ):
  > "If the combination of Topic/Genre/Second Genre is the exact same as the previous released title, a penalty of **-0.4** is applied to q [quality factor]"
  > "If the game is a sequel (or an expansion) to a game released **less than 40 weeks ago**, a penalty of **-0.4** is applied to q"
  > "If the game is a sequel (**not** an expansion) and uses the **same engine** as the previous game in the series, a penalty of **-0.1** is applied to q"
  > "If the game is a sequel and uses a **better engine** than the previous game, a bonus of **+0.2** is applied to q"
  (HIGH confidence — internally consistent with independent forum testimony below and with the Sales Algorithm's independent 40-week fan clause; COMMUNITY INFERENCE, reverse-engineered/decompiled, functionally equivalent to shipped-code ground truth)
- This confirms and dates **exactly** the community-repeated "40 weeks" rule: forum poster **JustMaffie** states plainly "you shouldn't release a sequel less th[a]n 40 weeks later," which "resets for each installment" in a chain, and recommends "a new engine is also recommended" though acknowledges having made "some good sequels on the same engine." (Greenheart Games Forum, "Tips for sequels", https://forum.greenheartgames.com/t/tips-for-sequels/23233 ; via WebFetch 12 Sep 2026; HIGH; DEVELOPER-COMMUNITY FORUM, i.e. COMMUNITY INFERENCE on the developer's own forum)
- Separately, on a different Steam thread, player **Lezard** independently states the same combined rule in one sentence: "[a review penalty] also happens when you publish a sequel too soon, and/or the same topic and gener[e] in succe[ssion]." (Steam discussion "Why Did it Fail?", https://steamcommunity.com/app/239820/discussions/0/666826703620742043/ ; via WebFetch 12 Sep 2026; HIGH; COMMUNITY INFERENCE) — note this correctly identifies the **two separate** −0.4 penalties (sequel-timing vs. identical-combo-in-a-row) as distinct rules that are easy for players to conflate; Project: Studio's package-ownership split (P15 market/genre crowding vs. P17 franchise fatigue) is exactly the right structural fix for this exact source of player confusion.
- What a sequel formally "inherits": the genre and (implicitly, per the multi-genre crash fix above) second genre carry over as the point of comparison for the same-combo penalty; there is **no evidence in any source found** that a GDT sequel inherits a numeric "quality floor," starting Design/Tech points, or any score value from its parent — unlike Game Dev Story (below), GDT's sequel bonus/penalty is purely a **modifier on the new game's own build** (engine delta, timing, same-vs-different combo), not an inherited baseline. This is an important comparator contrast: **GDT models sequel quality as "did you improve your tools and wait long enough," GDS models it as "did the parent already prove itself, and do you get a head start."** (DESIGN INFERENCE from the above two sourced sections; HIGH)

**Score relationship to the "original" more broadly — self-competition, not parent-inheritance:**
- Independent of the sequel-specific modifiers, GDT's entire review system is a "compete against your own escalating history" ladder: "you compete exclusively against your own previous high score;" Target Game Score ≈ Top Score + a capped delta, so that (absent a sequel) "you can either keep releasing games of the same Game Score as the last one over and over, and receive 7+ reviews… or releas[e] games with a Game Score just a little bit higher than before… and receive 8+/9+ reviews;" a >20% quality spike raises the bar to 120% of Top Score and is a trap ("it will be harder to calculate your target — so better not do it in the garage ever"). (Game Dev Tycoon Wiki, "Success Guide" and "Review Algorithm" pages, pulled via API 12 Sep 2026; HIGH; COMMUNITY INFERENCE, decompilation-derived)
- This means the much-repeated player complaint "my second game always sucks" is a **real, sourced, documented mechanic** (the escalating Target Score), and is a *different* mechanic from the sequel-specific 40-week/engine modifiers — a sequel simply adds its own extra +0.2/−0.1/−0.4 on top of this always-present ratchet. Steam thread title confirms the complaint pattern exists as community folklore: "My second game always sucks" (https://steamcommunity.com/app/239820/discussions/0/592897173815840161/ , title only, not deep-fetched; MEDIUM; COMMUNITY INFERENCE).

### 1.3 Hype mechanic
- Exact decompiled formula: "The hype factor h is defined from H the number of hype points gathered at the time of release: **h = H/500**, and h is then capped to 1." Hype increases sales when review score R≥5 (K increased by `0.05*M*h*((R-5)/5)`) and actively *decreases* sales when R≤5 (K decreased by `0.25*M*h*U*(R/5)`) — i.e., hyping a bad game is explicitly modeled as making the bad review worse, not better. (Game Dev Tycoon Wiki "Sales Algorithm" page, https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Sales_Algorithm , pulled via API 12 Sep 2026; HIGH; COMMUNITY INFERENCE, decompilation-derived)
- Hype is generated by conventions (G3, a once-a-year event; or your own convention once researched — "generates more hype than G3"), Marketing Campaigns, and "Management" bonuses. Player-summarized sources: "I seen hype happen when I market the game, or after a G3 convention. It will also happen if a reporter happens to mention your game" (Waverunner); "hype = more money but if the game is bad u will loose some fans" (Betelgeuse); a player-derived shorthand "Hype*fans=Sales-X". (Steam discussion "How does the Hype works?", https://steamcommunity.com/app/239820/discussions/0/864977564147885621/ ; via WebFetch 12 Sep 2026; MEDIUM-HIGH; COMMUNITY INFERENCE)
- Hype is a **release-time-only, spend-it-or-lose-it resource**: it is accumulated during development, consumed/capped at release, and there is no evidence in any source found of hype persisting or decaying between games — it resets to effectively zero for the next project. This is a materially different shape from Owner-direction C's MOMENTUM ("fast, decays to neutral") — GDT's hype doesn't decay toward a neutral baseline over time, it is simply zeroed by consumption at each release. (DESIGN INFERENCE from the formula above; MEDIUM-HIGH — no source explicitly states inter-game hype persistence in either direction, so absence-of-evidence is the basis, flagged as a gap in 14.)

### 1.4 Fans (closest analogue to Recognition/IP state)
- Fans are **one company-wide pool**, not per-title or per-franchise; the decompiled Sales Algorithm's `F` (fan increase) is added to/subtracted from a single running total, and the same pool determines self-publish sales boosts ("If self-published, K is increased by (fans * (R/10))"). No named per-IP "fan" object exists anywhere in the sourced material. (Sales Algorithm page, as above; HIGH; COMMUNITY INFERENCE, decompilation-derived)
- **The exact, sourced 40-week fan-loss rule** (independent of, and additional to, the −0.4 quality penalty above): 
  > "If R ≥ 7 and the game is either not a sequel or a sequel to a game that is more than 40 weeks old: F is increased by [5%↔10%] (of your current number of fans)…"
  > "If R ≤ 5 **or** the game is a sequel to a recent (< 40 weeks) game: F is **set** to [-0.25 * (# of fans) * (1 - R/10) ↔ 0]"
  (Sales Algorithm page, "Fan increase" section, as above; HIGH; COMMUNITY INFERENCE, decompilation-derived) — this is the single most important sourced fact for the P17 stress-test: **a review score of, say, 8/10 on a too-fast sequel does not merely fail to grow the fanbase — the code explicitly overrides the normal quality-based fan gain and instead forces a fan-LOSS roll, scaled by how far below 10 the score is.** A good game, made too soon, is coded to actively shrink your audience. This is the most literal possible implementation of a hard sequel-timing cliff with a punitive (not just neutral) consequence — exactly the shape Owner-direction D says Project: Studio should NOT build ("no hard sequel cooldown… timing alone is not bad").
- Fans have no documented decay-over-time absent a release (no "fans fade if you go quiet" mechanic found in any source) — the pool only moves on a sale event. (DESIGN INFERENCE from absence of any such formula term in the Sales Algorithm page; MEDIUM, flagged as open gap.)

### 1.7 Assessment (Game Dev Tycoon)
1. **Decision created:** (a) whether to wait out the 40-week timer and build a better engine before sequelling a hit, trading time-to-market against a coded quality/fan penalty; (b) whether to vary your genre/topic combo release-to-release to dodge the separate same-combo penalty; (c) whether to self-publish (enabling sequels) vs. take a publisher deal (blocking them) for a given title.
2. **What is fun:** the formulas are legible enough (once known) to feel like a genuine puzzle — "bumps up replay value" (Senarin) — and the engine-upgrade requirement gives sequelling a tangible, proactive R&D goal rather than a passive wait.
3. **What becomes tedious/exploitable:** because the penalty is a hard, uniform 40-week/same-engine cliff rather than a continuous curve, optimal play degenerates into **counting weeks and re-skinning an upgraded engine on a schedule** — a mechanical timer-watching loop, not a creative "is now the right moment for this IP" judgment call. The community's own "Starting Sequels" mod (removing the Y8M6 unlock delay) and the widespread "wait 40 weeks, use a new engine" one-line advice (repeated near-verbatim across at least three independent threads: JustMaffie, the "40 weeks"/"newer engine" WebSearch synthesis, and Lezard) show the mechanic collapses to a checklist almost immediately once discovered.
4. **Hard-timer optimization: YES, unambiguously.** Both the feature-unlock gate (Y8M6, fixed calendar) and the per-sequel penalty threshold (<40 weeks) are literal, sourced, decompiled hard timers with cliff-edge consequences (−0.4 quality, forced fan-loss roll) rather than continuous decay. This is the single cleanest shipped counter-example available anywhere in this atlas to Owner-direction D's explicit rejection of hard cooldowns.
5. **Sequel spam:** GDT does not have a documented late-game "chain forever" exploit *in the community's own vocabulary* ("sequel spam" and "sequel farm"/"sequel chain" as exact phrases returned no hits) — the practical ceiling is that the escalating Target-Score ratchet (1.2) means simply repeating "make a hit, then its sequel, then that sequel's sequel" does not compound score forever; each new entry is still judged against your rising personal best. The closest documented "farm" pattern is qualitative, not a named exploit: "If you get to make a perfect game, wait for a year and then make it again with the same platform, topic and type" (search-summarized forum/Steam consensus; MEDIUM; COMMUNITY INFERENCE) — i.e. players do treat "wait out the timer, then reuse a proven combo" as a reliable late-game production pattern, which is a mild form of the sequel-spam risk even without a named community term for it.
6. **ADOPT / ADAPT / REJECT for Project: Studio:**
   - **ADOPT** the idea of *two structurally separate levers* on a continuation's quality — one for production-values delta (engine tech level: −0.1 same / +0.2 better) and one for timing (−0.4 if too soon) — as a precedent for keeping Momentum/Fatigue/Recognition as genuinely separate signals (Owner-direction C) rather than one blended score. GDT already proves a shipped game can keep these as separate additive terms without becoming incomprehensible.
   - **REJECT outright** the hard 40-week cliff-edge itself (both the score penalty and, especially, the forced-fan-LOSS override) as a *mechanism* — it is precisely the hard-cooldown-with-punitive-consequence shape Owner-direction D forbids, and GDT's own player base treats it as a bureaucratic timer to satisfy rather than a creative decision, which is the exact failure mode Owner-direction D/C (continuous, bounded model; "timing alone is not bad") is designed to avoid.
   - **REJECT** the fixed-calendar (Y8M6) global unlock gate for the whole continuation *system* — Project: Studio's continuation types should be available whenever the underlying StoryProperty/predecessor state supports them (per Owner-direction M/H), not gated by an arbitrary campaign-clock milestone; GDT's own players independently flagged this as needing a mod fix.
   - **ADOPT** the same-combo-in-a-row penalty as a *separate, non-franchise* mechanic (GDT applies it to ANY two consecutive releases, sequel or not) — this maps cleanly onto Project: Studio's P15 market-crowding/genre-saturation ownership, and GDT is good evidence that keeping "genre fatigue" and "franchise fatigue" as two distinct systems (rather than one FranchiseScore) is workable and legible, reinforcing the Owner's existing package split rather than exposing any new risk to it.
   - **ADAPT** the hype-as-injected-resource shape (accumulate → cap → spend at release) as a rough analogue for a fast-moving campaign/marketing input, but do NOT copy its "hard reset to ~zero every release" behavior for Momentum — Owner-direction C specifically wants Momentum to *decay toward neutral*, not snap to a floor at every release event.
   - **REJECT as a source of design pattern** (not as a criticism of the game) GDT's total absence of remakes/reboots/spin-offs, cast/talent continuity, licensed/rented IP, pre-release greenlighting, and AI rivals — none of these existing in GDT means it offers **zero positive evidence** for those parts of Owner-direction A/F/G/K; Project: Studio must source those patterns elsewhere in the atlas (04-series real-franchise evidence, or design-original), not from this comparator.

## 2. Game Dev Story (Kairosoft)

### 2.1 Basic facts
- Kairosoft's Game Dev Story originated on Japanese feature phones in 2007–2010, then shipped in English on iOS/Android in 2010; the Steam PC port (appid 1847240) released 27 Mar 2022 and holds a "Very Positive" rating (275 positive / 22 negative of 297 English-language reviews sampled). (Wikipedia "Game Dev Story"; Steam store page https://store.steampowered.com/app/1847240/Game_Dev_Story/ ; Steam `appreviews` API pulled directly 12 Sep 2026; HIGH; CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL SOURCE + DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL for the Steam listing itself)
- Notably, Kairosoft itself announced a literal "**Game Dev Story 2**" as early as 30 Nov 2010, promising bid/negotiate salary systems and social leaderboards — but it never received a release comparable to the original's reach across platforms (no widely-available shipped version found in any source). (Pocket Gamer, "Kairosoft's Game Dev Story 2 to feature leaderboards, shared employees", 2010, via WebSearch synthesis 12 Sep 2026; MEDIUM; CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL SOURCE) — a small but relevant meta-note: even the developer whose own game formalizes "sequel" as a mechanic struggled to ship a real-world sequel to that game, illustrating (OBSERVED HISTORY, anecdotal, LOW-N) that the real-world friction Project: Studio is trying to model (a hit franchise's sequel stalling or under-delivering relative to hype) is not confined to fiction.

### 2.2 Sequel mechanics — Hall of Fame gate, inheritance, chain-death
- Verbatim rule set from the community wiki's "Creating games" page (this is the most load-bearing citation for GDS and is treated by the whole GDS community, including GameFAQs, as the authoritative mechanic description):
  > "A game enters the Hall of Fame when its total critic score is **32 or higher**" (of a possible 40, i.e. four critics scoring up to 10 each).
  > "The sequel must be started while the original game is still in the records (i.e. **one of the latest 32 games**)."
  > "Genre and type cannot be changed from original[;]" the sequel "can be made for a different console tha[n] the original."
  > "[The sequel] begins with some points carried over from the original game."
  > "Each game can only be sequeled once; the sequel, if it again gets a review score of 32/40 or above, can itself have a sequel[; but] if a sequel does not make the Hall of Fame on its own, **no more sequels can be made in that franchise**."
  (Kairosoft Wiki, "Creating games (Game Dev Story)", https://kairosoft.wiki.gg/wiki/Creating_games_(Game_Dev_Story) ; via WebFetch 12 Sep 2026; HIGH; COMMUNITY INFERENCE)
- This is a genuine, unlimited-length **chain** mechanic (a sequel's sequel's sequel is legal, matching GDT's chaining, not a two-game cap) — but with a **permanent kill-switch**: the instant one entry in the chain fails to re-qualify for the Hall of Fame, the entire franchise is permanently locked out of any further continuation, forever, with no revival path documented anywhere. This is the single sharpest, most literal shipped counter-example in this whole atlas to **Owner-direction P** ("Franchises never permanently unusable: ACTIVE / COOLING / DORMANT / REVIVAL CANDIDATE / ACTIVE AGAIN"). Flag this prominently: GDS is proof that a "permanent franchise death" design is shippable and popular (Very Positive on Steam), but it is the exact opposite of what the Owner selected, so it should be read only as a **cautionary structural data point**, not as a pattern to import.
- The inheritance model ("begins with some points carried over") is qualitatively different from GDT's: GDS explicitly gives the sequel a numeric head-start baseline derived from its specific parent, whereas GDT gives only relative modifiers (engine/timing) with no inherited baseline (see 1.2). ### 2.6 Assessment (Game Dev Story)
1. **Decision created:** whether to spend a Hall-of-Fame-qualifying game's "records" window (latest-32-games) on a same-genre/type sequel (safe head-start, locked genre) vs. a fresh new IP (full creative freedom, no head-start); once several qualifying parents exist simultaneously, *which* one to sequel becomes a portfolio-management decision.
2. **What is fun:** the chain is legible and rewarding — "each game felt like it did better than the last" (ephemierose) — and the Hall-of-Fame framing gives a clear, visible trophy case tying score history to continuation eligibility, which is a genuinely good UI/legibility pattern (see 3. ADOPT below).
3. **What becomes tedious/exploitable:** because a sequel "begins with some points carried over," repeatedly sequelling your best-scoring lineage is the **statistically safest** way to keep hitting the Hall of Fame, which the community's own strategy tips confirm is the intended/expected optimal play ("The key is sequels… produce 3-4 Hall of Fame games per year") — this is a mild, developer-sanctioned form of sequel-spam-as-optimal-strategy, softer than GDT's exploit but arguably *more* central to "how you're supposed to play well" rather than an edge case.
4. **Hard-timer optimization:** **partially.** There is no week-count cooldown (unlike GDT), but there IS a hard, binary quality-based gate (32/40) and a hard eligibility WINDOW (latest 32 games "in the records") that functions as an implicit soft-timer — wait too long (release too many other games) and the sequel option silently disappears rather than merely decaying. This is a *different* hard-edge shape than GDT's calendar timer, but still a cliff, not a continuous curve.
5. **Sequel spam:** **yes, and unusually, it is the developer-sanctioned optimal strategy**, not merely a discovered exploit — the community's own tips page states this plainly (2.5, quote 5).
6. **ADOPT / ADAPT / REJECT for Project: Studio:**
   - **REJECT outright** the permanent, unrecoverable franchise-chain-death on a single missed Hall-of-Fame sequel — this is the sharpest available shipped counter-example to Owner-direction P and should be treated as a documented risk pattern to avoid, not merely an unused option: a system that permanently and irreversibly locks out continuation on one bad release is the single most severe possible violation of "franchises never permanently unusable."
   - **ADAPT** the Hall-of-Fame/"records" framing as a **UI/legibility** pattern (a visible, rank-ordered list of your best titles that continuation options are drawn from) — this is a good precedent for how Project: Studio could surface which StoryProperties are eligible/attractive for a given continuation type, WITHOUT copying the binary pass/fail gate or the permanent-death consequence behind it; keep the display, discard the hard threshold and its finality.
   - **ADOPT** the general principle that a quality-conditioned "soft" inheritance (starting a bit further along because the parent did well) is intuitive to players and matches Owner-direction B/C ("good releases add excitement… no automatic sequel quality bonus" — note GDS's bonus IS automatic and unconditional-on-anything-but-score, which the Owner has explicitly rejected as a *default*, so this should be ADAPTED as an optional, importance-weighted nudge at most, not a flat inherited-points mechanic).
   - **REJECT** the eligibility-window-as-implicit-soft-timer ("latest 32 games") as a mechanism — it produces exactly the kind of decision-fatigue and "sequel picker clutter" complaints noted in 2.2, and functions as an arbitrary capacity limit rather than a meaningful Recognition/Momentum signal.
   - **REJECT as a source of pattern** (same reasoning as GDT 1.7) GDS's total absence of remakes/reboots/spin-offs/licensed-IP/cast-continuity/pre-release-greenlighting/AI-rival-agency — it offers zero positive evidence for those Owner-direction items.
