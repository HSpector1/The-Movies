## 0. Headline

Across nine tycoon/management comparators, NO shipped game implements the three-factor Recognition / Momentum / Fatigue split the Owner selected; each ships one or two of the three, and the missing one is where its sequel exploits live. The most legible shipped shapes are: a durable count with a visible cap (City Game Studio fans, CK3 fame levels, FM reputation 0–10000 shown as stars/labels), a fast quantity that is "easy to get, easy to lose" (Idol Manager casual fans, FM morale Abysmal→Superb, GDM/CGS hype 0–100% as a sales multiplier), and an accumulating penalty with explicit per-source decay (CK3 tyranny −0.25/month, Civ VI war weariness at −1 amenity per 400 points, Big Pharma oversaturation). The real-world literature is consistent with Owner directions B, C, D, F, H and Q: sequels released sooner earn more and having MORE prior installments helps rather than hurts (Basuroy & Chatterjee 2008), satiation is driven by sameness/numbering not by count (Sood & Drèze 2006), parent awareness and star continuity dominate sequel value with a large awareness×star interaction (Hennig-Thurau et al. 2009), and remakes reduce risk rather than raise revenue and depend on the original's awareness/image (Bohnenkamp et al. 2015).

## 1. City Game Studio (Binogure Studio; Steam appid 726840; released 2019, still updated — v1.28 announced for 23 Sep 2026)

### 1.1 Sequel mechanics
- Sequels are made from the game history panel; sequel names are auto-generated (a naming bug in the history panel was fixed in patch 1.15.1). (Binogure patch note "Patch 1.15.1 - Smoother Studio Rebuilding & Sequel Naming Fix", 2 Oct 2023, https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/5220291886500869668 ; proves sequels are created from the per-game history record; HIGH; DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL)
- No sequel count limit: the itch.io store text says you can "make sequels without limits – you can make 1 sequel or 2, and call it a trilogy". (binogure.itch.io/city-game-studio, search summary; page itself returned 403; proves unlimited chain; MEDIUM; DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL via search summary)
- A sequel gives pre-production points in every phase: "if you know a theme genre match and/or its a sequel, you get prepod points in all phases and in the des.phase too". (Steam guide "70% Of the Slider Rank + Tips & Tricks", comment by Tesba, 4 Mar 2019, https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1667394513 ; proves sequel = development head-start, not a reception bonus; MEDIUM; COMMUNITY INFERENCE)
- Community timing advice: "a big game score will attract more people if you do a sequel some years after, with a bigger studio or a bigger engine". (same guide, tip 6; proves community perceives sequel value as predecessor-score × better tech, with no hard cooldown; MEDIUM; COMMUNITY INFERENCE)
- Sequel/remake chain must hang off the ORIGINAL, not off an award re-release: "GOTY: Create sequels/remake of GOTY doesn't make sense. You can keep create sequel/remake of the original game." (Binogure patch note "Some additions and fixes", 7 Nov 2022, https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4732747437216731560 ; proves the game keys continuations to one canonical parent record; HIGH; DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL)
- Fan-driven sequel requests with an EXPIRY WINDOW. Player: "when fans ask for a sequel if you are in a middle of making a game ... when you finished the current game the sequel has expired." Developer Binogure: "this feature is here to force you to make a decision", suggesting the player pause the current project by unassigning studios, while warning "when you pause a game's development you take some risks." (Steam thread "Feedback on Consoles and creating sequel", andoblock and Binogure, 26 Sep 2025, https://steamcommunity.com/app/726840/discussions/0/598538723409599584/ ; proves an intentional hard-timer decision mechanic; HIGH; DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL)
- Fan request trigger conditions are described only by the community as "After 3 years, if a game has more than 90% score, fans can ask for a sequel". (search summary attributed to the CGS Steam news feed; I could not locate the exact post; LOW; COMMUNITY INFERENCE)
- The AI "Studio Director" (delegated development) could NOT make sequels until May 2026; now it can, default off, player-controlled: "The studio director can now make sequels. They can do it, but by default they won't." (Binogure devlog "Promise Kept: Studio Directors Can Now Make Sequels", 22 May 2026, https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/1833334318572360 ; and v1.27 notes 13 Jun 2026 https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/1835236783566240 ; proves sequels were treated as a player-judgement decision the automation was deliberately excluded from for 7 years; HIGH; DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL)

### 1.2 Fans (IP/recognition state)
- Fans are a single company-level counter, not per-IP. Developer on gain: "A rating between 80 and 90 doesn't bring a lot of fans. In fact, the bigger the rating the more the fans."; bigger game sizes bring more fans; fans also come from lowering price, GOTY ("fans for free (basically)"), publishing updates, interviews, hiring studio directors; a 90+ AAA free-to-play with subscriptions could yield "more than 100K fans (within a year)". (Steam thread, Binogure, 21 Jan 2025, https://steamcommunity.com/app/726840/discussions/0/744883366278524950 ; HIGH; DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL)
- Fans have a dynamic cap: "There's a dynamic cap. In 2025 it's something like 150 millions, then in 2030 it's 200 millions." A player at the cap reported losing "100k+ fans" per release despite 90+ ratings. (same thread, Binogure 3 Mar 2025 and Achim_Mentzel; proves a bounded, era-scaled audience pool; HIGH for cap, MEDIUM for loss-at-cap; DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL + COMMUNITY INFERENCE)
- Fans respond to price: a player with "less than 100 fans" into the 1980s self-diagnosed "i always put my game price to max :(" ; a community tip: click the hype "heart" ("talk to the press") on every project. (Steam thread, LittleFingers and Satior, https://steamcommunity.com/app/726840/discussions/0/3174449951075935958 ; proves fan growth is opaque enough that players fail to find it; MEDIUM; COMMUNITY INFERENCE)
- Fan mail events (v1.11.0): "your fans are going to write to you ... when they ask you for something, I advise you to listen to them, your wallet will be grateful. Fans are going to contact you mostly to talk about your games, to ask you for updates, sequels..." and releases have "a slight chance" to shift theme popularity. (Binogure "It looks like magic", 21 Feb 2023, https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/5059268099254993792 ; HIGH; DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL)

### 1.3 Fatigue / overexposure
- No named fatigue system. Community reports a genre-repetition penalty: "do not release RPG over RPG over RPG, people will get bored and even with perfect stat the game won't be good" (guide tip 7, same guide as above; MEDIUM; COMMUNITY INFERENCE) and "Generally after the 5th or 6th same genre/different theme have I noticed a dip in sales" (Satior, https://steamcommunity.com/app/726840/discussions/0/3133919455870247382 ; MEDIUM; COMMUNITY INFERENCE). Whether this is a genre-saturation rule or a score-derivation artefact is not documented by the developer.
- Market exit rule: "after 6 months of revenues below $1000 is the best condition to remove a game from the market." (7 Nov 2022 patch; proves catalogue pruning by revenue, not by IP state; HIGH; DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL)

### 1.4 Remakes / remasters / ports
- Remake (v1.3.0): "What you do when you remake a game is to enhance the features that were implemented at the time, rather than modify the original game. Thus, remaking an old game should be a profitable operation if the game engine has improved enough in comparison to the original game." Remaster ports also added. (Binogure "City Game Studio, let's remake classics", 29 Sep 2021, https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/3938951840876717083 ; proves remake value is keyed to tech delta since original, not to elapsed time; HIGH; DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL)
- Remake first-day explosion: a Big/AAA remake "sold for $11 billion and 3.5 million fans" on day one then "$38" on day three; developer diagnosed a custom-platform bug ("a lack of new players coming to your custom platform") and "reworked the sales algorithm a little bit". (Steam bug thread, Cola500 and Binogure, 28–31 Dec 2021, https://steamcommunity.com/app/726840/discussions/2/3202621452553890241/ ; proves the fanbase buys on day one and remake sales are driven by the existing fan pool; HIGH; DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL)
- Port loop exploit closed: "A player found a way to port the same game over and over to their own console. This allowed making tons of games and money in minimum time." (v1.25.0 notes, 10 Dec 2025, https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/1818752592121558 ; HIGH; DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL)

### 1.5 Ownership, licensed IP, early sequels, cast, rivals
- No licensed game IP; "licenses" are console licences only. Rivals' games are "inspired from reality" (name, genre, theme, release date, units). (Steam news "The BIG update!", 6 Jul 2022, search summary; MEDIUM; DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL) Rival sequel behaviour is undocumented (gap).
- Early sequel before predecessor release: effectively impossible for the player because the fan request fires after release and the whole studio is occupied; not a modelled concept.
- Talent continuity: none (staff are skill sliders; a suggestion thread asks for "important person in the rpg game community ... adds additional hype/fans" — unimplemented). (https://steamcommunity.com/app/726840/discussions/0/3133919455870247382 ; LOW; COMMUNITY INFERENCE)

### 1.6 Assessment
1. Decision created: "finish what you are making or drop it for the fan-requested sequel now" (explicit developer intent) and "port/remake old catalogue vs make new".
2. Fun: fan letters and trilogy naming give a franchise a story; remakes reward engine progress.
3. Tedious/exploitable: fan growth opaque (price/hype heart); port and remaster loops were real money exploits and had to be patched; genre repetition penalty undocumented.
4. Hard-timer optimisation: YES — the expiring fan request is a deliberate hard timer; players complained and asked for "an option to have more time so it doesn't expire".
5. Sequel spam: unlimited chains, no per-IP fatigue; spam is capped only by studio capacity and (undocumented) genre-repetition sales dip.
6. Project: Studio: ADOPT the single canonical parent record for continuations (GOTY rule) — maps to P16 StoryProperty keying. ADAPT fan mail as a Momentum-driven flavour event (a signal, never a legal gate). REJECT the expiring request window (it is exactly the hard-timer optimisation Owner D excludes; a decaying Momentum value already encodes "act while it is hot"). REJECT company-wide single fan counter as the IP state (Owner M wants per-Franchise state). Structural risk exposed: remake sales that are pure fan-pool draw-down produce day-one spikes and then nothing; P17 Recognition must feed P07/P15 as demand *awareness*, not as a purchasing pool.

## 2. Game Dev Masters (Hulgarth, solo dev; Steam appid 1478350; EA Jun 2021, 1.0 3 Jun 2022, 1.4 1 Oct 2022)

### 2.1 Sequel mechanics
- Sequels, remakes, DLC and ports are UNLOCKS per game size ("Game Extensions" in studio upgrades, Sep 2021) and can also be bought with meta-progression "Paragon" points on a new run (Dec 2021). (Hulgarth "Update 3.1 Is Live and it is spicy", 1 Sep 2021, https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4020014099083259572 ; "Update 6.1 Crypto Markets, Trend Tracking, And More", 1 Dec 2021, https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4224935490656394775 ; HIGH; DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL)
- Sequel inheritance is HYPE, sized by predecessor quality and size: "Sequels when you begin them will now generate hype based on the game size and the quality of the first game. This hype comes up-to 10% on small 25% on med 50% on large, based on quality." (Hulgarth "GDM Ver 1.2", 8 Jun 2021, https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4093189347062187443 ; HIGH; DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL)
- Hype is a sales multiplier capped at 100% after an exploit: "If you used hype perks, had a pet with 20 / 20 cuteness stat, and made a sequel of a good large / AAA game. It was possible to exceed 100% hype ... It is used as a sales multiplier so going beyond 100% was granting sales larger than expected." (Hulgarth "Patch 2.1 is Live", 31 Jul 2021, https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4038025597320372951 ; proves stacking additive hype sources without a cap is an exploit vector; HIGH; DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL)
- Sequel on the same engine is penalised: the archive shows the engine used "so you know if your current engine is the same and would hurt to make a sequel." (Hulgarth "Big Pre Launch Patch", 27 May 2021, https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4069544815355502484 ; HIGH; DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL)
- Repetition penalty deliberately REMOVED in favour of positive incentives: "Removed the penalty for using the same topic + genre back-to-back. With the new incentives for using fresh topics and genres, I feel most players will already want to do this. However, if you just want to make Fantasy RPGs over and over like some real companies, I think that is fine as well. The game promotes sequels / remakes / new topics and what not with other metrics I don't feel we need to punish anyone for just wanting to make the same game over and over anymore." (Hulgarth "New Season / New Patch", 1 Jul 2021, https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4032392655090219285 ; proves one shipped designer's explicit choice: carrot-only, no fatigue stick; HIGH; DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL)
- DLC only within 1 year of a Large/AAA release (itch devlog "GDM - DLC Creation Deets", search summary, page 403; MEDIUM; DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL via search summary) — a hard window for a derivative product.
- Audience expectations rise over time: the help section "Requirements menu describes the scaling minimums as the years pass"; store text: "Audience expectations rise over time". (27 May 2021 note above; Steam store https://store.steampowered.com/app/1478350/ ; HIGH; DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL)

### 2.2 IP state: licensed "Famous/Movie IPs" and the one Custom IP
- Licensed IP (1.0): "pay money to buy the rights to use a movie license as your game ... over 100 movie IPS ... you will be shown 3 potential IPS you can buy. They each have a cost and a % increase on sales. You can refresh these 3 IPS for a small fee and the cost + sales percent is randomly generated using a weighted procedural system ... the name of your game locked off ... game scoring is identical to non-IP games. However, if you used an IP, it will multiply your sales by its given %". (Hulgarth "Game Dev Masters Is Leaving Early Access", 3 Jun 2022, https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4469272893496287316 ; HIGH; DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL)
- Licensed IP nerfed after balance data: "in late game some of the sales increases were a little too good. I have reduced this system to be roughly half as powerful on larger games." (Hulgarth "Post 1.0 Changes", 15 Jun 2022, https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4480533160883459376 ; HIGH; DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL)
- Custom IP (1.4): one per playthrough, chosen from a past non-IP game; "you will see its current sales boost. The initial value for this boost is based off the game size and quality of the original game made. Games made using your custom IP cannot have sequels or remakes made from them, only new games that also use that same IP. Every game using that IP is technically a sequel, so the sequel feature was removed for that reason ... Every time you release a game using that Ip you will increase the current sales boost of the IP. This increase is based off the game size and quality of the game. The custom IP can and will eventually reach a 100% sales boost (maximum)". Licensed IP prices were cut because "the custom IP which will be free to use will eventually outperform the IP's you can license." Hard mode removed IP use so the leaderboard is "pure skill". (Hulgarth "Update 1.4 is Live~!", 1 Oct 2022, https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4588629064117304275 ; HIGH; DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL)
- No decay, no fatigue, no failure penalty on the IP boost is documented; it is a monotonic ratchet to 100%.

### 2.3 Rivals, cast, early sequels
- 15 "dynamically generated AI competitors", stock/acquisition play; whether AI studios use IPs or sequels is undocumented (gap). (Steam store; HIGH for existence; DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL)
- Talent continuity: worker "skills" slot system; no franchise-role association. Early sequel: the whole studio must be free to start a sequel ("You are still required to have the entire studio be free to begin game development projects, and this includes starting new sequels / remakes", 1 Nov 2021, https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4186652357037281537 ; HIGH; DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL) — so no overlap.
- Player-experience quotes: the discussion board is thin ("Is this game dead?", "Any tips to make a 10/10 game?"); Steam rating Very Positive 82% of 152 (search summary). No sequel-specific complaint thread found; the developer's own notes are the best record of exploits (hype >100%, IP too strong, 2.1-billion sales integer cap).

### 2.4 Assessment
1. Decision: which one game to consecrate as your permanent IP; whether to spend on a random licensed IP offer now vs wait for the custom IP to mature; same-engine vs new-engine sequel.
2. Fun: watching "your" IP's boost climb; the licensed-IP offer roulette.
3. Tedious/exploitable: once the custom IP exists every release should use it (the developer says it "will eventually outperform" licensed IP); no reason ever to stop; the designer had to halve licensed IP and cap hype.
4. Hard-timer: DLC 1-year window; otherwise none.
5. Sequel spam: by construction ("every game using that IP is technically a sequel") with zero fatigue.
6. Project: Studio: ADAPT "IP strength grows with each quality release, sized by scale × quality" as the Recognition accrual rule (Owner C/Q), but ONLY with the Fatigue counterweight the Owner specified; REJECT monotonic ratchet and REJECT sales-multiplier framing (Owner R: P17 mints no cash; effects are indirect via awareness/expectations). ADOPT the hype cap lesson: any additive Momentum sources must be bounded (Owner D "bounded continuous model"). Structural risk exposed: with no fatigue, "one IP for everything" is the dominant strategy; the Owner's C is the fix, so C must be visible in the UI, otherwise players will re-discover the GDM ratchet.

## 3. Software Inc. (Coredumping; Steam appid 362620; Beta 1.8.32 as of Jan 2026)

### 3.1 Sequel / IP mechanics
- Products belong to an IP; the automation layer ("Project Management") keeps a "Prototype Software" pool: "This is the pool of IPs that will be handled by this PM ... From this pool PM creates Sequels to the selected IPs, if Auto Dev and Only Sequels are both checked"; "Only sequels - PM will auto develop only sequels to the existing IP assigned above ... No new IP will be created"; a player-set "Release Cooldown - if Auto Development is checked, PM will wait x amount of months before starting next product, with 0 being right away"; "Existing Products" pool = support/port only, no sequels. (Steam guide "Setup Project Management & Automatic Updates (WIP)", appid 362620, https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2917338667 ; verified by reading the page: title/app = Software Inc.; proves IP-keyed sequel automation exists and that the cooldown is a player parameter not a game law; HIGH; COMMUNITY INFERENCE (guide) describing shipped UI fields)
- Community cadence rule of thumb: "every sequel should be released in (around) 3 years maximum span, except computer OS". (same guide; MEDIUM; COMMUNITY INFERENCE)
- Rival AI in the same automation context: "If Auto develop is on the AI will continue to develop single IPs and give them new names hence they are not sequels" (guide quoting LatN; MEDIUM; COMMUNITY INFERENCE) — i.e. even the automation distinguishes "same IP" from "sequel".

### 3.2 Followers (fan state) and carry-over
- Followers are per product and, per players, do NOT carry to sequels: "as far as i know followers dont carry over to sequels its just easier to get new followers if your prequel was succesfull" (xylomastero, 2 Nov 2017, https://steamcommunity.com/app/362620/discussions/0/1479857071252240924/ ; no developer reply; MEDIUM; COMMUNITY INFERENCE)
- Recognition is earned across installments: "new software starts with 0 followers, and it takes 4 sequels to get good recognition"; "create most quality software, follow with sequel every three years"; over-marketing backfires: "sending two press releases during one stage ... will backfire and you will get news You Company is spamming". (nosedigger, Jan 2023, https://steamcommunity.com/app/362620/discussions/0/3757724446266675136/ ; MEDIUM; COMMUNITY INFERENCE)
- Follower cap observed: "it stopped dead in its tracks for no reason at 35,009" (STEEL5K, 20 Jan 2020); "stuck at 36,788 each time" (Sreip, 28 Jan 2020); hypothesis "hard capped number based on a percentage of your total consumer reach" (StonyMike, 29 Jan 2020). (https://steamcommunity.com/app/362620/discussions/0/1746768126949745793/ ; no developer reply; MEDIUM; COMMUNITY INFERENCE)

### 3.3 Assessment
1. Decision: sequel cadence vs support/port of the current version; when to hand an IP to automation.
2. Fun: building a product line that finally "has recognition" after several installments.
3. Tedious: re-marketing every product from zero followers; opaque follower caps.
4. Hard-timer: none imposed by the game (cooldown is the player's own setting).
5. Sequel spam: bounded by dev time and the press-spam news event; no IP fatigue.
6. Project: Studio: ADOPT the "press spam" backlash as a precedent for Fatigue rising on oversupply of *messaging* (marketing efficiency is an Owner R lever); ADOPT the Existing-vs-Prototype split as the idea that "support/port" is not a continuation (P17 continuation types A). REJECT zero follower carry-over (contradicts Owner B: successful properties start with an audience). The "4 sequels to get recognition" feel is a good Recognition timescale reference (slow, multi-installment).

## 6. Kairosoft: Game Dev Story (2010), Anime Studio Story (2016), The Manga Works (2019)

### 6.1 Game Dev Story — sequels and fans
- Sequel gate: original must score ≥32/40 (Hall of Fame); "The sequel must be started while the original game is still in the records" (latest 32 games); may target a different console; "Genre and type cannot be changed from original"; "Begins with some points carried over from the original game"; "Each game can only be sequeled once; the sequel, if it again gets a review score of 32/40 or above, can itself have a sequel"; "if a sequel does not make the Hall of Fame on its own, no more sequels can be made in that franchise". (Kairosoft wiki.gg "Creating games (Game Dev Story)", https://kairosoft.wiki.gg/wiki/Creating_games_(Game_Dev_Story) and https://kairosoft.wiki.gg/wiki/Game_Dev_Story ; HIGH for the rules as community-documented; COMMUNITY INFERENCE)
- Fans: "Advertising will increase your number of fans and the Hype for any game currently in development"; repeated use of the same ad type has diminishing effect; fan letters raise/lower popularity by demographic; Gamedex booths from $150K to $7,000K. ## 11. Designer and academic literature on sequel timing, satiation and franchise fatigue

### 11.1 Empirical film-market studies (OBSERVED HISTORY)
- Basuroy & Chatterjee (2008), "Fast and frequent: Investigating box office revenues of motion picture sequels", J. Business Research 61(7):798–803, verbatim abstract: "sequels do not match the box office revenues of the parent films. However, they do better than their contemporaneous non-sequels, more so when they are released sooner (rather than later) after their parents, and when more (rather than fewer) intervening sequels come before them. Like other extensions of hedonic products, sequels exhibit satiation to the extent that their weekly box office collections fall faster over time relative to contemporaneous non-sequels." (https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbrese/v61y2008i7p798-803.html ; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY) — Proves: (i) faster follow-up = more revenue (Owner C/D "a huge recent hit may make a FAST follow-up MORE attractive"); (ii) installment COUNT does not by itself fatigue — more prior installments helped; (iii) real-world "fatigue" shows up as faster weekly decay (legs), not necessarily as a smaller opening.
- Sood & Drèze (2006), "Brand Extensions of Experiential Goods: Movie Sequel Evaluations", J. Consumer Research 33(3):352–360, verbatim abstract: "dissimilar extensions are rated higher than similar extensions. This reversal is moderated by the name of the sequel; numbered sequels (Daredevil 2) are influenced by similarity more than named sequels ... numbered sequels invoke a greater degree of assimilation with the parent movie, thereby increasing consumers' level of satiation of experiential attributes." (https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:jconrs:v:33:y:2006:i:3:p:352-360 ; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY) — Proves: satiation keys on SAMENESS (and on how the continuation is framed), which is the mechanism behind Owner C's "repeated mediocre similar output raises Fatigue faster".
- Hennig-Thurau, Houston & Heitjans (2009), "Conceptualizing and Measuring the Monetary Value of Brand Extensions: The Case of Motion Pictures", J. Marketing 73(6):167–183 (full PDF read, https://www.marketingcenter.de/sites/mcm/files/downloads/research/lmm/literature/hennig-thurau_et_al._2009_jm_conceptualizing_and_measuring_the_monetary_value_of_brand_extensions_the_case_of_motion_pictures.pdf): sequels average $175.0M vs $138.2M for matched non-sequels (t = 2.07, p < .05); in the sequel model parent-brand AWARENESS is the strongest predictor (β = .71), parent-brand IMAGE third (β = .18), STAR CONTINUITY (β = .15) and rating continuity (β = .11) are significant, with awareness×star-continuity (β = .11) and image×genre-continuity (β = .11) interactions; sequels are LESS RISKY (weighted MAPE improved 38%, RMSE 33%, CV 48%). Star continuity is measured as "'Starmeter' of the main actors of the parent movie appearing in the sequel in relation to the maximum possible 'starmeter' if all main actors from the parent movie would have appeared" — i.e. importance-weighted, not a same-cast flag. Simulation: Spider-Man 2 as filmed has a brand-extension value of +$52.67M; "were the sequel not to have starred Tobey Maguire (but an actor with identical star power and salary), the sequel's revenues would have decreased by $181.8 million and, consequently, would have resulted in a negative brand extension value of –$129.1 million ... it is not worth paying for the sequel rights unless Maguire's services can be contracted ... the parent-brand awareness × star continuity interaction accounts for 64% (or $116.6 million) of the loss". A time-window (gap) variable was tested and "remain[ed] insignificant" in their sample. Director/writer/producer continuity were measured but did not survive stepwise selection. (HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY) — Proves Owner B (inherited awareness dominates, expectations via image), Owner F (continuity matters by importance of the association and matters MORE for bigger franchises), and warns that the gap effect is sample-dependent (B&C 2008 found it; H-T 2009 did not).
- Dhar, Sun & Weinberg (2012), "The long-term box office performance of sequel movies", Marketing Letters 23(1):13–29: over a 26-year database, sequels get significantly more theatres and "generate more attendance in the first week and total attendance than non-sequels" after controlling for supply and demand. (https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/mktlet/v23y2012i1p13-29.html , search summary; MEDIUM; OBSERVED HISTORY)
- Bohnenkamp, Knapp, Hennig-Thurau & Schauerte (2015), "When does it make sense to do it again? An empirical investigation of contingency factors of movie remakes", J. Cultural Economics 39(1):15–41, verbatim abstract: "Using a dataset of 207 remakes released in North American theaters between 1999 and 2011 and a matched sample of other movies, the authors find that, on average, remakes do not increase revenues but do reduce financial risk. The authors also provide evidence of the contingency role of several factors, including the original movie's awareness and image and the relationship between the original movie and the remake." (https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/jculte/v39y2015i1p15-41.html ; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY) — Proves Owner H/I: remake value is a risk-reduction bet keyed to retained awareness of a SPECIFIC original and to the original↔remake relationship (comparison pressure), distinct from a sequel.

### 11.2 Industry / designer commentary (CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL SOURCE)
- Jake Parmley, "Fighting Off Franchise Fatigue", Game Developer, 8 Mar 2016: franchise fatigue "is more than a gaming audience growing tired or losing interest"; causes: annualization, troubled launches or marketing missteps, industry competition, unclear brand vision and failure to innovate; Assassin's Creed (Unity's launch) and Call of Duty (Ghosts → Black Ops III recovery) as cases; remedy shown by Ubisoft taking a year off to "re-examine" and "evolve the game mechanics" while keeping brand presence through side content. - Tadhg Kelly, "The Perils of Franchise Fatigue [Single Franchise Publishing]", What Games Are, Jan 2012: single-franchise publishing fatigues creators ("difficult for creative people to only work on one idea ... otherwise they become formulaic"). (https://www.whatgamesare.com/2012/01/the-perils-of-franchise-fatigue.html ; search summary, page 403; LOW; CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL SOURCE)
