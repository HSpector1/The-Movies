### Conventions
- **Money**: unadjusted USD. "Opening" = domestic (US/Canada) 3-day weekend unless noted. "Dom" = domestic total. "WW" = worldwide total.
- **Dates**: US theatrical release. The Numbers lists the *earliest* release date (often international or preview day), so where its date differs from the US date I use the US date from Wikipedia (e.g., Iron Man: US May 2, 2008; The Numbers shows Apr 30, 2008). Iron Man's opening is $98.6M on Box Office Mojo's 3-day convention vs $102.1M on The Numbers (includes Thursday previews); I cite the BOM-style figure with the alternate in brackets.
- **Gaps** are months between US release dates, rounded to one decimal.
- **Source tiers** used: OBSERVED HISTORY (dated box-office/trade record) | CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL SOURCE (trade press analysis) | COMMUNITY INFERENCE (fan/press narratives about *why*) | DESIGN INFERENCE (my proposed rule).
- Confidence: HIGH = two independent fetched sources or one primary table; MEDIUM = one fetched source or a widely reported figure; LOW = memory / not verified.

## PART 2 — DESIGN QUESTIONS (observed history → inference)

### A. Can rapid follow-ups capitalize on momentum?

**Observed (dated):**
1. *Infinity War → Endgame → Far From Home* (2018-04-27 → 2019-04-26 → 2019-07-02; gaps 12.0 and 2.2 months): openings $257.7M → $357.1M (+39%); WW $2.05B → $2.72B (+33%); FFH ten weeks later became the first $1B Spider-Man ($1.13B, +29% over Homecoming). Source: The Numbers MCU table; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.
2. *The Force Awakens → Rogue One → The Last Jedi* (2015-12-18 → 2016-12-16 → 2017-12-15; two 12-month gaps): three consecutive $1B+ grosses in 24 months ($2.07B, $1.06B, $1.33B). A spin-off with no returning lead cast earned $1.06B twelve months after the parent. Source: Wikipedia Star Wars table + The Numbers; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.
3. *Fast Five → 6 → Furious 7* (2011-04 → 2013-05 → 2015-04; ~24-month cadence with rising quality RT 78/71/82): WW $626M → $789M → $1,515M — steady cadence compounded. Source: Wikipedia F&F table; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.
4. Counter-cases: *The Incredible Hulk* 6 weeks after Iron Man (2008-06-13, $55.4M vs $98.6M) inherited nothing — different character, prior 2003 Hulk baggage, universe recognition ≈ 0 in 2008. *Solo* 5.3 months after The Last Jedi ($84.4M vs $220.0M, −62%) — confounded by an iconic-lead recast, a divisive predecessor, a mid-production director change, and a May slot; the studio blamed cadence ("too much, too fast", Iger Sept 2018). Source: The Numbers; IndieWire 2018-09; HIGH (facts) / MEDIUM (cause); OBSERVED HISTORY + CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL SOURCE.
5. *Brand New Day* (2026-07-31) came 12 months after a Marvel film and 3 months after Fantastic Four in a year of three soft MCU entries, yet set the all-time opening record ($360.1M). The MCU cadence was high, but the Spider-Man sub-property itself had a 55.5-month gap since NWH. Source: Deadline 2026-08; The Numbers; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.

**Safe to take:** Momentum is real and fast-decaying; an installment released while its *own branch/sub-property* Momentum is still positive inherits a large share of the predecessor's opening — no cooldown is warranted (owner direction D confirmed). Track Momentum per branch/SubProperty, not only per franchise (point 5).
**Must NOT over-generalize:** "Fast = good." Fast follow-ups inherit only what the predecessor left (reception-scaled), and only for the same branch/association; a recast iconic lead, a different sub-property, or a damaged predecessor erases the inheritance (Hulk 2008, Solo 2018).

### B. When does volume create fatigue — quality or cadence?

**Observed (dated):**
1. *MCU 2021-07 → 2023-11*: 10 films in 28 months plus ~10 Disney+ series. Openings for non-crossover entries fell $80M → $75M → $71M → … → $106M (−70% wk 2) → $46M (−78% wk 2). The weak performers carried the MCU's first-ever CinemaScore "B"s (Eternals, Quantumania, The Marvels) and sub-50% RT (Eternals 47, Quantumania 46); the strong-reception entries in the *same* window performed normally (NWH $260M/A+, Wakanda Forever $181M/A, GotG3 $118M/$846M/A). Iger, May 2024: cut to ≤3 films + ~2 series a year "to reduce output and focus more on quality." Source: The Numbers; TheWrap 2023-02; Variety 2024-05-07; HIGH (figures) / MEDIUM (reception markers from memory); OBSERVED HISTORY + CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL SOURCE.
2. *Fatigue lags quality*: 2025's *Thunderbolts** (RT 88 / CinemaScore A−, memory) opened $74.3M / $382M WW — below the 2018 Ant-Man sequel — while *Deadpool & Wolverine* (2024) and *Brand New Day* (2026) cut through with crossover/iconic hooks. Source: The Numbers; HIGH figures; MEDIUM interpretation; OBSERVED HISTORY.
3. *Batman 1989–1997*: cadence constant (36/36/24 months); dom $251M → $163M → $184M → $107M while RT went 77 → 81 → 41 → 11 and CinemaScore A → B → A− → C+. Cadence did not change; quality collapsed → the franchise stopped for 8 years. Source: The Numbers + Wikipedia "Batman in film"; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.
4. *Star Wars 2015–2019*: 5 films in 48 months; WW $2.07B → $1.06B → $1.33B → $0.39B → $1.07B; Solo (RT 69, A−, memory) fell far more than its reviews explain; TROS (RT 51, B+, memory) fell −19% vs TLJ with worse reviews. Both cadence and quality contributed; the studio's own diagnosis was cadence. Source: tables above; IndieWire 2018-09; HIGH figures / MEDIUM cause.
5. *Non-fatigue controls*: Despicable Me — six films 2010–2024 on a 2–3-year cadence, all $545M–$1.16B WW, no collapse; MCU 2012–2019 — 20 films in 7 years with openings trending *up*. Steady quality at steady cadence did not fatigue. Source: The Numbers; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.
6. *Fast & Furious 2015–2023*: steady ~2-year cadence, RT sliding 82 → 67 → 67 → 59 → 57, WW $1.52B → $1.24B → $0.76B (spin-off) → $0.73B → $0.71B while budgets rose to $340M+ — gradual erosion, not collapse. Source: Wikipedia F&F table; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.

**Safe to take:** Fatigue accumulates per installment in proportion to *mediocrity* (and similarity), not to count; steady quality at any reasonable cadence adds ~nothing; high cadence merely multiplies the number of mediocre exposures and erodes the "event" feel; fatigue decays slower than Momentum and depresses the *opening* of the next good film (legs recover first). This is exactly owner direction C.
**Must NOT over-generalize:** A per-year release-count penalty (Despicable Me and 2012–2019 Marvel refute it); or treating fatigue as instantly cleared by one good film (Thunderbolts 2025).

### C. Can one weak installment fail to kill a powerful franchise?

**Observed (dated):**
1. *Tokyo Drift* (2006-06-16; $62.5M dom, series low, no original principals) → *Fast & Furious* (2009-04-03; $71.0M opening, 3× Tokyo Drift's, April record, RT 29%) → *Fast Five* (2011; $626M WW). Recovery agent: the four original principals returned (Universal reportedly paid ≈$25M for Diesel + Walker — LOW-MEDIUM, see 1.3), plus a tonal shift to heist — and it happened with *worse* reviews than the flop. Source: Wikipedia F&F; CNN/EW 2009-04-05; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.
2. *Batman & Robin* (1997; $107M dom, RT 11%, C+) → 96-month dormancy → *Batman Begins* (2005; $205M dom, RT 85%) → *The Dark Knight* (2008; $1.0B). Recovery agent: reboot + new creative lead; recognition intact after 8 years. Source: The Numbers; Den of Geek; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.
3. *The Marvels* (2023-11-10; $206M WW, MCU low) → *Deadpool & Wolverine* 8.5 months later ($1.34B). *Solo* (2018; $393M) → *Rise of Skywalker* 19 months later ($1.07B). *Licence to Kill* (1989; $156M) → *GoldenEye* ($352M). *OHMSS* (1969; −42%) → *Diamonds Are Forever* (+80%). Source: tables above; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.
4. Counter-cases — a flop can end a *branch*: *Joker: Folie à Deux* (2024; $207M WW vs $1.08B) ended the Joker branch while *The Batman Part II* (scheduled 2028-02-18 per Wikipedia "Batman in film", fetched 2026-09-12; previously dated 2026 then 2027) proceeds; *Dark Phoenix* (2019; $246M) ended Fox's X-Men main line while the property was rebooted into the MCU after the 2019 rights transfer; *TASM 2* (2014; series low) ended Sony's second Spider-Man continuity, replaced by the MCU-shared third. Source: The Numbers; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.

**Safe to take:** A flop drives branch Momentum below neutral and adds Fatigue, but Recognition (StoryProperty level) is barely dented; recovery in every case required a concrete *change* (returning iconic cast, reboot, new creative, crossover) and then a well-received film. Weak installments end branches, not StoryProperties — matches owner directions N and P.
**Must NOT over-generalize:** Automatic mean-reversion. No case recovered by simply "waiting"; the franchise recovered when the next installment changed something and landed.

### D. How do successful spin-offs inherit recognition?

**Observed (dated):**
1. *Rogue One* (2016-12-16): opening $155.1M = 62.5% of TFA's $248.0M; WW $1.06B = 51% of parent. *Solo* (2018-05-25): $84.4M = 38% of TLJ's opening; WW 29%. *Hobbs & Shaw* (2019-08-02): $60.0M = 61% of Fate's $98.8M; WW $761M = 62% — but larger than Fast Five ($626M), i.e., the spin-off out-grossed early parent entries while staying below the current parent. Source: The Numbers; Wikipedia F&F; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.
2. *Deadpool* (2016-02-12; $58M budget): opening $132.4M vs parent DOFP $90.8M (2014); WW $782M vs $748M; three months later the parent's *X-Men: Apocalypse* opened $65.8M. By 2024 the spin-off (*Deadpool & Wolverine*, $1.34B) was the tentpole and the parent line had ended (*Dark Phoenix* $246M). Source: The Numbers X-Men; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.
3. *Minions* (2015-07-10): $115.7M opening vs DM2 $84.2M; $1.157B WW vs $975M — the spin-off out-grossed the parent and both lines have since coexisted at ~$1B each (DM3 $1.03B, Minions 2 $938M, DM4 $987M). Source: The Numbers Despicable-Me; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.
4. *Joker* (2019-10-04; $55M budget): $96.2M opening / $1.08B WW with no Batman on screen, exceeding the concurrent parent-continuity films (BvS $874M, JL $661M); its own sequel then collapsed to $207M. Source: The Numbers Batman; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.

**Safe to take:** A spin-off inherits a *fraction* of the parent's opening through the SubProperty association (observed 38–65% for Star Wars/Fast; >100% only when the SubProperty carried a distinct audience and a well-received film). The SubProperty then accrues its own Recognition and can out-grow the parent; the parent's Recognition is not consumed. Owner direction J (bounded named SubProperties) is the right unit.
**Must NOT over-generalize:** Spin-offs are not immune to the parent's Fatigue/oversupply (Solo), and inheritance is not additive with the parent's Momentum — a spin-off released into a damaged parent inherits the damage too.

### E. How do dormant franchises revive?

**Observed (dated):**
1. *Star Wars 1983 → 1999* (192 months): Recognition carried by home video, novels/games, and the 1997 Special Edition re-release ($138M dom; $35M opening for a 20-year-old film); Episode I opened $64.8M and grossed $1.05B (first $1B Star Wars). *2005 → 2015* (127 months): TFA brought back Ford/Fisher/Hamill and set the opening record ($248M). Source: Wikipedia Star Wars; SlashFilm/Statista on SE; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.
2. *Batman 1997 → 2005* (96 months): Begins opened *lower* than the 1995 record ($48.7M vs $52.8M) but legged to $205M dom on 85% RT; the reboot's second film (2008) hit $1B. Revival rebuilt on quality, not on nostalgia at the opening. Source: The Numbers; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.
3. *Bond 1989 → 1995* (76 months; cause = ownership/legal dispute, not audience): GoldenEye $26.2M opening (3× LTK's $8.8M), $352M WW (+125%). *2021 → ≥2028* (≥78 months; cause = Amazon's acquisition and creative-control takeover, Feb 2025): outcome pending. Source: Wikipedia Bond table; SlashFilm; Variety 2026; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.
4. *Jurassic Park 2001 → 2015* (167 months): Jurassic World opened $208.8M (record at the time) vs JP3's $50.8M; $1.67B vs $366M. *Top Gun 1986 → 2022* (433 months): Maverick $126.7M opening, $1.45B WW, with the original icon (Cruise) returning. Source: The Numbers; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.
5. *Star Wars 2019 → 2026* (77 months): *The Mandalorian and Grogu* opened at a Disney-era low ($81.7M 3-day / $98.1M 4-day; $345M WW; RT 62 / MC 53) despite a respectable CinemaScore "A−" (the same grade as the prequels and Solo) — the property's recognition had been carried by TV (a P18 channel), the last theatrical entries were divisive, and no original-trilogy icon returned. Source: Variety/THR 2026-05; Wikipedia film page + list table (fetched 2026-09-12); HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.

**Safe to take:** Dormancy decays Momentum to neutral and Fatigue toward zero while Recognition decays slowly and is sustained by *carriers* (re-releases, home media, TV/P18); revival strength tracks (a) how the last installments left the property, (b) whether an iconic association returns, (c) the new film's quality — not the gap length. Owner directions H and P are supported.
**Must NOT over-generalize:** A nostalgia bonus that scales with years dormant. 6.4 years produced a series low (2026); 36 years produced a record (2022); 8 years produced a modest opening and a giant second film (2005/2008).

### F. What makes a reboot different from a remake? (concrete pairs)

**Observed (dated):**
1. *Reboot — Batman Begins (2005)*: new continuity and new cast telling an origin the 1989 film never showed; no scene-for-scene relationship to Batman (1989); marketed on difference ("realistic, grittier ... to differentiate from Warner's original Batman movies", Wikipedia "Batman in film"). *Reboot — Casino Royale (2006)*: continuity restarts (Bond earns 00 status) yet Judi Dench's M carries over from the Brosnan films — audiences accepted the continuity "cheat"; series-record gross. Source: Wikipedia; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.
2. *Remake — Never Say Never Again (1983)*: a non-Eon retelling of *Thunderball* (1965) with the original star, released against the incumbent series' *Octopussy* the same year: $160M vs $183.7M. The remake was judged directly against both the 1965 film and the live continuity, and lost to the incumbent. Source: Wikipedia Bond table; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.
3. *The reboot that behaved like a remake — The Amazing Spider-Man (2012)*: restarted continuity but re-told the 2002 origin ten years later; opening $62.0M vs $114.8M (2002) and $151.1M (2007); WW −15% vs SM3; "too soon" was the dominant press frame. The next reboot (*Homecoming*, 2017) skipped the origin and plugged into an active crossover universe: $117.0M (+89%). *The Batman* (2022) likewise skipped the origin: $134.0M. Source: The Numbers Spider-Man/Batman; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY; "too soon" frame = COMMUNITY INFERENCE (MEDIUM).
4. *Casino Royale* as title: adapted three times (1954 TV, 1967 non-Eon spoof $41.7M, 2006 Eon reboot $600M) — title recognition did not transfer to the non-continuity parody.

**Safe to take (owner direction I confirmed):** REMAKE = explicit reference to one prior FilmResult; reception is compared against that film, with a redundancy/comparison penalty that grows with the referenced film's recency *and* quality. REBOOT = new branch under the same StoryProperty; inherits Recognition, resets branch Momentum/Fatigue, and takes only a *partial* remake-style comparison term if the previous branch's most recent installment is both recent and well-regarded (TASM). Do not model continuity law (Dench's M).
**Must NOT over-generalize:** "Reboot = fresh start with no comparison" (TASM was punished for redundancy) or "remake of a classic = guaranteed recognition" (NSNA lost to the incumbent).

### G. How important is returning cast?

**Observed (dated):**
1. *Bond — six recasts*: WW change at each hand-off: Lazenby 1969 −42%; Connery-return 1971 +80%; Moore 1973 +9%; Dalton 1987 +25%; Brosnan 1995 +125%; Craig 2006 +37–40% (despite the CraigNotBond backlash). Five of six hand-offs held or grew; the one that fell was reversed by the icon's return. The ROLE out-ranks any actor, yet the iconic association still moved results. Source: Wikipedia Bond table; Variety 2021; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.
2. *Batman — recast inside a continuity*: Kilmer replacing Keaton (1995) set the all-time opening record (+15% vs 1992); Clooney replacing Kilmer (1997) fell −19% opening / −42% dom with an 11% RT film. Recasting worked when the film worked and did not rescue a bad film. Source: The Numbers; Wikipedia "Batman Forever"; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.
3. *Fast & Furious — association strength*: losing ONE principal (Diesel, 2003) still grew the opening (+26%); losing ALL four principals (2006) halved it (−52%); restoring all four (2009) tripled it (+196%) with a 29% RT film. Universal reportedly paid ≈$25M to restore the iconic pair (LOW-MEDIUM; Walker's fee alone rose to a reported $7M) — the escalating-salary reality owner direction F wants bounded. Source: Wikipedia F&F; CNN/EW 2009; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.
4. *Supporting recasts ≈ neutral*: Cheadle for Howard (Iron Man 2, 2010: opening +30% over IM1); Ruffalo for Norton (Avengers, 2012: record). *Iconic-lead recast in a spin-off/prequel*: Ehrenreich as Han Solo (2018) — series low (confounded by cadence and production turmoil). *Returning icon cannot rescue a weak film*: Keaton's return in The Flash (2023) — $55M, −72.5%. Source: The Numbers; TheWrap 2023; HIGH figures; MEDIUM attribution.

**Safe to take (owner direction F confirmed):** Weight continuity by association importance — iconic lead ≫ recurring antagonist/major supporting ≫ minor — and apply the penalty for a within-continuity recast to Momentum (opening expectation), not to quality; a reboot resets the association so a recast there carries little penalty (Bale, Craig, Pattinson).
**Must NOT over-generalize:** Cast lock-in or a generic same-cast bonus. The Bond evidence says the role/StoryProperty carries most Recognition; the Clooney/Keaton-Flash evidence says returning stars cannot substitute for quality.

### H. How do ensemble/crossover casts change audience excitement?

**Observed (dated):**
1. *The Avengers* (2012-05-04): $207.4M vs the best prior solo ($128.1M, IM2: +62%) and ~3.2× the Thor/Cap openings ($65.7M / $65.1M); WW $1.52B = 2.4× IM2. Source: The Numbers; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.
2. *Spillover to the next solo of each participant*: Iron Man 3 (2013) $174.1M (+36% vs IM2); Thor 2 (2013) $85.7M (+30%); Cap 2 (2014) $95.0M (+46%). Source: The Numbers; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.
3. *Diminishing repeat*: Age of Ultron (2015) $191.3M (−8% vs the first crossover); Infinity War (2018) $257.7M and Endgame (2019) $357.1M regained the curve as event finales. *Post-Endgame*: the next three non-crossover solos opened $80M / $75M / $71M (COVID-confounded), then NWH $260M (legacy crossover) → MoM $187M → ordinary solos $106M / $46M → D&W $211M (crossover) → 2025 solos $74–118M → BND $360M. Source: The Numbers; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.
4. *Crossover is not automatic*: Justice League (2017-11-17) opened $93.8M — below its own predecessor BvS ($166.0M) and below Wonder Woman ($103.3M, June 2017, memory) — after a poorly received BvS (RT 29%, memory). Source: The Numbers Batman; HIGH figures / MEDIUM reception; OBSERVED HISTORY.

**Safe to take (later P17 scope, owner direction O):** A crossover multiplies the *existing* positive Momentum of its participating SubProperties (roughly summing their recognition) and spills Momentum back to each participant's next solo; but each crossover raises the expectation baseline, so subsequent ordinary solos read as disappointments (fatigue is relative to expectation).
**Must NOT over-generalize:** Crossover as a guaranteed spike (Justice League); or attributing post-Endgame declines solely to "fatigue after the crossover" (COVID + Disney+ day-and-date + quality all overlap).

### I. How can a celebrity cameo create attention without determining quality?

**Observed (dated):**
1. *Tropic Thunder* (2008-08-13): Tom Cruise's uncredited Les Grossman was deliberately hidden from all marketing; the cameo dominated coverage, earned a Golden Globe nomination, spawned meme culture and a still-discussed spin-off — but the film's $110.5M dom / $191–195M WW (The Numbers $191.1M, fetched 2026-09-12) and 82% RT rest on the Stiller/Downey/Black ensemble. Source: RT editorial "Ten years on ..."; CBR; Variety 2025 (McQuarrie/Cruise Grossman movie); HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY + CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL SOURCE.
2. *The Flash* (2023-06-16): the most cameo-dense superhero film of its era (Keaton after 31 years, Affleck, Nicolas Cage's Superman, Reeve/Reeves/West likenesses, Slater; Cavill paid $250K for a cut cameo) opened $55.0M, fell −72.5%, and finished $271.5M WW on a $200M+ budget. Attention was enormous; outcome was determined elsewhere. Source: TheWrap 2023-06; ScreenRant; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.
3. *Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness* (2022-05-06): Illuminati cameos (Stewart, Krasinski, Atwell, Mount, Lynch) drove a spoiler-averse $187.4M opening, then −67% on a "meh" CinemaScore (B+) — cameo hype front-loads the opening and steepens the drop. Source: Deadline 2022-05 "Doctor Strange 2 Has Big Second Weekend Drop With $61M"; Murphy's Multiverse; HIGH; OBSERVED HISTORY.
4. *Deadpool & Wolverine* (2024-07-26): months of press over a rumored Taylor Swift/Dazzler cameo that never happened (director Levy: worried about disappointing people); the film opened $211.4M on the Deadpool + Wolverine pairing and real legacy cameos (Evans as Johnny Storm, Snipes, Garner, Tatum). A *rumor* generated attention with zero effect on quality. *Solo* (2018): a Darth Maul cameo did not move a series-low result. Source: Variety cameo list; AceShowbiz/Levy; Wikipedia Solo (memory for Maul); HIGH / MEDIUM; OBSERVED HISTORY.
5. **Famous non-actor cameos (owner direction U — musicians/athletes/TV personalities):**
   - *Mike Tyson in The Hangover* (2009-06-05; boxer playing himself, ~2 scenes): the cameo was pushed in trailers/marketing and became the film's signature beat; the film opened $45.0M, legged to $277.3M dom / $465–469M WW on a $35M budget, and made Tyson a comedy fixture (he was called "the best thing about Hangover" and the sequel's release was briefly threatened by a lawsuit over his face tattoo). Tyson later admitted he did not even know he was filming it the night before — attention value was entirely independent of craft. Source: The Numbers "The Hangover (2009)" + Box Office Mojo tt1119646 (fetched 2026-09-12; $44,979,319 opening; $277.3M dom; $465.5–469.3M WW; $35M budget); SlashFilm/IMDb-news 2024 "Mike Tyson Didn't Realize He Was Going To Film His Career-Redefining Cameo"; FandomWire on the tattoo suit; HIGH (figures) / MEDIUM (attribution of marketing value); OBSERVED HISTORY + CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL SOURCE.
   - *David Beckham in King Arthur: Legend of the Sword* (2017-05-12; footballer, ~2-minute speaking cameo as "Trigger", his acting debut, in a film by his friend Guy Ritchie): the cameo generated a distinct news cycle (NME/IMDb/Inquirer "Beckham makes his acting debut") and then a distinct *mockery* cycle (New Statesman: "the worst thing about King Arthur"; Telegraph: "enough dramatic range to have played the stone the sword got stuck in"); the film opened $15.4M and finished $39.2M dom / $149.2M WW on a $175M budget, killing a planned six-film franchise. The cameo neither helped nor determined the outcome; it added attention (both directions) to a film whose reception was set elsewhere. Source: The Numbers "King Arthur: Legend of the Sword" (fetched 2026-09-12; $15,371,270 opening; $39.2M dom; $149.2M WW; $175M budget); NME 2017-05; New Statesman 2017-05; AceShowbiz "Beckham's Cameo ... Is Mocked by Critics"; HIGH (figures) / HIGH (press-cycle existence); OBSERVED HISTORY + CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL SOURCE.
   - *Tom Cruise in Tropic Thunder* (item 1) is the actor-side twin: $25.8M opening / $110.5M dom / $191.1M WW on $90M (The Numbers, fetched 2026-09-12).
6. Ritual cameos: Stan Lee appeared in every MCU film 2008–2019 — a recurring attention beat that never entered the quality conversation. Source: general record; MEDIUM; OBSERVED HISTORY.

**Safe to take (owner direction U):** A FEATURED/CAMEO slot contributes fame-scaled *attention* (awareness / opening reach / marketing efficiency) with ~zero weight in quality or starDraw-style appeal — and the fame source need not be acting (Tyson, Beckham: attention came from non-film celebrity; outcome came from the film); the attention front-loads the opening and steepens decay; diminishing returns per additional cameo (The Flash) bound cameo spam. A cameo can also draw *negative* attention (Beckham) without changing the film's underlying reception.
**Must NOT over-generalize:** Treating legacy co-leads as cameos — Maguire/Garfield in NWH and Keaton in The Flash were structural roles (a crossover/returning-icon effect, not a cameo effect); nor letting cameo attention rescue reception.
