## 1. Executive findings

Status labels: **CONFIRMED** (evidence supports the current Future Ops understanding), **QUALIFIED** (supported with a precise narrowing), **CORRECTED** (current understanding is wrong or stale), **OWNER-DIRECTION SUCCESSOR DESIGN** (no original-game precedent; net-new design under the Owner's selected direction).

1. **The original *The Movies* shipped no sequel, prequel, remake, reboot, spin-off, franchise or cameo mechanic of any kind.** Zero hits for those terms in the official manual, the developer-reviewed Prima guide, both GameFAQs guides and all 34 structured data files; Prima's complete post-release effects list has no inter-film linkage. [HIGH; OFFICIAL MANUAL + DEVELOPER-REVIEWED PRIMA; **CONFIRMED** — P17 is net-new.] (§2)
2. **Lionhead wanted exactly this system and did not build it.** Molyneux (GameSpot, Sept 2003): a sequel's success "will depend on when you decide to make it, and … how it compares to the original"; then-studio-head Gary Carr (Kotaku 2014): the feature was pushed back for scope, "there was no such system in the game", and it was nevertheless described in a live press demo. [HIGH that it was said / HIGH that it never shipped; PRE-RELEASE PROMISE evidenced by CONTEMPORARY PROFESSIONAL SOURCE; **CORRECTED** — cite as cautionary intent, never as parity.] (§2 row 15)
3. **The original's only inter-film memory ran through people and markets, not properties:** per-Star Movie Success/Performances recency (⅓ per film, decaying), per-actor and per-set Novelty (subtract-only), and studio-wide Genre Interest saturation including rival releases — the three "fatigue" primitives the Owner wants kept separate, none keyed to a title or story. [HIGH; DEVELOPER-REVIEWED PRIMA; **CONFIRMED**.] (§2 rows 9–10)
4. **The original hard-capped named acting seats at three for every era**, grew capability only by script-office tier, added a role *tier* (stuntmen) rather than more leads, gave extras zero fame value and withheld Star benefits from Stars in non-lead slots. Directions T and U have no retail precedent but match the ancestor's "capacity grows, never required" pattern. [HIGH; DEVELOPER-REVIEWED PRIMA + OFFICIAL MANUAL; **OWNER-DIRECTION SUCCESSOR DESIGN**.] (§2 rows 12–14, §13–14)
5. **Project: Studio today requires all three cast seats at every layer** (type, greenlight validator, casting-slate law, reception, Standing, rival packaging, save validators); `requiredSlots` is display-only. "A two-character drama must remain legal in 1995" is not satisfiable as written — read Direction T as "small-cast films remain legal when more seats exist"; the literal two-hander is a separate core decision. [HIGH; repo code @13370d42; **QUALIFIED**.] (§13)
6. **Every shipped comparator that uses a hard timer or a binary gate bred a checklist/exploit culture** (Game Dev Tycoon's decompiled 40-week −0.4 cliff; Game Dev Story's permanent chain death; City Game Studio's expiring windows), and the one comparator without a timer but with an inherent sequel bonus bred sequel farming (Hollywood Animal's "Trash King" six-sequels-in-a-year loop; a 400-hour reviewer "smashing the extend release button … off a 2/10 generic sequel"). The Owner's combination — no timer, no automatic bonus, visible separate Fatigue — is the one none of them ships. [HIGH; DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL + COMMUNITY; **CONFIRMED** Directions B/C/D.] (§3)
7. **Hollywood Animal promised "Franchises" + licensed IP on a dated roadmap (H2 2025) and has not shipped them 21 months later**, while shipping contracts, distributors and TV licensing instead — evidence that bundling rights + franchise + universe UI stalls; the P16 → P17 → P18 split is a scope safeguard, not overhead. [HIGH; PRE-RELEASE PROMISE + DEVELOPER/OFFICIAL roadmap; **CONFIRMED** package split.] (§3)
8. **The academic record supports the selected shape:** parent-brand awareness is the strongest predictor of sequel value (β=.71), star continuity is significant but secondary and importance-weighted (β=.15), sequels are less risky, sooner sequels earn more, satiation keys on *sameness* not count, remakes reduce risk without raising revenue. Two studies disagree on the gap effect — tuning must not assume one. [HIGH/MEDIUM; OBSERVED HISTORY (Hennig-Thurau 2009 read in full; others at abstract level); **CONFIRMED** B/C/D/F/H/I.] (§3, §4)
9. **Real franchises show a single flop never killed a property, consecutive mediocre installments did damage, dormancy length is not predictive of revival strength, spin-offs inherit 38–65% of the parent's opening, crossovers combine sub-additively (~80% of the naive sum) and raise the baseline for later solos.** [HIGH pattern / MEDIUM rule; OBSERVED HISTORY; **CONFIRMED** C/D/E/H/J/O/P.] (§4)
10. **Recommended R/M/F model:** peak-anchored property-wide Recognition with a ratchet floor (half-life ~12.5 y); per-continuity Momentum from surprise-vs-own-forecast × quality (half-life 24 wk); **property-wide** Fatigue from (1 − quality) × similarity with a satiation gate and quality-scaled paydown (42–104 wk) — one scalar per StoryProperty, so no continuity trick can launder it (revision 02); all three exact O(1) running scalars reproducible from the lineage edges + public results; descriptor bands derived, never stored. The seven paper histories behave as required *for those histories* (§16 bounds the claim): Case D flips COOLING on Fatigue alone with Momentum flat; Case B's two smash films accrue exactly zero Fatigue, and its fast spin-off SubProperty accrues 0.64 (its 74.5 quality sits just under the 0.75 satiation gate). [DESIGN INFERENCE; constants are starting points; **OWNER-DIRECTION SUCCESSOR DESIGN**.] (§5)
11. **There is no cooldown anywhere; timing acts only through continuous decay.** A fast follow-up to a huge hit is more attractive by arithmetic (Momentum decays before a slow sequel can capture it; an excellent film accrues no Fatigue); a rushed mediocre follow-up pays real Fatigue. [**CONFIRMED** Direction D by construction.] (§6)
12. **Expectations need no new number.** The locked greenlight forecast is the bar; P17 supplies **one** optional `ReceptionInputs` field (`inheritedAwareness01` net of Fatigue, `remakeComparison01`, `continuityCredit01`) that the forecast reads at greenlight (then locks) and reception reads again at release (live) — `ForecastInputs = ReceptionInputs` and both call the same helpers. The separate expectation multiplier proposed by two models is **not recommended** (it would bias the honest forecast and count inheritance twice). "Higher expectations" is emergent: the same gross that is a hit for an original reads as a miss for a famous property. [HIGH on the seam (`forecast.ts:54, 272, 468`); **QUALIFIED**.] (§7)
13. **Precision correction:** `computeStarPowerDelta` applies the forecast comparator only to the *gain* term; the *loss* term is scaled by the talent's own fame and audience reception. A famous property's flop narrows the fame upside and hurts the *franchise* (Momentum/Fatigue); it does not by itself cost the person more fame. Do not "fix" this in P14 on P17's behalf. [HIGH; `starPower.ts:67-120` verified; **QUALIFIED**.] (§7, §18)
14. **Remake and reboot are coherent as distinct mechanics:** a remake references one film and pays a comparison discount `0.5·q_ref·2^(−age/260 wk)` on the awareness input (×0.67 for a 2-year-old beloved original, ×0.99 by 30 years — no 20-year lock) and opens no continuity; a reboot opens a new bounded continuity, keeps property Recognition, resets that continuity's Momentum, **keeps the property's Fatigue in full** (Fatigue is property-wide — reboot is not a fatigue cure: Terminator, TASM), accrues at its own similarity (0.60), and pays 0.4× the comparison term only if the prior continuity's last film was recent *and* strong (Case R2). [DESIGN INFERENCE on OBSERVED HISTORY; **CONFIRMED** H/I.] (§8)
15. **SubProperties must be authored hooks, never mined** — the engine has no character/location entities and titles are mutable post-release. ≤3 live hooks per property, minted on request, never gated on success; Recognition transfers fractionally (0.38–0.65 by hook weight), Fatigue half-inherited (closes the laundering hole), Momentum never; spin-offs may outgrow parents but get no first-film bonus; promotion to an independent StoryProperty is a **P16** act. [**CONFIRMED** J with the minting discipline.] (§9)
16. **The Franchise root is small, lazy and studio-agnostic:** created at the first continuation, keyed to the P16 `storyPropertyId`, carrying **no owner field** (live P16 lookup — the smallest correction to Direction M's field list), no stored associations (derived from frozen `FilmParticipants`), **at most 3 concurrently open continuities with unbounded lifetime** (historical continuities preserved, never deleted, collapsed in the UI — revision 02 replaces M3's lifetime cap of 8, which Probe 6 shows exhausting a property's reboot option inside a 1920–2040 campaign), a closed milestone enum, ≈0.25–2.5 KB; any P17 root lands in the next governed save version and must join the identity walks. A rights sale writes zero bytes to P17. [HIGH on the save law; **QUALIFIED** M/L.] (§10)
17. **Legality never reads Recognition, Momentum or Fatigue** (rights, predecessor state, branch/SubProperty existence, era capacity only); a warning replaces every gate. The synthesis's proposed "no reboot unless the current branch is COOLING" rule is **not adopted** for that reason — with property-wide Fatigue a cheap reboot is no cheaper than a sequel except for its similarity bucket, without being illegal (product choice recorded in §19.2). [**CONFIRMED** E; **QUALIFIED** reboot governance.] (§5.5, §10)
18. **Talent continuity is importance-weighted, derived, and capped:** association weight = role importance × reach over ≥2 appearances (cap 4), reset by reboot, with an optional LEGACY carve-out *only if* P14 ever exposes a lifecycle fact (retirement/mortality are unselected systems — not a dependency; the default is ordinary recast); the effect is a ±25% swing on the inherited-awareness input, never on craft, never on legality, and **never priced into salary** — Direction R's indirect "salary expectations" consequence needs no P17 formula (fame growth through `computeStarPowerDelta` already reaches `salaryCurve`); a "franchise premium" multiplier would be an added, unselected P14 formula, kept separate and not recommended (§19.4). [**CONFIRMED** F with three guards.] (§11)
19. **Crossovers (later scope):** one film with ≥2 parent edges, `combinedR` as a saturating sum (0.6 decay, hard cap → two comparable properties ≈1.6×), shared discounted Fatigue on every participant, a derived post-crossover expectation band, no reception bonus; promotion to a shared-universe branch only if it spawns its own continuity. [**CONFIRMED** O with anti-snowball by construction.] (§12)
20. **Cast expansion should be additive optional arrays gated by a P13 `castCapacity` fact — Shape A (3 + featured/cameo ≤2) first, B (+1 principal) next, C (+2) last, never a widened `CastSlot` union (Shape D).** Six named principals is where relationship pairs (21), rival permutations (720 if ever permuted) and the 3-column UI all break. [HIGH on cost, MEDIUM on ranking; **OWNER-DIRECTION SUCCESSOR DESIGN**.] (§13)
21. **Cameos split cleanly into FAME VALUE (rank-decayed publicity, opening-only, bounded at ≤2× the top name) and PERFORMANCE VALUE (`CAST_WEIGHT.cameo` 0.10, excluded from cohesion), priced as a fraction of the fame-priced salary with a one-week booking and no audition.** "Ten celebrities per film" is a losing trade by the third name; famous non-actors need a P14 profession/audience-profile field first; a cameo never counts as a franchise association. [HIGH engine fit; **OWNER-DIRECTION SUCCESSOR DESIGN**.] (§14)
22. **Rivals can obey the same law with one new policy scalar** (`continuationAppetite`, `policy.version 2`) in `decide()` calling the unchanged `chooseIndustryPackage`, early bets at 0.3× appetite, reading only properties they own — **but rivals have no rights model at all today**; P16 must give rivals StoryProperty ownership before Directions K, L(rival side) and O are implementable. No comparator offers a copyable rival-franchise precedent. [HIGH on the gap; **QUALIFIED** K.] (§15)
23. **No dominant strategy survived the red-team pass among the attacks and paper histories actually run** once opportunity cost is priced: fast-sequel spam is self-limiting (Fatigue unbounded, inputs floor), dormancy earns nothing (Recognition only falls while waiting), spin-off laundering is halved, crossover stacking saturates, cameo stacking halves in value per name at constant fee, and **rotating sequels across continuities — which laundered 1.8× under revision 1's per-continuity Fatigue, lifetime cap included — is closed by property-wide Fatigue** (Probe 6). The claim is bounded to those cases: §16 lists the untested interactions (actual production lead times, a forecast bar that rises with inheritance, marketing, crowding, fame co-movement, cash). The residual risks are two product choices (Recognition floor; healthy-franchise reboot) and one implementation discipline (the P17 field must be threaded into *both* the forecast and reception paths — the forecast's `computeBoxOffice` call currently omits some late-bound factors). [MEDIUM; see §16.] (§16)
24. **Corrections to Future Ops assumptions** (§18): "trust/heat/reach" vocabulary must map to R/M/F with no fourth Standing channel; "Story DNA expansion" has no selected counterpart and should be dropped from the first checkpoint; "legacy sequel" is a dormancy case, not a sixth type; SIM-009's "dormant" franchise dimension is *absent*, not present-but-unused (a next-save-version event); the Success Blueprint's thirty-screenplay library ceiling is stale; the P13 catalogue's "no P18 exists" note is stale relative to the P12 contract; Direction M's "current rights owner ref" should be a live lookup.

## 5. Recognition / Momentum / Fatigue recommendation (Directions C, D, Q)

The [synthesis](models/M1-RMF-SYNTHESIS.md) took M1b as the base, re-derived it into an exact O(1) form, grafted the judges' converged fixes, and re-ran all seven cases plus a remake case and an eight-release spam probe (`models/M1-final-synthesis-calc.py` — every number below is script output). Two adversarial red-team passes then attacked the synthesis (§16) and found two root-cause defects (Recognition ratcheting with release count; Fatigue absent from the cash channel); the reviewer's corrected calculator [`redteam/R3-reviewer-corrections-calc.py`](redteam/R3-reviewer-corrections-calc.py) re-runs every case with the corrections C1–C7 listed in §16. **This section is the canonical specification** (§0): the synthesis's shape with those corrections, two reconciliations (stored lineage edges; a single reception seam instead of a separate expectation multiplier), and — in revision 02 — **property-wide Fatigue** (C3 revised, after Probe 6 showed that any per-continuity Fatigue read is laundered by rotating sequels across existing continuities) and the executed reboot comparison term (C4′). ### 5.2 Recommended rule

**Inputs, read once per release from the public `FilmResult` (never written):** `critic01`, `aud01` (via `filmAudienceScore`/`aggregateAudienceScore`, `receptionVerdict.ts:21-117`), `quality = 0.5·critic01 + 0.5·aud01` (starting blend), `reach01 = total/(total + 10,000,000)` (verbatim `starPower.ts` helper), `comparator = total / forecast.expectedTotal` (the ratio `fcMult` already reads), `surprise = clamp(0.5·(comparator − 1) + (quality − 0.5), −1, 1)`, plus `similarity` **derived from the continuation type** (direct sequel/prequel 0.90, remake 0.70, reboot 0.60, spin-off 0.50), where the type itself is **derived from objective greenlight facts** — same branch as its most recent installment → sequel family (prequel is a player label within it); a new branch citing prior branches and no SubProperty → reboot; cites a SubProperty → spin-off; cites exactly one prior film and no new branch → remake — never a free player declaration (**correction C6**, closing the mislabelling surface both red-team reviewers found).

**Recognition — StoryProperty-wide, peak-anchored, quality-gated, slow (Direction Q).** `score_i = reach01_i · quality_i · gateR(quality_i) · 2^(week_i/HL_R)` with `gateR(q) = clamp((q − 0.50)/0.25, 0, 1)` (**correction C1** after the red team showed the ungated kernel ratcheting to 100 on eight mediocre films — only genuinely good films build durable recognition; mediocre volume builds modest name recognition, ≈43 after twenty films); keep a running `bestScore` (the film currently holding the peak, `peakInstallmentId`) and `residualScoreSum` (every other film); `R(t) = clamp(100·(bestScore + κ_R·residualScoreSum)·2^(−t/HL_R), 0, 100)` with `HL_R = 650 weeks (~12.5 y)`, `κ_R = 0.15`; **ratchet floor** `R_reported = max(R, FLOOR_FRAC·rPeak)`, `FLOOR_FRAC = 0.35`. A later flop cannot pull the peak term down (it is a `max`, not a mean) and, gated, adds nothing — Film 1 smash + Film 2 dud leaves R at 80 of 87 after 78 weeks of ordinary passive decay (Case C). Not a lifetime average.

**Momentum — per branch, fast, decays to neutral.** `M ← M·2^(−Δt/HL_M) + 100·surprise_i` at each release, continuous decay between; `HL_M = 24 weeks`. A film that met its own locked forecast with a 50/100 blend contributes exactly zero.

**Fatigue — one scalar per StoryProperty, driven by (1 − quality)·similarity, pays down faster after excellent films (C3 revised, revision 02).** Founding-film exemption applies **only to the StoryProperty's first-ever film** (**correction C5**): a reboot's or spin-off's founding film accrues at its own similarity (reboot 0.60, spin-off 0.50) because audiences have seen this story before; satiation gate `gate = 0 if quality ≥ 0.75 else clamp((0.75 − quality)/0.10, 0, 1)`; `accrual = 100·similarity·(1 − quality)·gate`; paydown `effectiveHalfLife_F = 104 / (1 + 1.5·clamp((lastQuality − 0.5)/0.5, 0, 1))` (104 → ~42 weeks after the property's last film was excellent); `F ← F·2^(−Δt/eff) + accrual`. **Every continuity of the property reads the same Fatigue**; a new continuity resets Momentum, not Fatigue. A SubProperty is a separate object with its own scalar, minted at `0.5 × F_parent` (§9). *Why property-wide:* revision 01 kept Fatigue per branch and seeded a reboot with `γ = 0.2` × its parent's Fatigue; Probe 6 (§10, §16) showed that no seed rule can prevent laundering, because a sequel on **any** existing continuity is always legal — a player rotating mediocre sequels across old continuities read a paid-down line while the audience was tired of the property (1.8× the single-line awareness, *inside* M3's lifetime cap). One scalar per property closes the hole exactly and needs no seed; the only remaining difference between patterns is the similarity bucket (`SIMILARITY.reboot` 0.60 vs 0.90), a tuning constant. Excellent similar output adds nothing; mediocre similar output stacks visibly (Case D: 0 → 40 → 69 → 90 at 40-week cadence); rest recovers on the same clock — "rises faster for mediocre than excellent" is a magnitude-at-release effect, not a separate rate.

**Stored state (reconciled with §10).** Per StoryProperty: `bestScore, residualScoreSum, peakInstallmentId, rPeak, fatigue, fatigueLastUpdateWeek, fatigueLastQuality`; per continuity (branch): `M, lastUpdateWeek, lastReleaseWeek, priorGapWeeks`, plus a **display-only** `recentLedger[≤4]` `{installmentId, addedWeek, momentumContribution, fatigueAccrual, quality}` for the itemised tooltip (never read by a formula; it is where "this continuity's share of the property's Fatigue" is shown). The **lineage edges stay stored** for identity, UI and association derivation (§10, §11) — the synthesis showed they are not needed to *compute* R/M/F, which is what makes the meters exactly reproducible from edges + public results (an audit property, not a storage requirement). Save cost: ≈250 B for a one-continuity franchise, ≈1.5 KB for a fourteen-continuity outlier (Probe 6e), flat in lifetime installment count, plus ≈41 B per lineage edge.

**Descriptor bands — derived, never stored** (starting thresholds): NEW (no release yet); **ACTIVE AGAIN** if the branch's prior gap ≥150 weeks, the new release is <26 weeks old and M ≥ 0 — detected from the branch's own release gaps, so a brand-new reboot branch reads plain ACTIVE, never ACTIVE AGAIN (a `null` prior gap must never be coerced to "infinite" — a bug the synthesis made and caught); **REVIVAL CANDIDATE / DORMANT** if ≥150 weeks since the last release (split on `R_reported ≥ 25`); **COOLING** if `M < −5 or F ≥ 60` (M is the continuity's, F the property's); else **ACTIVE**. Every threshold is on a number the player can already see; no sixth hidden phase. The band is per continuity (its own Momentum and release gaps) over the property's single Fatigue; the same thresholds define "open" for the §10 continuity cap (a continuity is open while it is NEW or fewer than 150 weeks past its last release).

**The itemised tooltip is the calculation:** *"Film 2 contributes −12 to Momentum, fading by [date]; Film 3 added 31 Fatigue, paying down at the faster rate because Film 4 was excellent; your defining hit is still Film 1 (peak 87)."* Two-speed display idiom (adjective band for M/F, a level for R) per the comparator harvest.

### 5.3 The outputs P17 hands to other packages (reconciled seam — see §7 and §17)

P17 exposes **one read-model function** — `continuationInputs(storyPropertyId, branchId, type, referencedFilmId?, proposedCast) → { inheritedAwareness01, remakeComparison01, continuityCredit01 }` — consumed as optional fields on `ReceptionInputs` in the `setUplift`/`setNovelty` precedent (`reception.ts:87-103`: "ABSENT is the whole legacy world … a bit-exact IEEE no-op"). Because `ForecastInputs = ReceptionInputs` (`forecast.ts:54`) and the forecast calls the same `computeSegmentAppeal`/`computeBoxOffice` helpers (`forecast.ts:272, 468`), **the same field is read twice by the same formulas at two different times**: by P11's forecast at the continuation's greenlight (then locked) and by P07's reception at its release (live, with whatever R/M/F then are). This is the whole mechanism of Direction B and Direction G: a known property opens higher; its locked forecast is therefore higher; a shortfall against that bar is judged by the existing `fcMult`/`boxDelta`/broadcast machinery with **zero new formulas downstream**.

- `inheritedAwareness01 = clamp(0.8·(R_reported/100) + 0.2·(max(M,0)/100), 0, 1) × (1 − 0.5·min(F,100)/100)` — M is the continuity's, F the property's (a SubProperty's own for spin-offs); positive Momentum only (a downswing does not erase public awareness; it removes the tailwind), and **Fatigue discounts realized reach**, not merely a forecast bar (the reviewer's correction to the synthesis, where F entered only the expectation multiplier — a forecast-only Fatigue term would let a fatigued franchise systematically beat its own lowered forecast).
- `remakeComparison01 = 0.5·q_ref·2^(−age_ref/260 wk)` for the one referenced film (§8), applied as `inheritedAwareness01 × (1 − remakeComparison01)` — a discount through the same optional field, so it too is read at lock and at release (Case R: ×0.67 at 2 years, ×0.89 at 10, ×0.99 at 30). **Canonical coefficient 0.5** — revision 01 printed 0.6 here and in the closing table while the calculator and the reported numbers used 0.5; §8 carried M3's linear form; both are superseded (§5.7). A **reboot** pays `0.4 × remakeComparison01` computed against the most recent prior continuity's last film, **only if** that film is < 416 weeks old **and** its quality ≥ 0.65 (Case R2: fires for a 0.80-quality film five years old, ×0.92; not for a TASM-like 0.62 or a Casino-Royale-like 0.40; never beyond eight years).
- `continuityCredit01` (§11) scales the inherited awareness by `0.75 + 0.25·credit`.

**The separate "expectation multiplier" on `expectedTotal` proposed by `M2` and the synthesis is NOT recommended for the first checkpoint.** With the single-field design the forecast already rises with inherited awareness, so a second multiplier on the forecast alone would (i) bias the player-facing forecast away from an honest prediction and (ii) count inheritance twice (once in reception, once in the bar). "Higher expectations" is emergent and honest: the same absolute gross that reads as a hit for an original reads as a **miss** for a famous property. If the Owner wants *inflated* expectations beyond the neutral prediction (trade-press hype), that is an explicit product choice (§19.2), bounded and clearly labelled on the greenlight card.

## 6. Continuation-timing recommendation (Directions D, H)

**There is no cooldown, wait timer, release-count check or hard unlock anywhere in the rule.** Timing enters through exactly two continuous channels: (1) Momentum and Fatigue decay every week on different clocks (24 weeks; 42–104 weeks), so waiting loses a tailwind *and* a headwind at once; (2) the band function reads elapsed weeks as descriptive inputs, not permissions — releasing into COOLING or DORMANT is legal and merely gets whatever awareness the meters currently produce.

**Why a fast follow-up to a huge hit is MORE attractive.** Momentum's half-life is short relative to a production timeline: Case A's M = 92.5 at release is 82.4 at a week-4 greenlight and ≈16 at week 60. The awareness input rewards positive M directly; an excellent film's Fatigue accrual gates to exactly zero; there is no per-count or cadence penalty — so waiting strictly loses awareness value and gains nothing on Fatigue for a good film, while a *rushed mediocre* follow-up pays real Fatigue (Case D). "Ride the hit while it is hot" is the arithmetic, not a special rule — matching Basuroy & Chatterjee 2008 (sooner sequels earn more; satiation shows as faster decay), Hollywood Animal's shipped no-cooldown design (without its inherent bonus), and Infinity War → Endgame (+39% at 12 months).

**Type-specific behaviour (Direction H):** sequels / prequels / spin-offs — no wait, ever; prequels are bucketed with direct sequels (0.90) for the first checkpoint (flagged: no case tests a prequel-specific rule); remakes — the shared similarity Fatigue (0.70) **plus** a one-time comparison discount scoped to the specific referenced film, large when that film is recent and beloved and fading on a 5-year half-life (≈25% left at 10 years, negligible by 30 — no 20-year lock); reboots — a new continuity: its own Momentum starts at zero; **Fatigue does not reset** (it is the property's, §5.2); Recognition never resets because it never lived on the branch; the reboot's success raises the shared Recognition visible from every continuity but does not make the old continuity "active" (that needs a release on the old branch).

**What Fatigue is keyed to.** Sameness × mediocrity, never count or cadence: two smash films a year apart accrue exactly 0 and a 74.5-quality spin-off SubProperty accrues 0.64 (Case B); four mediocre sequels at 40-week cadence flip the band on Fatigue alone (Case D); the same four at 15-week cadence flip it one film sooner because less has decayed between them (spam probe). That is the entire "timing effect" — continuous, visible, and attributable to the rights-holder's own releases (never to others' — market crowding is P15).

No hidden future information.

**Worked CASE C / CASE G** (`M2` §7, real `computeStarPowerDelta` constants): a famous property's flop at a raised bar sits at the `fcMult` floor with **no fame loss** while the audience score stays ≥45 — the person's fame falls only if audiences actually turned; the property's excitement falls through Seam B regardless. That separation is intended.

## 8. Remake vs reboot model (Directions H, I)

Source: [`models/M3`](models/M3-franchise-object-branches-remake-reboot-rights.md) §3 (historical — its linear comparison term, per-branch Fatigue seed and lifetime cap are superseded here); real cases `04a` F / `04b` F; Bohnenkamp et al. 2015. **The canonical comparison terms are those of §5.3 (§5.7 row "Remake comparison" / "Reboot partial comparison"), executed in Cases R and R2.**

| | REMAKE | REBOOT |
|---|---|---|
| Reference | Exactly one prior `FilmResult` — a `LineageEdge{type:'remake', parentProductionId}` | No specific film; requires ≥1 prior branch on the StoryProperty |
| Continuity | **Does not open a continuity**; filed in the continuity active at greenlight; a later sequel to the remake is an ordinary sequel edge in the same continuity (no retroactive branch creation — forward-only save law) | **Opens a new continuity** (`type:'reboot'`, `parentBranchId: null`), subject to the open-continuity cap (§10); the old continuity stays historically intact, never deleted |
| Inherited Recognition | Property-level R, like any continuation | Property-level R, unchanged |
| Momentum / Fatigue | Momentum of the continuity it sits in; Fatigue is the property's | Momentum reset to 0 on the new continuity; **Fatigue is property-wide and does not reset** — reboot is not a fatigue cure (Salvation → Genisys → Dark Fate; Probe 4: a reboot the week after four mediocre films reads the same 86.8 the old line reads); the reboot's own film accrues at similarity 0.60 (C5). *M3's "γ(0.2) × decayed average of prior branches' end-Fatigue" seed is historical (§5.7).* |
| Comparison term | `remakeComparison01 = 0.5·q_ref·2^(−age/260 wk)`, applied as `× (1 − remakeComparison01)` on the awareness input — ×0.67 for a 2-year-old beloved original (Never Say Never Again vs same-year Octopussy), ×0.89 at 10 years, ×0.99 at 30 (dormancy/nostalgia helps; a continuous fade, no 20-year lock). *M3's linear `18·(q/100)·clamp(1 − age/780 wk)` is historical.* | `0.4 ×` the same term against the most recent prior continuity's last film, **only if** that film is recent (< 416 wk) **and** strong (quality ≥ 0.65) — Case R2: ×0.92 for a 0.80-quality film five years old; nothing for a TASM-like 0.62 reference, a Casino-Royale-like 0.40 reference, or anything older than eight years |
| Legality | Referenced film must be released (nothing to retell otherwise); rights via P16 | ≥1 prior continuity; fewer than 3 continuities currently open (§10 — a time fact, never R/M/F); rights via P16 |
| Seam | Optional `ReceptionInputs` field (same precedent) — P07 keeps the formula | Same |
| UI | A leaf under its continuity with a distinguishing label | Its own indented continuity row while open; collapsed into "historical continuities" once dormant |

Both terms are bounded optional inputs, never new P07 formulas; neither reads commercial success for legality (Direction E). The distinction is coherent and game-legible because each type answers one player question: "how does this compare to *that* film?" (remake) vs "does this start over?" (reboot).

## 9. SubProperty / spin-off model (Direction J)

Source: [`models/M4`](models/M4-subproperty-spinoff-crossover.md) Part A (numbers in `models/m4_worked_numbers.py`).

1. **What qualifies.** The engine has **no character, organization or location entity** — `FilmConcept` is eight fields with no lineage and no character names; `FilmParticipants` records which *person* played which of the three seats; titles are mutable. A SubProperty must therefore be an **authored, named hook** minted explicitly (by the player at the parent's greenlight or release, or by rivals through the same law), citing an exact `productionId` and, for character/organization hooks, the exact frozen `FilmParticipant` — never mined from a title, persona field or genre (SAF-009/INT-012 discipline applied one level down). Kinds: character (weight = `CAST_WEIGHT[slot]`), organization/team (mean of cited participants' weights, ≤1.0), location and major concept (flat `HOOK_WEIGHT_DEFAULT 0.5`).
2. **Not every film mints options.** Bounded: `MAX_LIVE_SUBPROPERTIES_PER_STORYPROPERTY = 3` (starting point), minted on request; eligibility = rights not blocked (P16) + exact-record binding + under cap — **never commercial performance** (Direction E).
3. **Importance matters** through the hook weight and the minting film's public reach (`reach01 = total/(total+10M)`, the same helper `starPower.ts` uses — reused, not duplicated).
4. **Recognition transfer** is fractional: `transferFraction = 0.38 + 0.27·hookWeight` (calibrated to the observed 38–65% opening-inheritance band), so `R_sp(t0) = transferFraction · R_parent`; `F_sp(t0) = 0.5 · F_parent` where `F_parent` is the parent StoryProperty's single Fatigue scalar (a liability that partially travels — closes the "spin-off launders Fatigue" hole without denying the real satiation benefit of a distinct property; thereafter the SubProperty's Fatigue is its own and neither feeds nor reads the parent's); `M_sp(t0) = 0` (Momentum is a property of a release cadence, not a character; prevents borrowing a hot week for a film years later). Parent R is **never consumed**.
5. **A spin-off can outgrow its parent** without cap (Deadpool, Minions, Joker) but gets **no first-film outgrowth bonus** (Creed took three films and eight years). **Promotion to an independent StoryProperty is a P16 act:** P17 exposes `promotionEligible` + payload; P16 mints the new id; the SubProperty id persists as a historical handle; the accrued R/M/F/installments carry over as starting state.
6. **Independent SubProperty rights** are a **P16 decision the report must not pre-empt**: interface `rightsHolderFor(sub) = P16.rightsOwner(sub.sovereignRightsId ?? sub.storyPropertyId)` — a live read that works whether or not P16 ever models sub-rights (a P16 charter question, §19.5).
7. **P18 reuse**: `SubPropertyId` is the opaque permanent handle exposed read-only on the public projection lane.

Minimal shape: `SubProperty { id, storyPropertyId, kind, mintedAtProductionId, mintedTick, hookWeight, sovereignRightsId?, recognition, momentum, fatigue, installmentProductionIds[], promoted? }` — id-keyed scalars/lists only. Keep exactly two levels (StoryProperty → SubProperty); crossovers stay edge-based (The Executive's "Universe" tier is the failure to avoid).

## 10. Persistent Franchise / branch model (Directions M, N, P, Q, S)

Source: [`models/M3`](models/M3-franchise-object-branches-remake-reboot-rights.md) (byte estimates in `models/m3_worked_numbers.py`); save law in `evidence/02` §f.

**Creation.** A `Franchise` is created **lazily** the first time anyone greenlights a continuation of a StoryProperty — never at an original's release (most films never get one). It is keyed to the exact P16 `storyPropertyId`; it carries **no studio id** and **no owner field** (§18 row 19).

**Minimum authoritative state (STORED):** `franchiseId`, `storyPropertyId`, `branches[] {branchId, type main|reboot|spinoff, parentBranchId, subPropertyId?, openedAtWeek, lastReleaseWeek}`, `installments[]` as lineage edges `{childProductionId, parentProductionId|null, type, branchId, similarity}`, `subPropertyIds[]`, `milestones[] {milestoneId (closed, versioned enum incl. `'legacyCameo'`), productionId, week}`, and the §5.2 scalars (property: `bestScore, residualScoreSum, peakInstallmentId, rPeak, fatigue, fatigueLastUpdateWeek, fatigueLastQuality`; per continuity: `M, lastUpdateWeek, lastReleaseWeek, priorGapWeeks`) — **key-talent associations are not stored**: they are derived on demand from the frozen `FilmParticipants` of the branch's installments (§11; boundary review A4), at most a display cache. *M3's "meter variant" (`recognitionPeak` with a relax) is historical; the running-scalar kernel of §5.2 is canonical.* **DERIVED (never stored):** lifecycle band; whether a continuity is *open*; current rights owner (live P16 lookup); legality; top-level `lastReleaseWeek`; all display strings (HIS-014: no persisted narrative labels).

**Size.** ≈830 B typical, ≈2.5 KB for a Bond-scale 25-installment franchise — smaller than one already-oversized `FilmResult` (3,146 B); with lazy creation and the continuity rate bound below (≤ 124 continuities in a 6,240-week campaign even in the pathological case, ≈7 KB at M3's 58 B per branch record), negligible against the 37.8 MB Hollywood root.

**Save law.** Any P17 root lands in **the next governed save version** — the successor of whatever save version is the accepted predecessor contract when P17 lands (V19 at the reviewed commit `13370d42`; P13–P16 may advance it, and this report assumes no number) — under the existing forward-only, root-additive law observed at that commit (root keys exact-checked; each version strips its new root and validates the frozen predecessor remainder); absent-safe on fragments from earlier versions; validated leaf-exact; joins `persistedProductionIds`/`persistedConceptIds` in both directions the week it lands; migrated from the predecessor by seeding an **empty** root (no inference of past franchises); `PROJECTION_VERSION` bump if projected. Sequence: `storyProperties` (P16) then `franchises` (P17); the rival `policy.version: 2` widening rides the same governed version step (§15).

**Bounded branching ("what connects to what").** One `main` continuity; a `reboot` branch supersedes (`parentBranchId: null`, reads old-branch history only for its conditional comparison term, §8); a `spinoff` branch is a thin pointer to its SubProperty (`branchId, type, subPropertyId`) — the spin-off's own Momentum/Fatigue live on the `SubProperty` record (§9; boundary review A5), never duplicated on the branch. Lineage is a tree (single parent) until crossovers add a *separate* multi-parent edge kind.

**Active-continuity cap, unbounded lifetime (revision 02 — replaces M3's lifetime cap of 8; evaluated in R3 Probe 6).** M3 chose a *lifetime* cap deliberately, arguing that a concurrent cap would need a disguised kill button or would let dormant branches "silently stop counting". Probe 6 shows the lifetime cap has the worse failure: **the ninth requested continuity is illegal forever**, and at a Batman-like 12-year reboot cadence a property founded in 1925 spends all eight by 2009 — its reboot and spin-off options are silently exhausted *inside* the 1920–2040 campaign; at a 20-year cadence they are exhausted by 2065, so any play past 2040 (a P15 direction under consideration, not a recorded ruling) guarantees exhaustion for every long-lived property. That is a kill button by another name, in tension with Directions H ("useful when continuity stale/damaged/dormant/confusing") and P. The lifetime cap also never did the economic job M3 gave it: under revision 01's per-branch Fatigue, rotating mediocre sequels across the eight permitted lines laundered Fatigue to 1.8× the single-line awareness (Probe 6c) — sequels on existing continuities are always legal, so no creation cap touches it. That job now belongs to property-wide Fatigue (§5.2), which leaves the cap with exactly one purpose: **bounded active and display complexity (Direction N).** Recommended rule:

- **Open continuity** = a `main`/`reboot` branch that is NEW (has a greenlit, unreleased production) or fewer than `DORMANT_WEEKS` (150, the same threshold the band function reads) past its last release. A time fact derived from release weeks — never stored, never R/M/F.
- **Legality precondition for opening a new `reboot` branch:** fewer than `N_MAX_OPEN_CONTINUITIES = 3` continuities are open (starting point; Batman 2017–2023 ran three concurrently — Batfleck, Pattinson, Joker — and no real case needs more). Spin-off branches are bounded separately by the ≤3 live SubProperties (§9). **Releasing on an existing continuity is never gated** by this cap (Direction D/E) — reviving a dormant line may briefly exceed three open; it self-resolves and creates nothing.
- **The ninth (or any) requested continuity:** legal whenever fewer than three are open; when refused, the greenlight card names which continuity must go quiet and for how many weeks — the option is deferred, never lost. Lifetime count is unbounded but rate-bounded: at most 3 new continuities per 150 weeks, i.e. ≤ 124 over a 6,240-week campaign and ≤ 208 over a 200-year sandbox in the pathological case (≈7–12 KB at 58 B per record); typical real cases need ≤ 4.
- **Historical continuities are preserved** (append-only, never deleted, still attributed, still decaying) and **collapsed** in the UI: the list expands the open continuities (≤3 plus any just-revived line) and the live SubProperties (≤3), and shows one row "historical continuities (k)" that expands on demand. The property page never shows more than ~7 expanded rows however long the campaign runs.
- **Laundering:** none through branches, because Fatigue is the property's (Probe 6: every pattern reads the same F; the reboot-heavy play the cap permits — 14 lines in 20 films — earns 0.125 versus 0.108 for a single line, the similarity bucket only, §5.5).

UI is a depth-2 indented list — never a graph — a hard acceptance criterion given the genre's IP-vs-Franchise confusion:

```
Story Property: [name resolved live from P16]
├─ MAIN continuity: Film 1 (origin) · Film 2 — sequel · Film 3 — sequel   [Momentum ▼ · Fatigue ▲]
├─ REBOOT continuity (opened wk 940): Film 4 — reboot · Film 5 — sequel   [Momentum ▲ · Fatigue neutral]
├─ SUB-PROPERTY "[Character X]" → SPIN-OFF continuity: Film 6 — spin-off
└─ historical continuities (2) ▸   [collapsed; expands to the dormant lines with their films]
   (remakes attach as labelled leaves under the continuity they were greenlit in; Fatigue is shown once, on the property)
```

**Lifecycle bands (Direction P) — derived from (the continuity's M, the property's F, weeks since the continuity's last release), never stored — the canonical band function is §5.2's:** ACTIVE → COOLING (Momentum < −5 or Fatigue ≥ 60) → after ≥150 weeks without a release, DORMANT (reported Recognition < 25) or REVIVAL CANDIDATE (≥ 25) → ACTIVE AGAIN (a transient flourish, < 26 weeks, when a continuity whose prior gap was ≥150 weeks lands a release with non-negative Momentum — detected from the installment list's own gaps and minted as a milestone). Thresholds are starting points (150 / 26 weeks; *M3's 104/260-week bands are historical*). No terminal state exists; every band is reachable from every other by time and a release. Recognition is deliberately kept as a separate always-visible axis ("how strong could a revival be") rather than folded into "whether one is due".

**Historical weighting (Direction Q).** Recognition is **peak-anchored** — the quality-gated max-then-residual kernel of §5.2 (canonical); *M3's monotonic `recognitionPeak` meter with a relax is a historical variant*. Film 1 = smash, Film 2 = dud leaves R at 80 of 87 after 78 weeks (Case C: ordinary passive decay only) while M swings negative and F rises. No lifetime average.

**Rights transfer mid-franchise (Direction L).** A P16 sale writes **zero bytes** to the P17 root: historical films stay attributed via the existing `studioHistory`/`IndustryFilm.studioId` facts; R/M/F and key associations travel with the property ("the audience does not care who owns it" — Deadpool & Wolverine opened $211M three years after the X-Men rights changed hands; Software Inc.'s non-transfer rule is explicitly rejected); contracts do not travel (P14); a transfer may leave the franchise DORMANT with no P17 penalty (Bond 2021→≥2028 is an ownership gap, not audience rejection) — no special "ownership dormancy" flag is needed because the band reads only (M, F, weeks).

**No kill button (Direction S).** No `status` field anyone can set; branches are never deleted; dormancy and "open" are reads of release weeks; closing a continuity is never an action; P16 may sell; `installments`/`milestones` are append-only.

**Legality matrix (Direction E — confirmed: no row reads R, M or F).**

| Type | Rights (P16, live) | Predecessor state | Branch / SubProperty | Era capacity (P13) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Direct sequel | owner controls the StoryProperty | released, **or** locked-committed (Direction G) | any existing branch | only if requesting an era-gated seat |
| Prequel | same | referenced film exists as ≥ a locked Production | any existing branch | same |
| Film spin-off | `rightsHolderFor(sub) = P16.rightsOwner(sub.sovereignRightsId ?? sub.storyPropertyId)` (works whether or not P16 ever models sub-rights) | referenced parent released or locked | a named SubProperty must exist (mint is a bounded precondition) | same |
| Remake | same | referenced film **released** | none — never opens a branch | same |
| Reboot | same | none on any film | ≥1 prior continuity; fewer than 3 continuities open (a release-week fact) | same |

Warning text replaces any gate (starting copy): *"[Property] is legal to continue — [rights holder] holds the rights. Its [branch] continuity is DORMANT (no release in N years). Recognition remains strong (R/100, near its peak), but Momentum has fully decayed and the property still carries elevated Fatigue from its recent output. Expect a reception discount versus a fresh continuity or a well-timed sequel."*

**Early continuation greenlight (Direction G).** "Sufficiently committed" = the predecessor has a locked `forecastSnapshot` (a `Production` exists) — the existing object, no new state. The continuation's greenlight reads **only** the predecessor's public locked forecast plus the *current* R/M/F; the predecessor's `FilmResult` does not exist yet, so there is nothing to withhold by construction. The continuation's own forecast locks at its own greenlight and is **never revised**; what updates when the predecessor releases is the live R/M/F — which P07 reads **at the continuation's release** (the live seam), so a predecessor's bomb catches up with an already-committed continuation as a legible forecast miss (`boxDelta`). A display-only risk descriptor (ON TRACK / AT RISK) recomputes every tick from current R/M/F next to the frozen number — the warning without rewriting the promise. Rivals make the identical bet through the identical seams (§15).

**State-rot guards** (`M3` §10): no cached owner → no orphan on transfer; edges reference `productionId` only and rows are never deleted → join the identity walks; `branchId`/`franchiseId` minted from a monotonic ordinal stored on the root (the `nextOrdinal` precedent) → Save-As branching cannot collide; closed milestone enum → no label creep; associations never substitute for a live P14 availability check; the open-continuity cap checked as a legality precondition from release weeks alone (nothing stored); crossovers must be a separate edge kind, never a widened `LineageEdge`.

## 11. Cast continuity / recasting model (Direction F)

Source: [`models/M5`](models/M5-cast-continuity-recasting-rivals-early-greenlight.md) Part 1 (numbers in `models/p17_m5_calc.py`); Hennig-Thurau 2009; `04a` G / `04b` G.

**Role importance is a band over one deterministic weight, derived on demand from frozen public facts — no new persisted collection.** For a talent's appearances `i` in a branch: `AssociationWeight = min(Σ_i ROLE_IMPORTANCE_WEIGHT[role_i] · reach01_i, CAP 4.0)` with `reach01` = the existing `starPower.ts` helper and starting weights lead 1.0 / director 0.9 / antagonist 0.7 / support 0.45 / writer 0.35 / **craft excluded**. Quality is deliberately *not* in the weight (H-T 2009 measures continuity as an importance-weighted share; the 29%-RT Fast & Furious reunion still anchored its cast) — quality acts at the next installment through the film's own result. Bands: ICONIC LEAD (lead-dominant, ≥2 appearances, weight ≥1.6), CORE ENSEMBLE, RECURRING SUPPORT (≥0.3), SIGNATURE DIRECTOR (≥1.3), MINOR APPEARANCE (default), and — **only if P14 ever exposes a lifecycle fact** — LEGACY (retirement/mortality are unselected systems today; they are not a P17 dependency). **`MIN_APPEARANCES_FOR_MAJOR = 2` is an explicit law, not an emergent threshold** — a single blockbuster credit never makes anyone iconic (worked in `M5` §1.3: a one-off lead at weight 0.963 would otherwise read CORE ENSEMBLE). Consequence: no continuity credit can exist before a branch's **third** film.

**Continuity effect — a discount on the inherited-awareness/expectation inputs, never on craft.** `PresenceMultiplier` = 1.0 same talent / `RECAST_RETENTION 0.35` different talent / 0 role class absent; `ContinuityCredit = Σ weight·presence / Σ weight`; the inherited-awareness input is scaled by `0.75 + 0.25·ContinuityCredit` (a gentle ±25% swing — Jurassic World opened at 4× the franchise record with one returning actor; Ghostbusters 2016 opened within $2M of Afterlife with zero continuity cast: property Recognition dominates, continuity is secondary, β=.15 vs β=.71). Worked: iconic lead recast + recurring support returns → credit 0.467 → multiplier 0.867 (≈13% discount on inherited awareness only); a strong `FilmResult` then rebuilds Momentum at full strength — the Fury Road pattern (signature director returns, lead recast, 97% RT). **The discount is paid once going in; it is not a tax on the outcome.**

**Departures.** Default rule: a talent who does not return is an ordinary recast (`RECAST_RETENTION 0.35`). Optional refinement, *only if* P14 ever exposes a retired/deceased fact: LEGACY — removed from numerator *and* denominator (no penalty by exclusion, not by a special multiplier). P17 does not require the fact and must not request a lifecycle or mortality system to get it. Rights transfer changes nothing (key is `(talentId, storyPropertyId, branchId)`, never `studioId`). **Reboot fully resets associations by key-scoping alone** (Bale/Craig/Pattinson) — old-branch associations remain queryable for history. Remakes get no continuity computation (comparison pressure is their mechanic).

**Three anti-lock-in guards.** (a) Legality never depends on cast — the greenlight validator requires three *distinct* ids, never a *specific* one; `ContinuityCredit` is read-only telemetry, unlike Hollywood Animal's forced carry-over. (b) `CAP 4.0` and the min-appearances law. (c) **Salary:** `salaryCurve` has no film/franchise argument and no release path re-stamps `Talent.salary` — the only escalator is fame growth via `computeStarPowerDelta`; P17 publishes the public band as read-only data and **must never** feed it into `salaryCurve`/`offerForTalent`/`freelancerFee`. Direction R's selected *indirect* consequence ("talent interest/salary expectations") is realised with **no P17 formula**: a bigger franchise film reaches more people, `computeStarPowerDelta` raises the returning talent's fame, and `salaryCurve` already prices fame — the ask rises because the person is more famous, not because the property is. A "franchise premium" multiplier reading P17's association band would be an **added, unselected P14 formula** — kept separate from Direction R, not recommended, and listed in §19.4 only so it is not built by accident (H-T 2009's awareness×star interaction says the temptation is strongest for the biggest franchises).

**Interfaces.** `BRIDGING_DISCOUNT` — a parent-branch MAJOR talent appearing in a spin-off runs the same `ContinuityCredit` function once more (Creed/Stallone helped; Furiosa's missing bridge hurt) — the SubProperty model reuses the function rather than defining a second one. Cameos never count toward associations (§14).

## 12. Crossover model (Direction O — later P17 scope)

Source: [`models/M4`](models/M4-subproperty-spinoff-crossover.md) Part B; evidence `04a` H.

- **Requirements (all read, none new):** rights/control of **every** participating StoryProperty (P16 — rivals have no rights model today, a hard P16 dependency); cast capacity for the extra principal seats (P13 era capacity + core casting Shape B/C, §13); lawful talent availability (P14 busy/contract law); one production greenlit as one film citing ≥2 properties (P17).
- **Recognition combines as a saturating sum, never additively:** `combinedR = min(Σ_i R_i · 0.6^(i−1), R_CAP)` — two comparable mid properties (50, 50) → 80 (1.6×); one strong + one weaker (60, 45) → 87; two already-strong (85, 85) → capped at 100 (1.18×); three-way (55, 55, 40) → capped. Calibrated to land two comparable non-saturated properties in the observed 1.5–1.7× band while the ceiling does the anti-snowball work (Avengers opened at ~80% of the naive sum of its leads' best openings; Justice League opened below its own predecessor — no floor is promised). `combinedR` is computed once per crossover film at greenlight and fed through the existing optional-input seam into `preMarketingAwarenessOf`/forecast; it is **never persisted** (that would be a second FranchiseScore).
- **Both audiences contribute**, capped twice (per-participant decay + absolute ceiling).
- **Expectation explosion** is real (post-Avengers and post-Endgame solos read as failures against the raised baseline; the pattern repeats with No Way Home and Deadpool & Wolverine). Recommendation: record the crossover's realised reach as a **milestone** on each participating Franchise root and surface a **derived** NORMAL/ELEVATED/EXTREME "post-crossover expectation" band on the next solo greenlight — a display over numbers that already exist, **not** a hidden "must beat the crossover" penalty.
- **Fatigue interaction:** a crossover is an installment of **every** participant at `SIMILARITY_DISCOUNT 0.5` (Batman in an ensemble is not another numbered Batman film, but the character still appeared — the Justice League case: two poorly received prior appearances within 18 months plus a discounted third).
- **Success/failure consequences:** the same per-installment R/M/F update fired once per participant with `share_i` from billing-decay weights normalised to 1 (2 participants: 0.625/0.375; 3: 0.51/0.31/0.18) — spillover (+30–46% to the next MCU solos) and shared damage both fall out.
- **Structure:** a crossover is **one `FilmResult` plus a `CrossoverLineage` edge-set with ≥2 parent StoryProperty edges** — never a new franchise object by default. Only if it spawns its own numbered continuity may it be **promoted** to a shared-universe branch through the same P17-requests/P16-mints interface (an optional later feature, §19.4; it would count against the open-continuity cap of §10).
- **Anti-snowball, four mechanisms, no new cap:** saturating combination; shared Fatigue on all participants at once; real seat/salary/capacity cost through P13/P14/P11; no reception bonus (`combinedR` buys awareness/opening only — Age of Ultron −8%, Justice League).

## 13. Optional cast-slot expansion (Direction T)

Sources: [`evidence/05`](evidence/05-cast-scale-and-cameo-design-evidence.md) (cost analysis, UI surface count, historical context), [`models/M6`](models/M6-cast-slots-cameo-recommendation.md) (final recommendation, arithmetic re-verified by `models/m6_arithmetic_check.py`), engine facts from [`evidence/02`](evidence/02-project-studio-architecture-READONLY.md) §b.2/§c.

**Engine fact that governs everything (CONFIRMED, HIGH).** Exactly three principal acting seats exist — `CastSlot = 'lead' | 'antagonist' | 'support'` (`src/core/types.ts:19`) — and *every* layer requires all three: greenlight validator (`actions.ts:391-411` throws on an absent id), casting-slate law (`castingSessions.ts:121-140`, two candidates per slot, ≥3 distinct people), reception (`reception.ts:42, 236-251, 496-507` iterate `CAST_SLOTS`), Standing (`standing.ts:77-79` mean of three fames), rival packaging (`hollywoodTick.ts:148-150` requires `actors.length === 3`), save validators (`save.ts:1587-1601`). `FilmConcept.requiredSlots` is read by exactly one display roll-up (`filmPackage.ts:286`) and cannot make a smaller film legal. **A two-character film is not legal in any year today.**

**Original-game precedent (CONFIRMED).** The ancestor capped named acting seats at three for every era and script source; capability grew only through script-office tier and never raised the cap; the one expansion added a new role *tier* (stuntmen), not more leads (§2, rows 12–13). Direction T is therefore OWNER-DIRECTION SUCCESSOR DESIGN with no retail precedent, but it matches the ancestor's "capacity grows with studio capability, never required" pattern.

**Cost analysis (HIGH on arithmetic, MEDIUM on value ranking).** With `n` named principal seats and `f` featured/cameo entries: audition reads `2n`; salary per added mid actor ≈ $229k (`25,000 + 150,000·s² + 600,000·f²`, `worldgen.ts:152-167`) or ≈ $585k for a star; relationship pairs `n(n+1)/2` with director = 6 → 10 → 15 → 21 for n = 3 → 4 → 5 → 6; rival billing permutations if optional seats were permuted 6 → 24 → 120 → 720; save Δ ≈ +0.6 KB per added seat per film (participants stored twice) — save size is **not** the binding constraint (even +1.8 KB × 400 films ≈ 0.7 MB against a 37.8 MB Hollywood root); the binding constraint is **type surface**: widening the `CastSlot` union touches every `Record<CastSlot,…>` (~96 core + 53 non-test UI lines, e2e golden manifests, Save V19/projection 29), whereas an additive optional array touches ~8 display sites and leaves golden fixtures byte-identical.

## 14. Celebrity cameo model (Direction U)

Sources: `evidence/05` §5, `models/M6` §14, engine locators in `evidence/02` §c.

**Engine fit (CONFIRMED, HIGH).** The engine already separates the two halves the Owner wants kept apart: *fame* enters reception only through `starDraw` (CAST_WEIGHT-weighted, Hill-saturated on opening reach when engaged, `reception.ts:493-507`) and `starAttention` (`standing.ts:77-79`); *skill* enters through `castSlotExecution = 0.6·effectiveSkill(acting) + 0.4·100·roleFit` (`talentSummary.ts:304-324`). Fame drives money only through `salaryCurve` at quote time (`employment.ts:206-291`). The ancestor withheld all Star benefits from Stars in non-lead slots and gave extras zero fame value (§2 row 14) — Direction U is net-new, and no shipped tycoon comparator has a cameo class (`03d` §10, `05` §7).

**Recommended transparent split.**

- **FAME VALUE** (publicity, opening-only, awareness): rank every cast member (principals + cameos) by fame; `publicityPool = Σ_k fameReach(fame_k) · CAMEO_RANK_DECAY^(k−1) · segmentFit_k` with `fameReach = f/(f+50)` (the existing Hill helper, `economy.ts:17-20`) and `CAMEO_RANK_DECAY ≈ 0.5` (starting point). Enters **only** the opening-only terms (`starDrawOpening`/`segmentAppealOpening`) and `starAttention` — never the linear `starDraw` that shapes legs. With decay 0.5 the pool is bounded at **≤2× the top name** however many names are added (4th name ≤12.5% of the first) — the Prima "each subsequently rated Star contributes a lower percentage" law made explicit.
- **PERFORMANCE VALUE** (craft): the cameo enters `castExecution` at `CAST_WEIGHT.cameo = 0.10` (vs support 0.35) using the unchanged `castSlotExecution`, so a famous non-actor is judged by acting skill and persona fit like anyone else, at small weight; cameos are **excluded from the cohesion centroid** (`ROLE_WEIGHT`) — a two-minute appearance must not move the film's delivered expression. Worked: three principals executing at 70 + a fame-90 non-actor executing at 25 → castExecution 70.0 → 67.8, craft −0.44, criticMean ≈ −0.29; opening publicity +fameReach(90)·0.5^(k−1) = up to +0.643 if the cameo is the most famous name. Small, legible, opposite-signed — the Beckham/Tyson/Rihanna pattern.

**The seven questions.** (1) Fame raises initial awareness/publicity — yes, and only that. (2) Acting skill still determines craft contribution — yes, at low weight. (3) Workload — one production week, no audition, no casting-facility occupancy, no rehearsal; modelled as a **booking** that marks the person `busy` (`busyTalentIds`) for that week. (4) Salary — fame already dominates `salaryCurve` (`600,000·f²`); a cameo fee is a **fraction** of the fame-priced salary (`CAMEO_FEE_FRACTION ≈ 0.35`, starting point), never a flat fee, so publicity is bought at a price that scales with publicity delivered; owned by P14 (fee law) and P11 (cash/committed cost) — a provisional fee-law suggestion, not part of Direction R's indirect consequences and not a P17 formula. (5) Poor acting can damage quality — slightly and legibly, via `CAST_WEIGHT.cameo`; optionally a presentation-only review note. (6) Genre/audience fit matters on the fame side via `segmentFit_k` (overlap of the person's audience profile with the film's segment shares — for non-actor professions P14 supplies a small profession→segment affinity table); off-segment cameos buy little publicity and still risk the craft hit (the King Arthur/Beckham pattern). (7) Spam is prevented by **opportunity cost, not caps**: rank-decayed publicity; fee ∝ `600k·f²` per name against value that halves per rank (by the third famous cameo the player pays the same fee for ~11% of the first cameo's value); scheduling cost (a booked star cannot lead a rival's or the player's own picture that week; contracted talent consumes a production credit); plus the structural bound that Featured is an array ≤2 (an era capacity fact, not a rule).

**Dominant-strategy checks (§16).** "Sign ten celebrities to every film" — NOT VIABLE: marginal `starDrawOpening` gain falls 100% → 28% → 11% → 5% → 2% for cameos 1–5 while the fee is flat per person. "Famous non-actors dominate trained actors" — NOT VIABLE: weight 0.10, excluded from cohesion, judged on skill×fit. "Cameo-spam is free publicity" — NOT VIABLE: fee never free; pool bounded at ≤2× the top name.

**Prerequisites and ownership.** Non-actor celebrities (musicians, athletes, TV personalities) need a **P14 profession/audience-profile addition** — `CreativeRole = writer|director|actor|craft` (`types.ts:18`) has no such kind; the D-9 24-skill profiles already let such a person carry low acting skill and high fame without any `Talent` type change. Role classes PRINCIPAL/SUPPORTING/FEATURED/CAMEO as credit categories, fame, availability, fee law → **P14**; era availability of the Featured class → **P13**; publicity pool and `CAST_WEIGHT.cameo` in reception → **P07**; awareness consumption → **P08**; segment demand → **P15**; cash/ROI → **P11**; rival parity (same fee, same busy law, greedy fill) → **P12**; crossover/franchise context (a franchise lead cameoing in a spin-off; "legacy cameo" milestone) → **P17 later scope**, flavour only — **a cameo never counts as a major franchise association** (Direction F guard); TV/streaming guest appearances → **P18**.

**Worked greenlight card** (`M6` §14.9): a drama with three principals considering a fame-85 / acting-20 / off-genre musician cameo: publicity 37.2 → 41.6 (+4.4, "off-genre fit reduces the gain"); cast-execution 70.7 → 68.4 (−2.3); critic ≈ −0.3; fee ≈ $164k; one busy week; permanent `'cameo'` credit; cohesion unchanged; franchise effect none. Four lines answer "is this cameo worth $164k".

## 15. Rival-franchise behaviour (Direction K)

Source: [`models/M5`](models/M5-cast-continuity-recasting-rivals-early-greenlight.md) Parts 2–3; engine `evidence/02` §e.

**Hard dependency (D1).** Rivals have **no rights model** today — nothing in `src/` mentions rights or property, `RivalBusiness` carries no StoryProperty reference. P16 must give rivals the same `StoryProperty` ownership record before any rival continuation, SubProperty or crossover is implementable. This is stated as a P16 prerequisite, not worked around.

**Compact rule.** One new policy scalar — `policy.version: 2 = { …v1 keys, continuationAppetite: 0..1 }` — seeded per studio from the authored manifest like `affinities` (some sequel-happy, some original-focused). In `hollywoodTick.ts` `decide()` step 2 (`:175-209`, today a genre roulette): filter the rival's owned properties by `momentum ≥ threshold && fatigue ≤ ceiling && free capacity && legalContinuationTypes(...) nonempty`; roll `continuationAppetite`; pick the property by `momentum − fatigue`, the type by genre affinity within the **shared** legality matrix, build the continuation inputs (the same optional field the player's greenlight uses), and score through the **unchanged** `chooseIndustryPackage` (`hollywoodPolicy.ts:19-66`) — no second forecast engine, no winning-film oracle. Rival releases resolve through the same `resolveReception`/`buildFilmResult`/`updateStanding`/`applyReleaseCareers` chain and update the same R/M/F. Register/validator/migration: `hollywoodValidation.ts:204-211` widens under `version === 2`; because `hollywood` is a validated leaf of the accepted save version, the widening rides the **same governed next-version step** as the Franchise root (one version step, not two; no number assumed — §10); id-keyed continuation references join the identity walks.

**Early bets.** When a rival's most recent installment is committed but unreleased, the rival may commission early at `continuationAppetite × EARLY_BET_SCALE(0.3)` per decision week (a 0.6-appetite rival ≈ 18%/week) — rare enough to stay a visible player choice, real enough that a rival can be seen committing to "Property Y, Part 3" while Part 2 underperforms (public projection `bridge/industry.ts`, "public facts only"). Rivals only ever read properties **they own** (one current rights holder per property), so no rival decision can read a player's private data — closed structurally, not by a permission check.

**Symmetry checks.** Self-limiting spam: a rival that repeats mediocre continuations raises its own Fatigue under the same law until the candidate filter drops the property. Early-bet stacking is bounded by capacity. Rights-squatting by production activity is impossible (blocking requires ownership, a P16 fact). No comparator offers a copyable rival-franchise precedent (`03a`–`03e` all silent); the rule above is original design on P12's bounded-policy architecture.

## 16. Dominant-strategy findings

**Method.** Two independent adversarial reviewers attacked the consolidated design after the models were written: [`redteam/R1-minmaxer-attack.md`](redteam/R1-minmaxer-attack.md) (economic min-maxer; drives the synthesis's own calculator, `R1_verify.py`, through 20-release spam chains, park-and-revive, promotion laundering and rival asymmetry) and [`redteam/R2-systems-ux-attack.md`](redteam/R2-systems-ux-attack.md) (state rot, double counting, information law, legibility, cross-model contradictions). Their findings were then re-run against the reviewer's corrected calculator [`redteam/R3-reviewer-corrections-calc.py`](redteam/R3-reviewer-corrections-calc.py) (output in [`redteam/R3-output.txt`](redteam/R3-output.txt)). **The red team found two real root-cause defects in the synthesis as written; both are closed by the corrections adopted in §5.** Revision 02 added Probe 6 (branch limits and fatigue laundering), which found a third defect in revision 01's own design (per-continuity Fatigue), closed by C3 revised. Verdicts below are for the design **after** those corrections.

**Root causes found (before correction).** (A) Recognition ratcheted with *release count*, not quality: the peak-anchored kernel's residual sum plus time inflation drove Recognition from 52 to the 100 cap on eight mediocre (0.9×-forecast) releases, and the durable floor with it — the GDM monotonic-ratchet failure the atlas rejects. (B) Fatigue never touched the cash-facing awareness channel — it lived only in the forecast-bar multiplier, so a cash-maximising player could ignore it entirely (forcing F from 0 to 500 left the awareness input bit-identical). R2 additionally found the reboot branch seeded at F = 0 in the calculator (three incompatible model-level positions), `continuationType`/`similarity` as an unvalidated free input, and an ill-defined tie-break in the cameo rank decay.

**Corrections adopted (all "smallest natural", none a cap or timer):** C1 quality-gate Recognition contributions (`gateR(q) = clamp((q − 0.5)/0.25, 0, 1)`); C2 Fatigue discounts the shared awareness input (`× (1 − 0.5·min(F,100)/100)`) and the separate forecast multiplier is dropped; C3 (**revised in revision 02**) Fatigue is one scalar per StoryProperty — revision 01's `γ = 0.2` reboot seed is retired because Probe 6 showed any per-continuity read is laundered by rotating sequels across existing continuities; C4 the remake comparison is a discount on the same input (`0.5·q_ref·2^(−age/260 wk)`), and C4′ the reboot's partial term (0.4×, recent-and-strong reference only) is executed (Case R2); C5 the Fatigue founding exemption applies only to the StoryProperty's first-ever film (a reboot's or spin-off's founding film accrues at its own similarity, 0.6 / 0.5); C6 `continuationType` is **derived** from objective greenlight facts (same branch → sequel family; new branch citing prior branches and no SubProperty → reboot; cites a SubProperty → spin-off; cites exactly one prior film and no new branch → remake), never a free label; C7 the cameo publicity pool is the additive rank-decayed sum with an explicit billing-order tie-break (so identical-fame cameos still decay and a low-fame cameo cannot lower it). All seven paper histories still behave as required under C1–C5 (R3 output: Case B's property Fatigue 0.00 throughout and its spin-off SubProperty at 0.64; Case D flips COOLING on Fatigue alone with Momentum flat; Case E R 83.7 → 29.3 with the floor binding at 20 years; Case F R 29.3 → 83.3; Case G Recognition now **unchanged** at 29.3, Momentum −36 and the property's Fatigue 36.6 read by the old continuity too — a failed reboot adds no recognition and fatigues the property).

**What the paper cases do and do not show (bound of the evidence).** The seven histories, Cases R/R2 and Probes 1–6 demonstrate that *those* histories behave as the direction requires and that *those* attacks are not viable. They do **not** show that every profitable strategy is balanced, nor that the production and market interactions are. Untested, and to be settled at implementation reconnaissance and tuning rather than assumed from this report: (i) **actual production lead times** — every case scripts the release week (Case B's spin-off is greenlit at week 91 and released at week 92); in the engine a production takes months, the locked read (greenlight) and the live read (release) diverge by exactly that lead time, and Momentum's 24-week half-life means the lead time itself decides how much of a hit's tailwind a continuation can capture; (ii) **the rising bar** — every case holds `expectedTotal` flat at $150M, whereas the engine's forecast rises with inherited awareness, so a continuation's `comparator` and Momentum are measured against a higher number than the paper shows (Momentum from a merely-good sequel will be lower in-engine); (iii) marketing spend and `efficientMarketingCapacity`; (iv) P15 crowding and `competitionFactor`; (v) P14 fame co-movement (a franchise lead's fame and salary rising across installments) and the cost side generally — cash, committed cost, loans and net worth; (vi) Standing's `audienceAwareness` stacking with the P17 input; (vii) rival competition for the same talent and release windows; (viii) the shape of P07's actual critic/audience distribution (57/55 and 85/80 are assumptions); (ix) the forecast's own error band; (x) crossovers (one real case). ## 20. Remaining uncertainties

1. **P16 does not exist as a charter.** Every P17 identity claim rests on a producer that has not been designed; rivals have no rights model at all (`src/` grep: zero hits) — Directions K, L and O are unimplementable until P16 gives rivals `StoryProperty` ownership.
2. **P14 lifecycle facts** (retired/deceased) do not exist and are **not a P17 dependency** — retirement/mortality are unselected systems; the LEGACY carve-out (§11) is an optional refinement and the default rule is ordinary recast. A profession/audience-profile field for famous non-actors does not exist either; that one *is* a prerequisite of Direction U (§14, §19.5).
3. **Threading the P17 optional input into both the forecast and reception paths** must be verified at implementation reconnaissance: the forecast's `computeBoxOffice` call (`forecast.ts:468-478`) does not currently pass every late-bound optional factor that reception applies (e.g. `setNoveltyFactor` at `reception.ts:813`), so a P17 field needs deliberate plumbing in both places for the "same formula, two read times" property to hold.
4. **All constants are uncalibrated starting points** (half-lives, the Recognition quality gate 0.50/0.25, satiation gate width 0.10, floor 0.35, similarity buckets — `SIMILARITY.reboot` 0.60 being the one that governs the only remaining branch-pattern residual —, the 0.8/0.2 awareness blend, the 0.5·F discount, the remake term 0.5/260 wk and the reboot partial term 0.4/416 wk/0.65, `N_MAX_OPEN_CONTINUITIES` 3 with `DORMANT_WEEKS` 150, `CAMEO_RANK_DECAY`, `CAST_WEIGHT.cameo`, fee fraction, transfer fractions 0.38–0.65, crossover decay 0.6, `EARLY_BET_SCALE` 0.3, association weights and caps). They were fitted to the shape of the evidence, not to in-engine reception distributions; co-tuning with P14's fame swings and with `Standing.audienceAwareness` stacking is untested.
5. **The quality blend** (0.5 critic / 0.5 audience) is unvalidated against real critic–audience divergence (comedy vs prestige franchises).
6. **Two academic studies disagree on the parent→sequel gap effect** (B&C 2008 vs H-T 2009); Sood & Drèze 2006 and B&C 2008 were read at abstract level only; Elberse 2007 via an HBS summary (403 on the primary). Tuning must not assume a strong gap effect.
