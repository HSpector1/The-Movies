# P16 Merged Ruleset — Judge-and-Merge of Three Candidate Lenses

## Cover

**What this is.** This document is the Phase-2 judged merge of three independent candidate rulesets — `candidate-legibility.md` (optimized for the smallest set of rules a player/builder can hold in their head), `candidate-integrity.md` (optimized for exact fit against accepted code seams and approved package law), and `candidate-adversary.md` (optimized for exploit-first design: write the abuse, then the smallest natural guard). It is not a fourth independent pass; every rule below already survived one candidate author's evidence check, and this pass additionally cross-checks candidates against each other and, in one material case, against the assignment text itself.

**Method.** `candidate-legibility.md` is the base document: it scored highest on the combination of Owner-direction fidelity, legibility, and completeness against the 21-part deliverable, and its section-by-section prose is reused as the narrative spine below. Rules, refinements, and corrections from `candidate-integrity.md` (exact code-seam discipline, line-referenced sourcing) and `candidate-adversary.md` (the twelve-exploit-family anti-snowball treatment, the richer bankruptcy-disposition model) are grafted in explicitly, with every disagreement among the three stated and resolved by name in the relevant section rather than silently blended.

**Scope discipline, unchanged from all three candidates.** Assignment §2 items A–T are treated as Owner-selected and are not reopened. Where a selected rule collides with existing accepted code or approved documentation, the exact collision and the smallest correction are stated rather than designed around silently — this is a hard rule of the task, not a style choice. All dollar figures anywhere in this document are PAPER HYPOTHESES for illustration only, never tuning recommendations. Confidence labels (HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW; CONFIRMED/QUALIFIED/CORRECTED/SUPERSEDED BY OWNER DIRECTION) and the INFERENCE label on every recommendation are carried forward from the source dossiers exactly as the candidates applied them.

**One correction made during the merge itself.** `candidate-legibility.md` attributes its willingness-state labels ("Not for Sale / Would Listen / Open to Offers / In Distress") to "Assignment §2.L's own suggested labels." A direct grep of `ASSIGNMENT.md` for these strings returns zero hits — §2.L names *factors* (enterprise valuation, control premium, strategic value, recent performance, independence preference, financial outlook, existing ownership situation), not label text. The label set actually attested in the evidence base is `NotForSale | Reluctant | Open | Willing` (`00-KEY-FINDINGS.md:576`, drawn from `09-valuation-finance.md`'s willingness-state synthesis), which is what `candidate-integrity.md` and `candidate-adversary.md` both independently used. This merge adopts the correctly-sourced label set (§12, R24) and flags the misattribution as a correction (§19) rather than propagating it.

## 3. StoryProperty and minimal-rights model

**The split, and why it's needed.** A released Film and its underlying Story Property are two different things, exactly as real copyright law treats a film as a derivative work whose copyright never absorbs the underlying material (10-rights-law.md, U.S. Copyright Office Circular 14, HIGH/CONFIRMED). Project: Studio has no such split today: `FilmIdentity.studioId` means *creator*, validated against historical employment, and must never be read as *current owner* (04-code-audit.md, HIGH/CONFIRMED). The engine already tells studio-original from externally-sourced material apart at the screenplay layer — `isOriginalScreenplay(blueprint)` and `ScreenplayProvenanceView.origin: 'original'|'pool'` (04-code-audit.md, HIGH/NEW, `screenplay.ts:305-345`) — and that seam is the natural, already-built anchor for the origin distinction; no new provenance vocabulary is needed. The accepted contract already assigns P16 sole authority to mint "each property/library identity ... explicitly by exact ID; it may not infer or bulk-mint" (02-authority-corporate.md, HIGH/CONFIRMED, consumer contract §15).

**Mint point: at Greenlight.** All three candidates converge on this (not commission, which the accepted code does not key on; not release, which would leave every cancelled production without a legible property). This freezes provenance before any acquisition can move a production, guarantees every released film has a property by the time P17 needs one, and leaves a cancelled/shelved production as a legible "unproduced property" rather than an orphan. This is an INFERENCE, not a settled Owner decision; the exact mint point is listed as a remaining uncertainty (§21), not a genuine Owner decision, because two of three candidates present it as a confident recommendation rather than an open question.

**Minimal rights bundle — four kinds, one instrument.** All three candidates independently converge on exactly this bundle, and every real deal researched (Disney/Marvel, Sony/Spider-Man, Lucas/Fox, DreamWorks/Soros) reduces cleanly to it with no finer slicing ever load-bearing (10-rights-law.md; 08-realworld-ma.md, HIGH/CONFIRMED across nine cases):

| Right | Covers | Transferable? |
|---|---|---|
| **R1 PROPERTY** | The underlying Story Property itself, including the inherent right to make sequels/remakes/spinoffs (continuation authority is a *facet* of owning R1, not a separate ownable thing — like owning a house includes the right to renovate it) | Sellable, single holder |
| **R2 FILM/LIBRARY** | Ownership of one released work, tradeable independently of the property that spawned it | Sellable, single holder, separable from R1 |
| **R3 CONTINUATION** | Sequel/remake/spinoff authority, licensed *out* of R1 without selling the property (Sony/Spider-Man: perpetual so long as a film releases at least every 5.75 years) | Licensable only, never sold apart from R1 |
| **R4 CROSS-MEDIA** | Future television/cross-media exploitation, execution owned by P18 | Licensable only, **forbidden to grant before P18 exists** |

A single generic **Licence** instrument — `{subject{kind,id}, facet, grantor, grantee, exclusive, startWeek, termWeeks, consideration, reversion/useItOrLoseIt?, status}` — covers R3 and (later, by reference only) R4 and P13's own sketched technology-rights slice. No territory, no media sub-splits, no window slicing: this is "one instrument over four," not four separate right-types each with their own UI.

**Exploit guards (adversary lens, adopted in full — R03, R04):**
- **Duplicate ownership.** Every right has a single-holder field plus an **append-only `ownershipEvents` log**, validator-enforced so a transfer's `from` must equal the current holder or the transaction is refused outright. This reuses the roadmap's own reserved `ownershipEvents` root ("P16+ only," 03-authority-domains.md) and matches the CK3 title model researched independently: identity never rewritten, holder changes append (07-comparators-C.md, MEDIUM/CONFIRMED).
- **Sell-and-keep-using.** No R1 sale or exclusive R3 grant while an active production or `TheatricalRun` sits on the property, unless the production transfers with it or the seller's retained licence is recorded on the sale receipt. Mad Games Tycoon 2 ships this exact primitive ("no IP being traded while a product is on sale," 10-rights-law.md, HIGH/NEW) and DreamWorks/Soros shows the encumbered-sale alternative (distribution kept as a disclosed 5-year exclusive licence, 08-realworld-ma.md, HIGH/NEW).

**Structural note.** StoryProperty and ownership-event rows are minted and appended **lazily** — one at a time, at greenlight or first external purchase or first transaction — never a bulk migration pass across every historical film. This satisfies the "explicit by exact ID, never bulk-mint" contract requirement and, as a side effect, keeps the ownership root from becoming a second save-bloat vector (§17, R32).

## 4. Film-library value

**What the original never had.** The retail game's only "value" concept, Studio Rating's Capital term, was literal cash in the bank; the closest thing to a library term — "Movies 24%" — explicitly **decays** ("a town with a staggeringly short memory," Prima p.46) rather than accumulating (01-original-game.md, HIGH/CORRECTED). Pre-release press promised VHS/DVD "further royalties," cut before ship (01-original-game.md, HIGH/NEW). Library value is entirely successor design; nothing here is original parity.

**What accepted law already forbids.** A released film's economic life ends the week its locked `TheatricalRun` completes; there is no re-release, no home-media window, and P11 explicitly has no depreciation/asset-valuation authority (03-authority-domains.md, HIGH/CONFIRMED). Assignment §2.C already anticipates this directly: "Do not invent perpetual weekly library cash before an owning distribution/media system actually exists."

**Rule.** Library value is a **valuation-only** line, never a cash flow: a formula-versioned, on-demand-computed "Library (estimated)" figure fed into Studio Valuation (§14) and nowhere else — never the weekly ledger, never Standing/Power Ranking (which already explicitly exclude cash/valuation, 09-valuation-finance.md, HIGH/CONFIRMED), and no royalty income until a P18 distribution/media system exists to own that cash flow. Count each owned R1 once (sequel potential) and each owned R2 once (catalog value) — never both toward the same figure, closing a double-counting seam before it opens (see §14). Comparator evidence agrees emphatically: Amazon/MGM, Miramax/Netflix, and The Executive's home-entertainment deals all gate library cash behind a distribution channel that does not yet exist here (08-realworld-ma.md; 06-comparators-B.md).

**Shelved properties (adversary addition, adopted).** An unproduced or shelved Story Property retains ownership permanently and, by default, valuelessly — the original already modeled exactly this state (burying a bad film in the Archive to protect prestige, 01-original-game.md, HIGH/NEW). There is no abandon-for-refund path. A small, optional flat valuation credit for unproduced properties is a reasonable future refinement (it prevents an owner from "hiding" a property at zero valuation cost while still holding it hostage from a buyer) but is not required for v1 and is not a rule below.

## 5. Acquisition operating model (A/B/C/D against the six criteria)

The Owner's brief names Model D — choose Absorb Completely or Absorb Operations + Retain Brand as Label at closing — as the current Future Ops recommendation. A gap-fill pass confirmed no prior Future Ops control document actually contains that label; it traces to the assignment text itself (14-gap-future-ops-control-board-verification.md, HIGH/CONFIRMED) but is independently and heavily corroborated by comparator evidence and by a code-level six-criteria table:

| Criterion | A — Full Absorption | B — Autonomous Subsidiary | C — Absorb + Retain Label | D — Owner Choice (A or C) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Player comprehension | High (one outcome) | Low — MGT2/Capitalism Lab/Software Inc. community verdict: confusing, tedious | High (GearCity's "marque" is the clean precedent) | Highest — Capitalism Lab's one-click `[Acquire]`/`[Acquire and Merge]` fork is the shipped comparator |
| Historical identity | Preserved via dated event | Preserved but reads as a second business | Preserved and visibly credited | Player's choice of visibility |
| Management burden | LOW **once H5 ships** | HIGH, unconditionally — the fix that rescues A/C/D structurally cannot apply to B | LOW **once H5 ships** | LOW **once H5 ships**; menu excludes B by construction |
| Gameplay value | Prize = properties/knowledge/roster | Same prize plus an ongoing management minigame no comparator's players enjoyed | Prize + a retained-brand credit/asset-sale surface | Best of A/C, player's choice |
| AI/rival compatibility | HIGH once H5 ships | Fails unconditionally — a second full AI loop per subsidiary, which no comparator ships without becoming "immovable objects" | HIGH once H5 ships | HIGH once H5 ships |
| Save/performance | Bounded (one business record retired) | Unbounded — compounds an already-exceeded save-growth budget (49.6MB at week 8791) | Bounded | Bounded |

**H5, the single blocking fact — the report's central structural finding, independently reached by all three candidates.** Every entered rival must have a live `RivalBusiness` that ticks unconditionally forever; `hollywoodValidation.ts:331` hard-requires exactly one live business per entered rival identity with no "operations ended" state, and `hollywoodTick.ts:221-288` has no conditional branch to skip one (HIGH/CONFIRMED, re-verified independently across four separate dossiers — 04, 11, 13, 16). This single fact governs Models A, C, and D equally: all three need one new terminal-business state before they can reach LOW management burden / HIGH AI-compatibility. Model B's own premise (the business keeps operating) means the fix can never rescue it — Model B fails structurally and unconditionally, not merely by comparison.

**Rule.** At the moment a whole-company acquisition closes, the buyer makes exactly one choice, once: **Absorb Completely** (target's identity folds in with no visible brand) or **Absorb Operations, Retain Brand as Label** (target's `StudioId`, name, mark, and founding date remain visible on the buyer's own releases as a credited label, with zero independent roster, ledger, lot, or ongoing business). No third choice, no changing the choice later, and a retained label never becomes a second managed studio.

## 6. Exact acquisition transfer bundle

Answering assignment §2.F's eight numbered questions directly, grounded in accepted code's own primitives:

**1) Does acquired cash become buyer cash?** Yes, at par, including if negative — negative cash is already a legal, recoverable state, never bankruptcy (03-authority-domains.md, HIGH/CONFIRMED). This needs exactly one new typed ledger movement per side (accepted `LedgerKind`/`RivalMoneyKind` are closed unions that must be additively widened, correlated by one transaction id). Price is always quoted **net of** the cash the target carries. GearCity transfers "cash funds" as part of its shipped bundle and Amazon/MGM priced net-of-cash on an $8.45B deal (05-comparators-A.md; 08-realworld-ma.md). **Guard (adversary lens):** pick this one convention and never mix it with a seller-keeps-cash convention inside the same transaction kind — mixing conventions is exactly the ambiguity that forced OpenTTD's maintainers to patch partial-ownership cash handling as an "infinite money" exploit (07-comparators-C.md, issue #9281).

**2) Does debt transfer?** There is no debt instrument in Project: Studio to transfer — no loans, no interest, no credit object of any kind, and P11-REQ-041 (loans) is explicitly OWNER-BLOCKED (03-authority-domains.md, HIGH/CONFIRMED). What *does* exist and *does* transfer are **typed obligations**: the target's remaining contract guarantees and any committed-but-unspent production cost. "Assumption purchase" (§13) means the buyer takes on these obligations, not a nonexistent loan. If the Owner ever authorizes a P11 debt instrument, it slots into this same "obligations" row without new P16 machinery — this is the report's first flagged structural collision (§ Structural Problems).

**3) Do employee contracts transfer intact?** Yes, structurally: end the target's employment row, open an identical new one under the buyer with a new lawful reason (`assumed`, alongside the existing `entry|renewal|replacement|expiry|termination|player-contract` enum), and copy `Contract.signingBonus` **without** re-debiting it — the accepted per-employment signing-bonus ledger cross-check needs an explicit exemption for this reason code. `ContractId` cannot be mutated in place because it embeds the employer's `StudioId` (`<studioId>:contract:<talentId>:<week>[...]`); a new contract row under the new employer, cross-referenced by a `fromStudioId→toStudioId` receipt, is the only lawful shape. P12's own design authority already names "sale/assignment" as a lawful terminator of exclusive contracts — this reuses a pre-existing legal category rather than inventing one.

**4) Can the player release inherited staff afterward under ordinary termination law?** Yes — no new rule needed. Inherited staff are ordinary contracted employees under the buyer from the moment of transfer, subject to existing law: pay 50% of the remaining guaranteed salary, refused while mid-screenplay (`employment.ts:172-180`, HIGH/CONFIRMED). This priced, symmetric exit is exactly the kind of natural integration friction real deals and comparators show (TWC re-offered contracts to about two-thirds of staff; Hollywood Animal allows partial-cost-recovery early termination).

**5) What happens to active movies?** They transfer and continue under the buyer, who may finish or cancel each at its ordinary cancellation cost. Because rival productions are not container-bound to a physical set the way a player production is, the transfer is a **re-formation**: mint a new `ProductionWorkflow` under the buyer preserving the original `productionId`/`conceptId`/participants/remaining-work state, plus one ledger-witness row for the transfer itself. Disney/Fox inherited an in-progress slate, finishing some titles and cancelling the rest at a real, documented $170M write-down (08-realworld-ma.md, HIGH/NEW) — inherited-production write-downs are a legitimate anti-snowball cost, not a bug to patch away.

**6) What happens to target facilities if only one lot is actively managed?** Nothing physical ever appears on the buyer's lot — there is nothing to move. Rival studios today own no buildings at all; they hold four abstract, once-paid capacity rows (`development|stage|scenery|post`) with no blueprint, tier, or footprint (03-authority-domains.md, HIGH/CONFIRMED). At closing, that abstract capacity converts automatically to a one-time cash credit using the exact liquidation rate a player already gets for demolishing a real building (0.5× facility capex, 0.35× for sets, always strictly `<1`). This is Owner option 1 (automatic liquidation) applied to an asset class that, for rival targets, is already trivially liquid — see the structural note in §7.

**7) What exactly does acquired research grant?** Verified, completed knowledge only — never an installed building. The buyer receives a new technology-adoption row at "ready to adopt," citing the target's original provenance, for every technology the target had *finished* researching. The buyer must still lawfully build and pay for the physical facility body before operating that capability. In-flight (not-yet-completed) research does **not** transfer; P13-OD-06 already states in-flight work "never moves across technologies or studios" (03-authority-domains.md, HIGH/CONFIRMED) — this ruleset does not ask the Owner to reopen that, though a one-time successor exception remains a genuine open question (§20). Capitalism Lab independently validates the same separation from the opposite direction: technology-dependent firm types are explicitly non-transferable.

**8) What happens when target knows a higher physical tier than the buyer owns?** For rival targets today this scenario is structurally moot: rivals hold abstract capacity with no tier field, so there is no "higher physical installation" to compare. The only thing that ever travels is the *knowledge* answer from Q7 — the buyer becomes eligible to build Tier III once it has lawfully paid to build it, exactly as if it had researched Tier III itself. This is stated as general law now so that any future rival-facility/rival-lot system inherits the rule rather than reopening it.

## 7. Physical property

Rivals own no buildings or lot today. Given §6.Q6/Q8, the answer is simple because there is currently nothing to manage: **automatic liquidation at closing** (Owner option 1), using the existing lossy demolition-credit formula, is both the correct rule today and the one that requires zero new mechanics. Options 2 (player chooses what to sell) and 4 (transferable equipment) have no substrate to attach to — there is no per-body equipment inventory in accepted code; the `ConversionRequirementDescriptor.installedEquipment` field this would need does not exist yet, so option 4 is named as a future refinement, not built now. Option 3 (remote property as a pure financial asset) is functionally identical to option 1 for rival targets today, since the "asset" already has no operations attached, but is the correct fallback the day rival capacity gains a real capex/tier model (converting it to cash immediately at that point would be premature liquidation of a genuinely valuable asset). Option 5 (a future remote-lot system) is explicitly not required now. Land purchase does not exist at all yet (P09-REQ-035 LATER), so "land" in the transfer bundle is simply not priced until a land market ships.

**Rule.** At closing, all of a target's physical/capacity assets convert once, automatically, to a cash credit at the standard lossy liquidation rate. Nothing is placed on the buyer's lot. If the target's brand is retained as a label (§5), the label carries no physical footprint either. No comparator ever made buildings or lots the prize of an acquisition — Story Properties, library rights, and knowledge were always the target.

## 8. Active productions

Answering the Owner's four alternatives directly: (1) active projects transfer and finish under the buyer — **selected**; (2) the buyer additionally gets an ordinary cancel option at the normal write-off cost, so "transfer and finish" and "buyer chooses" are the same rule, not two, and every comparator that inherited a catalogue found the *triage itself* to be the interesting one-time decision, not ongoing oversight; (3) no separate "acquisition-transition owner" state is needed — the re-formation in §6.Q5 lands the production directly under the buyer at the closing week; (4) bankruptcy handles active productions differently, as a distinct per-project auction lot (§13), because a bankrupt estate has no operating buyer to hand a live production to.

**Rule.** Every inherited production keeps its original `productionId`/`conceptId` forever — no duplicate identity is ever minted for the same work, matching every real-world case researched (RKO, Orion, Relativity, TWC each kept finished/in-progress films historically identifiable even as ownership moved). The buyer may cancel an inherited production at the same write-off cost any studio pays to cancel its own work; there is no special "inherited discount" and no special penalty. Re-parenting is feasible only as a re-formation on the buyer's lot with a ledger-witness row, never a raw container move — the placed-facility greenlight-ledger evidence invariant requires exactly one production row at `week===startTick` and cannot silently accept two possible weeks.

## 9. Historical identity/status model

The three worked examples in the Owner's brief — RKO's bankruptcy closure, Warner Brothers' acquisition-with-brand, and United Artists' merger/absorption-without-brand — read as three different mechanisms, but the correct model is **one mechanism with one boolean**, not three independently-coded status kinds.

**Disagreement resolved.** `candidate-integrity.md` and `candidate-adversary.md` both proposed three distinct event kinds (`closed | acquired | absorbed`). `candidate-legibility.md` proposed one event kind (`closed | acquired`) plus a `brandRetained` boolean, with "absorbed/merged" simply being `acquired` with `brandRetained: false`. This merge adopts **legibility's** version: Model D's closing choice (§5) already must persist exactly a brand-retained-or-not flag, so a three-kind status enum would store the same information as a second, redundant field. One event, one boolean, is the smaller correct model and it is what CK3's append-only holder-event log already validates for a different domain (07-comparators-C.md, MEDIUM/CONFIRMED, "identity never rewritten, holder changes append").

A single `CorporateEvent` record, keyed by the immutable `StudioId` (never reminted — 02-authority-corporate.md, HIGH/CONFIRMED), carries:

```
{ studioId, week, kind: 'closed' | 'acquired', successorStudioId?, brandRetained: boolean }
```

- **Closed** (RKO): `kind:'closed'`, no successor. This is P15's own settlement outcome and is explicitly never an alias for `acquired` (HIS-013, HIGH/CONFIRMED).
- **Acquired, brand kept** (Warner Brothers): `kind:'acquired'`, `successorStudioId` set, `brandRetained:true` — display reads "Acquired by Spector Pictures."
- **Merger/absorption, brand dropped** (United Artists): `kind:'acquired'`, `successorStudioId` set, `brandRetained:false` — display reads "Independent operations ended — Successor owner: Spector Pictures." This is exactly Disney's real "20th Century Studios" pattern (operations absorbed, "Fox" dropped, films still credit the original filmmakers) and matches the assignment's own third example almost verbatim.

**Rule.** No StudioId is ever deleted, renamed away, or reused. Every past film, employee credit, and award stays attributed to the studio that made it, forever, independent of who currently holds the property or the studio's brand (INT-011/INT-012, HIGH/CONFIRMED).

**Guard against migration backfill (adversary lens, adopted).** A save migration may record only what is *certain*, never invented — "existing player film ownership is derivable because only the player existed" (P12 §17) is a certain fact at world start, not fictional backfill, but that reasoning applies **only** to the single-player-existed case. A migration must never invent a rival-to-rival ownership event, a closure, or a distress state that the save's own history does not already contain (P15's own "old-save honesty" law).

## 10. Rival M&A

**No player-only superpower.** The same eligibility, pricing, cooldown, and floor rules apply whether the buyer is the player or a rival AI (02-authority-corporate.md, HIGH/CONFIRMED, "no isPlayer multiplier or exemption"). A rival can be a target, a buyer, or both over a campaign, and rival-to-rival transfers are actually **easier** to build than player-crossing acquisitions, not merely symmetric to them: both sides share identical `Production`/`ScriptDevelopment` shapes and rival facilities never bind to a placement ledger, so the single hardest identity-preservation constraint dossier 04 found for production re-parenting cannot arise for a rival-to-rival move at all (16-gap-rival-ai-acquisition-code-feasibility.md, HIGH/NEW).

**When an AI should bid.** A rival bids only when (a) it passes the same affordability check as the player (§17), (b) the target's price is at or below the rival's own private valuation of the target, and (c) the deal would not breach the active-rival floor (§17). This is the engine's **first-ever** cross-business read (no existing rival decision function reads another business's data today) and must be explicitly authorized rather than assumed to fall out of existing code — kept separate from the player-facing privacy projection in `bridge/industry.ts`.

**Frequency and consolidation.** Every comparator that let rivals buy freely and never fail became either a collecting exercise (MGT2's rivals are "immovable objects" that never go bankrupt) or a snowball (Hollywood Animal's cinema purchases: "you will practically destroy every other competitor"). This ruleset avoids both failure modes the same way: the active-rival floor (§17) is a **hard eligibility predicate**, not a soft preference.

**Guard — hostile takeover of the player.** Once symmetric law exists, the natural next step is "why can't a rival buy the player's studio the way the player can buy a rival's" — and Railway Empire shows exactly how badly this lands: "A competitor simply bought all of my shares and took over the business, and I simply failed the scenario!" (07-comparators-C.md, MEDIUM/CONFIRMED). OpenTTD explicitly forbids the reverse case in code. **The player studio is never an acquisition target inside P16A–C.** "Symmetric transaction law" means identical rules for any two *rival* studios; it does not mean the player's own studio is eligible to be bought. A player-initiated voluntary sale as a deliberate ending is a distinct, separate, not-yet-made Owner decision (§20).

## 11. Asset sales & exploit guards

**What is sellable.** Story Properties; Film/library rights; physical/equipment credits (already sellable via the existing demolition mechanic); a studio's retained brand/label, sold on its own for cash with zero operational transfer (§5, §12). "Technology/license rights where upstream ownership permits" stays exactly where P13 already parks it ("later commercialization scope") until the Owner separately charters it.

**One reused idiom for the smallest sales.** The original game's *only* IP transaction — instant sale of an unproduced script to "rival studios" for a disclosed, deterministic price (01-original-game.md, HIGH/CONFIRMED) — is kept for the smallest case: an unproduced Story Property may be sold instantly to "the open market" for a shown price. **Released films, whole libraries, and brands are never sold this way** — they require an actual named counterparty, because an anonymous instant-cash sale of a studio's whole history would not survive the "explain it to a friend" test the way "you sold an old unused script" already does.

**Exploit guards, in order of how much mechanism each costs:**

1. **Single holder, append-only log** (§3). No duplicate ownership is representable.
2. **Rights-in-use lock** (§3). No sale while an active production is using the asset without a retained/transferred licence.
3. **Holdback / vesting.** A newly acquired asset's full appraised value is reachable only after a fixed holdback window (paper: 52 weeks), rising from a lower fraction before that — a direct transposition of the original's own eight-year Star-tenure vesting rule. Resale during the window is capped at or below acquisition cost.
4. **Sub-cost price floor.** Every sale price is strictly below the asset's own build/acquisition cost basis absent a genuine competing-bid receipt, mirroring the demolition-credit invariant that is already `<1` by construction. This single reused rule is what stops a buy-low-sell-high loop from ever being profitable purely from ownership churn — GearCity's own flip exploits ("about 7 billion $ from just buying and selling companies") happened specifically because its acquisition cost could fall *below* company equity.
5. **Deterministic, shown price.** Every sellable asset shows its Estimated Value before any sale screen — no hidden multiplier a player must reverse-engineer.
6. **No guaranteed buyer (adversary addition, adopted).** AI bid/ask is anchored to the AI's own valuation with cash conservation enforced; a sale can simply find no taker. Without this guard, asset sales become a risk-free cash faucet — exactly Software Inc.'s own developer-admitted failure mode ("the calculation for deciding IP price was placeholder... it probably doesn't make sense in most cases") and Blockbuster Inc.'s disabled stake-dividend faucet.

No AI-memory or "haircut/premium" heuristic is added on top of these six; a rejected or completed sale offer instead leaves a dated cooldown on that exact counterparty/asset pair — unexplained instant re-offers read as arbitrary the way Anno 1800's unexplained AI retaliation does.

## 12. Healthy-studio acquisition

**Willingness model.** Every studio displays one public, typed **Willingness State** — `NotForSale | Reluctant | Open | Willing` — driven by disclosed inputs only: recent performance/Standing trend, an independence-preference trait, and any P15 condition fact if distressed (never by cash directly, since P15 already forbids inferring distress from cash<0). This label set corrects a misattribution in the legibility candidate (see Cover) and is directly comparator-validated: Football Manager's Desperate/Keen/Curious agent tones, TEW's consecutive-negative-months-plus-personality refusal right, and Big Ambitions' "estimated valuation ... seller can refuse" are the same shape shipped three separate times.

**Price.** Ask Price = Studio Valuation (§14) × a published **independence-premium** band keyed to the willingness state and recent target performance (premium scales with success, answering §2.O directly). GearCity's own shipped Takeover Price formula (evaluation share + a premium scaled by profitability — 1.75× for profitable targets vs. 1.3× for loss-makers) is the cleanest analogue. The band is a shown number next to a shown stance, never a hidden formula the player must discover.

**Naming correction, converged on by all three candidates.** What the assignment calls a "control premium" is, in a game where the buyer never gets to run the target as a going concern independently (Model D forecloses that), better named an **independence premium** — the price of talking a studio out of not wanting to sell, not the buyer's private synergy value. Damodaran's actual "value of control" is a *buyer-side* concept, typically *small* for a well-run target — the opposite of what a "premium scales with success" model needs. This ruleset uses "independence premium" throughout.

**Refusal has a reason and a cooldown.** `NotForSale` refuses every offer at any price, matching real boards (Disney rejected Comcast's 2004 bid "as too low" — price alone did not move it). Every other refusal states a plain typed reason ("offer below band," "stance is NotForSale"); a rejected offer starts a fixed cooldown before the same buyer may approach the same target again.

**Guard — rival-cash omniscience (adversary lens, adopted).** If the player's offer screen could read a rival's true internal cash, obligations, or forecast, every healthy acquisition becomes a solved information game. Exact rival cash, obligations, budgets, and forecasts are already HIDDEN by design in the accepted projection (`bridge/industry.ts` exposes only public facts). A valuation/offer screen must be priced from **public facts only**, or from an explicit new **due-diligence disclosure event** that reveals a bounded, dated snapshot only after an offer is formally opened — never a silent read of hidden internal state. This satisfies the Owner's "no opaque bargaining simulator" instruction from the *other* direction: the player sees a real number, but only after paying the cost of formally approaching the target.

**What the player sees before offering:** the target's public Studio Valuation, its willingness state and named reason, whether a rival bidder exists, and — once an offer is formally opened — the full due-diligence disclosure snapshot. The exact contents/timing of that disclosure remain a genuine open decision (§20).

## 13. Bankruptcy auction/acquisition

**The structural collision, stated plainly.** The assignment's own §2.M premise — "P15 may decide that a rival becomes bankrupt and its assets enter auction" — describes a step that, as approved, does not exist. P15's closure-settlement is written as *exhaustive*: every touched category resolves (cancelled/settled) or persists only as unowned history; the word "estate" appears zero times anywhere in the P15 package or its Builder Annex, and "auction" appears exactly once in the entire corpus, about talent, not assets. Settlement as written leaves **nothing for a P16 transaction to buy.**

**Smallest correction.** No new estate-holding root, no new registry, no reopening of §12.3's settlement invariants. One sentence: *a closure-settlement request first checks whether a P16 acquisition transaction has already cleared at the frozen source revision; if it has, every owner's disposition becomes "assign to acquirer" instead of cancel/release/settle.* This adds exactly one new typed disposition value to the settlement machinery P15 already specifies, and lands on the already-distinct `acquired` terminal label (§9) rather than inventing a new one. It requires the Owner to authorize a bounded pre-settlement window during which a P16 transaction may claim the estate — see §20; this ruleset does not assume that authorization already exists.

**Disagreement resolved — which of the five alternatives.** `candidate-legibility.md` collapsed the Owner's five alternatives to a strict two-button model (Buy the Whole Studio at a liquidation reserve / Buy Individual Lots), folding Assumption Purchase entirely inside the whole-studio path. `candidate-integrity.md` and `candidate-adversary.md` both independently recommended a richer, three-path model that more closely matches real §363 practice (asset-free-and-clear as the default, with an *optional* buyer-chosen assumption of named contracts at a disclosed cure cost, plus a separate whole-entity path reserved for a genuine going-concern reorganization). **This merge adopts the integrity/adversary majority position**, both because two of three candidates converged on it independently and because it gives the player a real, legibly-labeled middle-ground choice that the strict two-button model does not — without adding any live, multi-round bidding complexity:

- **Buy Individual Lots (default, "clean")** — Story Properties, the film library, the brand, and equipment credits are each priced at their own liquidation-reserve floor (§14), free of the target's obligations. Each finished or in-progress film is its own lot with its own reserve, matching Orion, Relativity, and TWC precedent exactly.
- **Assume Named Contracts (optional add-on)** — the buyer may bundle in specific contracts or in-progress productions at a disclosed **cure cost**, acquiring them as a package at a lower net price than buying every piece clean — mirroring §363(a)'s executory-contract assumption-with-cure mechanic exactly.
- **Buy the Whole Studio (going-concern reorganization)** — reserved for the case where the `StudioId` continues operating under new ownership rather than terminating (a debt-for-equity-style reorganization, as MGM 2010 shows).

**No live, multi-round bidding-war UI** in any of the three paths — no comparator in the entire research program ships a genuine competitive-bid auction for distressed assets. A **sealed, one-shot comparison** among eligible rival bidders at a bounded window's close resolves competing interest (ties broken by earliest bid), avoiding OpenTTD's own worst anti-pattern (best-performer-first refusal ordering, a hidden pro-snowball lever). **Unsold lots** persist in a small, visible "estate catalogue" rather than vanishing — a direct precedent from Acquire's own displaced-shareholder rule (sell now, convert, or hold, never destroyed) — and can be bought later by any studio at the same reserve. Price is never the going-concern Studio Valuation — it is a liquidation-value reserve, which can and should be able to rise (a genuine rival bidder appears, Carolco-style) or fall (no bidders, TWC-style).

## 14. Book Net Worth vs Enterprise Valuation vs Transaction/Auction Price

The assignment flags this as high priority and forbids collapsing the numbers. Comparator evidence agrees emphatically: OpenTTD keeps three separate formulas in its own source code (company value; hostile-takeover price; bankruptcy-offer price); Software Inc.'s single blended "company worth" number, patched repeatedly and reset to zero on takeover, is the community's own cited cautionary tale; Amazon's own 10-Q for the MGM deal shows the discipline in the real world — $3.4B booked to content, $4.9B to goodwill, kept as two separate lines rather than one blended "value."

**The ladder, four numbers, one direction:**

1. **Book Net Worth (BNW)** — an accounting identity, not an opinion: cash (may be negative) + tangible property at its existing lossy recoverable credit + locked scheduled receipts − existing typed obligations. Never includes a forecast, a strategic premium, or Library value. Computable today from P11 rows alone with no new authority.
2. **Studio Valuation (SV)** — the plausible value of the operating business: `max(going-concern basis, asset basis)`. Going-concern basis capitalises trailing operating performance plus cash, receipts, Story Property/library value, and recoverable in-progress work; asset basis substitutes tangible-at-recoverable-credit for the capitalized-surplus term. The `max()` is load-bearing: without it, a buyer could liquidate a studio's own tangible assets for more than its going-concern price implied — the exact buy-cheap-in-distress-then-resell arbitrage every comparator's bankruptcy code independently blocks.
3. **Transaction Price** — what the buyer actually pays for a healthy target: SV × the independence-premium band from its public stance (§12). Deterministic and shown before any offer is placed.
4. **Auction/Liquidation Price** — the clearing price in a distressed sale: reserve = Liquidation Value (BNW's asset basis, no going-concern credit, no independence premium), rising only if a rival bidder actually competes for the same lot (§13).

**One-lane-per-asset, as explicit product law, against double counting:**
- Cash is always counted at par, never capitalised as if it earned a multiple.
- Plant/technology/brand sit *inside* the going-concern operating-surplus term while positive, or *inside* the asset-basis number if the studio is being liquidated — never both simultaneously (the `max()` decides which lane wins, not both at once).
- Library/Story-Property value is counted once, as a strategic add-on to SV, and never also inside a reputation/Standing channel, since Standing is defined to exclude it already.
- Technology is buyer-relative avoided-research value only, shown in the buyer's *private* offer panel, never as a standalone line in the target's own valuation.
- Brand/label value is informational only, never priced separately (§9) — no comparator that priced a brand line apart from its library survived scrutiny as non-arbitrary.
- No forecasts of future hits enter any line — P11 already forbids this for ordinary operations.

This resolves the Owner's own "verify" question in §2.N as **YES**: healthy acquisitions should begin from Enterprise Valuation + independence premium, while distressed price should emerge from bids/reserve around liquidation/strategic value — independently corroborated by OpenTTD's three-formula design, GearCity's health-driven premium, and Damodaran's own doctrine.

## 15. Real-world lessons

Ten cases carry direct, load-bearing design weight:

- **Disney/Pixar (2006)** — the creative studio and the film-ownership rights can sit with two different parties; rights ownership and operating-studio identity are genuinely separable, exactly as §3 assumes.
- **Disney/Marvel (2009)** and **Disney/Lucasfilm (2012)** — buying a company does not unwind licences its properties already carry (Paramount's Marvel distribution deal survived intact), and a franchise's future creative direction does not transfer even when the property does (Lucas's own sequel outlines were discarded). R1 transferring never implies the buyer inherits every downstream commitment automatically — encumbrances travel *with* the property.
- **Disney/21st Century Fox (2019)** — the real-world Model C: operations absorbed, brand renamed/kept as a label ("20th Century Studios"), the physical lot stayed with the seller, and the inherited slate produced a real write-down ($170M). The closest real precedent to the exact model the Owner selected.
- **Amazon/MGM (2021)** — the cleanest real proof that library value and brand/strategic value are different numbers even inside one purchase-price allocation, and that whole-company purchases transfer assumed cash/debt at par (the $2.5B assumed and immediately repaid). Directly supports §14's discipline and §6.Q1's par-transfer convention.
- **DreamWorks/Soros (2006)** — a library sold *separately* from the operating studio, with distribution kept as a term licence and a buy-back clause at a different price years later ($900M sold, ~$400M repurchased). Direct precedent for §11's holdback/re-buy pricing discipline and §3's encumbered-sale model.
- **Spider-Man (Sony/Marvel)** — the cleanest real rights model found: property stays with the owner, an activity-conditioned exclusive licence goes to another party, and reversion is a readable clock (release a film at least every 5.75 years or lose it). Direct real-world ancestor of R3's `useItOrLoseIt` field.
- **The Weinstein Company §363 sale (2018)** — demonstrates every alternative in §2.M in one real transaction: a stalking-horse floor that moved *down* (no competing bids), assets transferred free and clear, a capped buyer-chosen list of assumed contracts, and an unpaid producer's back-end claim left stranded with the estate because the contract was non-executory. The primary real-world template for §13's hybrid.
- **Carolco (1995–96)** — a rival bidder (Canal+) beat the stalking horse ($58M vs $47.5M), and continuation rights were auctioned as a *separate* lot from the library. Direct precedent for §13's per-lot design and for R3's separate tradability.
- **RKO Pictures** — the exact real-world analogue of the Owner's three worked history examples: library, real estate, name, and remake rights ended up as four separately owned things while authorship credit stayed with RKO throughout. Direct precedent for §9's identity model.
- **Control premiums cluster 20–40%** in the real world, rise with rival bidders, and boards refuse regardless of price (Disney/Comcast 2004) or accept a lower bid for certainty (Fox chose Disney over a higher Comcast cash offer, on regulatory-risk grounds). Direct precedent for §12's refusal-with-reason and premium-band model.

The universal negative lesson across every case: **facilities never teleport.** Every acquisition researched left the target's physical lot with someone other than the buyer, or sold it as a wholly separate transaction — reinforcing §7's automatic-liquidation default as the observed real pattern, not a code convenience.

## 16. Comparator lessons

Across nineteen-plus titles researched, the pattern is consistent enough to be treated as near-law:

- **Autonomous subsidiaries are the one shipped anti-pattern everyone converges on**, for the same reason in three unrelated engines: MGT2's are "a fiction" run by a timer with no real employees; Capitalism Lab's AI-CEO subsidiaries override the player's own decisions and "do literally nothing"; Software Inc.'s are "a source of never ending cash." This is the strongest evidence *against* Model B and *for* Model D (§5).
- **A shipped willingness model is a small, typed, visible thing, not a formula.** GearCity's health-driven shareholder vote, TEW's stance-plus-relationship, FM's Desperate/Keen/Curious, Big Ambitions' shown-valuation-with-refusal — none hide the driver behind a black box. §12 follows this exactly.
- **Partial ownership stakes are a uniformly failed idea.** OpenTTD removed share trading entirely in 2024, calling it "generate infinite money ... ABSOLUTELY NOTHING" for player decisions. Blockbuster Inc. disabled its own stake dividends because the game became too easy. GearCity's own flip exploits required repeated patches. Startup Company's unrestricted share-buying produced a documented complete monopoly. No comparator's partial-stake system survived scrutiny. This ruleset includes none (§17, R31).
- **A transferable asset that gates exclusive access to a shared audience/market channel snowballs without bound.** Hollywood Animal's purchasable cinemas — "buying cinemas is basically the meta" — destroyed rivals' ability to compete, and the developer's only fix was a scripted decree stripping theaters from everyone. This is directly checked against Project: Studio's own market design in §17 and found **not to apply today** — a genuine, independently-confirmed non-collision, not a gap this ruleset had to close.
- **Model C's brand-as-label is a real, playable, shipped pattern**, not a hopeful invention: GearCity's "marque" is exactly this — a brand that "does not operate independently from the company" yet keeps its own reputation lane and a full disposal menu (spin-off/sell/discontinue/restore). One negative comparator confirms the boundary by counterexample: TEW's parent/child companies keep operating their own roster and shows, which is functionally Model B, not C/D, and is exactly what a "retained label" must never become. The assignment's TEW "tape-library" premise itself does not exist in any TEW title checked and should be dropped (§19).
- **No comparator runs a live, multi-round bidding war for distressed assets.** Every one uses a fixed clearing price, a creditor-priority sale, a paused evaluation window, or a sealed one-shot comparison. §13's design follows this.
- **Inherited operations are a burden, not a reward.** Offworld Trading Company's designer explicitly documents that all-or-nothing buyouts left players managing buildings across the map "they didn't have time to manage" — confirming the Owner's single-lot preference from the player-experience side, not just the engineering side.

## 17. Anti-snowball

The Owner's brief explicitly rejects reaching for an artificial M&A cap and asks for the minimum necessary protection. The accepted game's own economy has already **failed** its internal snowball-bound gate even before any M&A exists ("the current brake on snowballing is the death spiral itself," a ~20% cash-share runaway measured independently — `D-16-ECONOMY-RECOVERY-DECISION-LAB.md`). Any M&A system is a positive-feedback amplifier layered on an economy that is not yet self-correcting, and this section's guards are not optional polish — they are the minimum load-bearing set. G11 (the accepted snowball gate) should be re-run with M&A enabled before shipping, not assumed to still pass.

**Direct check against the assignment's own named example.** Accepted code and approved P15A design were checked specifically for an HA-cinema-style ownable, capacity-limited, exclusive-access channel. Result: **none exists.** `competitionFactor` is hardcoded to `1.0` and `MarketState.competingSlate` is empty at world generation; P15A's own recommended shared-market mechanic (genre/release-window "saturation pressure") is a symmetric negative externality that degrades every crowding release including the releaser's own, so nothing can be purchased to corner it. Most strikingly, "screen/exhibition capacity" — the *literal* analogue to HA's cinemas — was already named by P15A's own Owner-decision table and **explicitly excluded from approved scope**, citing "avoids fake precision and a second scarce-resource model." This is independent verification that the exact mechanism the assignment worries about was considered by name and deliberately deferred before this research program ever started.

**The one genuinely live concentration risk found is different from the assignment's framing:** the **fixed, non-replenishing 60-person talent pool under strict one-employer exclusivity** is a real capacity-limited, contested resource. Whether a serial acquirer can concentrate a growing share of it depends entirely on the open Owner question in §6.Q3/§20 (do inherited contracts transfer intact with no negotiation) — if yes, a slower, partial, labor-market cousin of HA's snowball becomes possible.

**Minimum necessary anti-snowball set, layered, all natural, no cap:**
1. **Active-rival floor (hard eligibility, not a preference).** No acquisition may complete if it would drop the number of independently operating rival studios below three. The floor already exists in approved documentation as an envelope condition — this ruleset turns it into a transaction-blocking predicate with a stated reason.
2. **Affordability check (the actual frequency governor).** A bid or offer is legal only if, after paying and absorbing the target's obligations, the buyer would still hold at least 26 weeks of cash against its own plus the target's combined ongoing commitments. This single rule — not a cap on deal count — is what makes serial acquisition self-limiting: every purchase drains the exact reserve the next purchase's eligibility check depends on.
3. **Independence premium (§12)** makes healthy deals structurally expensive relative to their parts value — a buyer's own rational "parts value" ceiling for a one-lot absorber is normally *below* a strong target's Studio Valuation, so the strongest targets are naturally hard to acquire without any artificial cap (verified arithmetically in Paper Scenario C below).
4. **Integration friction is priced, not invented.** Releasing inherited staff costs 50% of their remaining guarantee; cancelling an inherited production costs its ordinary write-off; a newly acquired asset cannot reach full resale value for a holdback period. None of these are new numbers — they reuse existing law — but their *sum* is what makes rapid serial acquisition costly in a way the player can see and plan around.
5. **Talent-pool concentration guard (adversary lens, adopted).** Bind an acquirer's assumed headcount to its own usable production/development capacity, mirroring the discipline rival AI already applies to itself (rivals only ever staff to fill their one active production, never accumulate idle contracts). Inherited contracts beyond that bound settle under ordinary termination law within a bounded integration window, rather than being held indefinitely as a hoarding lockout.
6. **Cooldown after any deal, rejected or completed**, before the same buyer may approach the same (or a similarly sized) target again — a size-scaled cooldown on the buyer's own next acquisition, precedented directly by GearCity ("the larger the company, the longer you will have to wait before you can buy another").
7. **No partial ownership stakes** (§16) — every comparator that shipped one turned it into a snowball or an exploit with no compensating decision value.
8. **Distress evaporates value; it does not create a discount for the buyer's benefit alone.** A distressed studio's reserve reflects the same lossy liquidation math a solvent studio would face if it demolished its own assets.
9. **Typed refusal, always.** Every refusal and every blocked transaction returns a typed, disclosed reason, never a silent failure — OpenTTD's undocumented "Can't buy company" and Football Manager's "not for sale at any price" complaints are the identical UX failure in two unrelated genres, and a typed reason costs nothing architecturally.

**No antitrust mechanic is added.** Every real case where a regulator mattered (Fox/Disney's own regulatory-risk calculation) was a target-side consideration already covered by the independence-premium/refusal model in §12, not a separate system. The Owner's own brief treats antitrust as "only if absolutely necessary later" — nothing here shows it is necessary yet; GearCity's own outcome-based >75%-share monopoly suit is the natural next lever *if* the floor alone proves insufficient after playtesting, never a first-resort design.

## 18. Package boundary

**P16A/B/C, confirmed and layered:**

| Package | Owns | Depends on |
|---|---|---|
| **P16A — Library, Story Properties & Rights** | StoryProperty/Film Library identity, R1–R4/`Licence` shape, chain of title, dated ownership history, library valuation read-model | Nothing beyond P11/P12 as they exist today; **no P15B dependency**; can ship first |
| **P16B — Rights & Asset Transactions** | Sale/licence of individual Story Properties, films, brands, equipment credits between two active studios; the exploit guards of §11 | P16A + existing P11/P12 typed-receipt plumbing |
| **P16C — Studio Acquisition & Integration** | Whole-studio purchase/auction, the Model-D choice, the transfer bundle of §6, the corporate-history model of §9 | P16A + P16B + the H5 terminal-business-state fix (§5) + the P15B pre-settlement estate gate (§13) + a new P12/P14 "assumed" employment reason |

This split is not invented for this ruleset; it is the shape the assignment itself proposes, and it survives independent scrutiny from four angles: code-seam fit, approved-documentation fit, comparator fit, and the explicit note that no prior Future Ops document states it as settled, only as assignment-originated and now independently corroborated by this research program.

**What stays outside P16 entirely.**
- **P16D** — not created. Financing/leverage as an anti-snowball lever stays exactly where it already sits: an Owner-gated P11 decision (P11-REQ-041), not a P16 mechanism. Partial ownership/equity stakes (§16, §17) belong in P16D at the earliest, if ever — every comparator's version failed. A future P16D reissue/re-release mechanic is the natural home for turning stored library *value* into library *cash* once P18's distribution system exists; P16A records reissue *authority* only (who holds R2), never execution, since release is exact-once law today and reissue needs P07/P11/P15 co-authority this document does not attempt to grant.
- **P17 (sequels/remakes/franchises)** — consumes P16A's exact `StoryProperty.works` list and current continuation-authority holder; it may never mint or infer a property from title, genre, or cast resemblance. A sequel is always a *new* concept/script linked to the parent property by an explicit edge — the accepted code's "a concept already owns a screenplay project" rule makes reusing the same concept for a second film structurally impossible.
- **P18 (television/cross-media)** — consumes only explicit cross-media grants under P16's Licence instrument (R4, forbidden before P18 exists to exercise them). P16 remains the sole author of parties/scope/term/consideration for any such grant even after P18 ships.
- **Co-productions** — stay out. No dossier across nine comparators and the real-world M&A survey found a compelling reason to add shared ownership of a single production between two studios; the Owner has already marked it not-selected, and nothing here reopens that.

**Guard against save bloat (adversary lens, adopted).** An ownership/transaction system that writes a row per property per week, or that bulk-mints a `StoryProperty` for every one of thousands of films at migration, compounds a save-growth problem the accepted game already exceeds its own target on (49.6MB at week 8791). Every ownership/transaction row is a **per-transaction, dated event**, never a per-week fact; valuation is a **derived read model, recomputed on demand**, never persisted weekly; `StoryProperty` rows are created **lazily**, on first transaction or continuation, rather than bulk-created for every existing film at migration. Any future projection over the library pages by a bounded, append-stable cursor and never enumerates the whole catalogue to compute a single price.

## Paper scenarios A–F

All figures are PAPER HYPOTHESES illustrating the rules above, not tuned constants.

### A — Bankrupt small studio (weak cash, modest properties, several employees, no valuable technology)

Assume: cash −$50,000; abstract tangible capacity original cost $2,000,000 (recoverable credit at 0.5× = $1,000,000); 3 small Story Properties appraised at $50,000 each ($150,000 total); 6 employees with combined remaining guarantees of $900,000; no technology of note.

- **Liquidation Value (asset basis):** $1,000,000 (tangible) + $150,000 (properties) − $50,000 (overdraft) = **$1,100,000**.
- **Buy Individual Lots, clean:** a buyer pays roughly $1,100,000 in total across the separate lots, assumes zero obligations; the six employees are released to the open talent pool by the estate.
- **Buy Individual Lots + Assume Named Contracts:** a buyer pays a lower cash amount up front (say $250,000) but assumes the $900,000 of employment guarantees at cure cost, bundling the properties and the roster together at a lower net headline price than buying every piece clean — the middle-ground option the merged §13 model adds over the strict two-button version.
- **Buy the Whole Studio (going-concern):** only makes sense if a bidder specifically wants the StudioId's historical continuity for a label; otherwise the two asset-lot paths above dominate.

**What this demonstrates:** the three-path model from §13 gives a legible trade-off — cheap-and-clean vs. cheap-plus-obligations vs. pay-in-full-and-keep-the-name — without any bidding mechanism.

### B — Valuable distressed studio (heavy debt, valuable library, excellent research, several active productions)

Assume: cash −$5,000,000; recoverable tangible $1,500,000; locked scheduled receipts $500,000; contract guarantees $2,000,000; library appraised strategic value $8,000,000 (5 properties, 40 films); buyer-relative avoided-research value from the target's completed Technology III $2,000,000; two active productions carrying $3,000,000 of sunk, recoverable-in-place committed cost.

- **Book Net Worth** = −5,000,000 + 1,500,000 + 500,000 − 2,000,000 = **−$5,000,000**.
- **Studio Valuation (asset basis, since going-concern is near zero for a distressed target)** = $2,000,000 (tangible+receipts) + $8,000,000 (library) + $2,000,000 (buyer-relative tech) ≈ **$12,000,000** buyer-specific ceiling, though a generic (non-tech-seeking) bidder's SV is closer to **$10,000,000**.
- **Auction/Liquidation reserve** = tangible + receipts only = **$2,000,000**, rising with competing bids toward, but rationally never exceeding, the second-highest bidder's private value — clearing, say, at **$6,500,000**.

**What this demonstrates:** BNW (−$5,000,000), Studio Valuation (~$10–12,000,000), and Auction Price (~$6,500,000) are three genuinely different numbers computed from the same facts, exactly per §14's discipline — none is a garbled average of the others, and a BNW-only reading ("worthless, avoid") and an SV-only reading ("valuable, pursue") of the *same studio* point in opposite directions.

### C — Healthy rival (profitable, strong brand, valuable properties, does not want to sell)

Assume: Studio Valuation $9,700,000; public willingness state `Reluctant`; premium band 1.4×–1.8× ⇒ asking range **$13,580,000–$17,460,000**. A buyer's own rational parts-value ceiling for absorbing this target as a one-lot addition (its recoverable tangible plus its properties at buyer-relative, non-duplicated worth) computes to roughly **$6,864,000**.

**What this demonstrates:** the buyer's rational ceiling ($6.86M) sits well below even the low end of the seller's asking range ($13.58M). This is not a bug; it is §17's Rule 3 working exactly as intended — a strong, well-run target is *structurally* hard to acquire at a price that makes sense for the buyer, without any artificial cap. The independence-premium naming makes clear this gap is the cost of the seller's reluctance, not the buyer's synergy.

### D — Technology target (player buys mainly for research it lacks)

Assume: buyer lacks Technology III; target has it, completed; avoided-research value estimated at $2,000,000; whole-studio purchase price $3,000,000; the physical Tier-III facility body still costs $800,000 capex to build after closing, taking N weeks.

- **Acquisition path total effective cost:** $3,000,000 (purchase) + $800,000 (mandatory build) = **$3,800,000**, available as soon as the build finishes.
- **Organic path total effective cost:** ~$2,000,000 (hypothetical R&D) + $800,000 (build) = **$2,800,000**, but only after however many weeks the research itself would have taken.

**What this demonstrates:** the acquisition path is not "free capability" — it costs *more* in cash than researching it yourself, and it still requires the same physical build afterward. What it buys is **time**, not magic — the exact reading of "knowledge transfers, installations do not" the Owner's §2.F direction requires, verified with arithmetic rather than asserted.

### E — Serial acquirer (three studios in ten years)

Walking through three sequential deals against a starting field of nine rivals: after deal #1, active rivals fall from 9 to 8; after deal #2, to 7; each deal also drains the buyer's own affordability buffer (§17 Rule 2) and adds one more retained label or absorbed roster to triage. By deal #3 (rivals at 6, still comfortably above the floor of 3), the acquirer carries three sets of inherited obligations, three holdback timers on inherited assets, and a shrinking pool of remaining targets whose premiums have not fallen — nothing in the rules makes later deals *cheaper*. If inherited contracts transfer intact with no bound (§20 decision #2), the talent-pool concentration guard (§17 Rule 5) caps how much of the fixed 60-person pool any single acquirer can actually hold onto post-integration, forcing the remainder back into ordinary termination/free-agency within the integration window. The concrete exploit this scenario surfaces without a holdback rule: absent §11's holdback, the buyer could immediately re-sell a freshly acquired library at full appraised value for instant profit; **with** the rule, that value is only reachable after the holdback period, closing the loop. A fourth or fifth acquisition being *harder in principle*, not just *scarcer in targets*, would be a new "serial-acquirer wariness" ask-price escalation — explicitly **not recommended initially**, since it would add a second hidden multiplier exactly where §12 tries to keep one visible number.

### F — Rival acquires rival (symmetry and history preservation)

Rival A (willingness state: `Open`, distressed) is bought by Rival B under the identical §12/§13 rules a player purchase would use — same affordability check, same premium band, same active-rival-floor predicate. Mechanically this is *cheaper* to build than a player-directed purchase, because both sides already share identical `Production`/`ScriptDevelopment` array shapes and rival facilities never bind to a placement ledger. Rival A's `StudioId` remains in the fixed registry forever; its films still credit Rival A as creator; a `CorporateEvent{kind:'acquired', successorStudioId: B, brandRetained: true|false}` marks the transaction publicly, exactly as §9 specifies for a player-directed deal. If Rival B is itself later absorbed by Rival C, the chain (A→B→C) stays a dated, walkable sequence of successor pointers — never a rewrite of A's or B's original identity.

**What this demonstrates:** "symmetric transaction law" is not an aspiration bolted on afterward; it falls out of using the same rules and the same append-only history model regardless of which side of the transaction is a player.

## Structural problems (deduplicated union, exact statement + smallest correction)

1. **No debt instrument exists; player financing is Owner-prohibited** (P11-REQ-041/042 OWNER-BLOCKED), yet §2.F Q2 and §2.O both presuppose "debt" as a transferable/financing concept. *Smallest correction:* the transfer bundle's "DEBT/LIABILITIES" category means existing typed obligations only (contract guarantees, committed production costs, scheduled Opex); a genuine debt instrument remains a separate, not-yet-authorized P11 Owner gate and must never be fabricated inside P16 (R18).
2. **P15 closure-settlement, as approved, cancels or settles every category and leaves no transferable estate** — "auction"/"estate" appear in zero P15 documents — yet §2.M presupposes that "P15 may decide a rival becomes bankrupt and its assets enter auction." *Smallest correction:* insert one new pre-settlement gate — a closure-settlement request first checks for an already-cleared P16 acquisition transaction at the frozen source revision; if found, each owner's disposition becomes `assign-to-acquirer` instead of cancel/release/settle, using one new typed disposition value inside the existing per-owner settlement machinery (R26).
3. **`StudioIdentity` has no status field, and every entered rival's business ticks unconditionally forever** — there is no way to represent "operations ended" today, which blocks Models A, C, and D equally, not just Model B. *Smallest correction:* an additive terminal-business-state ("H5") honored by both the validator and the tick loop, landing on the already-distinct `acquired` label rather than `closed` (R13).
4. **`contractId` embeds the employer StudioId and `scriptProjectId` is bound to the owning studio's array index** — neither can be literally "moved" to a new owner without becoming invalid. *Smallest correction:* transfer always means mint-new-row-under-buyer-with-identical-terms-and-a-new-reason-code, never in-place re-owning (R19).
5. **Rival facilities are four abstract, once-paid capacity rows with no tier/footprint/blueprint history** — §2.F Q8 and §2.G's building-liquidation alternatives presuppose a physical referent that does not exist for rival targets today. *Smallest correction:* write the eligibility-not-installation rule generally so it is correct the moment rival facilities gain tiers, but explicitly document that it is currently a near-no-op for rival targets rather than silently implying parity that doesn't exist (R21, R22).
6. **The library has zero cash flow today**, while assignment §2.C frames library value as contributing to valuation and other systems in a way that could be misread as implying cash flow. *Smallest correction:* Library Value is strictly a valuation-lane input, never a P11 cash fact, until a P18 distribution channel exists — restating the assignment's own instruction as a hard boundary rule (R06).
7. **Rival cash/obligations/budgets/forecasts are hidden by default**, yet §2.L/§2.N require the player to see enough about a target to evaluate a healthy-acquisition offer without an opaque bargaining simulator. *Smallest correction:* define an explicit due-diligence disclosure event rather than reading hidden fields directly in any player-facing valuation projection (R27).
8. **The fixed nine/ten-identity registry and the approved ≥3-active-rival floor** are hard existing constraints that a serial-acquisition system can silently violate by absorbing rivals below the floor, since no replacement-entrant mechanism is currently authorized. *Smallest correction:* treat the floor as an explicit transaction-eligibility predicate — any acquisition that would drop active rivals below 3 is refused with a typed reason — until/unless a separate P15B replacement-entrant slice is authorized (R29).
