# P16 Candidate Ruleset — Adversarial Economist Pass

**Lens:** design-by-attack. For every mechanic below, the exploit is written first, then the smallest natural rule that closes it — never an artificial cap, never a player-only surcharge (the one comparator that did this, GearCity, is flagged as the anti-pattern to avoid: 05-comparators-A.md, "player-only surcharge to fix stock exploits"). All dollar figures are PAPER HYPOTHESES for illustration only. Owner-selected direction (assignment §2.A–T) is treated as settled; where a selected rule collides with an existing structural law, the collision is stated explicitly with the smallest correction, never designed around silently.

Twelve exploit families are traced end to end: **flip loops, sell-and-keep-using, duplicate ownership, buy-cheap-in-distress-then-resell, serial acquisition snowball, talent-pool concentration, AI over-consolidation, valuation double counting, rival-cash omniscience, hostile takeover of the player, migration backfill, and save bloat.** Each is marked `EXPLOIT:` / `GUARD:` at its point of origin.

## 3. StoryProperty and Minimal-Rights Model

The original shipped zero rights concepts: scripts were scalar-valued consumables sold for "relatively minor" cash with no provenance, and a released film's only afterlife was a valueless, irreversible archive (01-original-game.md; manual_english.txt:428-431, prima_eguide.txt:411). Every rights system below is successor design, never parity (00-KEY-FINDINGS.md §02, HIS-013/INT-011 register rows).

**Minimal entity.** `StoryProperty{propertyId, title, originalTitle, genre, origin{kind: original|external, creatorStudioId IMMUTABLE, creatorTalentIds IMMUTABLE, mintedWeek}, works: FilmId[]}`. `propertyId` is deterministic from the originating `conceptId`/`productionId` — the same permanent, append-only anchor the accepted engine already uses for the film↔script↔concept chain (04-code-audit.md; types.ts:154-163, screenplay.ts:15-40). The engine already distinguishes studio-original from market-sourced material via `ScreenplayProvenanceView.origin:'original'|'pool'` (04-code-audit.md; screenplay.ts:305-345) — this is the natural, already-existing anchor for "studio-created" vs. "externally sourced," so no new provenance vocabulary is needed.

**Mint point: at Greenlight**, not commission or release. This freezes provenance before any acquisition can move a production, guarantees every released film has a property when P17 needs one, and leaves cancelled/shelved productions as legible "unproduced properties" rather than orphans (00-KEY-FINDINGS.md §10, design implication).

**Four rights, one instrument** — this is the smallest bundle that reproduces every real deal researched, with no territory or media sub-splits (10-rights-law.md; WGA Separated Rights, Sony/Marvel, DreamWorks/Soros):

| Right | What it covers | Transferable? |
|---|---|---|
| R1 PROPERTY | The underlying Story Property itself | Sellable (whole) |
| R2 FILM | Ownership of one released film's rights | Sellable (whole, per film) |
| R3 CONTINUATION | Authority to make a sequel/remake/spinoff | Licensable only |
| R4 CROSS-MEDIA | TV/cross-media exploitation (P18) | Licensable only; **forbidden before P18 exists** |

A single generic `Licence{subject{kind,id}, facet, grantor, grantee, exclusive, startWeek, termWeeks, consideration, useItOrLoseIt?, status}` covers R3 and (later) R4 and the technology-rights slice P13 has already sketched (10-rights-law.md; P13 §16a.2/16a.4). Exclusive licensee is treated as the holder of that facet for the term — real law already draws this line cleanly (10-rights-law.md; Copyright Alliance FAQ), so P17 checks the current exclusive licensee, not just the nominal owner.

**EXPLOIT — duplicate ownership.** Two studios both claim rights to the same property (a race condition between two pending transactions, or a save that never records a transfer's `from` side correctly), or an acquired studio's properties are copied rather than moved, leaving a ghost copy on the dead identity.
**GUARD:** a single-holder field per right plus an **append-only `ownershipEvents` log**, validator-enforced so a transfer's `from` must equal the current holder or the transaction is refused outright (10-rights-law.md design implication; roadmap's reserved `ownershipEvents` root, 03-authority-domains.md — "P16+ only... never enters a P15 root"). This is exactly the CK3 title model researched independently: identity never rewritten, holder changes append (07-comparators-C.md; CK3 Titles/History modding — "a certificate of land ownership... holder history is an append-only dated log").

**EXPLOIT — sell-and-keep-using.** Player sells a Story Property or an exclusive R3 license while a production against it is still shooting, pockets the cash, and finishes the film anyway using the sold rights — or sells R1 while quietly retaining a live R3 license that was never disclosed to the buyer.
**GUARD: rights-in-use lock** — no R1 sale or exclusive R3 grant while an active production on that property exists, unless the production transfers with it or the seller explicitly retains a licence recorded on the sale receipt (encumbered sale, exactly like DreamWorks/Soros where distribution stayed a disclosed 5-year exclusive licence: 10-rights-law.md; NBC/AP, Viacom 8-K). Mad Games Tycoon 2 ships this exact primitive ("Any IP being traded needs to have no games currently being sold": 10-rights-law.md; MGT2 discussion 3460471649935880326) and it is the cleanest shipped precedent found.

Permanence: transfer events write only `to/week/consideration/transactionId`; renames never touch ids; credits, creator provenance, release history and awards never transfer (this is Owner law, §2.D, and matches every real case researched — RKO/Orion/Lorimar/TWC all kept original credits while ownership moved: 08-realworld-ma.md).

## 4. Film-Library Value

**Structural collision, stated exactly.** The accepted engine has genuinely zero cash flow after a film's locked `TheatricalRun` completes — no theaters, no re-release, no home media (03-authority-domains.md; types.ts:304-316). The original's "Movies 24%" term, which the assignment's framing initially treated as a form of library value, is in fact the opposite: an explicitly *decaying* recency score ("a town with a staggeringly short memory" — 01-original-game.md; prima_eguide.txt:417). There is no P15/P16 substrate today that turns an old film into weekly cash, and inventing one before an owning distribution/media system (P18) exists is exactly what Owner direction (§2.C) forbids.

**Smallest correction:** library value is a **valuation read-model only** — a formula-versioned, on-demand-computed number ("Library (estimated)") that feeds Studio/Enterprise Valuation (§14) and nothing else. It is never a P11 ledger fact, never added to Cash, and never a Standing/Power-Ranking input (00-KEY-FINDINGS.md §09 — "Power Ranking and Standing explicitly exclude cash/valuation"). Count each owned R1 once (sequel potential) and each owned R2 once (catalog value); never both toward the same number twice — see §14's double-counting rules.

Every comparator with real library cash gates it behind a downstream channel: The Executive's home-entertainment distributor deals, Moviehouse's distribution-slot-limited "slight secondary source of profit," Hollywood Animal's planned whole-catalog vs. per-deal TV licensing (00-KEY-FINDINGS.md §06). Real-world confirms the same pattern at every scale: RKO's library only earned when TV rights were sold to C&C in 1955, MGM's 2004 library was priced against expected DVD cash flow, Miramax's library only earned once Netflix/Hulu deals existed (08-realworld-ma.md). **Library cash waits for P18; library *value* does not.**

Shelved/unreleased properties keep their ownership permanently and valuelessly — the original already modeled this exact state (burying a bad film in the Archive to protect prestige: 01-original-game.md; GameSpot walkthrough) — so a small flat valuation credit for unproduced properties is recommended (prevents an owner from "hiding" a property at zero cost from a valuation standpoint while still holding it hostage from a buyer).

## 5. Acquisition Operating Model (A vs. B vs. C vs. D)

Model B is not a close call. Every comparator that shipped an autonomous-subsidiary model produced the same complaint in different words: MGT2 subsidiaries are "a fiction" run by a timer with no real employees (06-comparators-B.md; thread 3416558908189280598 — "There are no employees working on games there, just a timer emulating that"); Capitalism Lab's AI-CEO subsidiaries override the player's own decisions and "do literally nothing" (05-comparators-A.md; forum t=4606); Offworld Trading Company's designer notes call inherited operations "a burden" players did not want (07-comparators-C.md; Soren Johnson, Designer Notes #17). TEW's parent/child model — initially suspected as a positive comparator for "retain brand as label" — turns out on direct verification to be the negative case: acquired companies keep running their own full roster and shows, which is functionally Model B, not Model C/D, and is exactly the second separately-managed studio the Owner does not want (18-gap-tew-ix-tape-library-brand-label-verification.md).

**Six-criteria table** (dossier 13's synthesis, built by re-verifying accepted code, not asserted):

| Criterion | A: Full Absorption | B: Autonomous Subsidiary | C: Absorb+Label | D: Owner Choice (A or C) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Player comprehension | High — one screen, one outcome | Low — a second thing to learn/manage every acquisition | High — one screen, one flag | Highest — same one screen, one extra toggle |
| Historical identity | Preserved (dated relation) | Preserved but confusingly "still operating" | Preserved and visibly credited | Preserved, player picks how visibly |
| Management burden | Low (once H5 ships*) | Unconditionally high — no fix exists (see below) | Low (once H5 ships*) | Low (once H5 ships*); player never forced into B |
| Gameplay value | Medium (assets only) | Medium-low (comparator tedium documented) | High (assets + brand-as-asset-sale surface) | Highest — player chooses per deal |
| AI/rival compatibility | High (once H5 ships*) | Unconditionally low — RivalAccount/production arrays grow forever, compounding the already-measured save overrun | High (once H5 ships*) | High (once H5 ships*) |
| Save/performance complexity | Low (bounded, terminal) | Unconditionally high (unbounded growth) | Low (bounded, terminal) | Low (bounded, terminal) |

\* H5 = a terminal `RivalBusiness` state. This is a genuine, previously-uncited **structural collision**: `hollywoodValidation.ts:331` hard-requires exactly one live business per entered-rival identity ("entry missing business"), and `hollywoodTick.ts:221-288` ticks every business unconditionally every week with no branch to skip one (13-gap-model-d-six-criteria-evaluation-table.md; 11-gap-p15-closure-settlement-estate-auction-reconciliation.md). **Smallest correction:** this single code fact must be resolved — either a `StudioIdentity` status field plus an amended predicate, or a frozen/emptied placeholder `RivalBusiness` row — before *any* of Models A, C, or D can ship absorption at all. Model B is structurally excluded from ever benefiting from this fix, because B's entire premise is that the business keeps operating; that is precisely the finding this collision names as unconditionally failing.

**Recommendation:** Model D, exactly as the Owner selected — Absorb Completely or Absorb Operations + Retain Brand as Label, chosen once at the closing screen. This is comparator-validated as a shipped, low-friction pattern (Capitalism Lab's one-click `[Acquire]` vs. `[Acquire and Merge]`, Software Inc.'s Takeover vs. Subsidiary choice at closing: 05-comparators-A.md), and is the real-world norm (Disney/Fox → "20th Century Studios," Amazon/MGM → "Amazon MGM Studios," both retained-brand absorptions with no autonomous operations: 08-realworld-ma.md). A retained label is never a second registry entry — see §9.

## 6. Exact Acquisition Transfer Bundle

Answering assignment §2.F's eight questions directly, grounded in accepted code and cross-checked against GearCity (the only comparator that publishes a complete, coherent transfer list) and real M&A:

**1. Does acquired cash become buyer cash?** **Yes, at par**, via one new typed `LedgerKind` (player side) / `RivalMoneyKind` (rival side) correlated to the transaction id — even if the acquired cash is negative, since negative cash is already a legal, recoverable state, never bankruptcy (03-authority-domains.md; 09-valuation-finance.md). This matches GearCity (cash transfers with the bundle) and Amazon/MGM (net-of-cash pricing) rather than OpenTTD's newer 14.0+ code, which leaves cash with the seller — a legitimate alternative, but mixing the two conventions inside one transaction type is exactly the "infinite money" exploit OpenTTD's own maintainers patched when partial share ownership blurred which side kept the money (07-comparators-C.md; changelog 14.0-beta1, issue #9281). **Rule: pick one convention per transaction kind and never mix it.**

**2. Does debt transfer?** There is no debt instrument to transfer — the accepted game has no loan, credit, interest, or liability object of any kind, and player financing is Owner-blocked (P11-REQ-041/042: 03-authority-domains.md; 09-valuation-finance.md). What *does* exist and *does* transfer are **typed obligations**: remaining contract guarantees, committed production costs, and ongoing Opex. The charter should say "obligations," not "debt," and reserve "debt" for a separate, still-Owner-gated financing decision. An "assumption purchase" (§13) assumes obligations now; it cannot assume debt that does not exist.

**3. Do employee contracts transfer intact?** Yes, as a new P12 employer-transition row: end the target's employment row, mint an identical-terms row under the buyer with a new reason code (`assumed`), one `fromStudioId→toStudioId` receipt (03-authority-domains.md; industryEmployment.ts:25-32). This must be **exempted from the signing-bonus ledger cross-check** — copying `Contract.signingBonus` into the buyer's state must not re-debit it (04-code-audit.md, flagged open question). This is a lawful pre-existing category in P12 design authority ("exclusive until expiry, lawful release, sale/assignment": 03-authority-domains.md §157) — acquisition uses the existing "sale/assignment" terminator, not a new P14D mechanism, so it cannot be "smuggled into a talent transfer implementation" as P14 explicitly forbids (03-authority-domains.md §90).

**4. Can the player release inherited staff afterward under ordinary termination law?** Yes — the existing 50%-of-remaining-guarantee termination cost applies unchanged (employment.ts:172-180), refused only while the person is on an active screenplay task, exactly as it already works for any employee. This is the natural, non-arbitrary integration cost, precedented by Hollywood Animal's "terminate employee contracts early and even recover part of the contract cost" and TEW's "six months' pay" release cost (06-comparators-B.md).

**5. What happens to active movies?** They **transfer and finish under the buyer** via re-formation on the buyer's own lot — preserving `productionId`/`conceptId`/participants/committed cost — with a ledger witness row correlating the move (04-code-audit.md; production re-parenting analysis). The buyer chooses continue-or-cancel-at-write-off-cost for each inherited production, exactly as Fox's slate was triaged after Disney's purchase, with a real, documented downside (a $170M inherited-slate loss: 08-realworld-ma.md). No "transition owner" state is required for a healthy acquisition; bankruptcy is different (§13).

**6. What happens to target facilities if only one lot is actively managed?** **Automatic liquidation at closing**, using the existing depreciated-recovery law already in the codebase (facility 0.5×, set 0.35×, strictly `<1` by invariant: placement.ts:1025-1028, tuning.ts:1598-1613). For rival targets this is nearly free to answer because rivals own no lot at all today — only four abstract, once-paid capacity rows (03-authority-domains.md; hollywood.ts:88-105) — so "buildings cannot teleport" is trivially satisfied; the liquidation credit is simply a cash line. **Option 3 (remote property as a pure financial asset)** is the fallback if the Owner ever wants abstract capacity to persist as a valuation line rather than convert to cash immediately.

**7. What exactly does acquired research grant?** **Knowledge only.** The buyer gains a new `TechnologyAdoption` row at `readyToAdopt` for anything the target had completed, citing the target's provenance (03-authority-domains.md; P13 §16a). The buyer must still lawfully install/build/convert the physical capability through the existing P09 job — cash never bypasses "facility capability or necessary work" (Owner Rulings §2.4.1/§2.4.3, already settled law). In-flight research is never carried across acquisition, because P13-OD-06 already forbids in-flight work moving across studios (03-authority-domains.md §83) — a general law, not an acquisition-specific gap. Capitalism Lab independently validates this from the other direction: technology-dependent firm types are explicitly non-transferable, and technology is bought as a licensed copy the seller keeps (05-comparators-A.md).

**8. What happens when target has a higher physical facility upgrade than buyer?** The buyer gains **eligibility for that tier** (a knowledge/adoption fact) but must still complete the actual install/conversion job through ordinary law — cash can accelerate a *qualified* research/build job, it cannot skip it. No physical upgrade ever appears on the buyer's lot by transaction alone. For rival targets this question is currently moot, because rivals have no tiered installations at all (only abstract capacity rows) — so the principle is stated as law now, ready for the day rival facilities gain real tiers.

## 7. Physical Property After Acquisition

The Owner's own alternatives (§2.G) collapse to one answer once the code is read: rival "facilities" are four abstract, flat-Opex capacity rows with no blueprint, tier, footprint, or capex history (03-authority-domains.md; hollywood.ts:88-105). **Option 1 (automatic liquidation)** is recommended as the default, using the existing depreciated-recovery credit — this produces no second lot, requires no new UI, and matches the real-world pattern where a target's lot is disposed of separately from its library in every case researched (Fox's physical lot stayed with Fox Corp even after Disney bought everything else; RKO's lot went to Desilu separately from its library and name: 08-realworld-ma.md). **Option 3 (financial-asset-only)** is the correct fallback the day rival capacity gains a real capex/tier model, since converting it to cash immediately at that point would be premature liquidation of a genuinely valuable, still-abstract asset. **Option 4 (transferable equipment)** has no current substrate — the `ConversionRequirementDescriptor.installedEquipment` field the Owner's own P09/P13 direction anticipates does not exist yet (03-authority-domains.md §78) — so it should be named in the charter as a *future* refinement, not built now. Land purchase does not exist at all yet (P09-REQ-035 LATER), so "land" in the transfer bundle is simply "not priced" until a land market ships.

## 8. Active Productions

Inheriting films-in-progress is the assignment's own stated draw (§2.H), and it is cheap to build correctly because the underlying `Production`/`ScriptDevelopment` shapes are identical on both the player and rival sides (16-gap-rival-ai-acquisition-code-feasibility.md). **Healthy acquisition:** every active production transfers and finishes under the buyer, who chooses continue-or-cancel per project at the moment of closing — a short, one-time triage list, not a permanent management surface (every comparator that inherited a catalogue found the *triage itself* to be the interesting decision, not ongoing oversight: 05-comparators-A.md, "catalogue triage is the real tedium"). **No transition-owner state is needed for healthy deals** — the buyer owns from the closing week forward. **Bankruptcy is different:** each finished or in-progress film becomes its own auction lot with its own reserve, exactly as Orion's frozen ten completed films, Relativity's held-up unreleased titles, and TWC's per-film disposition all show (08-realworld-ma.md). Maintaining exact production IDs/history end to end is already guaranteed by the accepted `productionId` invariant (`FilmId === productionId`, globally unique: 04-code-audit.md; productionIdentity.ts:9-101) — re-parenting is feasible only as a re-formation on the buyer's lot with a ledger-witness row, never a container move, because the placed-facility greenlight-ledger evidence invariant (`placement.ts:1811-1818`) requires exactly one production row at `week===startTick` and cannot silently accept two possible weeks.

## 9. Historical Identity/Status Model

The three §2.I examples map cleanly onto three distinct, dated, **never-deleted** events on the target's permanent `StudioId`, exactly matching the CK3-style append-only holder-event model (07-comparators-C.md) and the already-settled register law that "acquired ≠ closed" (HIS-013: 02-authority-corporate.md):

- **STUDIO BANKRUPTCY** — `closed` event, no successor, the P15B closure-settlement pathway (once authorized) applies unchanged.
- **ACQUISITION** — `acquired` event, `successorStudioId = buyer`, brand retained as a label (Model D's Absorb-Operations-Retain-Label branch).
- **MERGER/ABSORPTION** — `absorbed` event, `successorStudioId = buyer`, brand not retained (Model D's Absorb-Completely branch).

A retained label is **never** a second `StudioId`, roster, ledger, or business row — it is a P16 ownership relation referencing the existing, immutable identity (03-authority-domains.md, design implication — "no second registry: none creates another studio registry"). This is GearCity's marque exactly: a brand with its own image rating that "do[es] not operate independently from the company" (05-comparators-A.md; howto_marques) — and the smallest correction against TEW's negative example (§5): a label carries *zero* operations, never a roster or shows of its own.

**EXPLOIT — migration backfill.** A save migration invents plausible-looking corporate history for pre-P16 saves — e.g., silently fabricating that Rival R05 "acquired" Rival R02 in some past week to make an old save look populated, or retroactively assigning Story Property ownership to whichever studio currently holds the film.
**GUARD:** migration may record only what is *certain*, never invented. P12 §17 already supplies the boundary: "existing player film ownership is derivable because only the player existed" is a certain fact, not fictional backfill (03-authority-domains.md §156) — but this reasoning applies **only** to the single-player-existed case at world start. A migration must never invent a rival-to-rival ownership event, a closure, or a distress state that the save's own history does not already contain (P15's own "old-save honesty" law: "no pre-migration pressure, ranking, rival entry, distress, closure, recovery, or Legacy claim is invented" — 03-authority-domains.md §156). `StoryProperty` rows for pre-existing films should be minted **lazily**, on first transaction, rather than bulk-created at migration — both for honesty (avoids "explicit by exact ID" turning into disguised bulk-minting: consumer contract §15) and for the save-bloat reason in §18.

## 10. Rival M&A

Owner law (§2.J) is unambiguous and already settled: no player-only superpower, same transaction law for everyone (P15 §11/§16/§17: "no isPlayer multiplier or exemption" — 02-authority-corporate.md §153). Feasibility is good news: adding an AI acquisition-bid decision is architecturally cheap under the existing RNG law (`RngPurpose` is a closed, additively-extended union; `stream()` is pure and stateless, never touching `state.rngState` — 16-gap-rival-ai-acquisition-code-feasibility.md). The one genuinely new architectural capability is that **no existing rival decision function ever reads another rival's data** — an AI bid-eligibility check is the first cross-business read in the engine and must be an explicit, charter-authorized exception to the otherwise-total per-business isolation, kept separate from the player-facing privacy projection in `bridge/industry.ts` (16-gap-rival-ai-acquisition-code-feasibility.md).

Rival-to-rival transfers are actually **easier** than player-crossing acquisitions, not merely symmetric to them: both sides share identical `Production`/`ScriptDevelopment` shapes and one employment array with a reusable end-old/mint-new idiom already proven for renewals, and rival facilities never carry a placement-ledger row or `requiresSetBinding` flag — so the single hardest constraint dossier 04 found for production re-parenting (the placed-facility ledger-correlation invariant) cannot arise for a rival-to-rival move at all (16-gap-rival-ai-acquisition-code-feasibility.md).

**EXPLOIT — hostile takeover of the player.** Once symmetric law exists, the natural next step is "why can't a rival buy the player's studio the way the player can buy a rival's" — and Railway Empire shows exactly how badly this lands: "A competitor simply bought all of my shares and took over the business, and I simply failed the scenario!" (07-comparators-C.md). OpenTTD explicitly forbids the reverse case in code (`if (hostile_takeover && !c->is_ai) return CMD_ERROR`).
**GUARD:** **the player studio is never an acquisition target inside P16A–C.** "Symmetric transaction law" means identical valuation, premium, and eligibility rules for any two rival studios — it does not mean the player can be bought. Prior documentation independently reaches the same boundary (02-authority-corporate.md §176 — "the player studio can be a target only if the Owner separately decides a player-sale exit exists," which remains unmade and out of scope here). A player-initiated sale of their own studio as a deliberate ending is a distinct, separate, not-yet-made Owner decision (§20).

**EXPLOIT — AI over-consolidation.** Left unchecked, AI-to-AI acquisitions could consolidate the fixed nine-rival roster down toward a monopoly, since the accepted registry has no allocator for new studios and no rival-closure/replacement mechanism exists yet (02-authority-corporate.md §165).
**GUARD:** see §17 — the existing ≥3-active-rival floor becomes a hard **eligibility predicate** on every acquisition, and rival bidding frequency is governed by the same affordability gate applied to the player (§17, R29–R30).

## 11. Asset Sales & Exploit Guards

The Owner wants sellable Story Properties, film/library rights, and corporate assets (§2.K), with asset sales usable as a pre-bankruptcy recovery valve. The original already supplies the exact precedent and its anti-flip primitives: the Star & Script Selling Facility was cheap ($3,000), always available, buildable even in debt, and sold at a **disclosed, deterministic price** — but it already encoded two anti-flip rules that must be transposed forward: value rises with tenure to a full-value point at eight years, and declines with remaining useful life past a peak (01-original-game.md; prima_eguide.txt:519). Below are the exploit families named in the brief, each closed by the smallest natural rule:

**EXPLOIT — flip loops.** Buy a Story Property cheap from a distressed seller, immediately resell it to another (or the same) rival at a markup, repeat — GearCity's own community found exactly this ("about 7 billion $ from just buying and selling companies": 05-comparators-A.md).
**GUARD: holdback + anti-flip discount.** Resale of any acquired right is capped at ≤ acquisition cost for a fixed holdback window (paper: 52 weeks — the same order of magnitude as the original's real-money vesting curve, scaled down from years to weeks because the original's asset was a career-length talent, not a one-time IP purchase); after the window, price is governed by the ordinary market-value formula, never a resale-specific bonus. This is the transposed form of the original's tenure-vesting rule (00-KEY-FINDINGS.md §01, design implication).

**EXPLOIT — sell-and-keep-using.** Covered in §3 (rights-in-use lock). Restated here because it is the single most valuable exploit to close for asset sales specifically: without it, a player could sell library rights for cash while a sequel is already in silent production against the same property, collecting twice.

**EXPLOIT — duplicate ownership.** Covered in §3 (single-holder field + append-only, validator-enforced `ownershipEvents`).

**EXPLOIT — no-guaranteed-buyer money printing.** If every sale is guaranteed to find a buyer at a fixed formula price regardless of actual rival appetite or cash, asset sales become a risk-free cash faucet rather than a real market — this is exactly Blockbuster Inc.'s disabled equity-stake dividend and its self-described reason for disabling it ("this is disabled for now as the game was too easy": 06-comparators-B.md).
**GUARD:** AI bid/ask is anchored to the AI's own valuation of the asset with cash conservation enforced (an AI cannot buy what it cannot afford, and its bid price reflects its own strategic use for the property, not a blind formula) — Software Inc.'s developer explicitly named the opposite failure mode: "The calculation for deciding IP price was placeholder... it probably doesn't make sense in most cases," which players then exploited by having AI sell profitable IPs cheaply (10-rights-law.md). There is no guaranteed buyer; a sale can simply fail to find a taker.

**EXPLOIT — buy-cheap-in-distress-then-resell (liquidation arbitrage).** Buy a distressed target's tangible assets for less than their true recoverable value, then immediately liquidate them for a profit.
**GUARD:** covered structurally in §14 — Studio Valuation is defined as `max(going-concern basis, asset basis)`, which by construction prevents any lot from being priced below its own liquidation value; this single `max()` rule closes the arbitrage without any special-cased anti-flip rule for distressed deals specifically (09-valuation-finance.md, Scenario D check).

**Restrictions the Owner explicitly asked for** are satisfied by the above: no repeated flip (holdback), no selling-while-using (rights-in-use lock), no duplicate ownership (single-holder log). One further natural restriction: **a rejected or completed sale offer leaves a dated cooldown** on that exact counterparty for that exact asset, mirroring the cooldown pattern used for acquisitions (§17) — unexplained instant re-offers read as arbitrary the way Anno 1800's unexplained AI retaliation after a share purchase does (07-comparators-C.md).

## 12. Healthy-Studio Acquisition

Owner direction (§2.L) explicitly rejects "cross a hidden fixed multiplier and ownership automatically transfers." Every comparator and every real case researched agrees this is correct design instinct: GearCity's shareholders vote by target *health*, not price, and a healthy target simply refuses regardless of offer (05-comparators-A.md; howto_stockmarket); Disney rejected Comcast's unsolicited 2004 bid "as too low" and stated it would "stay the course with its current management" — price alone did not move the board (08-realworld-ma.md).

**Recommended model — a visible typed willingness state, not a hidden score:**

`NotForSale` → refuses regardless of price (Capitalism Lab's developer explicitly protects this state to preserve difficulty: 05-comparators-A.md) · `Reluctant` → will listen, asking price sits at the top of the premium band · `Open` → asking price sits mid-band · `Willing` (distress-adjacent, still not P15-distressed) → bottom of band. State is driven by disclosed inputs: recent performance/Standing trend, independence preference (a per-studio trait), and existing bid history — **never** hidden rival cash (see the disclosure guard below). Football Manager's `Desperate/Keen/Curious` agent tones plus a shown market value and time-limited price tags is the cleanest shipped precedent for this shape (07-comparators-C.md).

**Price = Enterprise Valuation × independence-premium band** (renamed from "control premium," see §14 for why). Real-world premiums cluster 20–40% and rise with rival interest (08-realworld-ma.md; Wikipedia "Control premium"); GearCity's takeover formula is the cleanest shipped analogue — a *larger* premium for profitable targets (1.75×) than for loss-makers (1.3×), i.e., **premium scales with recent success**, answering the Owner's §2.O question directly. **Repeated rejected bids have a consequence: a cooldown**, not an escalating price penalty — GearCity's one-turn cooldown after denial is the minimal shipped version (05-comparators-A.md).

**EXPLOIT — rival-cash omniscience.** If the player's offer screen can read a rival's true internal cash, obligations, or forecast, every healthy acquisition becomes a solved information game — bid exactly one dollar over the rival's known floor, every time.
**GUARD:** exact rival cash, obligations, budgets, and forecasts are already HIDDEN by design in the accepted projection (`bridge/industry.ts` exposes only public facts: identities, standing, film grosses, credits, current employer — 03-authority-domains.md §73, §155, 04-code-audit.md §232). A valuation offer screen must be priced from **public facts only**, or from an explicit new **due-diligence disclosure event** that reveals a bounded, dated snapshot only after an offer is formally opened — never a silent read of hidden internal state. This satisfies the Owner's "no opaque bargaining simulator" instruction from the *other* direction: the player sees a real number, but only after paying the cost of formally approaching the target, and the AI on the other side of a rival-initiated bid gets no unfair peek into the player's true position either.

**What the player sees, always:** Enterprise Valuation (band, not a hidden exact number pre-offer), the target's willingness state and its named reason, whether a rival bidder exists, and — once an offer is formally opened — a full disclosure snapshot (§14). No hidden multiplier ever exists in the formula; the formula itself should be one documented function, published in-fiction as "how our finance office estimates this," matching the transparency GearCity, OpenTTD, and Capitalism 2 all achieved and that opaque share-accumulation systems (Capitalism Lab's public-company path) conspicuously failed to.

## 13. Bankruptcy Auction/Acquisition

**Structural collision, stated exactly.** No P15 document contains the word "auction" or "liquidation" even once (02-authority-corporate.md §160). P15's closure-settlement invariant is *exhaustive*: every category (projects, obligations, contracts, people) either resolves or is cancelled/settled, and films/identity/history persist as unowned public record — there is no "estate" bucket left over for a buyer to claim (11-gap-p15-closure-settlement-estate-auction-reconciliation.md; P15 §12.3 lines 474-475, confirmed by a zero-hit grep for "estate" across the entire P15 package). As written today, **closure leaves nothing for a P16 transaction to acquire.**

**Smallest correction:** a closure-settlement request first checks whether a P16 acquisition transaction has already cleared at the frozen source revision. If one has, each owner's disposition becomes **assign-to-acquirer** instead of cancel/release/settle — landing on the already-distinct `acquired` terminal label (§9), never `closed`. This requires exactly one new typed disposition value added to the settlement machinery P15 §12.3 already specifies, and one new precondition check — no new estate-holding root, no new persistent catalogue (11-gap-p15-closure-settlement-estate-auction-reconciliation.md, smallest-correction finding). A bounded, deterministic window (paper: a fixed number of weeks while a studio sits `dormant`) is when this P16 transaction may run; if it does not clear before the window closes, ordinary settlement proceeds exactly as it does today.

**Which of the five alternatives (§2.M)?** **Hybrid, defaulting toward clean asset purchase**, matching both the real-world norm and every comparator with a distressed path:
- Default: **asset lots, free of the target's obligations**, each with its own reserve tied to Studio Valuation's liquidation floor (§14) — this is §363's actual shape (assets transfer free and clear, claims stay with the estate: 08-realworld-ma.md, Cornell LII §363/§365) and matches GearCity's creditor-sale memo ("this deal will come with an extra $X in liabilities" is the *opt-in* exception, not the default: 05-comparators-A.md).
- Buyer may **optionally assume named contracts or in-progress productions at a visible cure cost** to acquire them as a package at a lower net price than buying them clean (mirrors §363(a)'s executory-contract assumption-with-cure mechanic exactly).
- **Whole-entity purchase** is reserved for the case where the target remains a going concern under new ownership (a debt-for-equity-style reorganization, as MGM 2010 shows: "secured lenders exchange more than $4 billion in outstanding debt for most of the equity" — 08-realworld-ma.md) — this is the only path where the `StudioId` keeps operating under the buyer rather than terminating.
- **No live, round-based bidding war UI.** A sealed, one-shot comparison among eligible bidders at the window's close (ties broken by earliest bid) is simpler, avoids a tedious multi-round interface, and matches OpenTTD's fixed-window mechanic more closely than a real auction house — while avoiding OpenTTD's own worst anti-pattern, best-performer-first refusal ordering, which is a hidden pro-snowball lever (07-comparators-C.md). Rivals bid under the identical affordability and eligibility gates as the player (§17).

Price is never the going-concern Studio Valuation — it is a **liquidation-value reserve** (tangible recoverable value + any Story Property/library value that survives independently of the operating business), consistent with every real distressed case researched: Carolco's library sold for $47.5–58M against a studio that had cost far more to build; TWC's stalking-horse price *fell* from $310M to $289M with no competing bidders (08-realworld-ma.md). Distressed value can and should be able to evaporate — this is why Studio Valuation's `max()` floor (§14) matters more, not less, in bankruptcy, since it is the only thing stopping the reserve itself from being set arbitrarily low.

## 14. Book Net Worth vs. Enterprise Valuation vs. Transaction/Auction Price

**These must never collapse into one number** — this is the assignment's own highest-priority instruction (§2.N), and every mature comparator researched independently proves it is a few lines of code, not a design burden: OpenTTD keeps three distinct formulas (company/book value; hostile-takeover price; bankruptcy-offer price), GearCity publishes three (Price Evaluation, market cap, Takeover Price), and Capitalism 2's manual shows Net Worth beside Market Value explicitly to avoid double counting (09-valuation-finance.md).

**Ladder, in order:**

1. **Book Net Worth (BNW)** — a pure accounting identity, derived from facts P11 already tracks: `Cash + scheduled receipts + tangible at existing depreciated-recovery credit − typed obligations`. Never invented, never shown as a competing "Studio Worth" on P11's own surfaces (09-valuation-finance.md, design implication).
2. **Studio/Enterprise Valuation (SV)** = `max(going-concern basis, asset basis)`. Going-concern basis capitalizes trailing operating surplus (paper multiple, e.g., 3×, over a bounded trailing window) plus cash, receipts, Story Property/library value, and recoverable in-progress work; asset basis substitutes tangible-at-recoverable-credit for the capitalized-surplus term. **The `max()` is what closes the buy-cheap-in-distress-then-resell exploit (§11/§13)** — a lot can never be priced below its own liquidation floor.
3. **Transaction/Ask Price** — SV × the disclosed independence-premium band for a healthy deal (§12); a liquidation reserve for a distressed one (§13).
4. **Auction/Clearing Price** — whatever the sealed comparison actually resolves to; may sit below or above the reserve depending on rival interest, exactly like Carolco (Canal+ beat Fox's lowered $47.5M bid with $58M) or TWC (no bidders, price fell) (08-realworld-ma.md).

**EXPLOIT — valuation double counting.** The single most cited real-world failure mode: Amazon's own 10-Q had to separate $3.4B of video-content value from $4.9B of goodwill inside one $8.45B deal specifically *because* a blended number invites exactly this confusion (08-realworld-ma.md). Software Inc.'s "company worth" is a cautionary tale of the opposite failure — one recursive blended number, patched repeatedly, that resets to zero at takeover and produces player reports of feeling "robbed" (05-comparators-A.md; 09-valuation-finance.md).
**GUARD — one-lane-per-asset, as explicit product law:**
- Cash counted at par, never capitalized twice.
- Plant/tech/brand/people are inside the going-concern operating-surplus term *while that term is positive*; add the tangible **asset basis** only when it's the higher of the two lanes (the `max()`), never both simultaneously.
- Story Properties/library rights are counted once each (R1 for sequel potential, R2 for catalog value) as a **separate valuation line**, never inside the capitalized-surplus term (which reflects only current operating performance, not the library's static worth) and never inside asset basis either.
- Technology is buyer-relative avoided-research value only, shown in the buyer's *private* offer panel, never as a standalone line in the target's own valuation (a capability confers no monopoly over it — 09-valuation-finance.md §550).
- Brand/label value is informational only, never priced (§9) — no comparator that priced a brand line separately from its library survived scrutiny as non-arbitrary.
- No forecasts of future hits enter any line — P11 already forbids this for ordinary operations (types.ts:299-312 locked receipts; P11 law).

**Rename "control premium" → "independence premium."** Damodaran's actual "value of control" (optimal-minus-status-quo management value) is typically *small* for a well-run target — the opposite of what a large, arbitrary "control premium" implies (09-valuation-finance.md; Damodaran, "The Value of Control"). What the assignment is really describing is a **seller-side reluctance premium**: in a one-lot absorber, the buyer's rational ceiling (parts value: what the assets are actually worth to *this* buyer) is structurally below SV for a healthy target, so healthy deals are expensive *without any tuned multiplier at all* — this is verified arithmetically in Scenario C below. Naming it correctly in all player-facing text and documentation avoids importing a real-finance term that means something narrower and different.

## 15. Real-World Lessons

Five real cases carry direct, load-bearing design weight:

- **Amazon/MGM** proves library value and brand/strategic value must be two separate lines, never one blended figure ($3.4B content vs. $4.9B goodwill), and that a whole-company purchase transfers assumed debt at par ($2.5B repaid immediately at close) — 08-realworld-ma.md.
- **Disney/Fox** is the cleanest real Model C: brand retained as a label ("20th Century Studios," dropping "Fox"), operations absorbed, the physical lot stayed with the seller, and the inherited slate produced a real, documented loss ($170M) — proving inherited-production write-downs are a legitimate anti-snowball cost, not a bug to patch away.
- **DreamWorks/Soros** proves library, distribution rights, continuation rights, and brand are four *independently* tradable things even within one operating studio, with buy-backs occurring at different prices years apart ($900M sold, ~$400M repurchased) — exactly the encumbered-sale pattern §3's rights-in-use guard needs to support.
- **Spider-Man (Marvel/Sony)** is the single cleanest real rights model found in the entire research program: property stays with the owner, an activity-conditioned exclusive licence goes to another party, and reversion is a readable clock (release a film at least every 5.75 years or lose it) — this is the direct real-world ancestor of the R3 `useItOrLoseIt` field in §3.
- **The Weinstein Company §363 sale** demonstrates every alternative in §2.M simultaneously in one real transaction: a stalking-horse floor, a price that moved *down* with no competing bids, assets transferred free and clear, a capped, buyer-chosen list of assumed contracts, and old talent participations left stranded with the estate — this is the primary real-world template for §13's hybrid.

The universal negative lesson across every case: **facilities never teleport.** Every single real acquisition researched left the target's physical lot with someone other than the buyer, or sold it as a wholly separate transaction (Fox's lot, RKO's lot, the MGM Culver City lot Lorimar had bought and Sony later took) — reinforcing §7's automatic-liquidation default as not merely a code-convenience but the observed real pattern.

## 16. Comparator Lessons

The tycoon/management genre converges on a small set of hard-won lessons that the assignment's evidence base independently corroborates across nine-plus titles:

- **Autonomous subsidiaries (Model B) fail everywhere they ship**, for the identical reason in three unrelated engines (MGT2's timer-fiction, Capitalism Lab's AI-CEO override, Software Inc.'s "never ending cash" faucet) — §5's rejection of Model B is not a judgment call, it is a repeated, independently-observed genre failure.
- **A transferable asset that gates access to the shared market snowballs without bound** — Hollywood Animal's cinemas are the clearest cautionary tale ("Buying cinemas is basically the meta... You will practically destroy every other competitor"), and the only fix the developer found was a blunt, resented scripted reset. The good news, verified directly against Project: Studio's own accepted code: **no such resource exists here** — the shared-market design P15A recommends is a symmetric per-release saturation pressure that degrades every release in a crowded window equally, including the releaser's own, and screen/exhibition capacity (the literal cinema analogue) was already named and deliberately excluded from P15A's scope for exactly this reason before any comparator research began (12-gap-p15-shared-market-capacity-concentration-check.md). This is a genuine, independently-confirmed non-collision — no correction is needed here, only a forward flag: *if* screen/exhibition capacity is ever authorized later, its authoring package must adopt a per-studio, size-independent cap from day one.
- **Partial ownership stakes are a uniformly bad idea.** OpenTTD removed share trading outright as an "infinite money" exploit with "ABSOLUTELY NOTHING" added for the player; Blockbuster Inc. had to disable its own stake dividends because the game became trivially easy; Startup Company's unrestricted share-buying produced a documented complete monopoly. No comparator's partial-ownership system survived scrutiny (06/07-comparators.md).
- **Willingness must be a visible typed stance with a real refusal right**, never a hidden multiplier — GearCity, Football Manager, Big Ambitions, and OpenTTD's own hostile-takeover carve-out (AI-only, by design) all converge on this, and the counter-examples (Capitalism Lab's opaque share accumulation, Startup Company's no-refusal monopoly path) are the explicit anti-patterns to avoid.
- **Natural, non-arbitrary brakes beat every artificial cap tried**: size-scaled cooldowns (GearCity), integration/operating-cost burden (MGT2 players independently discovered this as "the natural brake"), inherited-slate losses (Fox), and world/entrant refill (GearCity's "new AI companies will be generated if the game world becomes too empty") all worked without ever capping the *number* of allowed acquisitions outright.

## 17. Anti-Snowball

Owner direction (§2.S) explicitly rejects artificial M&A caps and asks for the minimum necessary protection. Project: Studio's own accepted economy gives this urgency it would not otherwise have: the game's existing snowball gate **already fails** even before any M&A exists — "the current brake on snowballing is the death spiral itself," with cash concentration measured at ~20% world-share runaway (07-comparators-C.md; D-16/D-17B evidence). **Any M&A system is a positive-feedback amplifier layered on an economy that has not yet demonstrated it can bound snowballing without one; G11 (the snowball gate) must be re-run with M&A enabled before shipping, not assumed to still pass.**

**EXPLOIT — serial acquisition snowball.** A single successful studio buys three, five, ten rivals in a row, compounding cash, library, and roster advantage with no natural limit, exactly the failure OTC's designer diagnosed directly: "the winner would usually snowball forward" under an all-or-nothing buyout model (07-comparators-C.md).
**GUARD — layered, all natural, no cap:**
1. **Affordability gate**: a bid is legal only if post-close cash remains ≥ N weeks (paper: 26) of the bidder's *combined* own-plus-newly-inherited recurring obligations. This alone prevents "buy now, can't make payroll next week" and scales the brake automatically with deal size — the bigger the target, the harder the next one becomes to afford, without any explicit counter.
2. **Independence premium** (§12) already makes every healthy deal genuinely expensive relative to its parts value — a structural cost, not a tuned penalty.
3. **Integration friction** (§6: contract triage, production triage, physical liquidation) imposes real per-deal overhead that scales with target size.
4. **Size-scaled cooldown** on the *buyer's own* next acquisition, precedented directly by GearCity ("the larger the company, the longer you will have to wait before you can buy another") — this is the single most important lever the brief calls for and it is fully comparator-precedented.
5. **Per-target cooldown** after any rejected or completed deal (§11, §12), preventing repeated immediate re-approaches.

**EXPLOIT — talent-pool concentration.** The world's talent population is a fixed, non-replenishing 60 people under strict one-employer exclusivity, with no aging/retirement/entrant law yet (12-gap-p15-shared-market-capacity-concentration-check.md). If inherited contracts transfer intact with no bound, a serial acquirer could progressively corner a growing share of the entire fixed population — a slower, partial, labor-market cousin of Hollywood Animal's cinema snowball, and the *one* resource in the accepted game that is genuinely capacity-limited and contested across all studios today.
**GUARD:** bind an acquirer's **assumed headcount to its own usable production/development capacity**, mirroring the discipline rival AI already applies to itself (rivals only ever staff to fill their one active production, never accumulate idle contracts: `hollywoodTick.ts` `staff()`, `if(b.productions.length!==0)break` — 12-gap-p15-shared-market-capacity-concentration-check.md). Inherited contracts beyond that bound settle under ordinary P10 termination law within a bounded integration window, rather than being held indefinitely as a hoarding lockout. This is the smallest correction and requires no new arbitrary cap — it reuses a rule the engine already enforces on every rival every week.

**EXPLOIT — AI over-consolidation.** With symmetric law (§10), nothing currently stops rival-to-rival acquisitions from consolidating the fixed nine-rival roster toward a monopoly, since no allocator exists for replacement studios.
**GUARD:** the existing **≥3-active-rival floor** (already-settled envelope law: "minimum of 3 active AI rivals" — 02-authority-corporate.md §165) becomes a **hard eligibility predicate** on every acquisition transaction, player- or AI-initiated: a transaction that would drop active studios below the floor is refused with a typed reason, full stop, until a separately-authorized P15B replacement-entrant slice exists. This is not a new cap on *how much* any one studio can own — it is the existing law simply enforced at the one point (registry depletion) where it was not previously tested.

**Additional minimum protections**, all natural and already precedented: no partial ownership stakes anywhere in P16A–C (§16); every refusal and blocked transaction returns a typed, disclosed reason, never a silent failure (OpenTTD's undocumented "Can't buy company" and Football Manager's "not for sale at any price" complaints are the identical UX failure from two different genres — 07-comparators-C.md); rejected/hostile bids leave a dated relationship event rather than an unexplained consequence. **No antitrust/regulatory mechanism is recommended at this stage** — the ≥3-rival floor plus the layered cost/cooldown stack above are sufficient, matching the Owner's explicit "only if absolutely necessary later" instruction; GearCity's own outcome-based >75%-share monopoly suit is the natural next lever *if* the floor alone proves insufficient after playtesting, never a first-resort design.

## 18. Package Boundary

**P16A/B/C, as the Owner's current recommendation, is confirmed workable** against the accepted code's actual seams, with one important provenance correction: no prior Future Ops document (the Control Board Revision 03, the Deferred-Not-Dropped Register, or either sibling ops doc) actually states the P16A/B/C split, Model D, or the BNW/valuation/premium framing as a prior recommendation — an exhaustive keyword search of all four documents found zero hits for all three (14-gap-future-ops-control-board-verification.md). All three are genuinely **assignment-originated premises**, independently corroborated by this research program's code audit, comparator survey, and finance-theory review — a provenance-precision correction only; none of the three premises themselves needs to change.

| Package | Owns | Depends on |
|---|---|---|
| **P16A** — Library, Story Properties & Rights | `StoryProperty`/R1–R4/`Licence` identities, `ownershipEvents` root, mint-at-greenlight rule, library valuation read-model | Nothing outside P16; can charter first |
| **P16B** — Rights & Asset Transactions | Sale/licence transactions between active studios, deterministic pricing, holdback/anti-flip law, disclosure law | P16A + existing P11 ledger/P12 receipt patterns |
| **P16C** — Studio Acquisition & Integration | Whole-studio transaction (healthy + distressed), transfer-bundle execution, terminal identity events, integration friction/cooldowns | P16A/B + a new P12 `StudioIdentity`-status resolution (H5, §5) + P15B's distress/terminal slice for distressed targets + P14's existing "sale/assignment" employer-transition category |

Existing-package touch points, minimal and additive only: **P11** gains new `LedgerKind`/`RivalMoneyKind` entries for transaction cash movements (no new instrument, no debt object). **P12** gains the terminal-business-state resolution (H5) and the three dated terminal events (§9) on the existing immutable `StudioId` — no second registry. **P13** gains one additive route/provenance value for inherited knowledge and reuses its existing closure-disposition manifest with one new value (`transferredToSuccessor`). **P14** gains one new employer-transition reason code (`assumed`) using its already-legal "sale/assignment" category — P14D's broader advanced-mobility scope stays parked. **P15** is untouched in scope except for the one-sentence gate in §13 (check for a cleared P16 transaction before settlement); P15B's rival-closure slice remains a **separate, still-unmade Owner decision** that P16C depends on for distressed targets only — healthy acquisitions and asset sales (P16A/B) have no such dependency and can ship independently.

**P16D or P17/P18:** Co-productions remain explicitly parked outside P16A–C, exactly as currently scoped — nothing in this research surfaced compelling evidence to reopen that exclusion (no comparator's co-production system was strong enough to recommend importing, and the Owner's own framing already treats it as "not currently selected"). Partial ownership stakes/equity (§16, §17) belong in **P16D at the earliest, if ever** — every comparator's version failed, and no design in this report depends on one existing. A future P16D reissue/re-release mechanic is the natural home for turning stored library *value* into library *cash* once P18's distribution/media system exists (§4) — P16A should record reissue *authority* only (who holds R2), never execute it, since release is exact-once law today and a reissue needs P07/P11/P15 co-authority this report does not attempt to grant. **P17** consumes R1/R3 exactly as already accepted documentation requires (a sequel is a new concept/script linked by an explicit edge, never a join on `originConceptId`); **P18** consumes R4/R2 grants under exact P16 authority — both packages remain pure consumers, minting nothing.

**EXPLOIT — save bloat.** An ownership/transaction system that writes a row per property per week, or that bulk-mints a `StoryProperty` for every one of thousands of films at migration, compounds a save-growth problem the accepted game **already exceeds its own target on** (a 49.6MB receipt at week 8791 — 04-code-audit.md §231).
**GUARD:** every ownership/transaction row is a **per-transaction, dated event**, never a per-week fact; valuation is a **derived read model, recomputed on demand**, never persisted weekly (matching the existing pattern for `rankSnapshots`); `StoryProperty` rows are created **lazily**, on first transaction or continuation, rather than bulk-created for every existing film at migration (§9's guard against migration backfill doubles as the save-bloat guard here — the two exploits share one fix). Any future projection over the library must page by a bounded, append-stable cursor and never enumerate the whole catalogue to compute a single price (00-KEY-FINDINGS.md §02, design implication).

## Paper Scenarios A–F

All figures are PAPER HYPOTHESES for illustrating the mechanics above, not tuning recommendations.

**A — Bankrupt Small Studio.** Weak cash (−$40K), a modest 6-property library (paper value $180K total), 8 employees, no valuable technology. Asset basis = tangible recoverable ($60K) + library ($180K) + receipts owed ($0) = $240K; going-concern basis ≈ $0 (no positive trailing surplus to capitalize) → **SV = max($0, $240K) = $240K**. A whole-company purchase at anything near $240K makes little sense versus **asset lots**: the buyer pays only for the properties actually wanted (say 3 of the 6, $90K) plus, if desired, an optional assumed-contracts package for the 8 employees at a visible cure cost — cheaper and cleaner than a whole-entity buy that also inherits every contract and property whether wanted or not. **§13's hybrid correctly favors asset lots here.**

**B — Valuable Distressed Studio.** Heavy negative cash (−$2.1M, i.e., deeply committed obligations), an excellent 40-property library (paper value $6.2M), strong completed research (2 tiers ahead of the market average), 4 active productions (committed cost $3.4M). Book Net Worth is *negative* (obligations exceed cash + recoverable tangible) — a BNW-only view says "worthless, avoid." Enterprise Valuation tells a different story: asset basis = tangible + library ($6.2M) + recoverable production work (~$1.5M of the $3.4M committed) ≈ $7.7M+, comfortably positive **despite negative BNW**. This is precisely why the three-number ladder (§14) matters: a BNW-only reading and an SV-only reading of the *same studio* point in opposite directions, and the auction/transaction price will land somewhere in between depending on rival interest — never equal to either.

**C — Healthy Rival.** Profitable, strong brand, valuable properties (library $4.5M, strong recent Standing trend), willingness state `Reluctant`. SV (going-concern, capitalized trailing surplus) ≈ $9.7M. The buyer's own rational parts value — what those same assets are actually worth folded into the buyer's *own* one-lot operation, after integration friction and without double-counting the seller's now-redundant management overhead — is structurally lower, paper ≈ $6.86M (09-valuation-finance.md, Scenario C figure). **The independence-premium band (top of band, since `Reluctant` + strong recent performance) pushes the ask well above even SV**, to roughly $11–12M — meaning the buyer pays a real premium *over* a number that is already above their own parts value. This is the correct, non-arbitrary shape of "healthy acquisitions are expensive": no tuned multiplier was needed to produce the gap, only the honest comparison of two already-defined numbers (SV vs. buyer parts value) plus the disclosed band.

**D — Technology Target.** Player wants Studio X primarily for its Tier-III technology (player is at Tier-II). At close: player gains a new `TechnologyAdoption` row at `readyToAdopt` for Tier-III, citing Studio X's provenance — **no physical facility appears on the player's lot**. The player must still run the ordinary P09 install/build job for Tier-III capability. If Studio X had in-flight Tier-IV research underway, it is **not** inherited (P13-OD-06); it is resolved via the existing closure-disposition manifest (cancelled-with-receipt, or a new `transferredToSuccessor` value if the Owner authorizes one — §18). Verifies the Owner's "knowledge transfers, installations do not" principle exactly as stated, with the one clarification that "higher tier" never means "faster tier," only "eligible for the same install-time as if researched independently."

**E — Serial Acquirer.** Player buys three studios over ten years (roughly one every ~175 weeks). By acquisition 2, the affordability gate (§17) requires the player to hold 26 weeks of *combined* obligations post-close — after absorbing Studio 2's committed payroll and production costs, the player's next bid ceiling drops relative to raw cash on hand. By acquisition 3, the size-scaled cooldown (§17, GearCity-precedented) means a meaningful wait since the *second* deal closed, not just since founding. Talent-pool concentration (§17): if all three targets' full rosters transferred with no bound, the player could end acquisition 3 holding a double-digit percentage of the entire fixed 60-person world population — the capacity-bound guard caps this at whatever the player's own three-studio-worth of usable production/development slots can actually absorb, with the remainder settling under ordinary termination law rather than being hoarded. Registry-floor check: three absorptions against a fixed nine-rival roster is exactly the point where the ≥3-active-rival floor (§17) starts to bind — the fourth attempted acquisition, if it would drop active rivals below three, is refused with a typed reason regardless of the player's cash position.

**F — Rival Acquires Rival.** Rival R03 (healthy, `Open` state) is acquired by Rival R07 under the identical transaction law used for a player purchase (§10) — same valuation ladder, same independence-premium band, same affordability gate, same disclosure law (R07 never reads R03's true hidden cash; the transaction runs on the same public-facts-plus-disclosure-event basis a player bid would). R03's `StudioId` receives an `acquired` event with `successorStudioId = R07`; R03's films keep `studioId = R03` (creator, frozen forever) while a new P16 ownership relation records `currentHolder = R07`. If R07 retains R03 as a label, R03 continues to appear in every historical/searchable view exactly as before, now visibly tagged "Acquired by R07: Week N" — never rewritten as if R07 had always owned R03's catalogue. The scenario is, by design, cheaper to build than the player-crossing case (§10) because both sides share identical container shapes — confirming the same-seed player/rival state-swap symmetry harness recommended as a package-blocking test (09-valuation-finance.md, design implication).

## 20. Genuine Remaining Owner Decisions ONLY

These are decisions this report cannot make on the Owner's behalf, because Phase-1 evidence shows no existing law resolves them and no comparator or real-world case supplies a uniquely correct default:

1. **Does the Owner authorize a P15B rival-closure/settlement slice at all**, and if so, with the minimal pre-settlement transaction gate (§13) or a larger persistent estate catalogue? P16C's distressed-acquisition path has no substrate without this.
2. **Is a player-sale ending (the player's own studio as an eventual acquisition target or voluntary sale) ever wanted**, as a wholly separate, explicitly-gated terminal option? §10 assumes no; this remains open only as a future, distinct decision, never as ordinary P16A–C symmetric law.
3. **Which target-finance facts become disclosable to a bidder, and at which transaction phase** (§12's disclosure guard proposes a due-diligence event; the exact bounded snapshot contents and timing are an Owner call).
4. **Is any P11 debt instrument ever introduced** (currently Owner-blocked)? Without one, "debt transfers" and "debt to finance deals" remain unavailable as concepts, not merely undesigned.
5. **What entitlement class does an acquirer inherit from a target's `InventionProvenance`** — P13 already names "inherited entitlement" as an explicitly undecided later case (§6, Q7); this report recommends inheriting the buyer-relative knowledge advantage but the exact entitlement-record mechanism is P13's to specify.
6. **Whether the ≥3-active-rival floor becomes the permanent ceiling on total absorptions, or whether a P15B replacement-entrant slice is separately authorized** to allow the world to refresh over a very long campaign (§17).
7. **Exact tuning values** — the affordability-gate window (paper 26 weeks), the anti-flip holdback (paper 52 weeks), the going-concern capitalization multiple (paper 3×), the independence-premium band's exact percentages, and every cooldown length are all paper hypotheses awaiting playtesting, not owner-decidable in the abstract.
8. **Whether a small flat valuation credit for unproduced/shelved properties (§4) is wanted at all**, versus treating them as valueless exactly as the original did.

## 21. Remaining Uncertainties

- The mint-point recommendation (Greenlight, §3) is the strongest of the three candidates evidence supports (Greenlight/Release/Commission), but Release remains a defensible alternative if the Owner prefers "only films that actually exist have Story Properties."
- Whether original-parity unproduced-script sales (the Star & Script Selling Facility's script half) belong inside P16B at all, or stay a P12-owned `ScriptProject` transfer outside the rights system entirely, is unresolved by evidence either way.
- The exact remediation for the H5 terminal-business-state collision (§5) — a `StudioIdentity` status field with an amended validator predicate, versus a frozen/emptied placeholder `RivalBusiness` row — is an implementation choice this report does not resolve, only names as mandatory before shipping.
- Whether `assumed` contract succession (§6, Q3) is ordinary P12 employer-transition law (this report's recommendation) or falls under P14D's parked advanced-mobility scope is a package-authorship question, not a design question; both are internally consistent with the rules above.
- Real-world continuation-rights pricing tracked the *last film's* performance in every case researched (Terminator's six sequential valuations, each anchored to the prior installment); whether R3 pricing should read P07 box-office outcomes directly, or be left entirely to P17's own franchise-heat model, is not resolved here.
- Whether a single "shared-control / cannot exploit until bought out" flag (the real-world Bond/Eon, Hulk/Universal pattern) belongs in P16A's rights model or is P17's problem to represent is unresolved; no evidence forced a choice.

## RULE LIST

R01. **[P16A]** A `StoryProperty` is minted explicitly by exact ID at Greenlight, never inferred or bulk-created from title/genre/cast resemblance. *(10-rights-law.md; consumer contract §15 "explicitly by exact ID"; 04-code-audit.md screenplay.ts:305-345 origin split)*

R02. **[P16A]** Rights are exactly four kinds — R1 Property, R2 Film (both sellable, single-holder) and R3 Continuation, R4 Cross-Media (both licensable-only, never sold apart) — with no territory or media sub-splits. *(10-rights-law.md; WGA Separated Rights, DreamWorks/Soros, Sony/Marvel)*

R03. **[P16A]** Every right/licence has a single-holder field plus an append-only, validator-enforced `ownershipEvents` log where a transfer's `from` must equal the current holder. *(03-authority-domains.md reserved `ownershipEvents` root; 07-comparators-C.md CK3 title model)*

R04. **[P16A]** Rights-in-use lock: no sale of R1, or grant of exclusive R3, on a property with an active production against it, unless the production transfers with it or the seller's retained licence is recorded on the sale receipt. *(10-rights-law.md; MGT2 "no games currently being sold"; 08-realworld-ma.md DreamWorks/Soros encumbered sale)*

R05. **[P16A]** Library value is a valuation read-model only — zero weekly cash flow — until a P18 distribution/media channel exists; it never feeds Standing or Power Ranking. *(03-authority-domains.md types.ts:304-316; 09-valuation-finance.md "Standing explicitly excludes valuation")*

R06. **[P16B]** Every sellable asset shows a deterministic market-value price before any sale is offered; no hidden formula. *(01-original-game.md Star hover-price precedent; 05-comparators-A.md GearCity published formulas)*

R07. **[P16B]** Resale of an acquired right is capped at ≤ its acquisition cost for a fixed holdback window (paper 52 weeks); ordinary market pricing applies only after. *(01-original-game.md eight-year Star vesting; 05-comparators-A.md GearCity flip-exploit patches)*

R08. **[P16B]** No guaranteed buyer for any asset sale: AI bid/ask is anchored to its own valuation with cash conservation; a sale may simply find no taker. *(10-rights-law.md Software Inc. placeholder-price exploit; 06-comparators-B.md Blockbuster Inc. disabled faucet)*

R09. **[P16A]** Shelved/unproduced properties retain ownership permanently and (by default) valuelessly; no abandon-for-refund path. *(01-original-game.md GameSpot walkthrough shelving precedent)*
