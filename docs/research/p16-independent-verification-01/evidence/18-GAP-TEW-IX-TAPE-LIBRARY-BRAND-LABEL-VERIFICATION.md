## 1. Scope

Single question, assigned as gap 8: does **Total Extreme Wrestling IX** (Grey Dog Software / Adam Ryland, released August 2024 — a distinct, newer product from TEW 2020, appid 1148810, already checked and cleared by dossier06) ship a mechanic in which a player promotion buys a rival or defunct promotion's **video/tape library and/or historical brand**, retainable **purely as a label** (name, history, credit — zero ongoing operations)? This premise was the unresolved half of two independent prior research passes (dossier06 §3.H/F25 checked TEW **2020**'s handbook and found no tape/library mechanic there; dossier07 §3H/F22 tried the TEW **IX** developer-journal mirror directly and was blocked twice by HTTP 403, getting only as far as two suggestively-titled YouTube videos with mechanics unverified). This dossier does not revisit TEW 2020 and does not relitigate anything already settled in those two dossiers; it targets TEW IX specifically, using the four approaches the assignment specifies, and states a final verdict with a recommendation for the report.

## 3. Findings

**F1 (NEW). The official TEW IX developer's journal — the primary, most authoritative feature-announcement source for this exact product — contains no tape library, video library, footage archive, or brand-as-pure-label mechanic anywhere in its full run.**
- Source: Adam Ryland (Grey Dog Software, developer/administrator), "TEW IX Announcement & Developer's Journal," Grey Dog Software forum, topic 57253. OFFICIAL. Accessed via Wayback Machine snapshot (`http://web.archive.org/web/20250908210800/https://forum.greydogsoftware.com/topic/57253-tew-ix-announcement-developers-journal/`, captured 2025-09-08).
- Locator: full thread text, 25 dated dev-journal entries from "Posted July 1, 2024" (the announcement itself, confirming beta July 27 / retail release August 3, 2024, price $34.95) through "Posted July 25, 2024" ("Day 19"), i.e. the entire pre-release journal run for this title, plus all first-page reader comments. Keyword sweep of the full extracted text (139,831 characters): `library` = 0 hits, `footage` = 0, `archive` = 0, `buy out`/`buyout`/`bought out` = 0, `label` = 0, `promotion` (as a noun for a rival company) = 0, `tape` = 3 hits (all unrelated: "pre-taped" as an angle-scheduling status, and a "sex tape scandal" narrative event reworked into a "generic sex scandal"), `brand` = 7 hits (all "brand-new [feature]" or cosmetic "Brand Logos And Backdrops," none about a company's brand-as-asset).
- What it proves: the specific mechanic named in the assignment's premise — a rival's tape/video library and/or brand purchasable and retainable as a pure label with zero ongoing operations — is **absent from the single most on-point official source** for this exact title. This is a stronger and more direct result than either prior dossier obtained: dossier06 checked the wrong game (TEW 2020) and dossier07 could not reach TEW IX's own journal at all.
- Confidence: HIGH (primary developer text, full run, direct keyword verification, quoted above).
- Prior Project: Studio prose status: NEW (closes the open question dossier06/07 both flagged).

**F2 (NEW). TEW IX's actual company-ownership mechanic — confirmed present in this same journal — is the same parent/child acquisition model already documented from TEW 2020 in dossier06 F25 and dossier07 F22, not a library/label purchase; and it is the opposite of "zero ongoing operations."**
- Source: same developer's journal, entries dated July 10 and July 25, 2024. OFFICIAL.
- Locator, quoted verbatim: "**Request Call Up** When playing as the booker of a child company, the player can now choose to contact the parent company (whether the parent is controlled by the player or AI) and request that they consider calling up a worker." / "**Locking Child Production Values** By popular request, you can now set whether your child company is autonomous or not when it comes to changing their own production values or relying on you to do it for them." / "**Cutting Child Companies Loose** The user can now choose to 'cut loose' any of their child companies; this means that they become independent. This is something quite a few players have asked for over the years as it means you can get rid of a child company you no longer want / need without having to actually kill them off and lose all their history." / "**Removing Children From Alliances** If playing as a parent company, the user can automatically expel or remove their child companies from alliances when required."
- What it proves: TEW IX's ownership model is a live parent/child relationship in which the acquired ("child") company **continues to run its own product, roster and shows** — it can be made autonomous or non-autonomous, called on for talent, or "cut loose" back to independence, and only loses its distinct operating identity if the owner chooses to fold it. This is functionally closest to the Owner's **Model B (Autonomous Subsidiary)** — the model the assignment itself expects Future Ops research to find "expect this to conflict with Owner preference" — not Model C/D's "retain brand purely as a label, no ongoing operations." History is explicitly preserved even when a child is cut loose ("without... los[ing] all their history"), which does support the *history-preservation* half of the Owner's premise, just not the *zero-operations-label* half.
- Confidence: HIGH (primary text, verbatim, cross-confirms dossier06 F25's TEW 2020 description of the same "child company... absorbed by the parent" system carrying forward unchanged into TEW IX).
- Prior Project: Studio prose status: CONFIRMED (dossier06 F25 / dossier07 F22's parent/child description, now extended to TEW IX specifically) for the ownership mechanism; CORRECTED for the tape-library framing (see Finding 5).

## 4. Verdict on the assignment's premise

**The "buy a rival/defunct promotion's tape library and/or historical brand, retainable purely as a label with zero ongoing operations" premise is NOT VERIFIED for Total Extreme Wrestling IX, and the balance of evidence now points toward "this specific mechanic does not exist in TEW IX" rather than merely "not yet found."**

This is stronger than dossier06's and dossier07's status quo ("UNVERIFIED" from checking the wrong game / from being blocked outright), because this pass reached the actual, correct, fullest available OFFICIAL primary source for TEW IX (the complete pre-release developer's journal, the single document Grey Dog Software itself uses to announce every new mechanic) and did a systematic keyword sweep of its entire text, plus the developer's own product page, plus two independent community/media sources, and found the claimed mechanic in none of them — while positively confirming, in the same primary source, what TEW IX's company-acquisition mechanic actually is (F2: an operating parent/child subsidiary system carried forward from TEW 2020), which is materially different from and inconsistent with the "pure label, zero ops" premise. Absence-of-evidence across a developer's own feature-announcement thread does not amount to logical proof that no such option exists anywhere in the game (a specific menu label, an obscure end-game "close down and keep the name" flavor option, or in-game help text could in principle still contain something not covered by a journal entry) — hence the verdict remains formally UNVERIFIED-leaning-NEGATIVE rather than a flat REFUTED. But for the report's purposes, there is no positive evidence for this premise anywhere it has now been checked, across two independently-run dossiers and four search strategies.

**Recommendation for the report: drop the "TEW tape-library" framing entirely.** Do not cite Total Extreme Wrestling (2020 or IX) as a real precedent for "buy the library, keep the brand as a label, zero ongoing operations." What TEW *does* usefully evidence for P16/Model C/D (already captured correctly in dossier06 F25/§5 and dossier07 F22/F23 and reaffirmed here) is: (a) a persistent parent/child ownership relationship with history preservation even after divestiture (F2 here; F22 in dossier07), and (b) the real-world WWE tape-library acquisition analogue from dossier07 F23 (Wrestlenomics interview), which remains a legitimate, properly-sourced comparator for "library value materializes only through an owning distribution channel" — but that analogue is a **real-world WWE fact**, not a TEW **game mechanic**, and the two should not be conflated in the final report. If the Owner recalls a specific source for the "buy tape library, keep brand as label" claim, it should be re-checked directly against that source before appearing in the report; absent that, treat it as not evidenced by any TEW product.

## 5. Design implications for P16 (INFERENCE — not evidenced by TEW; offered only because the gap-closure surfaced a relevant contrast)

1. **INFERENCE.** TEW's actual parent/child model (F2) is a useful negative example for Owner Model C/D: it shows what happens when "retain brand as label" is *not* enforced as zero-ops — the child keeps its own roster/schedule/autonomy toggle, which is exactly the "second separately managed studio" the assignment's §2.E explicitly says the Owner does not want and warns against ("Labels should not become a second separately managed studio unless Owner later changes direction"). This is a confirming data point *against* importing TEW's model literally, not a comparator to emulate.
2. **INFERENCE.** Because no shipped comparator found across dossiers 06/07/18 implements "acquire → retain name as a pure, non-operating label," the "brand survives as a production label" element of Model C/D (assignment §2.E) should be treated as an **original design element for Project: Studio**, not a borrowed, pre-validated pattern — it should get correspondingly more design scrutiny (e.g., explicit paper-scenario testing per §3 of the assignment) rather than being waved through on "other games do this."
