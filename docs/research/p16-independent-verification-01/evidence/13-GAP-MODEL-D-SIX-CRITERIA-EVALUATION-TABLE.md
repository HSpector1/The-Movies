## 4. The single code fact that governs five of the twenty-four cells

Assignment's own framing anticipates exactly this: *"code today cannot represent a non-operating rival at all, which is a management-burden/AI-compatibility fact independent of any comparator."* That fact is dossier 04 **F2.3**, verbatim:

> `hollywoodValidation.ts:331`: `requireFact(businesses.size === h.identities.filter(s=>s.role==='rival'&&s.enteredWeek!==null).length,'entry missing business')` ... `hollywoodTick.ts:221-288` iterates `for(const b of h.businesses)` unconditionally: staff, decide, operate, release, pay runs, payroll/overhead/opex. Proves: there is no representation of "operations ended". An absorbed studio cannot be stopped without (a) removing its business (validator fails) or (b) leaving it running (it keeps hiring and greenlighting).

This single fact is the reason:
- Models A, C and D all show LOW management burden and HIGH AI/rival compatibility **only conditionally** ("once H5 ships") — the code cannot yet stop simulating an absorbed business at all, so "the target stops being a thing to run" is presently a documentation aspiration, not a shipped capability, regardless of which of A/C/D the Owner ultimately exercises per deal.
- Model B shows HIGH (worst) on both of the same two criteria **unconditionally** — B's whole premise is that the business keeps running, so the fix that rescues A/C/D (a terminal state that skips the tick and freezes reconciliation, dossier 04 H5) does not apply to B at all. B is not merely the worst choice today; it is structurally incapable of ever reaching the low-burden outcome A/C/D reach once H5 lands.
- Model B's save/performance cell is HIGH (worst) unconditionally for the identical reason: `RivalAccount.periods` and the business's production/development/run arrays (F3.2, F9.4) are unbounded and keep growing for as long as the business stays live — which under B is forever.

## 5. Design implications for P16 (my inference, explicitly labelled — not Owner direction)

1. **The Model-D recommendation is demonstrated, not merely asserted, by this table** — D dominates or ties A/B/C on every one of the six criteria and never inherits B's two unconditional failure modes (management burden, save/performance), because D's menu structurally excludes B. This synthesis is new; no prior dossier assembled it, though dossiers 00, 02, 05, 06, 07, 08 each independently supplied one or two of the underlying facts (see §6).
2. **H5 (a terminal `RivalBusiness` state that the tick loop and validator both honour) is a prerequisite for shipping *any* of A, C, or D**, not merely a nice-to-have — five of the eighteen non-B cells in the table are qualified "once H5 ships." Recommend P16C treat H5 as a blocking dependency on P15B's closure-settlement lifecycle (dossier 04 §5 point 3; dossier 04 OQ-1 asks explicitly which package owns the terminal edge) rather than a P16-only concern.
3. **Model B should not be offered as a menu option even as a "future" fourth choice**, because it is the only model whose worst-criteria cells (management burden, save/performance) are not fixed by the same engineering investment that fixes A/C/D. Every comparator source that made B tolerable (Offworld Trading Company, 07 F9) did so by stripping B down to a zero-operations financial asset — which is not autonomous management at all, it is Owner §2.G option 3, already in scope without needing a fourth acquisition model.
4. **The AI-compatibility column for D is the one place a design artifact already exists ahead of ratification**: dossier 09 §4.5 item 5's deterministic label-choice policy ("retain label if target `industryPrestige` ≥ buyer's") is unratified paper, not Owner-approved law (see Open Questions). Recommend the final report cite it as a *candidate* rule the Owner may adopt or replace, not as settled.
