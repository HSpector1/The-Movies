# 01-original-game.md

## Summary
Retail The Movies (2005) and Stunts & Effects (2006) contained NO acquisition, merger, subsidiary/label, co-production, licensing, sequel/remake/franchise, library-revenue, re-release, bankruptcy, or bankrupt-asset-sale mechanics. Every retail-class source (official manual, developer-reviewed Prima guide, S&E manual, GameSpot walkthrough and review, IGN review, three GameFAQs guides) is silent on all of them; Prima's rival chapter describes rivals only as arrival-year/genre-propensity personalities. The GameSpot E3 2002 First Look does say verbatim "one way to grow is to acquire your competitors" (Sam Parker, 21 May 2002) — pre-alpha, four months into development, never shipped. NEW finding: the GameSpot pre-release preview also promised "further royalties ... with eventual VHS and DVD rereleases", advisers, socialite challenges, contract poaching and "until the studio goes bust" — all cut. The only shipped ownership transaction was the Star & Script Selling Facility ($3,000, 1920, buildable in debt): instantaneous one-way sale to "rival studios" at a disclosed price = f(Star rating, actual age), rising to 55 then declining yearly, with full value only after eight years' tenure (an explicit anti-flip vesting rule); scripts sold by rating for "relatively minor" cash. Fired Stars instantly join rivals for nothing; rival Stars enter the player's queue with full state. Films earn until they stop, then archiving is a permanent, valueless tidy-up; a finished film could be shelved unreleased. Studio Rating's Capital = cash in bank; "Movies 24%" is a Success-weighted term that explicitly DECAYS ("a town with a staggeringly short memory") — the opposite of library value (assignment framing CORRECTED). Debt only gates construction; IGN confirms no losing condition. Buildings sold back for a depreciated refund. Rival roster (9 named) only grows. S&E added only stunts/effects/awards/Quick Start. Prior P15 §5.5 conclusions CONFIRMED; P16 has zero parity budget.

## Design implications (agent inference)
- INFERENCE: P16's parity budget is empty — StoryProperty, rights, library value, acquisition, auction and labels are all successor design; documentation must never label any of them 'original parity' (facts CONFIRMED; the decision to build them is SUPERSEDED BY OWNER DIRECTION).
- INFERENCE: Reuse the original's two precedented anti-flip primitives for §2.K asset sales — tenure vesting to full sale value ('eight years ... rising fraction') and value decline tied to remaining useful life (age 55) — transposed to Story Properties/library rights as a holding-period resale discount and a remaining-exploitation value curve; no artificial M&A cap needed for flip control.
- INFERENCE: Show a deterministic 'market value' per sellable asset before any sale (the original showed Star value on hover and in the Finance screen); this matches Owner's 'no opaque bargaining simulator' preference for §2.L/§2.N.
- INFERENCE: Separate the permanent, browsable film record (the original even made archiving irreversible) from economic library cash; do not re-import the cut pre-release 'VHS/DVD royalties' idea as a P16 weekly trickle — library cash waits for an owning P18 distribution/media system (§2.C).
- INFERENCE: Keep standing recency-decaying (the original's Movies 24% decayed by design) and put library value only in the separate valuation line for §2.N; otherwise old libraries become a standing snowball.
- INFERENCE: Keep 'shelved/unreleased' as a StoryProperty/film state that retains the property (the original allowed burying a finished film to protect prestige, but permanently and valuelessly).
- INFERENCE: People are never inventory (P14 law); P16 acquisition transfers contracts (P14 objects), never 'sells' people; the original's SSSF-for-Stars is the anti-pattern P14 already rejected.
- INFERENCE: The original's cheap, debt-buildable selling facility is the precedent for §2.K 'asset sales as a recovery mechanism before bankruptcy' — the recovery valve should be always available and cheap, while bankruptcy itself is wholly successor design (original had a soft debt lock only).
- INFERENCE: Facility depreciation (sell-back at 'a depreciated portion of its original purchase price') is a precedent for §2.G option 4 and for BOOK NET WORTH in §2.N: physical property converts to cash at depreciated value and never teleports.
- INFERENCE: Because the original roster only ever grew, P15/P16 closure and acquisition will be the first time a studio ever leaves the chart; the §2.I corporate-history register has no original precedent and must be designed from scratch (StudioId permanence is the right anchor).

# 03-authority-domains.md

## Summary
Across P07–P15 product law and the accepted code (commit 13370d42), no debt, loan, liability, asset value, depreciation schedule, book value, net worth or valuation exists; P11 forbids inventing a balance sheet and Owner-gates financing/bankruptcy. The ledger is one closed, typed, signed LedgerKind stream, so any P16 cash movement needs a new kind plus correlation id. Negative cash is a recoverable literal state, not distress. Rival finance exists internally but is hidden by default and Contribution/valuation display is NOT YET AUTHORIZED. Only one physical lot exists; rival facilities are four abstract capacity rows with no tiers, footprint or capex history, so "target facilities/upgrades" have no physical referent; demolition credit (0.5 x current blueprint capex, 0.35 for Sets, strictly <1) is the only existing liquidation value. P13's six facts make "knowledge transfers, installations do not" directly expressible as a buyer adoption row at readyToAdopt, but route/routeProvenance is closed (research|wait|purchase) and P13-OD-06 forbids in-flight work moving across studios; closure already resolves research via typed dispositions with no successor value; InventionProvenance reserves entitlementClass transferred / TechnologyRightsRecord for inheriting the inventor price advantage. ContractId is minted by P12 code and embeds the employer StudioId (P14's "P10 ContractId" is CORRECTED), so contract succession must be a new P12 employment row + new reason, never in-place re-owning; ordinary release costs 50% of remaining guarantee (tuning). Standing is per-studio current reputation with three mutation sources and no transfer producer. Film economic life ends at run completion: library value is zero cash-flow today; FilmId==productionId, creating studio immutable, no current-owner field.

## Summary

# 07-comparators-C.md

## Summary
Cross-genre M&A evidence converges on the Owner's selected direction. OpenTTD (source code, release 15.3) shows a shipped three-number separation (book asset value; bankruptcy clearing price = assets+cash ignoring loan; hostile price = assets + loan payoff + 2x four-quarter profit) and a staged warning->offer->deletion ladder, but also two anti-patterns: leaders get first refusal on bankrupt companies, and share trading was removed in 14.0 as an "infinite money" exploit. Offworld Trading Company's designer notes prove inherited operations are a burden, not a reward, and that price alone over-corrects into hoarding; legibility came from visible progress-to-control, escalating premiums and a target able to defend itself. Railway Empire ships the Model D fork (keep as passive dividend entity vs merge, then integrate vs liquidate) and demonstrates that symm […]"identity never rewritten, holder changes append" model. The TEW tape-library premise is UNVERIFIED; real WWE library deals show library cash requires an owning channel. Project: Studio's accepted economy already fails its own snowball gate (G11), so M&A anti-snowball must come from integrated costs (inherited obligations, capacity limits, premium schedule, refusal, cooldowns) rather than caps.

## Design implications (agent inference)
- (Inference) Minimal rights bundle = four kinds, all historically real: Story Property ownership; per-film ownership; continuation authority (sequel/remake/spinoff as one right); Licence (time- or activity-limited grant of film or continuation rights with a visible reversion week). No territory/media/exclusivity sub-splits are needed for any observed decision; P18 may add television/cross-media as a fifth kind later.
- (Inference) StoryProperty must record continuation authority separately from the studio that made the film: Disney's Circle 7 shows the owner of sequel rights can act without the creator; Terminator shows continuation rights trade independently of film distribution.
- (Inference) Library value is stored, not flowing: RKO 1955 (TV), MGM 2004 (DVD), Miramax 2011 and Amazon 2021 (streaming) show library cash appearing only when a downstream window exists. P16 should carry a Library Valuation line feeding enterprise value and auction lots, with no weekly library income until P18 defines a window.
- (Inference) Model D is the observed real norm; Model C labels are stable only as heritage tags (name + logo + founding StudioId + acquisition event) that own and manage nothing, with a player option to retire them. Model B should not be offered because its real success (Pixar/Lucasfilm) depended on contractual talent protections the game does not model.
- (Inference) Whole-company bundle answers from the cases: acquired cash becomes buyer cash (net-of-cash pricing); debt transfers in healthy purchases (MGM, DreamWorks, Miramax); employee contracts transfer intact then ordinary P14 termination law applies; active movies transfer and finish with buyer able to cancel at write-off cost; facilities never come to the buyer's lot (Fox lot, RKO lot, MGM Culver lot all sold or retained); research transfers as knowledge/eligibility with the people, never as an installed facility.
- (Inference) Smallest-correction flag: the transaction record must carry an explicit liabilitiesAssumed list, empty by default in a §363 auction and full by default in a healthy purchase, otherwise the two paths share one 'buy the studio' screen with silently different liability outcomes.
- (Inference) Active productions: healthy purchase = transfer and finish (buyer may cancel or sell); bankruptcy = each finished or in-progress film is a separate auction lot with its own reserve (Orion, Relativity, TWC); reorganisation-in-place keeps projects under the same StudioId. No 'transition owner' state is needed beyond 'in estate, unsold'.
- (Inference) Corporate history: keep the original studio as a dated, searchable entity with credits intact and an ownership relation to the successor (RKO, Orion, Lorimar, Miramax, TWC pattern); add LIBRARY SOLD and LABEL RETIRED as dated events only if those transactions exist.
- (Inference) Healthy premium model: show the player enterprise valuation, a visible asking-premium band (20–40% analogue, top of band for strong/scarce targets and high independence preference), and the existence of any rival bidder. The target board retains a refusal right independent of price; a cooling-off after refusal mirrors observed hardening (Disney 2004).
- (Inference) Distressed acquisition should be the assignment's HYBRID (option 5): asset lots free and clear by default, buyer may assume specific project contracts at a visible cure cost, a creditor-floor reserve stops give-away prices, and whole-entity purchase is reserved for the reorganisation route where the label and StudioId keep operating.
- (Inference) Book net worth, enterprise valuation and transaction price must stay three numbers; double counting risk is the goodwill line (Amazon: $3.4B content vs $4.9B goodwill). Valuation = library value + Story Property value + tangible net assets + a single brand/strategic component; premium applied once to the total.
- (Inference) Natural anti-snowball levers observed in reality: inherited-slate losses (Fox $170M), integration cost (Disney $2B savings = paid layoffs), talent departure risk (Lucas treatments discarded, Lasseter exit), encumbered IP (Marvel licences), rival bidders (Canal+, Comcast, Sony vs Time Warner), creditor floors (Relativity), leverage leading to foreclosure (Cannon/Parretti). No artificial M&A cap is needed; antitrust appeared only as a target-side preference.
- (Inference) Player asset sales have real precedents per category: library (DreamWorks/Soros, RKO/C&C), individual film rights (Lantern's three Tarantino films for $5.5M), continuation rights (Terminator), brand name (Carolco, RKO), development slate (Miramax 2010), lot (RKO/Desilu). Anti-flip friction should be price discovery (buy-backs at different prices, DreamWorks $900M sold / ~$400M repurchased) plus a repurchase cooling period and visible re-sale discount, not a hidden formula.
- (Inference) Rival M&A symmetry: every observed price-raising event came from a rival bidder, so giving rivals the same offer/auction law is the main source of tension; AI should bid when lot value exceeds reserve and affordability, and decline bidding wars past its own valuation (Time Warner 2004 behaviour).
- (Inference) A single 'shared control / cannot continue until bought out' flag on a Story Property (Bond/Eon, Hulk/Universal ROFR) captures partner encumbrances without a cap table; whether it belongs in P16 or P17 is a boundary question.

## Open questions
- Does P16's first slice need the Licence right at all, or only Sale? The most legible real mechanic (use-it-or-lose-it reversion) needs a per-licence reversion week and a release check, which is small but is a timer.
- Should Brand/Label be a sellable asset separate from the library (Carolco/RKO precedent) or only a heritage tag that travels with whole-company purchase? Owner preference for no second studio suggests tag-only, sellable only with the library.
- Inherited-slate write-downs (Fox $170M) require a P11 vocabulary for cancelling a transferred project at cost; is that covered by existing project-cancellation law? Not verified here.
- Credit bidding needs a lender entity and P11 has no debt object (roadmap lines 165–167); what stands in for the creditor floor in an auction, e.g. a fixed liquidation reserve?

- Partner-encumbered properties (Bond/Eon, Hulk/Universal): is a single shared-control flag P16 scope or P17 scope?
- Relativity auction figures and the RKO 1989 remake-rights sale rest on gated or archived articles (MEDIUM); re-verify if any rule depends on those specifics.
