**Verdict on the E3-2002 acquisition claim.** REFUTED as a shipped retail mechanic; CONFIRMED as an existing, verifiably pre-release-only design ambition. GameSpot's May 2002 First Look quotes the sentence prior Project: Studio prose remembered — "Competition from other studios raises the stakes for success, and one way to grow is to acquire your competitors" — in an article that also describes real-time "violence"/"romance" sliders and console versions, none of which shipped either. The negative evidence (zero hits across the official manual, the developer-reviewed Prima guide, three contemporary player guides, and two professional reviews) is as strong as it gets short of decompiling the executable. Confidence HIGH.

**Verdict on the pre-release VHS/DVD royalties claim.** REFUTED as shipped; CONFIRMED as a promised-then-cut pre-release idea, materially adding to the record. A GameSpot preview article states a player "may even be able to make further royalties on it with eventual VHS and DVD rereleases," in the same piece that promises poaching rivals' Stars "by offering bigger, fatter contracts" and a "goes bust" failure state — none of which shipped (see the Debt row above and the Archive-irreversibility finding). Confidence HIGH that the claim exists; HIGH that it did not ship. This means any P16 library-cash design is **successor invention**, never "parity restoration," and must be labelled as such wherever the report or its annex discusses library value.

**The Star & Script Selling Facility is the original's only shipped ownership transaction, in full.** Cost $3,000, Attractiveness −20, multiples allowed, available from 1920, and explicitly buildable while in debt (an engine-level `availableindebt` flag corroborates the debt exception) — a cheap, always-available liquidity valve usable as a distress tool. Mechanically it is a drag-and-drop instant sale with no negotiation and no named buyer ("rival studios," generically): drop a Star or script in, receive cash. Price is disclosed before the sale (shown on hover and on the Finance screen) and is fully deterministic — for Stars, a function of Star rating and actual age that rises until age 55 then declines, gated by an eight-year full-value vesting period (newly hired Stars, including those hired from rivals, earn only a rising fraction of market value until eight years' tenure); for scripts, a function of script rating alone. The only alternative disposal of a Star — firing — pays the owning studio nothing and sends the Star instantly to "march off the lot and join a rival studio," which is why the guides frame the Selling Facility as the financially rational choice. No sale in either direction is reversible, no counterparty is chosen, and nothing about rights, provenance, or continued use by the buyer is tracked. This is, in full, the entirety of the original's inter-studio asset economy. Confidence HIGH throughout (manual + Prima + three independent player guides + one technical-artifact corroboration).

**Corrected reading of "Movies 24%."** The official manual and Prima define Studio Rating as a weighted composite — Capital 24%, Movies 24%, Stars 24%, Lot Prestige 14%, Awards 14% (an engine `.ini` weight table of 0.239/0.239/0.239/0.144/0.144 corroborates the split under the internal field name "reputationfactor"). Prior framing of the "Movies" term as a proxy for library value is **CORRECTED**: Prima states explicitly that "the impact of individual movies decays over time, so you must regularly produce extremely high-quality films to retain the topmost rating in the factor," and a contemporary walkthrough even recommends *stockpiling* several five-star films and releasing them together to exploit the decay window. Movies 24% is a **recency-weighted reputation term that actively rejects durable library value** — the opposite of what a "library value" line would need to do. Capital, the only money-denominated term, is literal cash-in-bank on a nonlinear $50k–$1.6M scale, with no asset, building, or IP term anywhere in the composite. Confidence HIGH; this is the single most load-bearing correction this dossier group makes to prior Project: Studio assumptions about the original.

## 3. StoryProperty and minimal-rights recommendation

**A released Film and its underlying Story Property are two different subjects.** This is not a Project: Studio invention: U.S. Copyright Office Circular 14 and the WGA "separated rights" doctrine both treat the underlying literary property (characters, setting, premise) as severable from any one filmed exploitation of it — SOURCE VERIFIED (real world), HIGH, cited at [dossier 10 §3.2](evidence/10-RIGHTS-LAW.md). In CURRENT/ACCEPTED CODE, `FilmIdentity.studioId` already reads as *creator*, never *current owner*: the validator binds a film's credited people to employment at that `studioId` at commission/announcement week — ACCEPTED CODE FACT, HIGH, `https://github.com/HSpector1/The-Movies/blob/13370d428f0693f3279732f6f4cc360a7fcaa4df/src/core/hollywoodTypes.ts#L20-L27`, corroborated at `hollywoodValidation.ts:318` ("archived film credit lacks historical employer authority"), [dossier 04 H2](evidence/04-CODE-AUDIT.md). Prior Project: Studio prose that has ever conflated "who made a film" with "who currently profits from it" is CORRECTED by this fact, not merely restated.

**StoryProperty entity — smallest useful shape.** Anchored on `conceptId` (the one identity already shared across script, production, film, run and career-event records, and never orphaned or recycled — ACCEPTED CODE FACT, `screenplay.ts:71-90`, `productionIdentity.ts:9-101`, [dossier 04 §5.1](evidence/04-CODE-AUDIT.md)), the recommended record is:

```
StoryProperty {
  propertyId: string        // deterministic mint, exact-ID only, never bulk-created
  conceptId: string         // anchor; the one identity shared with script/production/film
  origin: 'original' | 'pool'   // reuses the existing screenplay.ts:305-345 seam
  creatorStudioId: StudioId // immutable; pool-origin = greenlighting studio (see below)
  createdWeek: number
  titleAtCreation: string   // frozen; FilmConcept.title is mutable post-release and must
                             // not double as the sold asset's name (dossier 04 H14)
  works: FilmId[]           // append-only
}
```
This is RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION / INFERENCE (HIGH confidence on each constituent fact it draws from, MEDIUM on the exact shape), assembled from [dossier 04 §5.1](evidence/04-CODE-AUDIT.md) and [dossier 10 §4.1](evidence/10-RIGHTS-LAW.md); it is not itself an Owner ruling. For pool/externally-sourced material, accepted code carries no author entity (`writerId=null`); `creatorStudioId` there records the **greenlighting studio as first rights holder** via the existing pool-purchase fee — an initial-holder fact, not a claim of authorship. True external-author representation stays an open question ([dossier 10 §5.5](evidence/10-RIGHTS-LAW.md)) and is not silently resolved here.

**Minting: at Greenlight, by exact ID**, atomically with the property's own first `ownershipEvents` row (holder = greenlighting studio). This is INFERENCE, not a settled Owner decision, and the alternative (mint at release) is explicitly logged as a live design question in the evidence base rather than foreclosed here. Greenlighting a production under an owned or *licensed* R3 continuation authority mints **no** new StoryProperty — the resulting `FilmId` is appended to the parent property's `works[]` list instead — closing an exploit where a one-time R3 licence fee could otherwise be laundered into permanent, wholly-owned R1 simply by greenlighting the sequel a studio was only licensed to make.

**The rights bundle — four kinds, one Licence instrument.** This is the smallest split that still reproduces every real-world pattern the research examined (DreamWorks sold R2 while keeping R1/R3/R4; Sony kept R1 on Spider-Man while licensing R3 exclusively with reversion; Lucas retained R3+R4 on the original trilogy) without needing territory or media sub-splits — RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION, HIGH, [dossier 10 §4.2](evidence/10-RIGHTS-LAW.md).

**Why no territory/media sub-splits.** The accepted consumer contract already states a ceiling for the future Licence object rather than an open invitation to sublicense arbitrarily (APPROVED DOCUMENTATION, `P11-TO-P12-AND-P12-FUTURE-CONSUMER-CONTRACT.md §15`, [dossier 02 F3](evidence/02-AUTHORITY-CORPORATE.md)); assignment §2.B explicitly asks for the *smallest* useful bundle, not real-law fidelity; and every comparator this program checked (Mad Games Tycoon 2, Software Inc., Hollywood Animal) models IP ownership as a single flag or a small enum, never per-territory law, without players missing it. Adding territory/media sub-splits now would create clerical bookkeeping with no corresponding decision for the player to make — the opposite of assignment §2.B's own instruction. This v1 policy choice does not foreclose a later, explicitly-chartered finer split; the accepted contract's own reserved room (`P12 §32`: "room for one film to have multiple future ownership/finance shares without changing its identity," [dossier 02](evidence/02-AUTHORITY-CORPORATE.md)) is the seam a later extension would use, not a P16A–C obligation.

**Creator ≠ owner** is restated as a permanent invariant, not a design choice: `FilmIdentity.studioId` and any StoryProperty's `creatorStudioId` are written once and never touched by a transfer. Current holdership lives entirely in a separate, dated, append-only `ownershipEvents` ledger (R03) — never inferred from, or written onto, the StoryProperty/Film record itself. A transfer whose stated `from` does not equal the ledger's current holder is rejected outright.

**Migration baseline law.** Because rows are minted lazily (first transaction or first continuation, never bulk-created at migration — closing the save-growth risk already measured at 49.6 MB for one receipt stream at week 8791, [dossier 04 F9.4](evidence/04-CODE-AUDIT.md)), a founding rule is needed for **pre-existing, pre-P16 films**: the founding `ownershipEvents` row always assigns the **historical creator studio** as initial holder, regardless of that studio's current active/closed/acquired status. If that studio is closed with no recorded successor, the property becomes **estate-held/unclaimed** and can change hands only through the same distressed-acquisition machinery (assignment §2.M) as any other closed studio's assets — never a free claim by whichever party happens to trigger the first transaction on an old title. This closes a genuine structural exploit (an "orphan-IP land grab") that a naive lazy-mint design would otherwise permit. A save migration may record only certain, derivable pre-existing facts and must never invent a rival-to-rival transaction, closure, or ownership event the save does not already contain (R32) — migration honesty, not migration convenience, governs.

**Rule summary (R01–R05).** R01 fixes the entity, its origin seam, and the no-mint-on-licensed-greenlight guard. R02 fixes the four-right/one-instrument bundle with no sub-splits. R03 fixes the single-holder append-only ledger and adds a symmetric guard against duplicate exclusive-licence issuance (double-optioning the same continuation right to two buyers is rejected outright if an unexpired exclusive grant already exists on that facet). R04 is corrected into three explicit clauses because its single merged source conflated three distinct guard conditions, leaving R2 sale unrestricted during an active theatrical run if left as one rule:
- **R04a** — no sale of R1, or exclusive R3 grant, while an active production sits on the property, unless the production transfers with it or a retained licence is recorded on the receipt.
- **R04b** — no sale of R2 while that film's theatrical run is `active`, unless remaining scheduled receipts are explicitly assigned to the buyer or netted out of price.
- **R04c** — no sale of R1/R2 while a live exclusive licence sits on it, unless the buyer takes it encumbered, with the surviving licence's term/exclusivity/consideration disclosed and bounded (never open-ended).

R05 fixes the atomic mint-plus-first-ledger-row sequencing and the founding-holder/orphan-IP rule above. All five are RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION, HIGH confidence on the underlying code/law facts they cite, and none reopens an Owner-settled premise from assignment §2.A/§2.B.

## 4. Film-library/value recommendation

**Library and Story Property value are a displayed, formula-versioned valuation read-model only — never a P11 ledger fact, and never a Standing/Power-Ranking input, until a P18 distribution/media system exists to actually pay it out.** This is the direct, non-negotiable consequence of assignment §2.C's own instruction not to invent perpetual weekly library cash before an owning system exists, reinforced by accepted code: a `TheatricalRun` is explicitly terminal (`status: 'active'|'completed'|'legacyCompleted'`, "kept as a HISTORY, never deleted" — ACCEPTED CODE FACT, `types.ts:304-316`, [dossier 03 §(f) film economics](evidence/03-AUTHORITY-DOMAINS.md)), so there is no live weekly channel a library value could plausibly feed today. This is the smallest correction to a real hazard: the original *The Movies*' own "Movies 24%" Studio Rating term explicitly **decays** ("a town with a staggeringly short memory") — it is the opposite of durable library value, and any prior-prose framing that treated original-game reputation as an ancestor of library value is CORRECTED, HIGH, [00-KEY-FINDINGS.md](evidence/00-KEY-FINDINGS.md).

**No reissue before P18 — exact-once release law.** Because a completed theatrical run is terminal by construction and no reissue mechanic exists in accepted code, a StoryProperty/Film's ownership record may note *reissue authority* (who would be entitled to authorize one — the R2 holder) as a durable fact, but reissue **execution** stays entirely deferred to a P16D-or-P18 co-charter. A reissue implemented today as "the R2 holder presses a button and receives cash" would be exactly the fabricated weekly-cash mechanic assignment §2.C forbids. This is RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION, HIGH, [dossier 10 §4.5](evidence/10-RIGHTS-LAW.md), and does not reopen or anticipate any future P18 decision — it only states what P16 must *not* pre-build.

**Historical value vs. current cash are kept in separate lanes by construction**, not merely by convention: valuation is a strategic, formula-versioned display term (count/quality of owned FilmIds and StoryProperties, awards where modelled); cash is whatever P11's ledger actually records. Nothing in P16A ever writes to a `LedgerKind`/`RivalMoneyKind` for the mere existence of library value. The **one-time realized proceeds of an actual sale** (§11 below) remain the single exception, and are always a typed ledger row — this is a scoping clarification of R06, not a loophole in it: "never a ledger fact" applies to the *valuation estimate*, never to a completed transaction's proceeds.

**Co-holding counts once — R06's arithmetic correction.** The naive rule "count each owned R1 once, each owned R2 once, never both toward the same figure" double-books the single most common case: a studio that holds both R1 and R2 for the *same* film, because R2's catalog-value term and R1's sequel-potential term are both ultimately derived from the same film's performance. The corrected rule: **when one party co-holds both R1 and R2 for the same underlying film, only the R2/catalog-value lane contributes to Library Valuation; R1's franchise-option premium is added only when R1 is held apart from R2** (an unproduced/shelved property, or a genuine split-ownership case). This closes a systematic overvaluation that would otherwise inflate the price of acquiring any prolific studio, feeding directly into the Transaction Price formula in §18/§14. Shelved/unproduced properties retain ownership permanently and, by default, valuelessly.

**Rule summary (R06–R07).** R06 fixes the valuation-only read-model, the co-holding correction, and the valuation-vs-proceeds scoping. R07 fixes that every sellable asset shows a deterministic Estimated Value before any sale screen is offered — a **shown appraisal ceiling, not a guaranteed clearing price**; whether a sale actually executes, and at what price, is governed separately by the AI bid/ask law in §11, intentionally undisclosed in advance, so no hidden multiplier sits behind the shown figure. Both are RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION, HIGH confidence on the underlying accepted-code and comparator evidence they draw from.

**Answers to assignment §2.F's eight numbered questions:**

1. **Does acquired cash become buyer cash?** Yes, at par, even if negative — computed as one reconciled transaction with the price adjustment, never as two independently additive steps (this closes an internal inconsistency the ruleset's own worked scenarios previously showed — one consistent rule: cash is never inside any valuation lane, always a separate line added at par). *R17, RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION, HIGH.*
2. **Does debt transfer?** `[R02]` No debt instrument exists in accepted code (P11 §33), and loans are now Owner-selected direction F — NOT YET IMPLEMENTED. When the loan slice exists, a healthy target's loan is repaid from the consideration or assumed by the acquirer under the identical law, priced once at principal + accrued interest (no cheaper exit exists, so face-value pricing creates no arbitrage); on the estate path the loan is a claim P15 settles before any clean purchase and is never silently inherited. Contract guarantees are **not** netted (Q4). *R18′, RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION, HIGH.*
3. **Do employee contracts transfer intact?** `[R02]` Not automatically — people are not owned. Every contract ends at closing, and in the same tick the acquirer's priority retention window lets it **continue** any contract on identical terms (the end-old-row/mint-new-row idiom, P12 sale/assignment reason, `signingBonus:0` because the original bonus was paid and settled), make a fresh P14A offer, or do nothing; a person may lawfully decline a fresh offer under the P14A chooser and a continuation only for a P14B-defined reason (NOT RECORDED before P14B — deterministic acceptance); unretained people enter free agency after the release step. *R19′/R33, RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION, HIGH; Reconciliation 02 §4.*
4. **Can the player release inherited staff afterward under ordinary termination law?** Yes — and per the **P14 Owner-direction amendment of 2026-09-11** (OWNER-SELECTED PRODUCT DIRECTION, `CODEX-P13-P15-OWNER-RULINGS.md §3.4`), the termination charge for a studio-initiated early release is the **lesser of** (a) all remaining guaranteed base salary or (b) **twenty-six weeks** of that person's base salary (`annualSalary/52`, signing bonus already paid excluded) — this **supersedes** the accepted-code 50% fraction (`HIRING_TERMINATION_FRACTION 0.5`, ACCEPTED CODE FACT), which the report treats as the fact P14 recalibrates, not as current law. `[R02]` **The earlier claim that netting the full guarantee into the price made an "assume cheap, fire immediately" round-trip unprofitable was unsupported** — it was the arbitrage: a $4,000,048 guarantee netted from the price and released for the $1,000,012 cap hands the acquirer $3,000,036 (and $2,000,024 under the 50 % code rule). The protection is that guarantees are never netted from the price at all; whoever ends a contract pays the ordinary charge once (the target for contracts not continued, from its own cash before transfer; the acquirer for any later release), and the P14 salary-floor guard prices any release-then-rehire by the acquirer through its own termination receipt. No change to the cap, no balancing fee. Full ledger: Reconciliation 02 §5.
5. **What happens to active movies?** They transfer and finish under the buyer via re-formation (development/scripts/casting/shooting/post/committed release all preserved) with a per-project continue-or-cancel choice; see §8 below. *R20.*

7. **What exactly does acquired research grant?** Verified **completed** knowledge transfers uniformly across every acquisition path — a new adoption row at ready-to-adopt citing the target's provenance — applied identically whether the deal is a healthy purchase or the cheapest clean bankruptcy asset lot, never gated inconsistently by deal type (R22). ## 7. Physical-property treatment

Assignment §2.G names five alternatives for target real estate/buildings: (1) automatic liquidation at closing; (2) player-chosen sell/liquidate of selected assets; (3) remote corporate property as a financial asset only; (4) transferable, resellable/reusable equipment; (5) a future remote-lot system.

**Recommendation: automatic liquidation at closing (alternative 1), scoped to facilities/equipment only.** Target facilities and equipment liquidate once, automatically, into a single cash credit computed at the **existing lossy recoverable-demolition-credit rate already used for the player's own lot** — 0.5× current blueprint capex for facilities, 0.35× for sets (ACCEPTED CODE FACT, HIGH; dossier 04 F7.1, ruleset-v2 R21). Nothing physical ever appears on the buyer's lot, and no second playable studio lot is created, satisfying the Owner's explicit "does not want multiple playable studio lots" statement directly (OWNER-SELECTED PRODUCT DIRECTION, assignment §2.G). **Land is excluded from this credit and is not priced or liquidated until a land market ships** — the ruleset's own earlier draft named "land" as something that automatically liquidates, but land purchase does not exist anywhere in accepted code (`P09-REQ-035 LATER`; `LotParcel.ownedFromStart:true`, "the studio owns its whole lot from week zero," ACCEPTED CODE FACT, HIGH; dossier 04 F7.1) — this is a wording correction to the rule, not a design change (ruleset-v2 §6 R21 correction).

Alternative 3 (remote financial asset) is the **correct fallback** the day rival facilities gain a real capacity/tier model of their own, and alternative 2 (player-parity sell/liquidate choice) is recommended to extend to acquired facilities of a tier the buyer lacks at that same future point — both are forward-looking notes, not present-day behavior, since rival physical plant is currently near-moot: rivals hold no location, no upgrade tier, and no per-unit resale basis at all (dossier 04 F7.2, dossier 16 G8, ACCEPTED CODE FACT, HIGH). Alternative 5 (a future remote-lot system) is explicitly not required now and is not recommended for this slice. *All: RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION, HIGH on the present-day scoping, MEDIUM on the forward-looking carve-outs; R21.*

## 8. Active-production treatment

Assignment §2.H asks how to handle development, scripts, casting, shooting, post-production and committed release schedules for an inherited film, and whether bankruptcy handles this differently from a healthy acquisition.

**Recommendation: transfer-and-finish by re-formation, never a container move.** Identity preservation (`productionId`, `conceptId`, participants, committed budget) is achievable — the target's ids are globally unique and not regex-checked by player-side validators — but only as an explicit **re-formation** on the buyer's side, not by moving the existing rival container: a new player-side production workflow is minted, reservations are re-allocated against the buyer's own single lot, the script project is re-minted under a new `script-NNNN` id (with a `scriptProjectId` re-mint specifically, since the rival's script row cannot carry over unchanged into the player's `scriptDevelopment.projects` shape), and a **ledger-witness row** is written at closing week so the buyer's own placement/greenlight-evidence law (which requires exactly one ledger row correlated to the production's start tick) is satisfied without falsely implying the buyer greenlit the picture (ACCEPTED CODE FACT + RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION synthesis, HIGH on the facts, MEDIUM on "feasible only this way"; dossier 04 F6.1-F6.3, ruleset-v2 R20). This covers development, scripts, casting, and shooting/post uniformly — each is a field on the same re-formed `Production`/`ScriptProject` pair, not a separately-modeled transfer.

**Valuation treatment (a recommendation, not a settled rule):** an active production's committed cost should be carried at the ordinary write-off/recoverable basis **inside Book Net Worth**, while any expected future value it contributes should be counted **only through the going-concern valuation lane**, never both — avoiding the double-count risk the paper scenarios exposed for other asset classes (RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION, MEDIUM; ruleset-v2 addendum B).

**Continue-or-cancel is a per-project buyer choice**, at the ordinary write-off cost for cancellation — with one hard exception: where the buyer's single managed lot cannot structurally satisfy an inherited production's reservation needs (for example, a reservation naming the target's own former facility id, which fails on re-formation), the re-formation is **capacity-refused**, and the production **auto-cancels at the ordinary write-off cost** rather than being left as an undefined buyer preference (RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION, HIGH; dossier 04 F6.3 hazard #1, ruleset-v2 R20 correction).

**Bankruptcy lots differ from a healthy purchase in one respect only: bundling, not mechanism.** A healthy acquisition transfers every active production by default under the R20 re-formation above. A distressed/bankruptcy disposition defaults instead to **clean asset lots** — each production may be bundled into a purchasable lot at a disclosed cure cost, or left to cancel with the estate — with a separate **whole-entity purchase, absorbed at closing** path for buying the entire estate as one lot — its in-flight productions included — when the target is worth more whole than in pieces (this is the addendum-corrected phrasing: "whole-entity reorganization" as originally worded collided with the Model-B exclusion and with the Owner's full-absorption direction — the entity never continues operating past the purchase; ruleset annex addendum B item 1). `[R02]` Both bankruptcy paths are entered only through the P15 disposal handoff record (P15 RECONCILIATION-02 §1.3–1.4, §5.4–5.6) inside its bounded window — OWNER-SELECTED PRODUCT DIRECTION (genuine failure) / NOT YET IMPLEMENTED / PRODUCER CONTRACT AND EXECUTION AUTHORIZATION REQUIRED, not an authorization question; a suspended estate production may be transferred to an acquirer who completes it, in which case its seated participants must be continued through release or the production cancelled (Reconciliation 02 §4.1). *R20, R26′.*

## 9. Historical identity/status model

**Recommendation: one dated corporate-event record per `StudioId`.** `{studioId, week, kind: 'closed' | 'acquired', successorStudioId?, brandRetained: boolean}` — never three independently-coded status kinds. Under the Owner-selected Full Absorption direction for this slice, `brandRetained` is always `false`: no label survives operationally, so every acquisition event under P16C's initial scope records the buyer as `successorStudioId` with no surviving production label (OWNER-SELECTED PRODUCT DIRECTION, addendum A.1, over RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION R14's general-purpose schema, which also supports a future `brandRetained: true` case if the Owner later authorizes the C/D label option).

**`successorStudioId` is a display/history pointer only** and must never be read by any code path as a substitute for the R03 `ownershipEvents` ledger when determining current ownership of a specific right — a property individually sold to a third party *before* its originating studio was later absorbed must always be read through the ownership ledger, never inferred from the corporate-event chain (RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION, HIGH; ruleset-v2 R14 correction, restated for visibility in ruleset-v2 §9).

**The assignment's three worked examples (§2.I), rendered under Full Absorption:**

- **STUDIO BANKRUPTCY — RKO Pictures — Closed: 1963 Week X.** No acquirer; a `closed` event with no `successorStudioId`.
- **ACQUISITION — Warner Brothers — Acquired by Spector Pictures: 1987 Week X.** Under the initial Full Absorption slice this renders as `{kind:'acquired', successorStudioId: SpectorPictures, brandRetained: false}` — independent operations end at closing; no "Warner Brothers" production label survives going forward, though every film Warner Brothers made before 1987 permanently keeps Warner Brothers as creator-of-record.
- **MERGER/ABSORPTION — United Artists — Independent operations ended: 1995 Week X — Successor owner: Spector Pictures.** Renders identically to the Warner Brothers case under Full Absorption — `kind:'acquired'`, `brandRetained:false` — since the assignment's own wording ("independent operations ended... successor owner") already describes exactly the Full Absorption outcome, distinct only in prose framing from the "ACQUISITION" example, not in mechanism.

**Acquired ≠ closed** (`HIS-013`, dossier 02/09 F10, SOURCE VERIFIED/APPROVED DOCUMENTATION, HIGH): a bankrupt studio with no buyer is `closed`; an absorbed studio is `acquired` — the two are recorded as distinct `kind` values precisely because their downstream meaning differs (an acquired studio's assets have a specific successor; a closed studio's do not, absent later distressed-acquisition activity per R05's estate-held rule). **No rewrite of creator/employer history**: acquisition never mutates who originally created a film or who historically employed a person — `FilmIdentity.studioId` continues to mean creator only, enforced by the existing credit validator, and every past film, employee credit, and award stays attributed to the studio that actually made it, forever (ACCEPTED CODE FACT, HIGH; ruleset-v2 R15). **Immutability**: `StudioId`, `PersonId`, `FilmId`, `productionId`, and `conceptId` are never reminted, renamed away, reused, or deleted by any ownership transfer or corporate event under any of R13–R16 (RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION restating settled invariants, HIGH). A migration may record only certain, derivable facts and must never invent a rival-to-rival event the save does not already contain (R32).

## 10. Rival M&A

**Symmetric transaction law.** Identical valuation, eligibility, premium, and disclosure law applies whether the buyer or seller is any two rival studios, or the player is the buyer — no `isPlayer` multiplier, exemption, or surcharge anywhere in the transaction, pricing, or disclosure law, **except** that the player's own studio is a categorical exclusion from the transaction's target-eligibility predicate (never an `isPlayer`-conditioned term inside the pricing/disclosure law itself). This carve-out must be written directly into the symmetric-law rule's own text, not left to surrounding prose, because a literal, unqualified reading of "no exemption anywhere" would otherwise silently reopen the single most tested failure mode in the comparator research — a rival buying the player, the exact pattern Railway Empire shipped and OpenTTD's own code explicitly forbids in the reverse direction (RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION → structural correction, HIGH; ruleset-v2 §10 R28 correction). `[R02]` **Restated as a recommendation, not an Owner direction:** no operating studio — player or rival — is acquired without its consent; the player's studio reads `NotForSale` by construction in the initial slice (no inbound offers are generated), and a failed player studio's estate is disposed under the same P15/P16 law as a rival's (Owner direction E: one law). A player-initiated sale ending is an optional later feature (R16′, RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION).

**The resulting operating model is symmetric too.** Whichever studio holds the buyer role, a completed acquisition resolves the same way: **Full Absorption (Model A)** is the OWNER-SELECTED PRODUCT DIRECTION for the initial P16C slice (addendum A.1, §5) — the target studio closes into the acquirer at closing per §6's transfer bundle, with no autonomous continuation and no surviving operating label, whether the acquirer is the player or a rival AI studio.

**AI bid heuristic.** A rival studio submits a bid or offer only when, evaluated at the same weekly decision boundary the player faces: (1) it is not itself in P15 warning/distress/integration; (2) its post-close cash would remain at or above the same reserve requirement the player must clear; (3) its need-weighted private value of the target (basis rows plus that studio's own gaps — unmet technology tiers, genre-strategy weights) is at or above the current price/ask; and only then does it bid — raising by the minimum increment in an auction up to the lesser of its private value and what it can afford, or offering exactly the ask in a healthy deal, never overpaying absent a competing suitor. This is original design work for this project, not a distillation of any shipped comparator's AI (no comparator anywhere in the research program lets an AI acquire another AI) — its correctness rests on the fact that every input it needs (post-close cash, a basis-point private value, a stable deterministic ordering key) is already a value the engine computes or could compute from existing per-business state (RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION, HIGH on architectural feasibility, MEDIUM on the specific heuristic's tuning; dossier 09 §4.5, dossier 16 §4(a)/(c) and G1-G3/G10).

**The first cross-business read must be charter-authorized.** Every existing rival decision function in accepted code (`staff()`, `decide()` in `hollywoodTick.ts`) reads only its own business's data — no code path anywhere has one `RivalBusiness` read another's fields. An acquisition-bid decision is therefore the **first** case in this codebase where one business's decision logic needs to read a *different* business's private state (target cash, standing, films, project costs) — this is new surface area, not an extension of an existing pattern, and the existing player-facing privacy boundary (rival cash/standing/policy are HIDDEN by default to the human player, per the `bridge/industry.ts` projection) says nothing about and does not constrain what the engine's own internal tick logic may read for this purpose. The P16 charter must authorize this read explicitly rather than leave it as an implicit side effect, and this report's recommendation is that it be **symmetric**: an AI bidder prices from the same public facts as the player and, to see private target facts, opens the same costed due-diligence disclosure event (R27) the player must open — no silent privileged read for the engine's own tick logic, which would be an information asymmetry the symmetric-law rule (R28) forbids (ACCEPTED CODE FACT + RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION, HIGH; dossier 16 G3, §5.2; §21 carries the same default).

**Derived RNG purpose.** Adding the acquisition-bid decision is low-risk under the existing determinism law: append one new literal to the closed `RngPurpose` union (e.g. `ownership-v1`) and call the existing pure, stateless `stream(seed, purpose, key)` function, which never touches `state.rngState` — the codebase's own history shows purposes added additively at least seven times with no changes to any prior purpose's output. The key must be a stable, non-recyclable identifier pair — `${bidderStudioId}:acquisition:${targetStudioId}:${roundOrdinal}` — never a bare calendar week alone, since nothing in `stream()` itself prevents two unrelated same-week decisions from colliding on an underspecified key (ACCEPTED CODE FACT, HIGH; dossier 16 G1-G2, `rng.ts:23-67,204-206`).

**No rival-population floor.** `[R02]` The earlier ≥3-active-rival predicate (R29) is withdrawn under Owner direction G (no artificial floor, no replacement studios; authored arrivals preserved) — see Reconciliation 02 §2. The registry remains a fixed set of ten identities with no allocator (ACCEPTED CODE FACT, HIGH), and that is now simply the world: consolidation to one or zero independent rivals is recorded history, disclosed at new-game and by pinned notices (P15 RECONCILIATION-02 D14, §6.5). The anti-snowball protection at the transaction layer is the independence premium, the R30′ affordability/leverage/cooldown law and the capacity-bounded retention window — friction, never a counter (RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION, MEDIUM).

## 11. Asset sales

**Sellable categories under the initial slice.** Consistent with assignment §2.D/§2.K and the later Owner addendum on the initial acquisition model, the initial P16B slice supports selling exactly three categories: **Story Properties (R1)**, **film/library rights (R2)**, and **facilities/equipment credits** at the existing lossy recoverable-demolition-credit rate. **Land is excluded** — land purchase does not exist in accepted code at all (`P09-REQ-035`, status `LATER`, [dossier 14 Finding 6](evidence/14-GAP-FUTURE-OPS-CONTROL-BOARD-VERIFICATION.md)), so land cannot be priced or liquidated until a land market ships; a prior rule text that named "land" as something that "automatically liquidates" is CORRECTED to scope strictly to facilities/equipment. **Labels are excluded from the initial slice per Owner addendum §A.1**: Full Absorption is the selected initial P16C model, and a retained-label relation exists in this report only as a later researcher option (§18) — until that option is separately authorized, there is no zero-operations label object to sell, so R10 (sell a retained label) and R12 (label as a zero-ops relation) are relabelled **LATER OPTION — not selected** and are not part of the initial asset-sale surface. This is OWNER-SELECTED PRODUCT DIRECTION (addendum §A.1), HIGH.

**Recovery-before-bankruptcy valve.** Because every sellable asset shows a deterministic, non-guaranteed Estimated Value (R07) before any distress state is reached, asset sales function as a legitimate cash-recovery mechanism a studio can reach for *before* P15 forced distress — sell an unproduced or underperforming property, or a facility credit, rather than waiting for a P15 trigger. This is RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION, MEDIUM (the mechanism is sound; whether P15 should read a sale as evidence *against* triggering distress is a P15/P16 boundary question this report does not resolve).

**Exploit guards, addressed one by one:**
- **Single-holder, append-only ledger (R03).** Every right has exactly one current holder; a transfer whose stated `from` does not equal the current holder is rejected outright — prevents duplicate ownership and "sell twice" replay.
- **Rights-in-use locks (R04a/b/c, §3 above).** No sale of a property/film out from under an active production, an active theatrical run, or a live encumbering licence, without explicit disclosure and either transfer or netting of the retained interest.
- **52-week holdback (R08).** A newly acquired or licensed asset's full appraised value is reachable only after a fixed window — paper: 52 weeks, NUMERICAL/CONTENT HYPOTHESIS. This is a **successor anti-flip guard justified by comparator evidence** (GearCity's and Software Inc.'s own repeatedly-patched flip exploits, [dossier 10 §4.6](evidence/10-RIGHTS-LAW.md)), **not** a literal transposition of the original's Star-tenure vesting rule. State this explicitly: the shipped original's only actual IP-asset-sale precedent — the unproduced-script sale via the Star & Script Selling Facility — had **zero** vesting; only *Stars* carried the eight-year-tenure/age-55-decline vesting curve, and that applied to people, never impersonal assets (SOURCE VERIFIED, HIGH, [00-KEY-FINDINGS.md](evidence/00-KEY-FINDINGS.md)). Citing the original's Star vesting for this holdback would overclaim parity that does not exist; it is cited only as the smallest-case idiom that a vesting-style anti-flip guard is not without precedent in this franchise, nothing more.
- **Sub-cost floor (R09).** Every asset-sale price floor sits **strictly below** the asset's own build/acquisition cost basis absent a genuine, receipted competing bid — mirroring the existing lossy demolition-credit invariant. This floor and the holdback cap are reconciled explicitly: R09's competing-bid exception **never overrides** an applicable R08 holdback cap, and where both could apply, the stricter (lower) ceiling governs. Outside a holdback window, the competing-bid exception is exercised only within the bankruptcy sealed-comparison process (§13), or an equivalent minimal second-bidder mechanism for ordinary sales *if* separately authorized — this is flagged as a genuine remaining Owner decision, not assumed. A further guard: the **first** post-holdback resale of any asset originally acquired below its own liquidation/property basis (i.e., sourced from a distressed disposition) stays subject to this same competing-bid discipline even after 52 weeks elapse — otherwise a distress-sourced asset could be warehoused through the clock and resold near double, risk-free, with only elapsed time as friction.
- **AI deal memory.** The evidence base identified and priced a fourth possible guard — an AI "remembers this deal" heuristic capping its rebid/re-ask within a window of a prior sale to guard against ping-pong arbitrage ([dossier 10 §4.6](evidence/10-RIGHTS-LAW.md), RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION, LOW/MEDIUM). This report does **not** carry it forward as an adopted rule: the AI valuation floor already in force (an AI never sells below, or buys above, its own valuation of a right, with cash conservation enforced) closes the same ping-pong-arbitrage risk without a separate memory heuristic or its added state. Prior-prose status: this is a considered-and-not-adopted option, not a dropped-in-error rule.
- **Minimum inter-transaction interval (R32).** Applies per asset regardless of counterparty, reusing the existing holdback/cooldown timers, so the append-only ownership log cannot be grown without bound by repeated, legitimately low-value trades of the same property rotated among counterparties — a save-hygiene guard as much as an exploit guard.

**Rule summary (R08, R09, R32).** All three are RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION; the 52-week figure, any haircut/premium bands, and the minimum-interval length are each individually a NUMERICAL/CONTENT HYPOTHESIS pending an Owner or Current-Ops tuning pass.

## 12. Healthy-studio acquisition

**Willingness is one small public label, not a hidden number.** Every studio carries a public, typed **Willingness State** — `NotForSale | Reluctant | Open | Willing` — driven only by inputs that are already public: recent performance/Standing trend and an independence-preference trait (ruleset R24; RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION built on OWNER-SELECTED PRODUCT DIRECTION assignment §2.L/§2.O, "a healthy studio should be allowed to reject an offer"; confidence HIGH that this satisfies the Owner's instruction, MEDIUM on the exact label set). Any P15-condition-derived component of that state stays hidden until the player opens the due-diligence disclosure defined below (R27) — a correction to an earlier draft of R24 that would have surfaced internal distress-adjacent state before any offer existed, contradicting the project's own visibility doctrine that rival internal condition is hidden by default (`evidence/03-AUTHORITY-DOMAINS.md`; HIGH/CONFIRMED). Willingness-state transitions require a minimum run of consecutive above/below-threshold periods, not a single-period read, precisely because the pricing term below reads a related but independently smoothed window — without that guard a player could manufacture one short-term dip (a deliberate genre/release-window saturation play, a lever §17 confirms exists and is player-controllable) and move both the eligibility gate and the price from the same engineered event.

**Why "control premium" is the wrong name here.** The asking price is Studio Valuation (excluding the target's own cash) × a published **independence premium** band keyed to Willingness State and recent performance — premium rises with success (R25). Standard valuation practice distinguishes the *value of control* (optimal management minus status-quo management, which Damodaran states plainly has "no rule of thumb... 20–30% of value cannot be right" and is structurally small for a well-run target) from a seller's simple unwillingness to give up independence (`evidence/09-VALUATION-FINANCE.md`, HIGH/QUALIFIED). Project: Studio's one-lot buyer never actually operates the target as a second going concern — it liquidates the target's tangible plant at closing (R21) and absorbs the rest — so there is no "better management" story to price at all; the premium is pure reluctance-to-sell, which is why the ruleset renames it **independence premium**. Paper Scenario C makes this concrete (`P16-RULESET-ANNEX.md`/`scenarios/SCENARIOS-CD.md`, all figures PAPER HYPOTHESIS): a `Reluctant` target with SV $10.1M and a published 1.4×–1.8× band prices an Ask of **$11.9M–$14.8M**, while the buyer's own rational one-lot ceiling (cash, liquidated tangible, buyer-relative library value, net of inherited obligations) works out to only **$7.4M** — the buyer's ceiling sits *below* the seller's floor by design, which is the intended anti-snowball outcome, not a bug.

**Acceptance, refusal, and cooldown.** An offer at or above the published Ask is accepted deterministically — no dice, no hidden multiplier (R07/R12's "no hidden multiplier" discipline extended by analogy from asset sales to whole-studio offers). `NotForSale` refuses every offer at any price, matching the real-world case of a board rejecting a bid "as too low" regardless of size (Disney's 2004 rejection of Comcast's unsolicited approach) as well as a board accepting a *lower* bid for certainty (Fox chose Disney over a higher Comcast cash offer on regulatory grounds) — both are SOURCE VERIFIED real-world precedent for "price influences strongly but does not compel" (`evidence/08-REALWORLD-MA.md`, HIGH/CONFIRMED). The other three states refuse *only* an offer below the published Ask, with a plain typed reason ("offer below band"), and every refusal — including `NotForSale`'s — starts a per-(buyer, target) cooldown. Opening the due-diligence disclosure event itself (the mechanism that reveals the SV figure and its lane breakdown once a player wants more than the public label) now counts as a formal approach for cooldown purposes and carries a modest one-time cash cost (R27 correction) — without this, a player could open diligence, read the snapshot, close, and repeat across the whole rival roster for free, defeating the exact visibility rule it exists to protect.

**What the player actually sees, end to end:** pre-offer, only the Willingness State label and its two public drivers; on paying to open due diligence, a dated snapshot of SV and its lane breakdown (and the cooldown starts); once an offer is live, the published Ask (or the band and formula version before that); and never, at any stage, the buyer's own private valuation ceiling — that figure is never something the seller UI discloses. No number in this chain is a black box the player cannot see coming.

**Repeated-bid escalation — left open.** No rule in R01–R32 makes the Nth rejection different from the first: the cooldown is flat and fixed-length regardless of how many times a target has been approached, and the willingness state moves only from the target's own performance/condition, never from approach count (`scenarios/SCENARIOS-CD.md` Scenario C finding). This is a defensible design — no comparator researched models "wearing down" a healthy target (GearCity's health gate and Football Manager's stance both key off the target's own state) — but it is a genuine open question, not something this ruleset has silently resolved, and is carried into §21 as a Current Ops item with a suggested default (an escalating cooldown on the Nth consecutive rejection with no intervening state change) rather than an Owner decision.

An accepted offer triggers Full Absorption (OWNER-SELECTED PRODUCT DIRECTION, addendum A.1): the target studio closes, its assets/rights/productions transfer and its people are reached through the priority retention window per §6's bundle `[R02]`, and its corporate-history record is written as acquired with the buyer as successor owner — never as a second managed lot and, for this initial slice, never as an operating retained label (that remains a later researcher-only option, §5/§9).

## 13. Bankruptcy auction/acquisition

Assignment §2.M names five alternatives. Scored on easiest / most fun / deepest / least accounting overload, using the real-world comparator base (`evidence/08-REALWORLD-MA.md`) and the comparator survey (`evidence/05-COMPARATORS-A.md`, `evidence/07-COMPARATORS-C.md`):

- **Asset auction (buy selected lots individually)** — easiest to understand per lot, and genuinely fun as a cherry-pick decision (Carolco's library and its Terminator continuation rights sold as two separate lots to two different winners is the clean real precedent, SOURCE VERIFIED HIGH), but shallow alone: it has nothing to say about a target whose real value is a going concern with active productions in flight (Scenario B's paradigm case).
- **Whole-studio auction (buy the entity)** — the deepest strategically, forcing the integration/cash/headcount trade-offs Scenario B and E exercise, but overloaded as the *only* path for the common small/weak case: Scenario A shows that buying the whole company and immediately firing everyone can be cheaper than buying clean asset lots ($600,000 all-in vs. $1,800,000), which is a legitimate legible trade-off, but the ruleset does not yet state a reserve formula for the whole-entity lot itself (Rule Gaps A-6/B-3 below).
- **Clean asset purchase (creditors settle debt; buyer takes chosen assets free of liabilities)** — the closest real analogue to a §363 sale: the Weinstein Company's 277-film library transferred "free and clear," with unpaid participations left stranded with the estate because the underlying contract was non-executory (`evidence/08-REALWORLD-MA.md`, HIGH/QUALIFIED). Legally the cleanest and lowest-overload option, but alone gives a strategic bidder no way to keep a target's in-flight productions running.
- **Assumption purchase (lower price, buyer takes on named liabilities/contracts)** — real and common (Lantern's stalking-horse bid for TWC assumed "up to $127.5M in liabilities"), but reintroducing liability accounting as the *default* path is exactly the "legal/accounting simulation overload" the assignment asks to avoid.
- **Hybrid** — recommended. Clean asset lots by default (matches the real-world §363 norm and the fact that no comparator researched ships a live multi-round bidding UI — Acquire's payout is a deterministic public table with zero seller agency and 18xx receivership's only novel mechanic, an automatic bank loan, cannot be imported without reopening the Owner's settled no-player-financing ruling, `evidence/15-GAP-BOARD-GAME-MA-COMPARATORS-ACQUIRE-18XX.md`, HIGH/N/A), with an *optional* bundling of named in-flight productions onto a lot (`[R02]` never contracts — people are reached only through the priority retention window of Reconciliation 02 §4.2), and a separate whole-entity path for buying the entire estate as one lot, absorbed at closing — the entity never continues operating (R26, RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION).

## 14. Book Net Worth vs Enterprise Valuation vs Transaction/Auction Price

**Four shown numbers, plus liquidation value as the floor that feeds two of them, never collapsed.** Standard finance doctrine treats these as categorically different: book value is not liquidation value, "expected value = max(status quo value, orderly liquidation value)," and enterprise value nets out cash/non-operating assets to avoid pricing anything twice (Damodaran, SOURCE VERIFIED, HIGH/N/A). Shipped tycoons already keep the split: OpenTTD runs three separate formulas — book-like company value, a hostile-takeover price that lets the seller keep its own cash, and a bankruptcy offer priced from assets plus cash with the loan discharged (`economy.cpp`, HIGH/N/A); Capitalism 2's manual shows Net Worth beside Market Value and lets some owners "refuse to sell their shares no matter how much you offer" (HIGH/N/A); GearCity derives a health-driven willingness state and transfers cash with the bundle at par (HIGH/N/A) — all `evidence/09-VALUATION-FINANCE.md`. R23 adopts this shape as new **P16** authority: P11 itself is explicitly forbidden from inventing or displaying any net-worth/balance-sheet concept (`evidence/03-AUTHORITY-DOMAINS.md`, P11 no-balance-sheet law, HIGH/CONFIRMED) — any prior draft framing Book Net Worth as "computable from P11 rows alone with no new authority" is CORRECTED: BNW is a new P16 read-model built over raw P11 ledger and P09 recoverable-credit fields. `[R02]` Superseded in ownership: Owner direction C (net worth and value displayed) and P15 RECONCILIATION-02 §2.4 row 8 place Book Net Worth, Guaranteed Obligations and the operating-value estimate in **P11 read models** consumed by P15A.2 and by P16; P16 defines only the transaction price and the seller's reservation basis.

**The ladder, named component by component** (R23; all dollar figures anywhere in this ladder are NUMERICAL/CONTENT HYPOTHESIS):

| Lane | Component | Rule |
|---|---|---|

**Cash discipline.** An early draft of Scenario A netted the target's overdraft directly into "Liquidation Value (asset basis)," while the corrected general rule and Scenario B both exclude cash from that exact lane, netting it only in BNW and once more, at par, into the final price. That inconsistency is fixed here: **cash is never inside any valuation lane; it is a separate line added at par to Transaction/Auction Price**, computed as one reconciled transaction together with the premium/bid — never as two independently additive adjustments, which is precisely the mixed-convention exploit OpenTTD's own inconsistent hostile-vs-bankruptcy cash treatment illustrates the risk of (`evidence/07-COMPARATORS-C.md`, F9, HIGH/N/A).

**Double-counting rules, made concrete.** (1) *Library counts once*: when one party co-holds both the underlying Story Property (R1) and the film/library right (R2) for the same title — the ordinary default case — only the R2/catalog lane contributes to Library Valuation; the R1 franchise-option premium is added only when R1 is held apart from R2 (an unproduced property, or a genuine split-ownership case). Paper Scenario C exercises this directly: five co-held titles contribute only their $2.0M R2 value, not that figure plus a separate R1 premium on top (`scenarios/SCENARIOS-CD.md`). (2) *Brand is informational only inside this whole-company ladder* — this narrows an earlier reading of dossier 08's Amazon/MGM analysis, which the ladder cites for the *opposite* lesson at the transaction level: Amazon's 10-Q purchase-price allocation put **$3.4B on video content and $4.9B on goodwill** of an $8.45B deal (SOURCE VERIFIED, HIGH/CONFIRMED, `evidence/08-REALWORLD-MA.md`) — proof that library value and brand/strategic value are genuinely separate numbers in the real world. The ladder does not contradict that lesson; it scopes the "informational only" clause specifically to this four-number whole-company ladder, where no approved P11 authority exists to invent a priced goodwill line — a standalone brand-only sale under R10/R07 keeps its own independently priced Estimated Value regardless. (3) *Technology is buyer-relative, never a seller-side asset* — the same completed capability is worth its full avoided-research value to a buyer who lacks it and $0 to one who already has it (R22; demonstrated numerically in Scenario E, §17 below). (4) *The going-concern multiple must not double-price the same optionality the library addend already prices*: an unbounded, perpetuity-style earnings multiple already capitalizes the firm's ongoing ability to exploit its catalog, so R23's operating-surplus term is computed on a bounded, non-perpetuity basis (a short multiple on realized cash flow, no terminal-value growth assumption) rather than stacking an open-ended multiple on top of an explicit library figure — closing exactly the "tangled web" double-count Damodaran's own cited work warns against.

**Active productions** are recommended to carry a committed-cost recoverable-work basis in BNW (a paper convention — 0.5 × committed cost × completion, `evidence/09-VALUATION-FINANCE.md` K5 — proposed but not yet carried into the formal rule text, a rule gap logged for §21) and their expected value only through the going-concern lane of SV, never counted in both places at once (RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION responding to assignment §2.N).

**Answering the assignment's question: yes, with caveats.** A healthy price does start from Studio Valuation plus an independence premium (§12), and a distressed price does emerge from sealed bids clustered between liquidation value and strategic value (§13) — Scenario C's structural result (buyer's rational ceiling $7.4M below a $11.9–14.8M Ask) and Scenario B's four genuinely different numbers ($−2.9M BNW / $15.4M SV / $13.0M bid / $9.0M net cash) both confirm this pattern under the corrected rules. `[R02]` The caveats, updated: the whole-estate lot's reserve is its liquidation-basis value (Reconciliation 02 §5.5 closes Rule Gaps A-6/B-3 by that default); Scenario B's "$9.0M net cash" is superseded because no cash transfers on the estate path; and the distressed side is entered only through the P15 disposal handoff record — OWNER-SELECTED PRODUCT DIRECTION / NOT YET IMPLEMENTED / PRODUCER CONTRACT AND EXECUTION AUTHORIZATION REQUIRED.

**Twelve FUN management-game principles (all RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION / labelled inference):**

1. **"You are buying the people, not the shelf."** (Pixar) A target valued for its creative team is worth more only if the team stays; price/risk it as a talent purchase, not a library purchase, when the buyer already owns the films.
2. **"Encumbered IP is discounted IP."** (Marvel) Show which Story Properties are already licensed out, and when they revert, before the offer — price should visibly reflect it.
3. **"The property is not the print."** (Lucasfilm/Star Wars) Story Property ownership, per-film ownership, and continuation authority are three separate facts; a rival can own one old film forever while the player owns the franchise.
4. **"Absorb, rename, and eat the slate."** (Fox) The label survives as a name; the people mostly do not; the inherited slate is a visible write-off risk, never free revenue. Releasing inherited staff uses ordinary termination law — under P14's 2026-09-11 Owner amendment (OWNER-SELECTED PRODUCT DIRECTION), the charge is the lesser of remaining guaranteed salary or 26 weeks' base salary, superseding the accepted-code 50% fraction (`HIRING_TERMINATION_FRACTION` 0.5).
5. **"Two numbers on the deal sheet, never one."** (Amazon/MGM) Library value and brand/strategic value are two separate valuation components; the control premium attaches once, to the total.
6. **"Pick your lots, leave the ghosts."** (TWC §363) A bankruptcy purchase takes assets free and clear of unchosen liabilities; the buyer assumes only a chosen, capped set of contracts; everything else — including unpaid talent promises — stays with the dead studio.
7. **"The floor is not the price."** (Carolco, Relativity) A stalking-horse/reserve bid is a floor, not a guaranteed price: it rises under a genuine rival bid, or falls if nobody shows up.
8. **"Leverage buys studios and then buys you."** (Cannon, MGM 2010) Debt-financed acquisition is self-limiting — default converts ownership to the creditor, a natural anti-snowball lever, not an artificial cap.
9. **"Ownership moves; authorship doesn't."** (RKO) A closed studio's films keep their original creator credit forever; a buyer acquires the right to exploit, never to rewrite who made it — matching the Owner's history model.
10. **"Use it or lose it."** (Spider-Man, Daredevil, Hulk/Namor) A licensed continuation right should carry a visible reversion week; a missed deadline returns it automatically and can still leave a residual encumbrance worth showing the player.
11. **"The sequel right is its own poker chip."** (Terminator) Continuation authority is independently sellable, priced off the franchise's most recent outing, not the studio's overall health.
12. **Healthy-target premium and refusal are both real and visible.** Premiums cluster 20–40%, topping out for scarce IP (Marvel 29%, Amazon/MGM ~41%); a board can refuse regardless of price (Disney vs. Comcast, 2004) or accept a lower bid for certainty (Fox chose Disney over a 19%-higher Comcast offer). Translation: publish a valuation band and premium drivers, and let the target genuinely say no — never a hidden multiplier that auto-transfers ownership.

## 17. Anti-snowball recommendations

**The minimum protections, and why each is load-bearing rather than decorative.** (1) The **independence premium** (R25) prices success-scaled reluctance, not buyer synergy, so acquiring strength costs more precisely when strength would matter most — Scenario C's buyer ceiling sits structurally below the seller's Ask floor. (2) A **26-week cash reserve requirement** (R30) is the affordability governor today; `[R02]` loans are Owner-selected direction F (NOT YET IMPLEMENTED), so when they exist the same test also requires the buyer's post-close leverage to satisfy the P15 loan caps and measures reserve after scheduled instalments (R30′) — the earlier "cash-only economy where loans remain Owner-prohibited" framing is superseded (`evidence/09-VALUATION-FINANCE.md`, HIGH/QUALIFIED) — Scenario E shows this rule genuinely bites, not merely exists on paper: a second, larger acquisition priced legally at week 260 fails the post-close reserve check by roughly $1.35M and is delayed a full 40 weeks until the buyer accumulates enough cash (`scenarios/SCENARIOS-EF.md`, all figures PAPER HYPOTHESIS). (3) `[R02]` **Guarantees are never netted into the price** (R18′): the earlier "netted at full value" rule did not close the "assume the contracts cheap, then fire everyone" flip — it *was* the flip (a $4.0M guarantee netted, a $1.0M cap paid, $3.0M kept). Under the corrected ledger every contract not continued costs the acquirer exactly its capped charge (Scenario E re-run: +$104,000 and +$975,000 instead of −$700,000 and −$1.2M; Reconciliation 02 §5.6). (4) **Integration friction** runs through re-formation of inherited productions under exact IDs (R20) and the atomic terminal-state commit a target's closure requires across every touched package (R13) — inherited operations are documented as a burden, not a reward, independently by Offworld Trading Company's own designer (`evidence/07-COMPARATORS-C.md`, HIGH/N/A). (5) `[R02]` **Continued headcount is capped at usable capacity** (R30′), and the priority retention window's default when the player takes no action is to continue up to capacity and not retain the remainder — a recommendation, not an Owner approval ("mandatory settlement" is withdrawn as a label). Scenario E re-run: 2, then 15 people not retained, each costing the P14 charge (26 × weekly) paid by the target from its own cash before transfer — $104,000 and $975,000 (NUMERICAL/CONTENT HYPOTHESIS; Reconciliation 02 §5.6). (6) **Need-weighted diminishing strategic value** (R22, buyer-private only) makes the same completed technology worth its full avoided-research value to a buyer who lacks it and $0 to a buyer who already owns it — but Scenario E is explicit that this diminishes only the buyer's private *reason* to buy, never the seller's market-facing SV or premium; nothing in the ruleset makes a target's *asking* price cheaper because the buyer has already made other deals, which is a confirmed, intentional scope choice rather than a gap. (7) **Rival bidders** compete under identical law (R28). `[R02]` There is no population floor (R29 withdrawn under Owner direction G) — Scenario E had already shown the floor never bound; R30′'s cash, leverage and headcount friction is what paces a serial acquirer. (8) A **minimum inter-transaction interval** (R32) bounds low-value repeated trading of the same asset among rotating counterparties, protecting ledger integrity rather than pricing consolidation directly.

**Why no artificial M&A cap.** Assignment §2.S explicitly asks for the minimum necessary protection and rejects a blunt cap, and the comparator survey supports that instruction on its own evidence: every shipped anti-snowball lever found across nineteen-plus titles was cost- or friction-based — lock-out windows, categorical not-for-sale classes, upkeep, contested shared pools, distress-evaporating value (`evidence/06-COMPARATORS-B.md`) — never a hard transaction counter. The negative comparator is instructive: Mad Games Tycoon 2's AI rivals cannot fail economically at all ("plot armor"), which is what makes its acquisitions read as collecting rather than competition; the fix for snowballing is a real possibility of loss and real friction on the winner, not an arbitrary ceiling on how many times a player may transact.

**The economy P16 sits on top of has not itself passed this test.** Independently of any M&A mechanic, the accepted game's own snowball gate already fails: "G11 | Snowballing bounded | ... **FAIL** ... the current brake on snowballing is the death spiral itself" and, in the Owner-reviewed evidence pass, "cash half regresses to 19.6–19.7% mean runaway and remains world-led" (ACCEPTED CODE FACT, HIGH/CONFIRMED, `p12-accepted/docs/D-16-ECONOMY-RECOVERY-DECISION-LAB.md` line 49 and `D-17B-OWNER-EVIDENCE.md` line 224, commit `13370d42`). Every guard listed above is layered on an economy independently measured to concentrate roughly 20% world cash share as a runaway property even with zero acquisition mechanic present. That means these eight protections are necessary but cannot, by themselves, be certified sufficient — the accepted **G11 snowball gate must be re-run with M&A enabled before shipping**, not assumed to still pass (RESEARCHER RECOMMENDATION, `evidence/07-COMPARATORS-C.md`). This is flagged here as a required verification step for Current Ops, not something a documentation-only report can close by drafting alone.

**The clean-asset-lot path is the least-frictioned route, and needs its own bound.** Comparing Scenario E's three deals directly: R18's obligation-netting and R30's headcount/reserve friction bite hard on healthy whole-company deals (Deal 2 cost an extra ~$65,000-per-head termination charge and a 40-week delay) but are essentially inert on the clean bankruptcy asset-lot path, where nothing is assumed by design — no obligations, no inherited headcount, negligible reserve impact. That asymmetry creates a real, rational incentive for a patient acquirer to prefer waiting for rivals to fail and buying clean lots over paying full freight for healthy targets, `[R02]` The "talent cherry-picking" gap this paragraph logged as Owner Decision #2 is closed by direction: the priority retention window *is* a choice of specific people (Owner: "a priority opportunity to retain talent"), bounded by the capacity cap and the ordinary economy; the clean-asset-lot path reaches no people at all (they are free agents with P15 claims), so the asymmetry it worried about — keep one Star from an estate for free — no longer exists: a Star from an estate is either continued on identical terms in the retention window or hired from free agency like anyone.

- Whether a sold Star reappears at a named rival, returns to the player's queue later, or vanishes entirely is undocumented in any source. *Suggested default: no special reappearance guarantee — a sold Star is ordinary rival roster churn from that point on, since no source or code models a distinct "sold" pool.*

**Design items for Current Ops (each a NUMERICAL/CONTENT HYPOTHESIS or drafting gap, not an Owner decision).**
- **BNW fixed-asset basis** — no book-value schedule distinct from the liquidation rate exists. *Default: reuse the existing recoverable-credit rate (0.5×/0.35×) explicitly as BNW's tangible line rather than inventing a separate schedule.*
- **Story-Property distress floor** — no sub-cost floor is defined for a property sold as an individual bankruptcy lot. *Default: apply the same strictly-below-basis floor R09 uses for ordinary asset sales.*
- **Liquidation surplus disposition** — undefined what happens to surplus once an estate's obligations clear. *Default: destroyed, not distributed — the only reading consistent with there being no receiving party and no snowball risk.*
- **Whole-entity auction reserve** — `[R02]` closed by default: the whole-estate lot's reserve is its liquidation-basis value (Reconciliation 02 §5.5).
- **Deferred negative-cash burden ledger mechanism** — `[R02]` closed: no such burden exists (on the estate path no cash transfers; on the healthy path the target's post-exit cash transfers at par, negative included).
- **Reserve-fall trigger** — magnitude of a catalogue lot's one-time reserve reduction after a bidder-less window is unstated. *Default: one fixed fractional step (illustratively 20%) per unsold interval, floored at liquidation basis, never trending to zero.*
- **Holdback clock start for an unreleased film's R2** — undefined whether the 52-week clock starts at acquisition or release. *Default: at release/first-sellable date, since the asset is not sellable R2 before then.*
- **Repeated-rejected-bid escalation** — no rule makes the Nth rejection different from the 1st. *Default: none — a flat, fixed-length cooldown repeats until the target's own condition changes, matching every comparator checked.*
- **Partial-research transfer reconciliation** — verified-complete knowledge transfers cleanly, but in-flight, uncompleted research is untouched by any transfer rule. *Default: the in-flight work order cancels at closing per P13-OD-06; already-verified partial progress survives under P13's own Owner Direction 7A as it would survive any cancellation — a P13/P16 reconciliation item, not a new Owner decision.*

## Appendix C — Method and process record

**Phase 1 — Evidence gathering (read-only research).** Eighteen structured evidence dossiers were produced by parallel research agents and synthesized into one key-findings digest: `01-ORIGINAL-GAME` (original *The Movies*/*Stunts & Effects* reconstruction), `02-AUTHORITY-CORPORATE` and `03-AUTHORITY-DOMAINS` (accepted-documentation and package-boundary authority), `04-CODE-AUDIT` (accepted-runtime audit at commit `13370d42`), `05`–`07-COMPARATORS-A/B/C` (management/tycoon comparator surveys), `08-REALWORLD-MA` (real-world entertainment M&A case studies), `09-VALUATION-FINANCE` (valuation/finance doctrine), `10-RIGHTS-LAW` (rights/IP law), and eight targeted gap-closure dossiers (`11`–`18`) opened specifically to close holes the first ten dossiers exposed — the P15 closure-settlement/estate reconciliation, the P15 shared-market capacity/concentration check, the Model-D six-criteria evaluation table, Future Ops Control Board verification, board-game M&A comparators (Acquire/18xx), rival-AI acquisition code feasibility, an MGT2 cash/debt-transfer verification, and a TEW IX tape-library/label verification. All eighteen dossiers and their synthesis are read-only research and analysis: no repository edits, no branch creation, no runtime testing, and no private campaign/profile mutation occurred at any point in this program.

**Phase 2 — Ruleset drafting and merge.** Three independent candidate rule-set lenses were drafted from the Phase 1 evidence base and judged/merged into a single `merged-ruleset.md`.

**Phase 3 — Adversarial repair pass.** Every rule in the merged ruleset was run through two further lenses — **authority** (does the rule actually match settled Owner law, accepted code, and package boundaries?) and **exploit** (can a rational player or AI break the rule as written?) — producing seventy verdicts. The smallest correction was applied for every verdict judged a valid refutation (four severe defects, roughly twenty moderate gaps, roughly fifteen minor documentation/citation gaps), and every rejected verdict's reasoning was recorded, in the resulting `P16-RULESET-ANNEX.md` (R01–R32, its own §22 Repair Log). No Owner-selected direction from assignment §2 was reopened at this stage.

**Phase 4 — Paper scenario verification.** Six independent paper scenarios (A–F, matching assignment §3) were run strictly against the ruleset annex's own rule text, independently of the annex's own worked examples, specifically to surface contradictions a self-review pass might miss. This pass found and logged the corrections folded into this report via the binding addenda: the R26 "whole-entity reorganization" wording collision, the Scenario-A cash-netting inconsistency, the missing active-production valuation treatment, the R22 partial-research-transfer cliff-edge, and ten missing formulas now carried in §21 as Current Ops defaults rather than Owner decisions.

**Phase 5 — Owner/Future Ops addenda.** Two later addenda were relayed after the research above was substantially complete and were applied retroactively wherever they touched this report's content: (1) Full Absorption (Model A) selected as the initial P16C operating model, superseding "Model D is the sole model" in the ruleset annex's R10–R12 and dossier 13's own conclusion; (2) the P14 26-week-capped early-termination charge as Owner-selected product direction, superseding the accepted-code 50% fraction (`HIRING_TERMINATION_FRACTION 0.5`) as the figure this report cites for releasing inherited staff.

**Phase 6 — Report writing and assembly.** Five section-writing passes drafted this report's twenty-one numbered sections and closing table from the Phase 1–5 evidence base, each carrying forward every locator, confidence level, and evidence-class label from the underlying dossiers and ruleset annex; a final assembly pass merged the five drafts into this single document, renumbered and de-duplicated cross-references, verified every "Model D"/label/subsidiary/full-absorption passage against the binding addenda, and replaced any remaining scratchpad or session-local reference with a package-relative or commit-pinned link.

**Model allocation.** Phase 1 (the eighteen evidence dossiers) ran on the frontier model; phases 2–6 (candidate-lens drafting, judging/merging, the adversarial authority + exploit repair pass, paper scenarios, and report drafting/assembly/consistency checking) ran on Claude Sonnet, and the citation spot-checks on Claude Haiku, per the Owner's instruction to run subagent work on models cheaper than the frontier model. The independent second reviewer read the assembled report in full, verified the load-bearing code and documentation locators directly against the pinned commits, and made the final edits recorded in the package review index (`P16-INDEPENDENT-VERIFICATION-REVIEW-INDEX.md`).
