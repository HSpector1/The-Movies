## 1. Product proposition

A crossover candidate enters the studio's field of view with a real prior identity, some observable public evidence, uncertain screen performance, individual ambitions, and possibly binding outside commitments. The studio may investigate, test, offer a suitable first role, build a vehicle, cast against expectation, wait, or accept a refusal. The result becomes one continuous screen career attached to the same person.

This is not a generic “famous person” modifier and not a simulation of wrestling, sport, music, comedy, theatre, dance, broadcasting, modelling, or online entertainment. Origin categories authorize no stereotypes. Only a particular fictional person's authored and subsequently observed evidence matters.

The core design law is:

> **Prior visibility can reduce uncertainty about awareness. It never supplies free acting craft, role Fit, screen experience, contractual availability, or guaranteed ticket demand.**

## 3. Screen-credit and evidence taxonomy

The game should not collapse these into a single `hasActed` flag:

| Evidence class | What it may establish | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Origin-field broadcast or music video | Camera familiarity or public visibility when specifically evidenced | Scripted acting craft or film draw |
| Documentary/concert film/self appearance | Persona visibility and source-audience interest | Character acting or dramatic range |
| Cameo/self appearance | Bounded production experience and recognition in context | Lead readiness or transferable demand |
| Scripted television guest | Screen-specific performance evidence for that role/medium | Feature-leading draw |
| Supporting or ensemble feature | Collaboration, delivery, and project-specific film evidence | Independent vehicle demand |
| Leading persona vehicle | Whether a specific audience/project proposition converts | Broad role range or durable career |
| Against-expectation role | Evidence about range beyond public persona | Guaranteed future demand |
| Sustained credited career | Accumulated screen experience and release-derived Star Power | Availability, desire, or Fit for every new role |

P10 remains the source of credited screen history. Authored background records only pre-simulation provenance; it does not create fictional credits or entire seasons, tours, catalogues, matches, audiences, or awards.

## 4. The player decision

### 4.1 Discovery

A fictional candidate may become visible through a scout/referral, a public event, a casting search, an intermediary, or a project-specific recommendation. Discovery exposes a bounded dossier:

- authored origin discipline and era-appropriate public evidence;
- an estimated reach band by geography and relevant audience segment, with provenance and confidence;
- current public persona/expectation tags, never mandatory personality stereotypes;
- relevant recorded experience, if any;
- known outside-commitment windows and uncertainty;
- interest in screen work and the kinds of engagement the person will consider;
- existing P10 public profile and P04 audition/screen-test observations.

Discovery does **not** reveal exact acting ability, exact hidden potential, a general fan-conversion rate, or a guaranteed acceptance response.

### 4.2 Offers and why each can be sensible

| Choice | Sensible when | Principal risk | Evidence gained |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Screen test / read-through** | Craft, camera presence, or role Fit is materially uncertain | It costs time and remains contextual; a test can be wrong | P04-style observation with explicit confidence and context |
| **Supporting-role debut** | Project has a suitable bounded part and strong collaborators | Limited ability to estimate independent draw | Delivery, collaboration, film-set experience, role Fit |
| **Ensemble casting** | Existing persona adds texture but should not carry the whole proposition | Ensemble can hide weaknesses or produce ambiguous attribution | Work behavior and audience response within a real release |
| **Leading persona vehicle** | Audience overlap is strongly evidenced and the project genuinely fits | Concentrates financial, craft, and overlap risk; can trap expectations | Direct evidence about that exact proposition |
| **Against-expectation casting** | Test/work evidence suggests range and the person wants it | Existing fans may not overlap; marketing may confuse | Range evidence and new screen identity if the film works |
| **Limited engagement / wait** | Outside obligations or uncertainty make a full deal unsafe | Competitor may act first; opportunity can disappear | Availability-respecting choice without false ownership |
| **Decline / accept refusal** | The performer prefers another field, education, rest, or a different role | No screen project occurs | Agency is preserved; history records the proposal outcome, not a fabricated career |

An offer must use the accepted P10/P11/P12 contract and finance seams. “Crossover contract” is not a separate agreement system.

### 4.3 Outside commitments

The minimum model is an authored, bounded availability commitment: type, start/end or window, reliability, source, and known conflict. It may represent a season, tour leg, broadcast obligation, competition, stage run, or public appearance block, but does not simulate the originating business.

Recommended ownership is a P10 availability projection validated against P12 employer/exclusivity/interval law; P14 may later supply market/lifecycle provenance. A conflict produces an exact reason and alternatives:

- move the film phase or shooting pattern;
- make a shorter/supporting offer;
- choose a different performer;
- wait until the commitment ends;
- release the offer without penalty;
- let the candidate decline or choose a career transition.

It must never silently treat a person as free because the game does not simulate their league, tour, show, or stage production.

## 5. Fame, audience, and the no-double-counting law

### 5.1 Accepted-code audit

At accepted TypeScript `2753e18...`:

| Existing fact / symbol | Current meaning | Constraint on crossover design |
|---|---|---|
| `Talent.fame` and `src/core/starPower.ts::computeStarPowerDelta` | Screen-specific commercial recognition/draw built from credited releases | Do not seed it from an origin category. |
| `src/core/reception.ts` star-draw contribution | Cast fame contributes to segment appeal/opening response | A second full-strength origin bonus here would duplicate audience pull. |
| `preMarketingAwarenessOf` | Blends studio audience awareness, film appeal, and paid marketing into discoverability | Origin reach must not also masquerade as paid awareness. |
| `src/core/standing.ts` audience awareness update | Release reach and cast fame affect later public awareness | Do not grant Standing before the studio has earned it through a real release. |
| `Talent.acting` / P04 role Fit | Craft and project-specific suitability | Origin fame never modifies either without actual evidence/work. |

### 5.2 Recommended single-entry law

`XOV-007 — OWNER DECISION REQUIRED`

An origin profile may supply an **estimated relevant audience overlap**: a bounded, provenance-bearing interpretation of source awareness for the film's era, territory, audience segment, medium, role, and proposition. Before demand is calculated, origin reach, existing audience awareness, earned screen Star Power and paid marketing aimed at overlapping people must pass through a provenance-aware union/cap or another explicit de-duplication rule. The origin evidence enters **once** inside that reach/discoverability result. It is uncertainty-adjusted and auditable. Real screen outcomes supersede uncertainty about screen conversion; they do not automatically erase independently current source-field awareness.

It must not:

- add to Star Power and then be added again as marketing or awareness;
- count the same reachable people again because origin, screen awareness and paid marketing have different labels;
- create studio Standing before a release;
- convert every source follower into a ticket buyer;
- grant acting craft, role Fit, or permanent audience ownership;
- multiply revenue independently after already affecting demand.

The rejected alternatives are (a) broadening Star Power into all-purpose celebrity, which destroys P10's screen-specific meaning, and (b) adding a separate marketing multiplier, which duplicates the same people. Aggregate star research finds selection-sensitive and context-dependent effects rather than a universal premium ([Hofmann et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S016781161630115X)); research on field migration likewise treats recognition as having a field-specific exchange rate ([Chard & Litherland](https://eprints.hud.ac.uk/id/eprint/33851/1/Hollywood%20Hulk%20Hogan_Chard_Litherland_Revised_Converis%20%281%29.pdf)). No numerical law is approved.

## 6. Fictional career journeys

### 6.1 Rhea Vale — wrestling visibility to a real, uneven screen career

Rhea Vale is an original fictional performer known in 1998 for a charismatic wrestling persona and unusually broad family-audience interview reach. Her authored background records that fact and two existing appearance blocks; it does not invent match results.

1. A studio action-comedy has a supporting rival role. Rhea's origin overlap is promising, but acting evidence is sparse.
2. A P04-style screen test returns strong physical storytelling, adequate comic timing, and uncertainty on quieter dialogue. The player can cast her, use a conventional actor, or wait.
3. The supporting role finishes within her outside commitments. The film's release supplies the first authoritative screen outcome, credit, work development, and Star Power change.
4. A competitor offers a full persona vehicle. The player's similar vehicle is expensive and has weak overlap outside the original audience. If produced, it opens well in two segments but has ordinary legs and mixed craft evidence—no automatic blockbuster conversion.
5. Rhea asks for a smaller against-expectation comedy rather than another persona sequel. Accepting creates a real development opportunity; refusing may lead her elsewhere. Either decision is recorded without ownership language.
6. By 2007 she has a dependable ensemble career and occasional leads, not the game's largest star. Her wrestling origin remains biography; her current screen draw comes from actual releases.

This proves that a successful crossover can be valuable without copying a famous real career or ending in universal superstardom.

### 6.2 Tomas Ardent — elite sport to one suitable film, then another path

Tomas Ardent is an original fictional sprinter in 1964 with strong regional awareness, little family-audience overlap, a calm broadcast manner, and a protected competition/training window.

1. A period ensemble needs a supporting courier whose movement background is relevant. Tomas accepts a limited test; it shows natural physical delivery but uncertain dramatic range.
2. The player casts him in the bounded part. His training window forces a production choice: reschedule his scenes, choose an alternative actor, or release him. Ignoring the conflict is impossible.
3. The finished film performs modestly. Tomas earns one authentic credit and some screen experience; the origin following does not become global film demand.
4. A lead vehicle is offered. Tomas declines because he prefers broadcasting after retirement from sport. That refusal is a valid outcome, not a failed loyalty check.
5. Years later he accepts one commentator cameo as himself. His history shows sport provenance, a supporting acting credit, a declined lead, and broadcasting availability. It does not generate an unseen filmography.

This is the required non-blockbuster path: useful discovery, a sensible role, a real constraint, and a person whose ambitions do not become the studio's plan.

## 8. Paper validation before any implementation

The complete proof register is in the [implementation plan](TALENT-ORIGINS-IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN-AND-REQUIREMENT-REGISTER.md). The minimum crossover paper fixtures must show:

1. a famous outsider with limited screen craft versus a skilled unknown, with neither objectively dominant;
2. identical source-popularity bands producing different relevance by project, audience, geography, and era;
3. one origin-following contribution in the demand explanation and no second revenue contribution, including a fixture where origin reach, earned screen Star Power and paid marketing target the same segment and overlapping people are unioned/capped rather than counted repeatedly;
4. a modest/ended crossover attempt;
5. an outside commitment blocking film work with a readable alternative;
6. two same-name people retaining distinct `PersonId` values;
7. one `PersonId` through origin, first credit, employer change, profession change, and later career;
8. rival use of the same constraints;
9. inspection that consumes no gameplay mutation and no RNG.

No prototype or test modification is authorized by these proof ideas.
