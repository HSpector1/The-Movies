## 2. Executive decision

The final package name is **Package 14 — Talent Market, Relationships & Career Lifecycle**.

Its fantasy is not “manage every human meter.” It is:

> Build a studio people choose to join, keep promises they can remember, discover creative partnerships through actual work, lose talent honestly to a shared market, and preserve every career after the last contract ends.

The coherent package is three sequential slices:

- **P14A — Contested Talent Market:** one expiring person, one intermediary route, explicit player and rival offers, revalidation, and one atomic decision that calls the P10 contract and P12 employer/exclusivity authorities while preserving linked market/career evidence.
- **P14B — Professional Bonds:** typed promises and trust consequences plus a sparse collaboration graph derived primarily from shared work; mentorship and rivalry are earned classifications, not buttons.
- **P14C — Career Lifecycle:** birth-derived age, life phase, read-only P10 development context, voluntary/eligibility retirement, alumni, renewable cohorts, and full historical continuity.

**Explicit parking label, not approved P14 completion scope:** **P14D — Advanced Labor & Contract
Mobility** would contain in-term approaches, negotiated release, buyout/compensation, contract break,
and tampering law. It cannot enter P14B or P14C. The Owner must later decide whether it returns as a
post-P14 follow-on or remains in P16+; this report parks it in P16+ by default.

**PRELIMINARY RECOMMENDATION:** implement in that order only after P10, P11, P12, P13, and their Owner-accepted changed paths exist. P14A must not include in-term contract breaking, promises, relationship simulation, aging, or retirement. That exclusion is the architectural proof, not a lack of ambition.

**PRELIMINARY RECOMMENDATION — package-number reconciliation:** P14A re-homes only P12's deferred **P12B live competitive labor-market** slice under the Owner's present P13–P15 spine. It does not supersede P12A. P12 remains authoritative for `StudioId`, current employer ownership, the one-employer exclusivity index, employer intervals/transitions, and rival decision scheduling. P10 remains authoritative for contract terms, validation, activation, renewal, and expiry. P14 owns competitive cases/proposals, intermediary and knowledge state, chooser receipts, and atomic orchestration across those authorities. The reallocation remains subject to Owner approval of the package boundaries.

1. place a person in a contested but explainable labor market;

### Original pain and retained soul

**SOURCE VERIFIED:** the original made human careers visible on the lot, carried recognizable state between studios, and required repeated dragging for practice, social progress, stress relief, and status objects. **INFERENCE:** at campaign scale those repeated actions form maintenance grind, so the successor should preserve visible careers while removing clerical repetition.

**PRELIMINARY RECOMMENDATION:** retain physical arrivals, named people, collaborations, consequences, departures, and retirement punctuation. Remove repetitive social/practice/needs servicing. A person becomes memorable because of films, employers, promises, partners, setbacks, and dates—not because the player clicked “chat” forty times.

## 10. Product fantasy

The player first meets a career as a person in the world:

- a director leaving a soundstage after a breakthrough;
- an actor arriving for an agency meeting while another studio's offer is known;
- a veteran collaborator whose renewal window has become consequential;
- a former employee returning for an award or alumni retrospective; or
- a young prospect whose body, profile, and history all resolve to the same identity.

The market creates authored tension, not random theft. “Can we keep her?” becomes a legible question of contract timing, role, pay, creative opportunity, trust, relationships, and competing plans. “Why did he leave?” has durable reasons. “What did they make together?” opens exact credits. “What happened after retirement?” keeps the same profile and chronology.

The player manages infrequent commitments:

1. prepare for renewal or free agency;
2. request/intercept a market route through an intermediary;
3. shape, review, submit, revise, or withdraw the studio's proposal while all material terms and deadlines remain visible;
4. keep or break typed promises through actual studio action;
5. create opportunities for productive collaborators without social micromanagement;
6. use P10-authored development focus and observed progress as career context, without allocating development in P14; and
7. respond to retirement/succession with an archive, not a deletion.

## 11. Binding design laws

1. **One person, one immutable identity.** Employer, profession focus, active status, age phase, retirement, and display presentation may change; `PersonId` does not.
2. **One active employer.** The expiry-phase chooser and settlement are one atomic TypeScript transaction that closes incompatible proposals and prevents two current employer intervals; no player input accepts a person or chooses the winner.
3. **No card-instance transfers.** A person never becomes a new record because a rival signs them.
4. **P10 remains person authority.** P14 references the P10 person/profile/contract/career spine; it does not duplicate craft, Star Power, credits, or active assignments.
5. **P11 owns money.** P14 describes terms and obligations; P11 remains the accounting, affordability, and forecast authority.
6. **P12 owns studios and rival agency.** P14 never invents `StudioId`, rival budget, or employer state in a client.
7. **Offers are versioned proposals, not conversations.** Every visible term, deadline, party, route, and reason is authoritative. Any material term mutation invalidates prior review and requires a fresh submission; there is no client acceptance state.
8. **No hidden poaching dice.** Eligibility, interest band, refusal, competition, deadline, and settlement are explainable from typed reasons. Hidden preference inputs may affect behavior only through honest public descriptors/ranges.
9. **No one-click in-term poaching.** P14A handles expiring/available people. Early break, release, buyout, compensation, and tampering are parked in Owner-gated P14D/P16+ and cannot enter P14B/C.
10. **Intermediaries provide access and information.** They never override eligibility, fabricate an offer, or guarantee consent.
11. **Promises are typed commitments.** A promise has issuer, beneficiary, predicate, deadline, progress, version, outcome, and evidence. Free text cannot alter simulation.
12. **Trust is not a mood meter.** Trust is an explainable professional record derived from kept/broken commitments and material conduct. It is not serviced daily.
13. **Relationships require drivers.** Every material edge has typed work/event evidence. No unexplained “chemistry +12%” is permitted.
14. **Work is the primary social action.** Collaboration grows through shared credited/rehearsal/production experience; no relationship click-grind or required social venue ladder.
15. **Sparse graph, bounded effects.** Only material pairs retain active summaries. No all-pairs weekly update, traversal, or projection.
16. **Development stays with P10.** P14 may display and consume P10-authored focus, observed progress, and work-derived development context; it never authors focus selection, development events, individual stat allocation, or exact hidden potential.
17. **Age is derived, not incremented.** Store a truthful birth datum/provenance and derive age/life phase from authoritative calendar week.
18. **Decline, if approved, is bounded and visible in drivers.** It is never a universal hidden cliff or exact potential reveal.
19. **Retirement changes eligibility, not existence—and never breaks a contract silently.** At the approved P14C effective boundary, P14 alumni/eligibility, P10 assignment/contract/person, explicit P11 obligation-or-none, and P12 roster/employer/exclusivity/interval receipts commit in one candidate or none. Uncleared obligations defer/refuse with typed reasons; the person, profile, credits, awards, history, relationships, and alumni summary persist.
20. **Supply renews deterministically without stealing identity authority.** P14 schedules an era-aware cohort request; P10 atomically mints/registers each `PersonId` and returns an idempotent receipt before P14 commits lifecycle/market provenance. Retirement never empties the market, P14 never mints a person, and failure creates no partial cohort.
21. **Player and rivals obey the same market law.** Different strategy is allowed; different legality, hidden exemptions, or player-only penalties are not.
22. **No fabricated past.** Migrated saves begin recording new facts at migration; earlier employer, promise, or relationship history says “Not recorded.”
23. **TypeScript owns all outcomes.** Unity projects people, work, offers, reasons, and history; it never calculates legality, affinity, trust, retirement, or settlement.
24. **Attention is exception-based.** Deadlines, newly competitive offers, promise risk, and lifecycle decisions can interrupt; routine relationship drift cannot.
25. **History is paged and summarized.** Current views are bounded; century-long career/event collections are never fully embedded per frame.

## 12. State model

### 12.1 P14A offer and employment state

```text
person eligibility
  ├─ contracted_outside_window
  ├─ renewal_window
  ├─ expiring_to_market
  ├─ free_agent
  ├─ retired_or_ineligible
  └─ unavailable_for_recorded_reason

market case
  discovered → intermediary_contacted → accepting_proposals
           → proposals_open → decision_pending
           → settled | declined | expired | invalidated

proposal version
  draft/projected → reviewed → submitted
  material P10 or P14 term change → new offerVersion at draft/projected
  submitted → selected | not_selected | withdrawn | superseded | expired | invalidated

deadline decision and settlement (one atomic TypeScript transaction)
  freeze and revalidate every eligible proposal at the expiry phase
  invalidate failed proposals with typed reasons
  apply the deterministic person-choice rule to the remaining set
  if none remain, record decline/free agency
  otherwise invoke the P10 contract, P11 obligation, and P12 employer-transition authorities
  commit the P14 chooser receipt and linked P10/P12 career/employer facts together
```

P14 does not copy P10's contract schema. A proposal carries a stable reference to a P10-authored contract draft/quote plus its terms version and digest, alongside P14-owned opportunity, route, knowledge, and deadline facts. A changed P10 material term creates a new version and invalidates prior player review before submission. The player may submit, revise, or withdraw the player's proposal; the client never chooses the winning employer or sends a settlement command.

### 12.2 P14B promise/trust and relationship state

```text
promise: proposed → active → satisfied | broken | waived | impossible_external | expired
relationship edge: absent → observed → established → notable → dormant
relationship classification: collaborator | mentor_pair | professional_rivals | strained
```

Classifications are derived from driver evidence. They are not mutually exclusive personality labels, and they do not replace the edge's underlying public reasons. Dormancy bounds active computation; evidence and summary history persist.

### 12.3 P14C lifecycle state

```text
scheduled_cohort → P10_registration_pending → emerging → established → veteran
                                     ├→ retirement_eligible
                                     ├→ retirement_announced
                                     └→ active_until_effective_week
retirement_announced → retired_alumni
retired_alumni → archived_alumni (presentation tier only; identity unchanged)
```

“Prime” is not a hidden exact age interval. If used, it is a visible descriptive life phase driven by authored role-specific rules. “Archived” means cold/paged presentation storage, not deletion.
`retirement_announced → retired_alumni` is not a P14-only write. The scheduled coordinator preflights
P10/P11/P12; an outstanding assignment, contract, obligation, roster, employer/exclusivity, or interval
guard records a typed deferral and next check. A clear boundary commits every owner receipt plus the
P14 retirement/alumni event once; any failure leaves authoritative state unchanged.

### 13.1 Additive roots

For P14C cohorts, the root stores a bounded immutable request body and digest, schedule/worldgen/rules
versions, requested count (hard maximum 32), exact slot-to-P10-registration-to-`PersonId` mappings for

The lifecycle root also stores the P14 retirement announcement/settlement receipt and foreign P10,

These are interface-level ownership concepts only; the Builder Annex sketches shapes without authorizing names or schema.

### 13.2 Immutable identity rules

- `PersonId` is created once and never contains employer, role, name, age, or array position.
- P14 never creates that `PersonId`; a new cohort member must carry the exact committed P10 registration receipt that created it.
- `StudioId` comes from P12 and never contains display name or ownership state.
- P14-owned `OfferId`, `NegotiationId`, `PromiseId`, `RelationshipEdgeId`, `RetirementEventId`, and `IntermediaryEngagementId` are stable and domain-specific. P10 `ContractId`, P12 `EmployerIntervalId`, and existing career-event IDs remain foreign authoritative references, never P14-minted substitutes.
- Relationship pair identity uses canonical ordering of two distinct `PersonId`s; `(A,B)` and `(B,A)` cannot coexist.
- Every event records effective week, recorded week, source/system version, and provenance.
- Display-name changes never rewrite historical IDs. Views resolve current names while a dated display-name event may preserve period context where P12/P10 law allows.
- Deletion is not a career operation. Even a retired, unavailable, or future acquired-studio person remains addressable.

### 13.3 No hidden data contract

The client may receive:

- a public interest tier/range with typed visible drivers;
- current offer terms and authoritative comparison rows;
- explicit deadlines and consequences;
- public trust descriptors and evidence summaries;
- public relationship classifications and recent drivers;
- visible life phase/retirement eligibility and recorded reasons; and
- paged exact career/employer/promise/relationship event summaries.

The client must not receive hidden skill, exact ceiling/potential, AI preference weights, random seeds, unrevealed rival maximum offers, unpublished future bids, or internal relationship influence coefficients.

## 14. World-first interaction

World-first does not mean every negotiation is performed by dragging a body.

### Primary route

1. A person, Agency Office/meeting marker, Stage School arrival point, or production collaboration marker shows a restrained status glyph and text.
2. Selecting it identifies the exact person/event and states the immediate fact: “renewal opens,” “agency meeting available,” “offer changed,” “retirement announced,” or “collaboration became notable.”
3. `Open Talent Market`, `Open Profile`, or `Review Career` retains the exact `PersonId`, `StudioId`, market case, and source world context.
4. The retained workspace performs comparison and `Review & Submit Proposal`; it never accepts the person or chooses the winner.
5. Back returns to the same selected world subject if still valid; otherwise it explains why the subject is no longer available and preserves safe camera context.

### World consequences

- a signed arrival uses the existing lot arrival/identity language only after settlement;
- a departing person exits after the effective week, never before the final obligation;
- collaborators may be seen working in the same authoritative production context, but Unity does not infer a relationship;
- retirement can stage a farewell/portrait/history moment without deleting the person;
- an intermediary is represented by an office/meeting/visitor route only if an authoritative engagement exists; no ambient NPC implies a market action.

### Prohibitions

- no dragging a person into another studio;
- no floating hearts as the only relationship explanation;
- no repeated “socialize” action;
- no body position used as employer/offer/relationship truth;
- no physical rival lots before P12/P15 prove value;
- no client-created negotiation or retirement outcome.

## 16. Economy and resource consequences

P14 must create choices P11 can explain:

- salary, signing bonus, guarantee, term, and approved compensation obligations;
- intermediary commission and service time;
- timing risk when a proposal expires or a person remains unsigned;
- staffing/production opportunity cost of a promised role or development path;
- retention versus replacement cost;
- possible P14D/P16+ buyout/release compensation, only if separately Owner-chartered;
- veteran/alumni program cost only if a real benefit/system exists; and
- market scarcity generated by supply, demand, eligibility, and rival needs—not an arbitrary price multiplier.

Money never directly buys trust or relationship affinity. A better salary may satisfy an explicit term or visible professional concern, but it does not erase a broken promise. A costly agent does not guarantee acceptance. An expired proposal does not silently reserve funds forever.

Every displayed cost must carry effective date, payer, recipient, recurrence/one-time status, contingency, and P11-provided cash/obligation effect. P14 must not recalculate balance-sheet totals or forecast cash in Unity.

Rivals may value genre fit, prestige, budget, opportunity, or succession differently. That is strategy, not cheating. A rival cannot sign someone without an offer record, ignore exclusivity, see hidden player bids, or escape compensation that binds the player.

Every due case freezes its offers against the same pre-settlement snapshot. P14A.1 proves one case and
therefore does not pretend to solve portfolio scarcity. Before any multi-case expansion, every case
receives a scheduler-authored, persisted, visible `caseAdmissionOrdinal` when admitted to its due-week

replacement bounded admission law is approved. A person's selection among valid offers emits visible

## 18. Save/migration/RNG/determinism

### Save law

- Each implementation wave receives an explicit save version and validator before content expansion.
- New roots are additive. Frozen `Talent`, `Studio`, `Standing`, and `EraConfig` leaves are not widened casually.
- References validate existence, kind, distinct pair IDs, interval ordering, current-employer uniqueness, version/state consistency, and event sequence monotonicity.
- Every new P14 history row persists the scheduler-owned semantic phase ID, immutable sparse ordinal,
  and phase-order version. A later phase catalogue cannot remap old rows; legacy rows without evidence
  remain in a fixed `legacy_phase_unspecified` bucket with `phasePrecision: not_recorded`.
- Old saves do not receive invented offers, former employers, relationships, promises, birthdays, retirement decisions, or alumni events.
- When prior age exists but birth truth does not, migration may store `birthWeekEstimate` plus provenance `derived_from_legacy_age_at_migration`, bounded to the same displayed age at migration. It must never claim an exact birthday.
- Existing film/career events remain byte-stable in meaning. New indexes can reference them; no rewrite or re-award occurs.

### RNG law

- P14A eligibility, rival-offer creation, interest/refusal, proposal revalidation, talent choice, and expiry settlement are deterministic and consume no RNG.
- UI order, paging, camera state, locale, frame rate, and save/load count cannot change outcomes.
- A later Owner-authorized cohort or lifecycle system may use a named domain stream only for bounded timing/variety that rules cannot decide; the stream is keyed by immutable IDs, effective week, rule version, and purpose and persists an outcome receipt.
- Any authorized random tie/variation publishes an honest reason category; it can never conceal poaching eligibility, affordability, contract legality, or the winning employer.
- Settlement tie-break is authored and deterministic. No dependence on object/map iteration order.
- Relationship evidence from a known event is deterministic; relationship projection never samples.

### Replay law

A replay consists of initial accepted save + content/rules versions + ordered authoritative commands + deterministic scheduled events. The proof checks market cases, contracts, active-employer index, career events, relationship summaries, lifecycle states, finances by reference, and state digest at checkpoints. Replay need not hash an entire save for every view or frame.

## 19. Long-horizon performance/storage

The design target is approximately 6,240 weekly ticks from 1920 through 2040.

### Bounded work laws

- No weekly scan of all career events, all offer history, all retired people, or all relationship pairs.
- No all-pairs relationship matrix. An active sparse edge exists only after qualifying evidence.
- P12's current-employer lookup is indexed by `PersonId`; its interval history is referenced, append-only, and paged rather than mirrored in P14.
- Deadlines use a due-week queue/bucket, not a scan of all open/closed cases.
- Promise evaluation registers only on relevant events and a bounded due-date index; it does not poll every promise every week.
- Relationship updates consume the current week's typed collaboration events and touch only named pairs.
- Lifecycle transitions use a due-week/age-boundary queue, not a full talent scan.
- Retired/alumni records leave hot active indexes but remain in cold paged history.
- Current snapshots carry bounded summaries and cursor metadata, not complete event arrays.
- Views never hash the full save repeatedly. Digests are produced at authoritative transition/checkpoint boundaries with incremental or bounded strategies.

### Planning envelope, not a promise

**PRELIMINARY RECOMMENDATION:** capacity tests should include at least 10 studios, 2,500 immutable people created over the century, 500 simultaneously active workers, 50,000 career/market/lifecycle events, 20,000 employer intervals/offers, and 25,000 retained relationship-driver events after compaction. These are stress fixtures, not final content numbers.

- routine new available candidates;

## 27. Golden journeys

1. **World renewal route:** select an expiring employee on the lot, open the exact market case, compare the current renewal, return to the same person.
2. **Known rival competition:** a rival proposal becomes known; the case shows its source, effective week, known terms/unknowns, and why competition matters.
3. **Intermediary preview:** contact the authorized informational intermediary, see expected demands/time/cost, decline without opening a proposal.
4. **Submit player proposal:** review all material terms and consequence, submit once, receive a stable `OfferId`/version and no employment change yet.
5. **Revise terms:** change salary or term; the prior review/submission token becomes invalid and the UI clearly requests review of version two.
6. **Stale submission:** attempt to submit/revise against an old state revision; TypeScript refuses without charging cash or changing employer.
7. **Player wins:** settlement closes the prior interval, activates/references one contract, invalidates rival proposal, appends one career event, and places the person correctly.
8. **Rival wins:** the same `PersonId` leaves at the legal effective week, retains credits/profile, appears under the rival employer, and no duplicate remains on the player roster.
9. **Person declines both:** both offers close with typed reasons; the person becomes a free agent without a phantom employer.
10. **Offer expires:** deadline passes; proposal is expired exactly once, reserved/quoted money is not silently charged, and history records the material outcome.
11. **Offer withdrawn:** player withdraws before deadline; the action is explicit, logged, and cannot later settle.
12. **Insufficient funds:** P11 affordability rejects settlement or submission at its authoritative gate with exact consequence; no partial state is written.
13. **Assignment conflict:** a person finishing committed work cannot transfer early; the effective date and blocker are explicit.
14. **One-employer collision:** replay two duplicate deadline events/commands; the atomic decision receipt is consumed once and at most one P12 employer interval opens.
15. **Save in open case:** save with both proposals open, reload, and retain IDs, versions, deadline, knowledge, and outcome stream.
16. **Replay:** replay identical commands from the same seed/content versions and obtain the same proposal/settlement/history digest.
17. **Legacy migration:** load a pre-P14 save; P12-anchored current employment is referenced from known truth, earlier employer history says “Not recorded,” and P14 fabricates no employer interval, relationship, or promise.
18. **Unknown competitor terms:** player knows a rival is interested but not its exact offer; the comparison shows explicit unknowns instead of false numbers.
19. **Keyboard-only proposal route (P14A.3):** reach person, compare offers, inspect reasons, submit/withdraw, and return with predictable focus and no pointer; no settle action exists.
20. **Controller-only proposal route (P14A.3):** perform the same route with stable selection, shoulder/tab navigation, proposal confirmation only, and a usable Back path; the person chooses at expiry.
21. **Narrow layout:** compare offers as stacked semantic cards with the same terms/reasons and no horizontal truncation.
22. **Relationship evidence later:** two people complete repeated shared films; P14B creates one sparse edge with film references, not a free bonus or daily alert.
23. **Broken promise later:** an active typed opportunity promise reaches deadline; outcome cites assignments/credits and changes public trust summary once.
24. **Mentorship later:** senior/junior collaboration crosses an authored evidence threshold; profile shows why the mentorship label exists and can page the shared work.
25. **Professional rivalry later:** repeated competition/disagreement events establish a rivalry; it has bounded consequences and no unsupported personal hostility prose.
26. **Age derivation later:** advance across a birthday/phase boundary, save/load before and after, and derive the same age from birth provenance without weekly mutation.
27. **Retirement announcement later:** a veteran announces an effective week; roster, commitments, succession attention, and profile agree.
28. **Retirement effective later:** active eligibility ends, contract/employer interval resolves legally, body leaves active staffing, and the same profile becomes alumni.
29. **Alumni film lookup:** open an old film/award and reach the retired person's same ID, exact credit, employer-at-time, and paged career chronology.
30. **Century cohort endurance:** deterministic P14 requests prevent depletion; P10 atomically registers every unique ID and returns idempotent receipts, entrants have honest start provenance, duplicate/failed dispatch leaves no partial person, and no historical person is reminted.
31. **Phase-order evolution:** a new scheduler phase catalogue version is added after a mixed-domain
    same-week career page is issued; old cursor order/replay is unchanged and legacy events gain no
    inferred phase or causal wording.
