# 1. Executive decision

Production should feel like an **autonomous film company visibly occupying a real studio plant**.
The player runs strategy and exceptions; cast, craft, rooms, scenery and time carry out routine work.
At a glance the lot should say which picture is where, what phase it is in, whether the company is
working or waiting, and what deserves attention. Selecting the place explains the present operation.
Opening the picture explains the whole project. Neither surface invents a second simulation.

The core grammar is:

> **Greenlight forms a company → departments carry it forward → a Stage and Set become exact
> physical commitments at Rehearsal → scenery visibly travels at Shooting entry → named company
> members attend authoritative workplaces → the player handles only current legal approvals and
> capacity/set exceptions → shooting becomes watchable → wrap releases the Stage → Post becomes the
> next owner.**

The decisive product rule is:

> **Routine production is autonomous. Information becomes attention only when it changes planning.
> Attention becomes a player stop only when TypeScript publishes a current legal decision.**

This is not a progress bar with scenery. It is also not *The Movies*' scene-by-scene labor of
dragging unhappy Stars and workers back to their marks. The modern successor keeps the original's
physical company, travel, sets and visible filming, while replacing chore-like direct handling with
structured status, explicit causes, exact Locate, and the narrowest truthful remedy.

## 1.1 Ten governing rulings

1. **Stage, Set and Production remain three entities.** Stage is capacity/location; Set is the
   mounted creative environment; Production is the project/company. Never merge their IDs.
2. **Greenlight does not magically light a Stage.** Current authority forms the picture in
   Development. A Stage and Set bind atomically only when Rehearsal is entered.
3. **Local place first, picture second.** A selected Stage answers what is happening here; `Open
   Production` earns the retained project workspace.
4. **The honest progress unit is Phase + current state + authoritative weeks remaining + next
   milestone.** No invented scene count, shot count or simulated percentage.
5. **Scenery transit is work, not a decision.** New V14 pictures show origin, destination and
   authoritative weeks remaining. P05A must settle a due arrival automatically—including the
   current due-at-call edge—and must never offer an early `Clear` button. Grandfathered saves retain
   their existing click honestly.
6. **Capacity/set waits explain four facts:** what waits, what it needs, who holds it, and how to
   relieve it. `studioQueueView` already owns these facts.
7. **Shooting must be visibly discontinuous from idle.** At minimum the exact stage changes light,
   activity, company presence, equipment/crew dressing and local status from the same authoritative
   state.
8. **Named people use exact attendance truth; decorative crew never become implied headcount.** A
   nearby body is not an assignment.
9. **No camera hijack.** Focus and Locate remain explicit. A future `Watch Shoot` is a named,
   escapable presentation mode—not a double-click side effect and not part of P05A.
10. **P05A exposes the engine already present.** It does not create scenes, call sheets, reshoots,
    stunt law, manual trucks, a new clock, or Unity-side production rules.

## 2.2 Movie cards, local information and navigation

**Verified fact, Very high:** the right-side movie cards carried production state through distinct
icons—being written, ready for Casting, ready to shoot, filming, ready to release, released/earning,
and archival. Hover/right-click information bubbles could state what held work up. Clicking a card
located the current set. Keyboard `3` cycled active sets; `4` located the Production Office.
[Official manual, printed pp. 6–8 and 36](https://cdn.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/7900/manuals/manual_english.pdf?t=1447351040).

**Inference:** these cards solved persistent identity and navigation better than they solved
operations. They answered “where is my movie?” but spread cause and remedy across bubbles, people,
sets and rooms. Project: Studio should keep the always-addressable production identity and exact
Locate, while replacing bubble hunting with one local inspector and one project workspace.

## 2.3 What made filming delightful

- The project stopped being an abstract card and became a visible company moving through a set.
- Sets had a reason to exist beyond decoration; different scripts changed the route through the lot.
- Performance animation, camera rigs and Director activity made the result feel authored even when
  the player did not author the individual scene.
- Completion physically sent the film onward.

These are the original's strongest production principles and should survive.

## 2.4 What was already weak in 2005

- Repeatedly dragging Stars/staff to sets, repairing between scenes, and treating moods during every
  break turned studio leadership into character hauling.
- First-come set conflicts were consequential but poorly explained and offered little scheduling
  agency.
- The player could see activity without consistently understanding causal contribution to quality.
- The movie-card/bubble system prioritized compactness over durable comparison and accessibility.
- Many direct manipulations were materially committing actions, a poor fit for Package 02's inert
  selection law.

The Prima guide explicitly calls babysitting a stressed Star a distracting amount of
micromanagement (printed p. 43). This is primary-era evidence that the chore problem is historical,
not merely a modern expectation.

## 2.5 Director view and Advanced Movie Maker boundary

**Verified fact, Very high:** double-left-clicking an idle set produced a fly-by. Double-left-clicking
an actively filming set entered a special director's view that allowed live slider/backdrop changes.
The standard campaign did not thereby become the full Advanced Movie Maker.
[Official manual, printed p. 39](https://cdn.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/7900/manuals/manual_english.pdf?t=1447351040).

Advanced Movie Maker was a separate authored workflow: choose genre/structure, sets, scenes,
roles/costumes and variants; after shooting, rearrange/cut scenes and add audio/subtitles in Post.
No reliable source grants normal generated productions manual scene reordering.

**Ruling:** retain the desire to inspect an active shoot, reject the overloaded double-click and
live scene-authoring implication. Package 02 double-select remains Focus. `Watch Shoot`, if built,
is an explicit presentation mode. Scene authoring belongs to a separate future creative system.

# 3. *Stunts & Effects* / Superstar-era findings

## 3.1 What *Stunts & Effects* actually added

The official expansion manual establishes the following at **Very high confidence**:

- stunt performers and whole-picture or per-scene doubles;
- stunt difficulty from 1–5 and performer stunt skill;
- likeness as a cost of substituting a double;
- a choice between a Star doing the stunt (greater potential value and personal risk) or a trained
  double (safer skill profile but possible likeness penalty);
- success/failure, condition loss, skill gain, injury, Hospital interruption, and consequences for
  reviews/box office/overall quality; and
- Advanced Movie Maker FreeCam/keyframed two-position movement, effects scenes and more authored
  control.

Source: [official *The Movies: Stunts & Effects* manual, PDF pp. 3–6](https://shared.fastly.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/7910/manuals/The_Movies_Stunts_Effects_Manual.pdf?t=1447351041).

This created a genuinely strategic production exception: risk, specialist skill, spectacle and
schedule exposure were coupled. It was not merely another animation pack. It also added a
condition, training and recovery subsystem with potential production interruption.

## 3.2 Superstar Edition evidence

The required [Macinplay Superstar Edition review](https://macinplay.de/spiele-tests/macintosh-games/the-movies-superstar-edition/)
is a secondary source. It describes Superstar as the base game bundled with *Stunts & Effects*, not
a third production ruleset. It corroborates the complete company rehearsing, travelling through
sets, and being enjoyable to watch (section **Alles dreht sich um den Film**). It separately
criticizes the need to keep feeding Stars and throwing them back onto the set, manual archiving,
staffing scarcity, and opaque scoring (section **Mikromanagement, das auch mal nervt**).

**Later-edition verdict:** the later bundle preserved production's strongest fantasy and amplified
spectacle/risk, but exposed rather than repaired the original's intervention burden and causal
opacity.

## 3.3 Project: Studio stunt rulings

| Expansion feature | Ruling | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Specialist/double versus Star risk tradeoff | **CORE FUTURE VALUE** | A legible, voluntary risk/resource decision can make production distinctive. It requires authoritative roles, safety, condition and outcomes. |
| Difficulty, skill, likeness and success/failure | **ERA/GENRE SYSTEM LATER** | Valuable only with honest scene/stunt demand and perceived evidence; none exists in current V14 production law. |
| Visible rehearsal and stunt spectacle | **PRODUCTION FOLLOW-UP** | Presentation value is high after ordinary shooting grammar works. |
| Injury/Hospital/production stall | **LATER** | Requires talent condition, schedule and recovery authority; never infer it from animation. |
| Per-scene manual assignment and FreeCam authoring | **REJECT FOR P05; separate creative mode** | It would turn the bounded operating slice into a movie editor and conflate ordinary play with AMM. |

## 4.1 Comparator conclusions

The best modern pattern is not “more dashboards.” It is a loop of **autonomous work, persistent
project identity, local operational truth, and exception-led escalation**. Parkitect supports the
principle that backstage resource routes can make layout matter without asking the player to carry
every box; visible service motion is our translation. Planet Zoo shows that autonomous staff become
legible when their prerequisites are named. Software Inc.'s historical first-party post demonstrates
a persistent phase object while work runs. Planet Coaster 2 supplies global lists, notification
hierarchy and operating views; Project: Studio adds the exact portfolio-to-place/Back contract. Two
Point supplies the plain-language blocker grammar. *The Movies*
still supplies the irreplaceable reason to care: watching a film company make a movie.

# 6. Production doctrine

## 6.1 The operating model

Project: Studio production is an **exception-managed pipeline with a witnessed workplace**:

- the company autonomously performs routine scheduled work;
- the lot expresses which facilities are hot, dark, waiting, loading or clearing;
- the selected place explains its current operation in five seconds;
- the selected picture explains the whole company and schedule in a retained workspace;
- a blocker is never only a red badge—it names cause, impact and the smallest current remedy; and
- only a current TypeScript intent can commit, advance or resolve anything.

The player is not an assistant director clicking “continue” after every animation. The player is
the studio head deciding capacity, resolving genuine conflicts, authorizing current gates, and
choosing where to invest.

## 6.2 Information classification

| Class | Current examples | Presentation / time law |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ROUTINE AUTONOMOUS WORK** | Development, Pre-production, Rehearsal progress; second Shooting week after completed take; Post; release-ready countdown; company travel; due scenery arrival | Visible locally and in workspace; no alert; Living Time runs. |
| **ATTENTION-WORTHY INFORMATION** | phase changed; Stage assigned; scenery departed/in transit; Stage hot; wrap; Post became next destination | Restrained world cue/receipt; no automatic camera; normally no auto-pause. |
| **PLAYER DECISION / REQUIRED OPERATION** | current `assignShootingDirector`; current `scheduleShootingTake`; explicit build/repair/strike/cancel actions when chosen | Exact action with consequence preview; auto-pause only when authority publishes it as the current decision. |
| **HARD/OPERATING BLOCKER** | `facility-capacity`; `set-unavailable`; scenery transit while not yet arrived | Explain cause/consequence and route to remedy. Capacity/set waits continue with other departments running. Scenery transit is waiting work, not a player decision. |
| **DEEP MANAGEMENT** | queue/holders/remedies, company, locations, locked forecast, set drivers, multiple-picture comparison | Production/Queue/Set workspace; never forced into the local Stage card. |

The two current shooting approvals are honest existing actions but weak strategic choices when only
one outcome is legal. P05A may present them faithfully; it must not pretend they are richer than they
are. Whether unconditional call/schedule gates should later become automatic is a follow-up Owner
and simulation ruling after playtest—not a reason to invent alternatives in Unity.

# 7. Greenlight → Production handoff

Package 04's boundary remains binding. A successful immediate Greenlight atomically creates the
exact `Production`, exact committed company/costs, managed workflow and **current Development &
Casting reservation**. It does **not** promise a Stage or Set at that moment. A queued greenlight
creates no production, ID, reservation, debit or assignment until admission.

## 7.1 Required player experience

1. Greenlight workspace closes to the same mounted lot and same camera.
2. A formation receipt names the title, company, initial phase and current owner:
   `NIGHT HARBOR formed · Development · 8 weeks remaining`.
3. Development/Annex receives positive active treatment. Casting loses its pending-package state.
4. A Production chip/row becomes available immediately and opens the exact picture.
5. `Locate current work` is explicit. If invoked, it selects/focuses Development and opens its local
   inspector; Greenlight itself does not move the camera.
6. Stage buildings remain dark/idle until the allocator grants one at Rehearsal entry.

The handoff must not say “Stage 7 assigned” unless a current exact reservation says so. It must not
show a Shooting command or lighting state at formation.

# 8. Production phase model

Current TypeScript authority uses one eight-week countdown. Package 05 does not add phases.

| Remaining ticks | Authoritative phase | Exact held capability | Player-facing lot behavior | Attention / action |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | Development | `development-casting` | Production identity at Development/Annex; writer + Director presentation canon there | Formation receipt, then routine work |
| 7 | Pre-production | `development-casting` | Same operating owner; writer + Director; readiness for Stage/Set transition visible in project | Routine unless next transition waits |
| 6 | Rehearsal | one exact soundstage plus atomically bound standing Set | Exact Stage becomes occupied/hot; Director + Lead/Antagonist/Support travel/attend | Stage-assignment cue; no manual attendance |
| 5 | Shooting, first week | retained Stage/Set + `set-scenery`; one `ShootingTask` | Call → scenery transit/load-in → ready → take scheduled; Director/cast at Stage, craft at Scenery Shop | Only exact current command pauses; transit itself does not |
| 4 | Shooting, second week | retained Stage/Set + `set-scenery` until wrap | `completed` shooting beat; visible shoot continues as settled-week presentation | Routine advance; no repeat scheduling |
| 3–2 | Post-production | Post slot | Stage/Set/scenery already released; Director + craft at Post | Post is next owner; Package 05 stops at handoff |
| 1 | Release Ready | none | No production facility claim | Future Release package |
| 0 | existing release pipeline | none | Existing release result | Outside Package 05 |

### Phase transition law

- Rehearsal acquires a soundstage and standing usable Set as one composite; a picture never holds
  one while waiting for the other.
- Rehearsal retains Stage+Set into Shooting.
- Shooting completion releases Stage, Set and Scenery **before** attempting Post. A wrapped picture
  waiting for Post holds nothing and must not keep a stage lit.
- Transitions are deterministic, fixed-point, longest-waiting first with numeric production ordinal
  tie-break under current V14 law.
- A blocked production holds its own countdown while studio time, payroll, overhead and other
  departments continue.

`Load-in` and `wrap clearing` are operational substates/presentation beats, not new persisted phases.
Current `firstFilmJourney.ts` labels Rehearsal as `LOAD-IN`, but current derived scenery transit does
not begin until the Director call creates a Shooting blocker. P05A must correct that guidance:
Rehearsal is company/stage preparation; `LOAD-IN` is reserved for the exact Shooting transit/task.

# 9. Stage / Set / Production ownership

## 9.1 Stable entity law

| Entity | Owns | Does not own | Default selected surface |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Stage** (`facilityId` + world building ID) | physical capacity, location, reservation slot, doors/lights/activity, current occupancy | picture identity, final quality, generic “the set”, project schedule | Stage inspector: place-first |
| **Set** (`setId`) | mounted environment, blueprint/type, name, stage mount, standing/build/retired state, public quality/novelty/condition/genre weights | stage capacity, company assignment, production phase | Set/Scenery inspector; cross-link to occupying picture when exact |
| **Production** (`productionId`) | title/concept, company, budget/forecast lock, phase/countdown, workflow, blocker, current locations | world transform, visual actor position, presentation timing | Production inspector/workspace: project-first |
| **Person** (`talentId`) | identity, role on picture, current authoritative engagement/presence | assignment inferred from proximity | Person inspector/Profile with `Open Production` |

## 9.2 Selection relationships

- Selecting a Stage selects the Stage. Its occupancy section links to the exact Production and Set.
- Selecting a mounted Set selects the Set. It links to its Stage and exact holder if one exists.
- Selecting a Production from portfolio/workspace keeps the project selected; `Locate current work`
  explicitly focuses its exact current world owner.
- Selecting a company member selects that person. The person card names the exact picture and role;
  `Open Production` deep-links without changing camera.
- If a future production uses several locations, the Production workspace lists all exact locations;
  no “primary” location is invented. `Locate` requires choosing a named current location unless one
  exact current-work target is authoritative.
- An outdoor Set remains a Set/location with its own world anchor; it must not impersonate a
  Soundstage. Current P05A only exposes the locations current TypeScript can prove.

# 10. What the player does during production

## 10.1 World-native / local

The selected Stage may expose:

- `Focus Stage` (Package 02 camera command);
- `Open Production`;
- `Open Set`;
- exact `Call <Director> to <Stage>` when the current authoritative browser command / opaque bridge
  intent exists;
- exact `Schedule shooting take` under the same current-state rule;
- `Locate cause` / `Open Scenery Shop` / `Open Queue` for current blockers; and
- person rows that select the exact Director/cast member without committing anything.

Local inspection never advances time, assigns a different Stage/Set, or resolves a blocker merely
because it was selected.

## 10.2 Retained workspace

The Production workspace earns the larger surface for:

- the complete phase rail and next milestone;
- whole company and exact current attendance/destinations;
- Stage/Set/Scenery/Post relationships;
- schedule hold and queue-holder/remedy explanation;
- current locked forecast/exposure summary;
- known production drivers and concerns; and
- multiple current pictures/portfolio routing.

## 10.3 Deliberately absent

- per-scene call sheets or shot order;
- manual performer pathing;
- manual truck driving or freight loading;
- sliders for “work harder/faster” without an authoritative tradeoff;
- a generic `Fix` button that guesses the remedy;
- repeat acknowledgment buttons for phase transitions; and
- an editable quality meter.

# 11. Shooting as spectacle

## 11.1 Minimum world contract

The following differences must be legible without opening a deep workspace. They are driven from
the exact Stage operation plus `studioWeekTheater`/`studioPresence`, never from a local timer or
visual inference.

| Stage state | At management scale | At medium scale | At close scale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Idle / dark** | no hot beacon; subdued stage; `AVAILABLE` only on selection/semantic label | closed/quiet apron, no production group | inspectable empty/standing Set truth only; no fake crew |
| **Rehearsing / preparing** | occupied light and title/phase marker | Director/cast travel or attend; modest door/apron movement | rehearsal blocking/marking poses; Set visible; shooting beacon/equipment performance off |
| **Scenery in transit / load-in** | delivery cue and Stage `LOAD-IN`; not `SHOOTING` | P05A target: service vehicle/freight acknowledges the exact projected source and destination; its cosmetic road path is not simulation truth; craft activity remains at Scenery | flats/crates/crew dressing, doors/apron active, TypeScript-derived weeks remaining in UI after the structured projection is added |
| **Waiting** | amber/shape-coded hold marker; lights occupied but not shooting | company waiting cue or empty released site, depending on authority | blocker placard; cause/remedy in inspector; no take animation |
| **Shooting** | hot beacon/roof light/activity silhouette; title + `SHOOTING` | exact Director/cast presence, stage-door movement, active lighting | existing camera, slate, boom, practical lights and production-role choreography; decorative crew bounded and nonselectable |
| **Wrap / clearing** | shooting beacon off; clearing/freight cue | equipment/crew exits, set/apron clears | one restrained wrap punctuation; exact previous title if event proves it |

Current reservation truth always outranks historical theater. If the released Stage is acquired by
another picture in the same authoritative week, the new holder/title/company/state paints
immediately; the old wrap survives only as a one-shot receipt and never leaves clearing lights or
the former company on an occupied Stage. With no new holder, clearing is an event-sequence cue that
decays to neutral—it is not a persistent state reconstructed from event history.

## 13.2 Required current seam correction

Current core V14 correctly refuses `clearSceneryLoadIn` while scenery is in transit, and tick step
0.7 automatically clears a blocker that is already present when its derived trip becomes due.
However, `sceneryLoadInFor.calledWeek` is the Stage-acquisition week. The interactive Director call
can create the blocker only after that week's tick step has run, by which point the derived trip may
already be due. In that edge case the current manual `Clear` is legal until the next tick; when it is
not yet due the same command rejects. Meanwhile `nextProductionOperationsDecision` and the browser
Production Board publish `clearSceneryLoadIn` for both cases, so presentation cannot distinguish
waiting work from a genuine decision and Living Time can pause behind a rejecting action.

P05A must **extend the TypeScript read/decision boundary** so:

- a derivable load-in publishes structured origin/destination/distance/total/remaining;
- while it is not due, it publishes no player command and the journey's next action is authoritative
  week advance;
- if it is already due when the Director call creates the blocker, authority settles the same
  `sceneryArrived` transition immediately (or in the same authoritative action transaction), rather
  than exposing a manual acknowledgment or waiting for an otherwise unnecessary week;
- either automatic arrival path creates `ready`, which then publishes `scheduleShootingTake`; and
- only grandfathered load-ins retain the manual clear command.

This is a small authoritative settlement correction, not a Unity workaround or a new gameplay
mechanic. It makes “arrival is not a player choice” true at both timing boundaries and makes the
player surface agree with legality.
