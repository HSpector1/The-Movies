## 2. Executive decision

Package 15 should make Hollywood feel like one connected, finite, remembered market and then let
the 2040 finale interpret what actually happened. It should not become a corporate-empire grab bag.

The final package name is **Corporate Hollywood, Shared Market & Studio Legacy**. Its recommended
internal sequence is:

- **P15A — Shared Market:** symmetric genre/release pressure, decay, disclosed upcoming releases,
  and later a separately authorized comparative Power Ranking.
- **P15B — Corporate Fate:** only if approved, bounded warning, distress, recovery, later entrants,
  and dormancy under the same pre-terminal player/rival gates. P12 already permits rival failure while
  the player has no mandatory hard-bankruptcy game-over; exact rival closure and any separate player
  terminal ending remain distinct Owner decisions, not a symmetric default.
- **P15C — Studio Legacy:** an evidence-linked 2040 interpretation with multiple valid archetypes,
  setbacks, recoveries, rivals, films, people, technology, finance, awards, and market history.

**PRELIMINARY RECOMMENDATION:** acquisitions, mergers, labels/subsidiaries, co-productions, and
library/IP ownership transactions move to P16+ **Studio Empire & Ownership Transactions**. That
system needs valuation, rights, contract assumption, finance, and ownership-history law that would
overwhelm P15 and contaminate its first proof.

The bounded first checkpoint is **P15A.1 — One Symmetric Shared-Market Consequence**:

> one player release + one rival release in the same governed window and genre → one
> formula-versioned pressure/decay rule applied symmetrically → one visible consequence for each →
> typed causal reasons linked to source films and studios.

It includes no Power Ranking, screen-capacity simulation, distress, entrant, closure, acquisition,
co-production, or finale.

## 5. Original *The Movies* reconstruction

### 5.1 Comparative charts

**SOURCE VERIFIED:** The original shipped separate Studio, Star, and Movie Charts. The
[official manual](https://cdn.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/7900/manuals/manual_english.pdf?t=1447351040)
says they showed the player in relation to competitors and exposed contributing factors. The
[developer-reviewed Prima guide](https://archive.org/details/The_Movies_Prima_Official_eGuide)
documents the Studio Rating headline weights: Capital 24%, Movies 24%, Stars 24%, Lot Prestige 14%,
Awards 14%. This was a current comparative composite, not persistent Legacy and not Project: Studio
Standing.

### 5.2 Shared genre pressure

**SOURCE VERIFIED:** The
[developer-reviewed Prima guide](https://archive.org/details/The_Movies_Prima_Official_eGuide)
says public genre interest responded to how many films all studios were releasing in that genre.
Saturation could make a strong film underperform after rival output. World events also shifted genre
taste. The exact exposure window, saturation curve, recovery curve, and rival decision implementation
were not recovered.

### 5.3 Comparative research and talent movement

**SOURCE VERIFIED:** The
[developer-reviewed Prima guide](https://archive.org/details/The_Movies_Prima_Official_eGuide)
describes early research as a temporary advantage over competitors and says every studio received a
pack at its natural unlock date. The [official manual](https://cdn.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/7900/manuals/manual_english.pdf?t=1447351040)
and Prima evidence establish rival Stars entering the player's Stage School, unhappy or fired player
Stars joining other studios, and Stars/scripts being sold to rivals. These prove connected
competition, not a modern contract market or acquisition system.

### 5.4 Awards and studio arrival

**SOURCE VERIFIED:** The [official manual](https://cdn.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/7900/manuals/manual_english.pdf?t=1447351040)
and [developer-reviewed Prima guide](https://archive.org/details/The_Movies_Prima_Official_eGuide)
establish that Awards occurred every five years and compared studios, films, and people. Prima
documents nine named AI rivals in a ten-slot chart including the player. Several predated the 1920
start; later rivals entered within variable authored windows through approximately 1971. Exact entry
RNG and opening count remain unresolved.

### 5.5 Rise, fall, and corporate outcomes

**OPEN QUESTION:** no reliable inspected retail source establishes rival bankruptcy, closure,
disappearance, merger, ownership change, acquisition, or replacement after closure. A [GameSpot
pre-release preview](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/the-movies-preview/1100-6089840/) mentioning a studio
going bust is **SOURCE VERIFIED AS PRE-RELEASE REPORT ONLY** and cannot establish retail law.

**SOURCE VERIFIED AS PRE-RELEASE REPORT ONLY:** [GameSpot's E3 2002 First Look](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/e3-2002-first-look-the-movies/1100-2866856/)
said acquiring competitors was one possible growth path.

**REFUTED:** acquisition was a verified shipped mechanic. It appears in no inspected retail
[official manual](https://cdn.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/7900/manuals/manual_english.pdf?t=1447351040),
[Prima section](https://archive.org/details/The_Movies_Prima_Official_eGuide),
[retail walkthrough](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/the-movies-walkthrough/1100-6140049/), or
[expansion manual](https://shared.fastly.steamstatic.com/store_item_assets/steam/apps/7910/manuals/The_Movies_Stunts_Effects_Manual.pdf?t=1447351041).
Any successor acquisition belongs to P16+ and must never be labeled parity.

**REFUTED:** co-production, merger, library/IP ownership transfer, subsidiaries, and labels were
verified shipped mechanics.

### 5.6 Campaign ending and lifetime achievement

**SOURCE VERIFIED:** The
[developer-reviewed Prima guide](https://archive.org/details/The_Movies_Prima_Official_eGuide)
states that the original may continue after 2005, while its reward structure ends at the 2005
ceremony. Gold required all nine Achievement Awards; Platinum added prescribed ceremony-award counts
and unlocked a set plus alternate credits. This was a checklist/tally, not one blended score and not
a complete historical interpretation.

### 5.7 What the original does not authorize for P15

- no trustworthy release-overlap formula beyond genre saturation;
- no modern momentum Power Ranking;
- no exact closure/failure law;
- no acquisition, merger, co-production, or ownership-transfer parity;

## 10. Product fantasy

The player sees Hollywood breathe around the lot: two studios crowd the same audience, a rival's
release changes the opportunity without cheating, a once-dominant company struggles, becomes
dormant, or recovers under the same gates, new challengers enter, careers and technologies cross
decades, and the 2040 finale remembers both triumphs and scars.

The fantasy is not “conquer every studio.” It is:

> Build one fictional studio through 120 years of a shared industry, understand why the world moved,
> and discover what kind of institution your actual decisions created.

## 11. Binding design laws

1. **One market, one law.** Player and rivals consume the same formula/version and equivalent inputs.
2. **Consequences need sources.** Every pressure, rank movement, distress transition, and Legacy claim links to typed facts and stable IDs.
3. **Standing is not rank.** P08's three channels remain persistent reputation; Power Ranking is periodic momentum; History is permanent fact.
4. **No single Legacy score.** Several archetypes may coexist; none crowns a universal winner.
5. **Negative cash is not bankruptcy.** P11 cash and obligations inform distress only through approved staged law.
6. **No identity death.** Closure, dormancy, retirement, or future ownership change never deletes or re-mints studio, film, or person IDs.
7. **No retroactive fiction.** Migrated saves never receive invented rival releases, awards, market pressure, closures, or recoveries.
8. **No player-only penalty.** The same market consequence path applies to every eligible release.
9. **No hidden difficulty subsidy.** Rivals cannot receive secret cash, free talent, instant films, technology grants, or outcome multipliers.
10. **No fake public precision.** Formula version, window, major drivers, and uncertainty are visible; private rival strategy stays private.
11. **Events, not weekly biographies.** Persist material transitions; aggregate routine periods; page archives.
12. **No quadratic scans.** Weekly work indexes only active exposures, projects, contracts, and due events.
13. **Unity presents; TypeScript decides.** Ranking, market consequence, status, archetype, eligibility, and reason facts are server/core-authored.
14. **Corporate transactions wait.** Acquisition, merger, co-production, and IP/library ownership live in P16+.
15. **2040 is governed.** The finale trigger and post-finale mode are explicit, versioned, and Owner-approved.
16. **Studio transitions are all-owner transactions.** A later entrant, dormancy, re-entry, or closure request cannot change P12 operating state until P13, P10, P11, P12, and P14 each supply an idempotent initialization/settlement receipt and the complete participant manifest commits atomically. No package's partial receipt becomes authoritative alone.

## 12. State model

### 12.1 P15A shared-market lifecycle

```text
P12 scheduled/disclosed release fact
  → P15 exposure-eligible
  → release-batch-frozen
  → assessed + exposure-active
  → exposure-decaying
  → archived
```

P12 owns schedule, disclosure, delay, cancellation, and release facts. P15 derives eligibility and
future previews from them; it does not copy an industry calendar. A market assessment is produced at
the governed release boundary from active exposure indexes and one frozen same-week batch. It
references source films/studios, batch ID, formula version, window, inputs, outputs, and reasons. It
never rewrites an already frozen P07 result after that result is committed.

**Binding same-week law:** freeze all eligible releases for week `W` and the pre-batch active-exposure
snapshot. For each subject release, calculate against the same snapshot plus every other eligible
batch member, with explicit self-exclusion. Build one off-state `GameState` candidate containing every
P15 assessment, every P07 prospective result/input receipt and history reference, every exposure and
the batch commit receipt. Validate the complete candidate, then commit it once; if any subject's P07
preflight/application or any cross-root invariant fails, commit none of it. Duplicate delivery returns
the durable batch receipt and cannot repeat a result, assessment, exposure, event, or debit. Canonical
`(effectiveWeek, releaseId)` order controls candidate bytes only; it cannot give the first ID a weaker
or stronger market. Swapping player/rival ownership or IDs while preserving facts must swap the
normalized outputs exactly. If the then-accepted P07 boundary cannot participate in this all-or-none
candidate, P15A.1 stops at reconnaissance.

### 12.2 P15A.2 ranking lifecycle

```text
insufficient-history → eligible → snapshot-published → superseded-but-archived
```

Rank has cohort, cadence, effective week, trailing window, formula version, tie rule, current/prior
position, movement state, and one to three source-backed reasons.

**PRELIMINARY RECOMMENDATION — P15A.2 candidate definition, not approved scope:** publish quarterly
after one complete trailing 52-week window. Show three independent public 0–10 lanes: (1) film-outcome
momentum from at most the four strongest P07 frozen released-film outcomes in-window, (2) recognition
momentum from actual P08 honor outcomes in-window, and (3) delivery momentum from P12 conserved,
publicly disclosed scheduled-to-released facts in-window. The versioned definition maps exact source
facts into authored bands; it never reads Standing, cash/valuation, private slate, technology adoption,
talent popularity, or a client calculation. Candidate ranking points are the unweighted lane sum
(0–30). Equal totals share a dense rank; immutable `StudioId` may order equal-rank rows for stable
presentation but cannot break the tie. Each row exposes all three lane values and one to three typed
top reasons with source IDs. The Owner must approve/revise/reject the lanes, bands, four-film cap,
52-week window, unweighted sum, and dense-tie law before P15A.2; P15A.1 does not depend on that ruling.

### 12.3 P15B corporate fate lifecycle

```text
active → warning → distress → recovery → active
                    ↘ dormant → recovery
```

Only an Owner-approved policy may activate these states. A single negative-cash week cannot skip
directly to dormancy. The recommendation is the same warning/distress/recovery/dormancy predicates
and equivalent typed remedy capability families, prerequisites, conserved costs, timing, and effects
for player and rivals; only presentation and selection mechanism may differ. **PROJECT AUTHORITY
VERIFIED:** P12 permits rival failure while protecting the player campaign from mandatory hard-
bankruptcy game-over. A separately approved rival path may therefore add `dormant → closure-settlement
→ archived`, while the player graph remains recoverable dormancy unless the Owner separately authorizes
a player terminal ending. Settlement invariants remain strict for every applicable path: projects,
obligations, contracts, people, films, immutable identity, and public history resolve or persist explicitly.

Every future operating-state edge uses one participant manifest frozen at a source `GameState`
revision. For entry/re-entry, P13 records only the entry-week public-standard baseline, P10 registers
any starter people/contracts, P11 initializes conserved finance, P12 prepares registry/projects/
capacity/roster plus current-employer/exclusivity/interval facts, and P14 initializes only its market/
lifecycle facts. For dormancy/closure, P13 resolves every
active research/adoption order and P14 resolves every open case/proposal/promise/commitment through
typed dispositions; P10 settles contracts/person references, P11 settles finance, and P12 settles
projects/capacity/roster/employer/exclusivity/intervals. P12 commits the registry transition only
inside the same validated candidate. Duplicate requests return the existing manifest/receipt, and one
failed participant leaves every root unchanged.

Every variable-size participant owner must prove completeness, not merely return one opaque receipt
ID. A genuinely fixed-size result may use a fixed receipt only when its schema states the hard maximum,
affected count, exact subject set, and digest. Otherwise P13/P10/P11/P12/P14 supplies a request/source/
rules-bound root manifest, affected-set count/root digest, ordered chunk-chain digest, and immutable
typed chunks of at most 100 rows with each affected subject exactly once. P11 and P12 must meet this
same prerequisite for finance/obligations and registry/projects/capacity/roster/employer/exclusivity/
intervals respectively. Their accepted package ownership is unchanged; if either bounded participant
contract is absent, P15B stops.

### 12.4 P15C finale lifecycle

```text
not-due → 2040-eligible → source-snapshot-frozen → interpretation-built → presented → archive-browsable
```

Endless Mode, if approved, begins through a separate one-time transition after the frozen finale. It
does not alter the finale inputs.

## 16. Economy and resource consequences

P15A market pressure may affect a release only through a versioned, previewable authoritative
reception input applied before result commitment. It does not debit cash, synthesize tickets, or alter
P07 run arithmetic after release.

P15B consumes P11 truth:

- literal cash;
- scheduled receipts and unavoidable obligations;
- payroll and facility operating costs;
- active project commitments;
- contract guarantees and legal consequences;
- exact authoritative affordability.

Distress must have at least two legitimate recovery routes—such as reducing future obligations,
delaying/cancelling an uncommitted plan, completing a conserved release, or entering dormancy—before
it may demand attention. Each route belongs to a typed remedy capability family with the same eligibility
predicate, conserved cost, timing, and state effect for player and rival studios; P12 rival policy chooses
from that same legal set. Loans, bailouts, investors, forced sales, or acquisition are not implied.

**PRELIMINARY RECOMMENDATION:** apply the same warning, distress, recovery, and dormancy predicates
and equivalent remedy capabilities to player and rivals. The player may receive richer decision UX and
rivals may use deterministic P12 selection policy, but neither gets a secret pre-terminal rescue, waived
cost, faster effect, or harsher undisclosed guard. Terminal eligibility is intentionally not symmetric:
accepted P12 law permits rival failure while the player has no mandatory hard-bankruptcy game-over.
P15B must define and obtain Owner approval for exact rival closure/settlement; any player terminal
ending is a separate Owner gate, not a prerequisite for rival closure.

## 19. Long-horizon performance and storage

The campaign covers approximately 6,240 weeks. Required laws:

- active exposure updates are O(active exposures + due events); a same-week batch builds bounded
  genre/window aggregates once and subtracts the subject's own contribution, never comparing every
  film pair or activating one member early;
- complete same-week membership, assessment IDs, and P07 receipt refs persist once in an exact-count/
  root/ordered-chain-digest manifest with immutable chunks of at most 100; each assessment references
  that batch and one aggregate digest instead of copying all other release/exposure IDs, so storage is
  O(batch members + contributors), not O(batch²);
- studio/genre indexes are persisted or deterministically rebuilt once, not rescanned per view;
- monthly/quarterly/annual aggregates are bounded and separate from immutable material events;
- raw rejected rival candidates, weekly rank rows, routine zero-change weeks, and UI seen-state are discarded;
- P15-domain histories page with requested/applied size (default 25, hard maximum 100), snapshot/version
  metadata, `asOfP15DomainSequence`, and tuple `(effectiveWeek, phaseOrdinal, 'P15',
  p15DomainSequence, eventId)`; a merged view freezes, for every source, its revision, ordering kind/
  version, source-order high-watermark, and one bounded phase-lineage reference (`oldestVersion`,
  `newestVersion`, `lineageDigest`), then merges by `(effectiveWeek, phaseOrdinal, domainId,
  sourceOrderOrdinal, eventId)`; each row retains its exact phase-order version and no downstream
  package invents a legacy source order;
- no query hashes or projects the entire save repeatedly;
- no full-history bridge snapshot is appended to the living-lot bundle;
- finale construction uses bounded indexes and one frozen manifest, not repeated whole-save scans.

The **expected** 120-year fixture uses 16 studios, 20,000 films, 500 simultaneously public/active
release records, 250,000 material cross-package history rows, and a same-week batch of 32 releases.
The **hostile** fixture uses 64 studios, 100,000 films, 4,000 simultaneously public/active release
records, 1,000,000 material rows, and a same-week batch of 512 releases with dense genre overlap.
These are endurance inputs, not shipping-content promises. Exact byte/time budgets require measured
implementation reconnaissance; this report does not invent them.

The endurance gate must complete 6,240 weeks under normal and hostile fixtures, round-trip saves at
multiple eras, prove no duplicate IDs or cross-studio double ownership, and compare deterministic
terminal hashes without hashing on every UI view.

## 27. Golden journeys

1. **See upcoming overlap.** Open Shared Market before either release; both known films, studios,
   genre, window, and disclosure dates appear without private rival data.
2. **Inspect source film.** Open the rival release and Back; exact market filter, scroll, selected
   genre, and lot camera return.
3. **Freeze one release batch.** At the authoritative boundary, freeze the player and rival releases
   plus the same pre-batch exposure revision before calculating either assessment.
4. **Evaluate before activation.** Each film evaluates against pre-batch exposures plus the other
   eligible batch film with explicit self-exclusion; both assessments finish before either exposure
   becomes active.
5. **No player flag.** Swap player/rival ownership in a fixture while preserving inputs; normalized
   assessment outputs swap exactly.
6. **Pressure decays.** Advance to each decay boundary; direction and next recovery horizon are
   deterministic and do not emit weekly spam.
7. **Cancel announced rival film.** Cancellation before release removes future contribution through
   a typed event and never leaves phantom active pressure.
8. **Delay player film.** A legal schedule change recalculates preview only; no historical assessment
   is mutated before release.
9. **Same-week ID neutrality.** Simultaneous assessments use batch-frozen facts; stable immutable-ID
   order governs commits only. Swapping film IDs or player/rival ownership swaps normalized outputs
   exactly and survives save/load.
10. **Different genre.** Move one film outside the governed genre; it no longer contributes, with a
    clear reason rather than a hidden multiplier.
11. **Unknown rival project.** An undisclosed film may affect authoritative market state only at its
    legal reveal/release boundary; UI never leaks its title or private plan.
12. **Open explanation.** Every pressure value exposes formula label, time window, source films, and
    plain-language reasons; no raw private weights appear.
13. **Standing separation.** P08 Awareness/Prestige/Confidence remain unchanged in meaning and never
    appear as the market-pressure value.
14. **History separation.** P08 History records the release/event link but does not become the live
    pressure calculator.
15. **Frozen P07 result.** Reopening an old Film Result after later pressure decay does not recompute
    its release consequence.
16. **Old save migration.** Load a pre-P15 save; market history starts at the migration boundary and
    says earlier pressure was not recorded.
17. **No retroactive rival releases.** Migration creates no fabricated rival slate, rank, distress,
    or Legacy evidence.
18. **Save round trip.** Save during active exposure, reload, and continue; source IDs, decay dates,
    assessment bytes, and next event remain identical.
19. **Reconnect/stale projection.** Change authoritative state while Shared Market is open; stale
    data refreshes in place and no old preview commits.
20. **Observer neutrality.** Open/close/filter/page Shared Market repeatedly; save bytes and future
    assessments remain unchanged.
21. **Controller journey.** From lot pulse to Shared Market to source film and Back, controller focus
    is visible and restores exact context.
22. **Keyboard journey.** Navigate graph-equivalent table, reasons, glossary, paging, and Back with no
    hover-only fact.
23. **Narrow/text-scale journey.** At narrow width and 200% text, film identity, window, pressure,
    reasons, and source action remain readable.
24. **Reduced-motion journey.** Pressure and graph transitions update without motion; no camera move.
25. **Long history page.** Query a century fixture by cursor; page size is bounded and no full-save
    digest/projection repeats per page.
26. **6,240-week run.** Normal and hostile fixtures finish with no duplicate assessment/event IDs,
    no quadratic scan, no cross-studio double release, and deterministic terminal hashes.
27. **Ranking absent.** P15A.1 exposes no `#1`, rank arrow, Power Score, or hidden rank field.
28. **Corporate scope absent.** P15A.1 cannot create distress, entrants, closure, acquisition,
    co-production, or finale records.
29. **Phase-order evolution.** A scheduler catalogue adds one reserved phase after a mixed-domain
    same-week market/history page is issued; old P15 and upstream event order/cursor continuation is
    unchanged, while legacy unknown-phase rows remain explicitly noncausal.
