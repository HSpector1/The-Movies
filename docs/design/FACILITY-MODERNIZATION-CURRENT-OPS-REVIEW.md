Caution: the museum page documents the bundle, not a prohibition on purchase; the stronger "could not purchase, only rent" phrasing rests on a general-reference source, and two corroborating institutional pages returned errors, so treat the absolute prohibition as uncorroborated. OWNER GAMEPLAY RULE: the originator keeps own-use rights and a purchase benefit when it appoints a supplier. IMPLEMENTATION RECOMMENDATION: carry both as explicit agreement terms so the benefit survives supplier appointment (design §16a.2).

**Claim 3: conversion difficulty depended on the building, not only the equipment.** PARTIALLY VERIFIED. SOURCE FACT: sound conversion involved months of purpose-built structural work and large capital at named studios. Caution: the source does not state the comparative claim itself and gives no general durations or costs; a second source on retrofitting an existing silent stage could not be fetched, so the comparison between an older and a nearly compatible building remains a design assumption. **The corrected comparison, over one 52-week period.** The earlier version had no sequence behind its dates, charged the researchers for eleven weeks rather than the period, and omitted the sound Post capability its own prerequisite graph requires. Earliness is **eleven weeks, not twenty-six**. Starting a department does not fit E1's starting cash. A studio that will research exactly once pays a **114% premium**, not 149%.

**Starting a department is a different decision from running one.** With the laboratory, instruments and staff already paid for, the same technology costs $1,040,000 to develop against $1,100,000 to buy: developing is **$60,000 cheaper**, and with no laboratory to build it is operational at week 24 against week 52, twenty-eight weeks earlier. Charge the project its own payroll and it is 2.5% dearer. Across stages of one technology the inventor is ahead at every count and gains $75,000 per stage; make the department carry its own $1,732,000 of laboratory, instruments, salaries and standing charges on top of the research and installation figures, and the crossover moves out to twenty-four stages, which no estate approaches. So "no studio would ever wait" is withdrawn: waiting stays cash-dominant by $1,698,400 for a studio researching once, and developing wins at the margin for a studio that keeps a department busy.

**Concentrating researchers is not always right.** Six fixtures in [catalogue §9.4](./STUDIO-UPGRADE-AND-RESEARCH-CATALOGUE-01.md#94-research-allocation-when-concentrating-helps-and-when-splitting-helps) vary workload, deadline, useful staffing limit and equipment. With no cap, sequencing by earliest deadline is optimal and splitting one laboratory buys nothing while delaying the first result by eleven weeks. Cap two briefs at two seats each and splitting finishes both at week 22 where concentrating finishes the second at week 44, with two seats idle throughout. The interface should show the idle seats and both completion dates rather than recommend an arrangement.

**Renovation is not automatically cheaper.** Converting an owned stage is $1,600,000 cheaper and eight weeks sooner in E1, counting from the week the capability is in hand, and that gap is the same under either credit rule. For a studio still waiting to buy, the release gate binds before either stage does and only the cash separates the routes. Building still wins on three counts the cash comparison hides: the conversion takes a working stage out of service for twelve weeks and the new build takes nothing out of service; the new build adds capacity, which is a different good from capability; and a conversion that strips and replaces services is a rebuild at a conversion's price.

- **Cancellation differs by scope.** Recommend no voluntary abort after committing the first ordinary P09 retrofit. P13 already promises adoption cancellation; technology conversions must honor that with atomic physical/financial/technology dispositions. The ordinary recommendation cannot repeal it. *(Amended 2026-09-10: the Owner selected option B for technology installations, pay completed work and restoration, recover refundable unused commitments; aligning ordinary retrofits with option B is recommended.)*
