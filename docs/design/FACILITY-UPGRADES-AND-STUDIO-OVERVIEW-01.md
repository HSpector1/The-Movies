- Shared office capacity helps when ready work is refused for that resource. It does not provide available writers/actors, shorten each task, improve actor craft/Fit or guarantee film income. - A higher development standard can improve first-draft estimated strength, but consumes more original-writing time before writer/clamp effects. `assessFirstDraft` changes actual and perceived first-draft strength and caps it at 100: a hypothetical baseline of 98 gives 100 under both II and III, so the displayed script increment is zero even though the standard input rises by five. ## 8. P09 / P11 / P13 ownership reconciliation

P09 owns physical facility identity, placement, conversion job, occupied ground, work progress and physical completion. P13 owns availability, research/wait, adoption episode, capability eligibility/compatibility and technology history. P11 supplies cash/ledger/consequence semantics; it never advances renovations. Core owns time/phase order. P12 retains studio/rival identity and capacity authority.

**Collision found:** original P13 design §12.2 and Annex §4 give `AdoptionWorkOrder` required/accumulated work, reservations and P11 references; design §12.4/Annex §3.6 advance it. A P09 physical job must not acquire another independent clock, reservation, charge or capability grant for that same conversion.

**Recommended interface refinement, requiring disposition before activation:** retain `AdoptionWorkOrder` as the technology adoption episode/coordinator. Its physical work component references the one P09 job and receipts; physical progress/cost/reservation fields are read projections of that job. Nonphysical research/training remains P13 work, explicitly typed and separately costed only when it is actually distinct work. A typed participant links adoption ID/work-order ID, technology ID, studio ID, physical subject/provider IDs, P09 job ID, pinned eligibility/capability-rule versions and P11 consequence/commit references. Each component has exactly one work owner.

One physical commit preflights all participants and applies one candidate state; one P09 completion receipt identifies the changed physical/provider state. P13 validates eligibility and materializes its capability result exactly once as part of that shared atomic boundary. Its own phase consumes the receipt and records adoption/history; it does not perform the physical work again. A physical completion can leave the adoption waiting on a real nonphysical prerequisite, but cannot imply operational technology early. Freeze the due set and order public milestones/standard dispositions before technology completion eligibility; preserve joint-earliest cohorts and publish only after the whole scheduler transaction commits.

Technology being known or publicly available means eligible to consider installation, not freely installed in every building. Ordinary capacity renovations have no blanket P13 dependency. A Stage sound conversion requires the actual P13 law and downstream provider compatibility. Rival capacity can later participate through P12/P09 typed adapters under symmetric technology law; this does not require rival 3D lots or expand P12 now, and does not drop P13A's required rival consequence.

**Cancellation compatibility:** P13 Annex §3.4 already permits queued/active/paused adoption cancellation and immutable receipts/new IDs on restart. The ordinary no-abort recommendation does not override it. A technology conversion needs an authored P09 safe physical restoration/retention disposition, P11 cost/refund/Opex disposition and P13 readiness/work disposition in one transaction. Cancellation must never leave P13 cancelled while a physical job keeps progressing or capacity remains falsely granted. Preserve the existing right; if a chosen conversion cannot honor it, stop that conversion's activation and present the departure for explicit disposition. Do not silently disable Cancel or invent a refund. Retain P13 §12.5/Annex §3.7 all-owner hold/cancel/completed-before-transition receipts for later studio transitions, and safe handling of already committed work when eligibility or public standards change.
