# 1. Executive decision

Project: Studio should make every authoritative creative person a **persistent professional identity** whose present work, ability, specialty, contract, recorded credits, development, and consequences remain legible from the lot through retirement. Current detailed filmography is complete only where frozen participant/career records exist; legacy records must state their limits. It should not recreate *The Movies* as a queue of manually serviced human meters.

The professional grammar is:

> **See the person working → understand identity and current obligation locally → open one coherent career profile → make only material decisions → return to the exact lot context.**

The current simulation already owns more of that spine than the presentation exposes:

- stable `Talent` identity across writer, director, actor, and craft professions;
- distinct actual and perceived skills, with only perceived truth published;
- discipline-specific OVR, visible estimated-potential bands, work ethic, and per-discipline genre experience;
- commercial **Star Power**, deliberately separate from craft ability;
- contracted, freelancer, engaged, free-agent, and unavailable employment states;
- exact active assignments and authoritative conflicts;
- fixed-term contracts, renewal quotes, expiry, guaranteed obligations, and early release;
- per-film development and frozen career-event history;
- deterministic current-week world presence as presentation, not outcome law; and
- a reusable browser Talent Profile and Studio Roster.

P10 therefore does **not** authorize a new people simulation. It first turns existing truth into a professional human-information spine.

## Binding design laws

1. **A person is an identity, not a card instance.** Stable TypeScript ID joins world body, roster row, contract, assignment, film credit, career event, and future alumni record.
2. **Profession, ability, fame, rank, and history are different axes.** `Actor`, `OVR 78`, `Star Power 41`, an eventual market rank, and an acting award may coexist; none substitutes for another.
3. **“Star” is not a fifth profession.** The original used Star as the deep-simulation class for Actors and Directors. Project: Studio should use Actor/Director/Writer/Craft as professions and reserve Star for a future TypeScript-authored reputation/status state. Until then, show **Star Power**, not an unsupported `Star` badge.
4. **Perceived truth stays perceived.** Unity never receives hidden skills, exact ceilings, or actual genre experience. Potential remains an honest estimated band; no fake scouting-confidence system is invented.
5. **Career growth comes from authoritative work.** Current development occurs when credited work releases. There is no training action, XP bar, current-form score, aging tick, or autonomous practice system to present today.
6. **Availability is authored, not inferred from walking.** A person near a Stage is not assigned there. World presence renders current authoritative context and fails closed when joins are stale or ambiguous.
7. **Routine human needs are autonomous or absent.** Eating, drinking, rest, breaks, casual socializing, and morale recovery never become recurring player chores. A future human-condition system earns implementation only if it creates infrequent scheduling, workload, facility, or risk decisions.
8. **Human attention is exception-based and grouped.** Contract expiry, exact assignment conflict, retirement, and an authoritative serious condition may request attention; one alert per employee is rejected.
9. **Contract commitment is explicit.** Inspecting or selecting never renews, hires, fires, or releases. The consequence sheet uses current quote, cash, term, obligation, and legality; TypeScript revalidates on submit.
10. **History survives employment.** Expiry, free agency, future retirement, and removal from the active lot never erase name, credits, honors, or durable career identity.
11. **Decorative people stay decorative.** Ambient extras, grips, and stagehands do not acquire profiles, salaries, assignments, or historical claims unless TypeScript promotes them to stable records.
12. **No fabricated rankings.** The current `standingPct` is a percentile of perceived primary-discipline OVR inside the generated talent population. It may describe market-relative ability qualitatively; it is not a Hollywood rank, Power Ranking, Fame, or Star Power.
13. **Studio Standing is persistent reputation; Power Ranking is comparative momentum; Studio History is durable legacy.** Current three-channel Studio Standing remains authority. Power rankings wait for a real comparative market/rival model.

## One bounded next checkpoint

> Select a hired authoritative person on the lot → read identity, profession, current work, destination/availability, and one important status locally → open a large retained Profile → understand perceived ability, genre specialty, contract, assignment, and recorded career → return to the exact world context → open Roster → filter/sort without losing truth → Locate that exact person → see a grouped contract-attention state remain consistent → open a historical linked person without manufacturing missing history.

No Owner decision is required before P10A. Decisions about the future meaning of a `Star` status, mortality, relationship simulation, human condition, training, and comparative ranking remain deliberate later gates, not hidden P10A assumptions.

## 2.2 What the player physically did

The original player repeatedly:

1. watched named hopefuls queue at a physical Stage School;
2. hovered or opened bubbles to inspect traits and urgent condition;
3. dragged a hopeful into a role room to hire them;
4. dragged Actors and Directors onto Sets to practice genre skill and avoid boredom;
5. dragged stressed people to leisure, food, drink, or Rehab;
6. placed people near one another or at progressively stronger social venues to build relationships;
7. assigned Stars to trailers, entourage, makeovers, press events, scripts, and shoots;
8. monitored salary, mood, stress, boredom, addictions, image, relationships, rating, and chart position across separate bubbles;
9. returned errant people to production when human state interrupted work; and
10. eventually received retirement/career punctuation.

That interaction made the lot and its people memorable. It also required the player to perform the same low-level remedial gesture dozens of times. The modern successor should preserve **visible cause, named identity, physical work, and long consequences** while moving routine self-care and ordinary deployment into autonomy.

The opening tutorial and ordinary campaign are not interchangeable evidence. The tutorial supplied an initial script and staged a particular facility/hiring sequence; a player skipping it needed the Script Office route. Its teaching order proves a physical onboarding grammar, not a universal normal-campaign recruitment or staffing law.

## 2.3 What remains excellent

- A deliberately bounded set of people received more simulation depth than anonymous staff.
- Genre experience produced recognizable careers rather than one universal skill.
- Work, status, salary, relationships, and public recognition could pull in different directions.
- Staff shortages manifested as actual production constraints.
- People physically traveled, practiced, socialized, worked, interrupted production, and retired.
- The lot communicated human life before the player opened a spreadsheet.

## 2.4 What aged poorly

- Information was fragmented across bubbles and hidden formulae.
- Selecting the right repeated venue or bodily need became a memory/gesture test, not strategy.
- Manual idle practice was nearly always correct and therefore clerical.
- Salary, trailer, entourage, press, and image created overlapping upkeep meters.
- Pairwise relationship grinding scaled quadratically.
- A Star's current state could interrupt production without presenting a professional scheduling remedy.
- Ordinary hiring compared candidates poorly and had no robust roster planning surface.
- Deep career history disappeared behind a transient retirement presentation.

## 2.5 Historical unknowns and non-claims

- This report does not claim formal negative feud mechanics; positive pairwise affinity is secure, lower relationship labels are not enough to prove a full rivalry system.
- It does not claim a universal pre-retirement attribute decline curve.
- It does not claim death, permanent base-game injury, modern firing/severance law, or ordinary employment contracts.
- It does not use Prima's internally inconsistent trailer subcomponent percentages.
- It records, rather than averages away, the writer-experience speed-versus-quality source conflict.
- It does not import *Stunts & Effects* mechanics into the base game.

# 3. *Stunts & Effects* / Superstar findings

## 3.1 *Stunts & Effects*

The expansion added a distinct Stunt Performer pipeline: Stunt School, trainable Stunt Skill, Condition, specialist training facilities, stunt difficulty, doubles, likeness, failure, Hospital recovery, and stunt-related recognition. Stars and Extras gained Condition and Stunt Skill but developed more slowly than specialists. Training and performance consumed Condition; failure could stall production or send someone to Hospital. The player could force an impaired person back to work at a performance cost.

**Historical ruling:** the expansion proves that **risk, readiness, specialization, substitute availability, and recovery** can generate memorable human decisions. It also proves that a second manually serviced meter/training loop compounds the original's burden.

Project: Studio classification:

- specialist/stunt identity and role suitability: **CORE FUTURE VALUE**;
- condition, injury, substitute, safety, and schedule tradeoff: **PRODUCTION FOLLOW-UP / future authoritative system**;
- dedicated training facilities and era/genre stunt content: **LATER**;
- forcibly returning an impaired person to set, repetitive condition training, and Unity-authored injury: **REJECT**.

## 3.2 Superstar Edition as a later-package lens

The required [Mac Superstar Edition review](https://macinplay.de/spiele-tests/macintosh-games/the-movies-superstar-edition/) is secondary reviewer evidence for the integrated later package. It praises the lively people and filmmaking fantasy while describing Actors as “Star-gotchis” and criticizing repeated bar placement, feeding, stress/boredom upkeep, returning people to Set, and implausible staff scarcity. It also observes that ordinary staff were easier to manage than Stars/Stunt Performers.

The later edition therefore:

- **preserved** the original's strongest people fantasy;
- **preserved** physical recruitment, visible work, careers, and status;
- **exposed** the scaling failure of per-person bodily and social maintenance; and
- **did not solve** formula opacity or staff-market friction.

The successor law is not “remove human condition.” It is:

> **Routine welfare is autonomous. Only a repeated, severe, or strategically consequential condition becomes a player decision, and that decision operates at workload/schedule/facility/policy scale.**

## 4.1 Why not simply copy Football Manager?

Football Manager earns density because a football squad is managed primarily through documents. Project: Studio's lot is home. The correct translation preserves approximately one-third to two-fifths of the desktop for the selected world context, uses a portrait-led profile, limits the initial profile to current work plus the handful of creative/business axes that change a decision, and places the exhaustive filmography/history deeper. It does not import contract agents, tactical positions, injury tables, weekly press conversations, or dozens of attribute columns.

## 4.2 Power Rankings: exact ruling

The requested sports-franchise study produced a useful distinction and an equally useful negative finding.

The strongest exact published **movement** presentation remains the official [College Football 26 Top 25 hub](https://www.ea.com/games/ea-sports-college-football/college-football-26/college-football-top-25-hub) and its published polls: the [final regular-season poll](https://www.ea.com/games/ea-sports-college-football/college-football-26/news/college-football-26-top-25-ranking-final-regular-season) shows `Jump` and Biggest Movers, [Week 14](https://www.ea.com/games/ea-sports-college-football/college-football-26/news/college-football-26-top-25-ranking-week-14) shows `UNR` plus a Dropped Out list, and the [end-of-year poll](https://www.ea.com/games/ea-sports-college-football/college-football-26/news/college-football-26-top-25-end-of-the-year) shows `NC`. [College Football 27](https://www.ea.com/games/ea-sports-college-football/college-football-27/faq) is the latest released entry; CFB26 is used because those public pages expose the interaction most concretely. Together they separate:

- team OVR/offense/defense from poll rank;
- current rank from prior movement;
- results from reputation/context;
- `#6 → #3 ▲3` movement from the reasons the move occurred; and
- the current poll from durable season history.

The latest announced NBA franchise material changes the earlier negative picture. [NBA 2K27 MyNBA](https://nba.2k.com/2k27/features/mynba/), announced 18 August and still pre-release on the authority date, explicitly says daily free-agency recap screens provide league Power Rankings beside major signings and remaining high-value talent; it describes a separate final offseason summary for winners/losers. Its preceding scrollable depth chart also exposes positional scarcity as people leave the pool. That is strong evidence for presenting comparative rank with **relevant market context**, not evidence that 2K exposes causal reasons for individual movement. It does not document a formula, previous-rank arrow, per-row reason, category rank, or in-season cadence, so those details cannot be copied from it. The same page's binding promises, rival/friend demands, relationship-derived OVR buffs, and XP-heavy 100-year Legacy mode are deliberately rejected for P10; only its contextual recap and long-history seams are relevant.

The current playable NBA 2K26 [King of the Court](https://nba.2k.com/en-GB/2k26/king-of-the-court/) instead ranks an explicitly disclosed four-game-score sum. That is evidence for transparent leaderboard law, not a MyNBA momentum model.

Finally, the pre-release official [NHL 27 Connected Franchise](https://www.ea.com/games/nhl/nhl-27/features/nhl-27-connected-franchise) material and its [deep dive](https://www.ea.com/games/nhl/nhl-27/news/nhl-27-connected-franchise), inspected on 25 August 2026, document ratings, roster management, standings, game results, and history but do not document a Power Rankings interaction. This is scoped negative evidence from the inspected pages, not proof about every eventual shipped screen; recheck after release if a ranking campaign begins.

**P10 ruling:**

- **REJECT now:** `#1 Actor`, `Top 10 Directors`, `Rising Stars`, genre rankings, and studio rankings. The current population is not a complete rival-backed market, and no ranking authority exists.
- **ADOPT now:** keep OVR, Star Power, Potential, Studio Standing, awards, and history visibly separate.
- **ADAPT current `standingPct`:** label it only as an approximate **ability percentile among the current generated same-discipline talent population**. Prefer `Market ability: Top 10%` over `Industry rank #4`. It may remain candidate context; it is not a career leaderboard.
- **LATER:** a TypeScript-authored `TalentRankingSnapshot` may support Actors, Directors, Writers, Newcomers, and sufficiently populated genre categories. It must publish cohort, cadence, current rank, previous rank, movement, 1–3 authoritative reason facts, and snapshot date.
- **LATER after rivals:** a `StudioPowerRankingSnapshot` may compare commercial performance, critical performance, audience momentum, and awards/prestige. It may present `#6 → #3 ▲3`, but every movement needs published causal reasons.

The three product terms are binding:

| Term | Meaning | Time character | Current authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Studio Standing** | Persistent reputation state: Awareness, Prestige, Commercial Confidence. | Accumulates/changes through authoritative studio outcomes. | Existing TypeScript three-channel Standing. |
| **Power Ranking** | Comparative current form/momentum inside a defined peer population. | Periodic snapshot with previous rank and movement. | None; requires market/rivals and TypeScript. |
| **Studio History** | Durable legacy facts and records. | Permanent historical record. | Existing film/career/history seams, extended by Package 08. |

No UI layer may synthesize Power Rank from Standing, cash, OVR, Fame, or recent FilmResults.

# 8. Person profile doctrine

## 8.1 Layer 1 — world selection

The compact inspector answers only the question “who is this and what are they doing?”

Order:

1. portrait, name, profession/employment badge;
2. current task or authoritative absence reason;
3. production/screenplay assignment and destination where published;
4. availability and one material status (contract attention, conflict, stale target);
5. actions: **Focus**, **Follow** when world-present, **Profile**.

It does not show the full contract, all four discipline ratings, a genre grid, filmography, hidden potential, salary history, or a morale placeholder.

## 8.2 Layer 2 — retained person profile

The retained profile uses the current applicant-dossier visual language: prominent portrait, strong name/role hierarchy, one large OVR anchor, short driver labels, readable cards, and restrained period-aware materials. It retains 32–40% of the lot on a normal desktop and 60–68% for the profile. Opening it does not move the camera or clear selection.

Above the fold:

1. **Identity header:** portrait, name, credited career identity, home profession, employment/availability.
2. **Current work:** project/assignment, authoritative destination/presence, expected release/term boundary where available, exact blocker/conflict.
3. **Creative snapshot:** primary-discipline OVR/tier, top two genre specialties, estimated potential range, work ethic label.
4. **Commercial snapshot:** Star Power with plain-language definition; no rank.
5. **Contract snapshot:** status, term, salary basis, guarantee/obligation, expiry, legal renewal state.

Below the fold:

- all four discipline proficiencies with `Primary`, `Proven`, or `Capable but unproven` qualification;
- full perceived genre experience for the selected discipline;
- persona/temperament only where it changes or explains a modeled decision;
- career event timeline/filmography using frozen release facts;
- contract actions behind explicit consequence previews;
- old-save provenance notices when detailed history did not exist.

## 8.3 Layer 3 — career/history

Career history is a chronological fact surface, not generated biography prose. Detailed rows are available where `TalentCareerEvent`/frozen `FilmResult.participants` exist; legacy/M0A results may have only partial attribution and must show that provenance. Each supported row may show:

- film title/date and credited discipline;
- frozen before/after perceived OVR or Star Power changes where currently recorded;
- genre experience/development changes where recorded;
- film result link;
- awards/honors when Package 08 authority exists;
- studio tenure/contract milestones only when durable events exist.

No row says “breakout,” “decline,” “legend,” or “typecast” unless TypeScript publishes the classification and drivers. Presentation may summarize obvious facts (“highest recorded Star Power”) only when derivable from retained history and labeled as a record, not a gameplay status.

## 8.4 Narrow and controller layouts

At narrow width the profile becomes a full-width bottom sheet/page with a fixed identity header and one-column cards. The lot is restored on Back; it is not squeezed behind unreadable panes. Every row is focusable in reading order; tabs, if used, expose proper names and are not the sole holder of urgent state. Text scales without hiding contract consequence or action labels.

Founding applicants use a bounded, deterministic role pool and Gate world presentation. After founding, TypeScript exposes a rotating contract market and freelancer market from a finite generated Talent population; former employees enter free agency. The browser `HiringMarket` is the current deep surface. There is no authoritative Stage School, Script Office, Crew Facility, agent, recruitment department, applicant-arrival cadence, or facility-specific supply law after founding.

## 25.2 Forward interaction rule

Long-term recruitment should eventually have two coherent entrances over the same authority:

1. **Need-native entrance:** exact shortage/expiring contract/empty role → `Find Actor`, `Find Writer`, or `Find Craft` → prefiltered Hiring workspace.
2. **World-native entrance:** future authoritative recruitment facility/queue → select locally → understand available discipline and market state → open the same retained Hiring workspace.

The deep Hiring workspace earns filters, compare, contract/freelancer mode, profile, and exact offer consequence. It never becomes a duplicate market with different candidates from the roster route.

P10A:

- retains existing generic Hiring Market authority;
- routes a real shortage to that surface with a profession filter where the current read model supports it;
- does not invent physical post-founding applicants or facility ownership; and
- reuses Package 01 dossier language and Package 04 comparison hierarchy.

# 26. Talent supply

Current supply is finite world-generated Talent with deterministic 13-week rotating visible subsets for contracts/freelancers plus free agents. It is not renewable arrival generation, a rival-poaching market, an era-aware labor pool, or candidate expiry pressure.

P10 presentation must therefore:

- say `Current market` rather than `New arrivals this week` unless true;
- preserve stable identity when a person rotates out of the visible subset;
- distinguish `Unavailable` from `retired`, `employed by rival`, or `left industry`, none of which exists;
- never imply that ignoring a candidate destroys them;
- retain former employees as free agents under current law; and
- make scarcity exact: `No currently available Writers`, not `Hollywood has no Writers`.

Long-horizon risk: a 120-year campaign cannot rely forever on one finite, non-aging generated population. A future talent-market campaign must define generation/entry, era cohorts, retirement, rivals, re-entry, and history before rankings or demographic claims. P10A only exposes current truth.

# 27. Staff shortages

The useful warning is not “Not enough staff.” It is:

> **Night Harbor needs a Writer**<br>
> No available Writer can begin the draft.<br>
> Ramon Lee is assigned to *Copper Sky*.<br>
> **View available Writers**

or, where only aggregate capacity exists in a future system:

> **Stage 7 needs 4 Crew capacity**<br>
> 2 of 6 required are currently available.<br>
> Shooting cannot begin.<br>
> **Open Production staffing**

Every authoritative shortage exposes:

1. blocked subject/workflow;
2. exact profession/capacity missing;
3. current cause (none employed, all busy, contract expired, unavailable market, future facility/capacity);
4. consequence;
5. narrowest current remedy; and
6. Locate/Profile routes for conflicting named people where relevant.

Current code supports specific writer/roster assignment scarcity, not a general ordinary-staff department model. P10A uses only existing shortage reason codes/read models and routes to a prefiltered Hiring/Roster surface. It does not create Crew headcount law.

# 28. Staff overview / roster

The Roster is the studio-wide human-management surface; the lot remains home.

## Default desktop view

The default roster is a readable 7-column table/list, not a 30-column spreadsheet:

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| Person | 48–56 px portrait, name, profession/career identity. |
| OVR | Primary discipline + OVR/tier. |
| Specialty | Top perceived genre or `No clear specialty`; never hidden. |
| Current work | Project/task or `Available`. |
| Availability | Available, Working, Engaged, Free agent, Unavailable, or exact conflict. |
| Contract | Expiry/relationship and legal-window state; compensation detail opens Profile. |
| Attention | One highest-priority reason with text/icon, never color alone. |

The entire row opens Profile. Explicit row actions are **Locate**, **Profile**, and overflow for current legal contract actions. Row click does not hire, renew, release, assign, or move the camera.

## Filters and sorts

- employed roster / current freelancers / all known / future alumni;
- profession: Actor, Director, Writer, Craft;
- availability/current assignment;
- contract horizon/renewal open;
- specialty/genre;
- attention only;
- sort: name, profession, OVR in chosen discipline, Star Power, contract end, salary basis, availability;
- search by exact name.

The chosen discipline must be visible when sorting OVR. A row cannot show one OVR while sorting by another.

## Responsive view

At narrow width each person becomes a card with portrait/name, profession, OVR, specialty, current work, contract horizon, attention, Profile, and Locate. Filters move into a sheet; sort remains one visible control. The card never hides availability or the fact that a person is off-lot.

## Context retention

Roster preserves filter, sort, search, scroll anchor, selected row, and originating route. Profile opens over/within it and returns to the same row. `Locate` preserves roster context in Back history and follows Package 02 camera law.

# 29. Human attention

## Severity law

| Tier | Meaning | Examples where authoritative | Presentation | Time/camera |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **INFO** | Useful fact; no decision. | Person begins assigned work; film credit recorded; market subset rotates. | Local status/history; no alert. | No pause; no camera. |
| **ATTENTION** | Planning may be useful. | Proposed 52/26-week contract warning; person becomes unavailable; role depth is thin. | Grouped HUD/Calendar/Roster badge. | No pause; explicit Locate only when a valid world capability is published. |
| **DECISION** | Current legal material choice. | Renewal window open; release consequence; exact offer; future retirement response if modeled. | One grouped attention item → retained consequence surface. | Existing next-event law only; no camera hijack. |
| **BLOCKING** | Named workflow cannot proceed. | Required Writer/Director/Actor/Craft unavailable; ambiguous/stale assignment; authoritative person conflict. | Subject + cause + consequence + narrowest remedy. | Blocked workflow waits; other time continues unless core law says otherwise. |

## Grouping law

- Group contract items by decision window/cohort, then list named people.
- Group shortages by blocked project/department, not by every missing body.
- A person with multiple issues appears once with the highest tier and secondary facts inside.
- Resolving the underlying state removes the attention item wherever it appears.
- Opening another workspace never causes a surprise person modal.

No current stress/morale alert exists. Do not populate the system with hypothetical human problems.

# 36. Era safety — 1920 to 2040

The interaction grammar is era-stable; its presentation is not.

Stable across eras:

- identity, profession, assignment, availability, contract truth, perceived ability, specialty, career history, Locate/Profile/Back;
- explicit commitment and consequence;
- authoritative person/world joins; and
- durable alumni history.

Era-aware presentation seams:

- portrait/headshot treatment and media;
- contract document styling/terminology;
- recruitment venue and representation;
- workplace clothing/equipment;
- publicity/celebrity vocabulary;
- training/welfare facilities if later built; and
- free-agency/agent/labor-market presentation if historically modeled.

Do not hard-code a 1948 paper dossier, studio-contract assumption, wardrobe, casting practice, or celebrity-media form into schemas. Conversely, do not invent century-specific labor law in UI. TypeScript publishes the legal employment state; presentation selects era-aware assets/copy.
