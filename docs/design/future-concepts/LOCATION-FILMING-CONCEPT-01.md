> EXPLORATORY FUTURE PRODUCT RESEARCH
> OWNER INTEREST CONFIRMED — MECHANICS NOT YET APPROVED

> NOT AUTHORIZED FOR IMPLEMENTATION
> SUBJECT TO CURRENT OPS REVIEW AND FUTURE ACCEPTED-BASE REFRESH

**Concept code:** LOC
**Current-ops verdict:** genuinely new off-lot production extension; adjacent to, but not contained by, P05 physical production.
**Working question:** Can leaving the studio create a legible production choice—place and fit versus control and logistical exposure—without building a second lot or awarding a location “quality” bonus?

## 1. Player fantasy and journey

The player reads a script requirement that could be achieved on an existing Stage/Set or by taking the production out into the world. A compact production dossier makes the choice concrete: what scenes and look the plan serves; who and what must leave; access, daylight, sound, travel, support, and contingency facts; the expected window; and honest uncertainty.

The fantasy is not tourism. It is the producer-director judgment of deciding when a real place is worth surrendering control. The studio lot remains home. An away unit departs with exact people and equipment, encounters at most meaningful exceptions, returns, and leaves truthful schedule, financial, availability, continuity, and history consequences. The later film record can say where work was actually made; it cannot claim that location work was automatically better.

Journey:

1. A scene group exposes eligible physical plans: Stage, backlot, nearby practical, distant practical, or a mixed plan. Ineligible stage-only work does not offer false alternatives.
2. The player compares preflight dossiers with factual consequence bands and reasons.
3. Commitment reserves exact access, spaces, people, equipment, and time and charges only the applicable costs.
4. Ordinary work proceeds autonomously. The player is interrupted only for a forecasted decision, a material exception, or a requested override.
5. Completion, delay, fallback, pickup need, departure, and return become typed facts. Home-lot work continues with the resources that actually remain.

## 2. Existing Project: Studio plan

### Already present or substantially directed

- **SOURCE FACT:** the original manual treats Sets as owned, physical on-lot structures; unavailable, damaged, or occupied Sets can block filming, and cast/crew travel distance adds time/cost.
- **RESEARCH/DESIGN SOURCE:** P05 treats Stage/Set reservations and production holds as exact resource facts. Only its implemented subset is accepted runtime authority.
- **RESEARCH/DESIGN SOURCE:** P06 continues exact production state into post/release rather than granting a hidden quality outcome; only its implemented accepted subset governs runtime.
- **PROVISIONAL / UNSEALED DIRECTION:** P08 supplies sparse, truthful history; P09 allocates property, facilities, capacity, and legality; P10 keeps ordinary attendance/work autonomous; P11 allocates cash truth and exact consequences.
- **OWNER-APPROVED PRODUCT DIRECTION, NO IMPLEMENTATION:** P13 allocates era capabilities.
- **CURRENT CODE FACT:** the accepted model already has `FilmShape`, `Promise`, production participants, timing/forecast, budget, physical resources, typed studio events, and deterministic saves.

### Genuinely new

- A secured, temporal off-lot access path that can satisfy an eligible scene group’s physical requirement.
- Company-move, away-resource, external-condition, support-base, and return consequences.
- A location-plan dossier and provenance-bearing location history.

This concept does **not** rename existing Sets as locations. In the original game, “location” commonly means the on-lot Set selected by a script. Here, “off-lot location” means access to a place outside the owned studio-lot resource graph. The distinction must remain visible in language and data.

### Ownership boundaries

The producer/consumer allocations below use the authority tiers recorded in the synthesis §1. Present-tense “owns” is not acceptance of an unimplemented package, root, or schema.

- P05/P06 continue to own production state and holds.
- P09 continues to own lot property/facilities/capacity; temporary access is not land ownership.
- P10 owns people and autonomous work; this concept may make people unavailable but does not rewrite their identities.
- P11 owns affordability and exact charging; there is no location wallet.
- P13 owns which production capabilities exist in an era.
- P16 direction owns `StoryProperty`/Film Library identity, its relationship to an upstream origin-work ID, chain of title, rights, licensing, restoration/reissue, and authorized ownership transactions. It does not remint the upstream film/work identity. A location-access agreement is not a story right.

The label `LocationAccess` is conceptual shorthand, not an approved type, root, or schema decision.

## 3. Real-world practice findings

Real location management is a feasibility and coordination discipline, not a scenery picker. ScreenSkills’ location-manager checklist covers breakdown, research, scouting, recces, permissions, access, restrictions, facilities, parking, technical needs, neighbors, risk, and reinstatement. FilmLA’s planning material similarly makes jurisdiction, community, street, parking, notification, and support constraints visible. These justify a dossier and mitigation model, not a universal permit checklist: practice changes by country, period, scale, and production.

David Kronke’s *DGA Quarterly* account of *Breaking Bad* distinguishes the creative value of Albuquerque locations from the burden of moving a full production package, protecting daylight, placing equipment, scouting workable alternatives, and sometimes favoring stages for logistical control. It supports the core control-versus-place tension, not a reusable cost or delay formula.

Studio technology has repeatedly changed this boundary. The Library of Congress describes Edison’s Black Maria as a daylight-dependent studio whose roof opened and building rotated toward the sun. Its HABS record describes a 1926 Culver stage relying on artificial lighting and thick soundproof walls in the transition to sound. “Stage versus location” therefore needs era capabilities, not a permanent modern rule set.

Operational abstractions that are both plausible and playable:

| Plan | Strength | Exposure |
|---|---|---|
| Stage | Control, repeatability, known support | Build/prep time, occupancy, artificial capability, imperfect natural fit |
| Backlot | Exterior control, support proximity, reuse | Lot/Set capacity, weather/noise, construction and fit |
| Nearby practical | Architecture and place with limited travel | Access window, sound, parking/base, neighbors, company move |
| Distant practical | Distinctive geography or architecture | Travel/lodging/transport, away resources, daylight/weather, pickup cost |
| Mixed | Selective place plus controlled coverage | Both reservations, moves, continuity and pickup coordination |

No row is intrinsically superior. The useful design question is whether its exact demands fit this production, era, cast, schedule, resources, and risk tolerance.

## 4. Shipped comparator findings

The original *The Movies* is the strongest comparator and the clearest boundary: scripts name physical on-lot Sets; cast and crew travel across the lot; ownership, occupancy, repair, and distance matter. Off-lot production is not part of that loop. This supports physicality but does not support silently stretching a Set into a remote region.

Anno 1800 expeditions demonstrate a management-game abstraction in which the player prepares a bounded party and supplies, those resources become unavailable to the home session, events occur during travel, and survivors/resources return. RimWorld caravans similarly make exact people, inventory, route, travel time, and temporary absence matter. Neither comparator is a film-production model; their relevance is the bounded-excursion structure and a home simulation that continues.

Comparator lessons retained:

- prepare a finite party and support package;
- show travel/window/known risks before commitment;
- make removed resources truly unavailable;
- resolve a small number of consequential exceptions rather than repeated chores;
- return resources and record consequences;
- keep the away layer bounded.

Rejected translation: a second freely navigable production map with continuous micromanagement. It duplicates the lot fantasy, multiplies pathing/presentation scope, and is unsupported by the first player-value question.

## 5. Recommended design model

### Physical-requirement law

For a location-eligible scene group, a secured temporal off-lot plan may satisfy the declared physical requirement instead of an eligible Stage/Set plan. Stage-only work remains stage-only. A mixed plan declares and reserves both footprints. This is an explicit extension point for future authority review, not an instruction to modify P05.

### Pre-commit dossier

The default summary leads with the few deltas that change the Stage-versus-location decision; supporting logistics remain one action away. It can expose:

- scene group and visual/physical requirement served;
- plan mode and access window, including prep, shoot, strike, and likely pickup exposure;
- exact cast, crew, equipment, Stage/Set, and support-resource effects;
- travel/overnight assumptions and company moves;
- base, parking, power, accommodation, sound, daylight, weather, and local-access facts where relevant;
- known restrictions, uncertainty bands, mitigation/cover choices, fallback, and the consequence of abandoning the plan;
- incremental access, transport, lodging, and logistics costs, with payroll counted once through existing authority.

Unknowns stay labeled unknown. A range must say why it is a range. The interface must not manufacture precise weather or delay certainty.

### Execution and exceptions

Ordinary travel, setup, filming, strike, and return are autonomous. At commitment the player may pre-authorize a disclosed cover/fallback within exact bounds. A forecast that remains inside that policy resolves without another prompt. The system interrupts only when new material information exceeds the policy and the player has a real choice: spend a disclosed resource, use another eligible fallback, accept delay, change the plan, or escalate. The packet shows the changed fact and actual alternatives. Delays extend actual participant/equipment absence and reservations. They do not create a generic quality loss.

### Commit, change, and release integrity

A preflight range reserves no cash. At commitment, the exact current price and consequences are redisplayed and revalidated. One stable plan-operation identity binds a deduplicated union of exact resource/time keys. The owning transition either rejects a conflict with no state change, or atomically debits each owning cost once, establishes each reservation once, and emits one receipt; retry returns that prior result rather than charging or reserving twice.

A delay amends the same owned reservations. Cancellation or fallback releases only unused future intervals owned by that plan and never refunds consumed payroll, access, travel, work, or other cost without an owning refund rule. A pickup is a new operation with new validation. Later exposure is labeled as an included immediate charge, an already committed obligation, or an optional future spend; “contingency” is never a shadow wallet.

### Continuity and pickup

A pickup uses recorded facts. It may require reacquiring the same place/access class, people, equipment, and viable window, or choosing a disclosed Stage/Set match plan. “Continuity risk” must name the mismatched requirement; it is not a hidden score.

### History and presentation

Record actual plan commitment, departure, material exception/decision, work completion or fallback, return, and credited location fact. Hollywood Wire may render those typed facts as production news; it may not invent weather, conflict, prestige, or success.

## 6. Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Advantage | Why it is not the lead recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Cosmetic location tag | Cheap, easy to understand | No consequential choice; falsely implies off-lot production without resources/access |
| Flat “authenticity” bonus | Immediate feedback | Becomes a dominant quality rule and contradicts source-first, no-magic-score law |
| Menu-only random expedition | Bounded scope | Risks detached event gambling unless tied to exact production facts and preflight choices |
| Full remote map / second lot | Strong spectacle and visitability | Very high scope; dilutes home-lot identity; premature before the decision loop proves value |
| Location profession simulation first | Realistic specialization | Invents staffing/career authority before P10/P14 and gates a basic production choice |
| Treat access as owned property | Reuses property language | Misstates temporary access and collides with P09; invites needless real-estate simulation |

## 7. Meaningful choices and failures

Meaningful choices are plan-specific: controlled Stage coverage versus a practical place; nearby versus distant access; full move versus a mixed plan; daylight window versus cover; contingency spend versus accepting delay; return for a pickup versus a Stage match.

Legitimate failures include:

- access never secured, so the plan cannot be committed;
- an exact cast/equipment/window conflict is exposed at preflight;
- a disclosed condition occurs and consumes contingency or time;
- delayed return blocks another exact obligation;
- the player chooses fallback and records which work changed;
- pickup access cannot be reacquired under current facts.

Failure must remain recoverable where facts allow: reschedule, revise requirements, substitute an eligible plan, secure additional resources, or accept a changed schedule/cost. The concept must not turn every cloudy day into an interruption, silently erase work, or label a film bad.

## 8. Anti-tedium and accessibility

- One dossier at the decision level, not permits and transport clicks per scene.
- Consume the existing shared attention-aggregation authority: one causal event produces one cross-feature decision packet and at most one blocking prompt.
- Persistent plan and contingency choices; no daily reconfirmation.
- Aggregate routine progress and low-severity condition notices.
- Interrupt only when a choice is actionable and materially changes exact consequences.
- Text and icons convey every state; color is redundant, not sole meaning.
- Ranges include plain-language reasons. Dates/times have explicit units and calendar context.
- Do not require camera navigation, rapid response, drag-and-drop, or audio cues.
- Provide pause/inspect, consistent focus order, scalable text, controller/keyboard paths, reduced motion, and configurable noncritical notifications.
- A bounded away view, if ever approved, is optional presentation; every decision remains available in the dossier/timeline.

## 9. Authority and data boundaries

- No accepted schema change is proposed.
- Access does not confer P09 property ownership, P16 rights, permanent facility capacity, or a story identity.
- Costs and affordability flow through P11’s single cash truth. Location plans cannot reserve an undisclosed second balance.
- A plan preview does not reserve cash. One authoritative commit/revise/cancel operation owns exact reservations, charges/releases, idempotence, and receipt; partial commit is invalid.
- People/equipment are exact production resources. The same unit cannot work home and away simultaneously.
- Current legal commands and final validation govern commitment, delay response, fallback, and cancellation.
- History records only actual events and explicit authored metadata; no backfill.
- Hidden talent, preferences, or demographic traits never affect the player-facing location recommendation.
- Any condition that can affect time, cash, resources, goals, or outcomes must be inside deterministic run identity and factual history for a comparable challenge. Otherwise the run is non-comparable from the outset; no consequential randomness may be treated as merely “external.”
- Any file/import/network/location-library design is deferred and would need separate security/privacy review.

## 10. Prototype, fuller vision, and deferred scope

### Smallest decision prototype

One production has one eligible coastal exterior scene group. The player compares an existing Stage/Set plan with a nearby outdoor plan. The dossier shows fit/control, schedule window, one cast-availability consequence, one production party, one equipment package, incremental cost range and reason, one forecasted condition, one preparation/cover choice, and fallback. Commitment produces departure, one possible material exception, return, and truthful history. The lot continues without the away resources.

The proof question is whether the player can anticipate and own the tradeoff, then understand the consequence without busywork. It is not whether the location looks spectacular.

### Fuller vision if proven

Era-aware access catalogs and availability windows; Stage/backlot/near/distant/mixed plans; multiple company moves; specific equipment/support/accommodation/base needs; continuity and pickup planning; better forecast knowledge as capabilities improve; a bounded optional visual away view.

### Explicitly deferred

Navigable region, second buildable lot, open world, global location browser, real jurisdictions and incentives, exhaustive permits/insurance/safety simulation, a new location-department profession tree, bespoke vehicle simulation, detailed weather model, formulas, balance values, quality/prestige bonuses, online sharing, and AMM/shot-level control.

## 11. Owner decisions

1. Is the essential fantasy satisfied by a dossier/timeline and truthful away absence, or is a bounded visual visit required?
2. Should the first proof include only Stage versus nearby practical, or also a mixed plan?
3. Is one material exception enough to test recovery without creating event fatigue?
4. May a future plan expose ranges for uncertain cost/time, provided the causes are explicit?
5. Is `LocationAccess` acceptable as temporary research vocabulary while final ownership/type naming stays open?
6. Does this concept continue, hold, or stop after Current Ops refresh?

Approval answers discovery questions only. It does not approve mechanics, schema, art, content volume, implementation, or schedule.

## 12. Proof plan and falsification

Test a paper/clickable decision prototype with production-state fixtures only after separate authorization. Compare attention and understanding with the current Stage/Set/manual flow; measure comprehension before preference:

- Can players identify which exact resources leave and what home-lot work remains possible?
- Can they explain why each plan’s cost/time range differs without seeing a score?
- Do they notice the fallback and understand a delay’s downstream availability effect?
- Does the exception feel like ownership of a prior choice rather than a random punishment?
- Do they still regard the studio lot as home?
- Across repeated/no-event/duplicate-event cases, record setup time, repeated inputs, blocking prompts, dismissals, backtracking, missed material exceptions, and retained understanding.

Falsify or redesign if most players pick the practical solely because it appears “better,” cannot predict resource absence, experience the dossier as paperwork, need a quality number to decide, or want to micromanage/ignore every away step with no useful middle. Evidence of spectacle alone does not validate the management loop.

No production estimate, implementation spike, schema migration, art prototype, or game launch is authorized by this proof plan.
