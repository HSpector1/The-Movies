**Concept code:** CRE
**Current-ops verdict:** creative identity is substantially covered by existing direction; enforceable production constraints are an additive, underspecified decision layer.
**Working question:** Can a player deliberately accept a production constraint that changes how a film must be made, then see a truthful body-of-work identity emerge—without adding taste points, rewarding “bravery,” or duplicating Shape, Promise, Fit, persona, careers, or StoryProperty?

## 1. Player fantasy and journey

The player decides not merely what a film is about, but what discipline will govern making it. A one-location production, dialogue-light approach, long-take plan, natural-light dependency, tightly limited cast, or fixed production window can open some possibilities by closing others. Before commitment, the player sees exact requirements, conflicts, redundancies, and recovery choices. The constraint is meaningful because production facts enforce it—not because a badge grants quality.

Across credited work, the studio can later describe an observed creative pattern: a director has repeatedly made intimate, low-cast films; a writer’s credited films often carry kinetic promises; a studio has alternated large controlled productions with place-led work. This is a lens on recorded choices, not a permanent essence or hidden trait. A person may be inconsistent, experimental, collaborative, or too lightly sampled to characterize.

Journey:

1. Shape, Promise, roles/cast, resources, schedule, and era expose a small set of relevant constraint options.
2. A constraint preflight states what is substantive, what it requires, how it overlaps with existing choices, and what breach/revision would mean.
3. The player commits, revises, or declines. Ordinary work remains autonomous.
4. Production records compliance, explicit revision, or unresolved breach as facts; it does not secretly grade taste.
5. After enough credited observations, an inspectable identity view describes tendencies, sample, time window, collaborators, and exceptions.

## 3. Real-world practice findings

The retained sources show that deliberate restrictions can shape process; they do not establish that restrictions make work good, cheap, original, or ethical.

The Danish Film Institute’s Dogme history documents an explicit rule set covering location, sound, props, camera, light, genre, period, and director credit. Later “confessions” and participant reflection expose ambiguity, breach, improvisation, and practical compromise. A DFI production account specifically describes the difficulty of location-sound rules. The useful lesson is an auditable commitment with real production consequences and an honest revision path—not a purity meter or Dogme-branded content copy.

Sundance’s account of *Tangerine* links iPhone capture to limited money, immediacy, mobility, and less intimidating street work with first-time actors. The BFI’s *The Feast* interview says a real house affected script choices and visual metaphors while a dialogue-light design increased reliance on performance. AFI’s *Son of Saul* cinematography interview describes long takes as demanding concentrated performance, background coordination, variable-light handling, and elaborate rehearsal. AFI’s catalog for *The Thing* records severe location/logistics demands and the separation of effects work to preserve adjustment/reshoot flexibility.

Together these cases support non-linear consequences:

- a restriction can reduce one demand while increasing another;
- place, technology, cast, rehearsal, support, and schedule interact;
- execution depends on relevant capability and planning;
- constraint and outcome should be recorded separately;
- no case establishes a general quality or prestige bonus.

They are notable-film case studies and institutional interviews, subject to survivorship and retrospective storytelling. They justify candidate tradeoff categories, not balance formulas.

## 4. Shipped comparator findings

Game Dev Tycoon’s shipped development sliders let players choose relative focus within fixed stages while staff capacity and features force tradeoffs. Official developer explanations clarify that this is about allocating finite work, not simply raising every category. Its configurable, stackable non-interrupting notifications also offer an anti-tedium pattern.

The useful translation is not to copy sliders or hidden recipes. It is to make creative intention collide with finite capability in a legible way. Project: Studio already has richer semantic inputs—Shape, Promise, roles, Fit, resources, schedules, and eras—so another abstract percentage panel would duplicate rather than deepen the design.

One-location film lists show diverse results inside the same broad limitation. That is evidence against treating “one location” as one genre, personality, or outcome. Constraint vocabulary must describe a production fact; creative identity must emerge from the surrounding pattern, not the label alone.

Comparator lessons retained:

- constraints should expose tradeoffs, not secretly encode an optimal recipe;
- finite capability makes focus meaningful;
- overlapping choices require clear accounting;
- low-priority feedback should stack rather than interrupt;
- a label alone does not identify the finished work.

## 5. Recommended design model

### Constraint contract

A candidate constraint is offered only when the current era and production can represent it honestly. Its preflight contains:

- plain-language intent and whether the rule is substantive or descriptive;
- exact covered production/scene scope;
- requirement predicates using existing facts: people/cast, place/Set/Stage, equipment/capability, schedule/window, budget/resource, rehearsal/preparation, or production method;
- known conflicts, overlap, redundancy, and concrete cases where the choice adds no new production requirement;
- fulfillment evidence and when it is evaluated;
- allowed revision, breach, fallback, and exit consequences;
- history/credit wording if completed or revised.

A substantive constraint changes eligibility or resource/schedule demands. A descriptive pledge can be recorded only as role-play/intention and must not claim enforcement, rewards, or identity evidence. It is omitted from the first proof. The two need visibly different treatment if descriptive pledges are ever retained.

### Consequence model

Constraints do not carry intrinsic positive or negative value. Effects arise only from actual interactions. A small cast may reduce some scheduling conflicts but increase dependence on specific performers. Long takes may reduce some coverage patterns while increasing rehearsal, coordination, and failure exposure. A fixed exterior window may offer fit but reduce schedule control. These are examples to test, not authorized formulas.

Stacking constraints receives no rule-count multiplier. If two constraints demand the same fact, the cost/benefit is counted once through existing authority. If they conflict, preflight blocks commitment or requires an explicit revision. If an exploit emerges—adding cosmetic rules for identity/reward—the design has failed.

A constraint is an input/predicate of the owning production plan; it never owns cash or reservations. Commit, revision, or exit uses one idempotent legal plan operation that revalidates current facts, preserves consumed time/work/cost, releases only unused future reservations owned by the replaced plan, and applies authoritative net-new consequences once. A factual revision receipt records what changed. Preflight alone reserves or charges nothing.

### Observed filmmaker identity

An identity statement is a query over credited, visible history, not stored truth about the person. It discloses:

- the credited role being discussed;
- the observations and time window used;
- recurring Shape/Promise/constraint/scale or production-choice facts;
- sample sufficiency and exceptions;
- meaningful collaborator/context caveats.

Use language such as “often,” “recently,” “across the disclosed credited sample,” or “no stable pattern yet.” Never use hidden actual skill, ceilings, demographic inference, or moral language. Different roles may yield different patterns. An older body of work remains history; a recent tendency need not overwrite it. Only fulfilled, observed production facts—not declared labels—feed the interpretation. There is no identity progress bar, stability score, unlock, or reward.

### Proposals and preferences

Before P14 authority, the player may choose or decline constraints directly. A later filmmaker could propose one only when P14 explicitly establishes that preference/proposal and any promise, trust, relationship, or lifecycle consequences. Person/profile/contract and credited-career truth remain with P10; employer/interval mutation remains with P12 direction. CRE cannot infer “they demand long takes” from successful films or silently manufacture a promise.

### Contrasting film pair

| Proposed film | Actual decision pattern | What the contrast proves / requires |
|---|---|---|
| **The Last Table** | An intimate chamber drama uses a small exact cast, one controlled on-lot Set, a concentrated schedule, and an intimacy-forward Promise. A substantive contained-place constraint reduces location changes but increases dependence on the chosen cast, Set availability, performance Fit, and recovery if either becomes unavailable. | Mostly reuses P03/P04/P05/P10 facts. The pair differs in people, physical resources, scheduling surface, recovery exposure, and dependency—not just in labels. Neither pattern receives an intrinsic outcome bonus.

| Alternative | Advantage | Why it is not the lead recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Constraint grants quality/prestige | Strong immediate incentive | Turns style into an objective hierarchy; invites stacking and contradicts no-magic-score law |
| Creative courage meter | Clear identity fantasy | Moralizes risk, penalizes conventional craft, and duplicates persona/career systems |
| Many free-form toggles | Expressive | Hard to explain, balance, test, localize, and prove; cosmetic-rule exploits proliferate |
| Hidden auteur archetype | Strong character flavor | Stereotypes people, uses unobservable authority, and makes identity self-fulfilling |
| Identity chosen at creation | Immediate clarity | Replaces earned credited history with a permanent class |
| Copy Game Dev Tycoon sliders | Familiar | Duplicates Shape/Promise and encourages recipe solving |
| Require AMM/shot editor | Detailed authorship | Makes a separate large investment mandatory and shifts the abstraction level |
| Make constraints P14 promises now | Adds interpersonal stakes | Pre-empts explicit preference/promise/career ownership not yet accepted |

## 7. Meaningful choices and failures

Meaningful choices include accepting a rule that fits one team but not another; rescheduling to meet a window; selecting capability and collaborators that can execute the plan; refusing an attractive constraint because it duplicates current limitations; revising intention when the production facts change; and deciding whether the body of work should continue or break a recent pattern.

Legitimate failures:

- preflight reveals infeasibility and blocks commitment;
- later loss of an exact capability/person/window creates a disclosed breach risk;
- the player revises or abandons the constraint and absorbs the real reschedule/resource/history consequence;
- completed work lacks enough evidence to claim compliance;
- a filmmaker has too few credited observations for an identity statement;
- a history shows conflicting tendencies, so the system describes plurality rather than forcing a class.

There is no “bad filmmaker” outcome from violating a stylistic rule. A breach is a factual mismatch between commitment and execution. If a public/professional promise later exists under P14, its relational consequence belongs there.

## 8. Anti-tedium and accessibility

- Offer a small contextual set; do not make players audit a giant checklist at every greenlight.
- Consume the existing shared attention-aggregation authority: one causal event produces one cross-feature decision packet and at most one blocking prompt.
- Preflight combines requirements, overlap, and conflicts in one view.
- Persistent rules do not demand weekly confirmation.
- Routine compliance checks are automatic; interrupt only for a material recoverable breach.
- Stack low-severity production updates and allow notification-category control.
- Every visual tendency has a text explanation, source observations, and sample window.
- Never rely on color, animation, or an unlabeled radar chart alone.
- Support scalable text, clear focus order, keyboard/controller operation, reduced motion, and pause/inspect.
- Descriptive identity can be disabled or minimized without hiding operational facts.
- Avoid dense film-theory vocabulary; use concrete production language and optional detail.

## 9. Authority and data boundaries

- No accepted schema, root, type, or formula is proposed.
- Shape/Promise remain P03-owned inputs; Fit remains P04-owned; production remains P05/P06-owned; history remains P08-owned; people/work/persona remain P10-owned; cash truth remains P11-owned; era capability remains P13-owned.
- P10 retains person/profile/contract and credited-career truth; P12 direction retains employer/interval mutation; P14 direction may establish proposals, explicit preferences, promise/trust, relationships, and lifecycle facts. Each consequence posts through its owning domain.
- P16 owns Film Library/`StoryProperty`; P17 owns continuation/franchise/Story DNA. Similar creative tendencies never establish shared property identity.
- AMM remains optional and separate.
- Only fulfilled, observed, visible credited production facts feed identity. Declarations alone do not. No hidden skill/ceiling, protected characteristic, inferred psychology, uncredited rumor, or fabricated history.
- Compliance is evaluated through the same exact production facts and legal commands. It cannot mint resources, bypass availability, or apply a hidden outcome multiplier.
- Hollywood Wire may phrase recorded commitment/revision/completion facts. It cannot invent a movement, controversy, preference, or acclaim.

## 10. Prototype, fuller vision, and deferred scope

### Smallest decision prototype

**CRE-A: constraint choice.** For one prepared production fixture, offer a few legible choices, including “no added constraint.” Select one substantive constraint with one real benefit/exposure pair and a concrete enforcement test—for example, a limited-cast plan whose scheduling surface and performer dependence can be shown from existing facts. Preflight shows requirements, overlap, conflict, fallback, and no quality number. A simulated fixture records completion or explicit revision. Descriptive pledges are absent.

**CRE-B: identity comprehension.** Separately, use prepared credited histories to test a read-only tendency view, including mixed and insufficient-evidence cases. This prevents declaration behavior in CRE-A from manufacturing the evidence being evaluated in CRE-B. Neither proof has an identity meter or reward.

The proof question is whether players can explain why they chose the discipline and how it changed the production plan—not whether they chase a reward.

### Fuller vision if proven

An era-aware curated constraint vocabulary; combination/conflict rules; filmmaker proposals after P14; history-aware tendency views by credited role; studio-level bodies of work; recoverable revision and truthful public/professional consequences through owning systems.

### Explicitly deferred

Balance values, quality/prestige/bravery points, permanent archetypes, arbitrary custom rules/scripts, dozens of traits, psychological inference, procedural critic consensus, demographic identity, moral/political scoring, P14 preference/promise effects, P16/P17 IP inference, movement-generation system, AMM, shot editor, camera grammar, user-authored content, and online sharing.

## 11. Owner decisions

1. Should the concept contain only mechanically substantive constraints, or also clearly labeled descriptive pledges?
2. Which one production fact is safe enough for the first constraint proof without adding new simulation authority?
3. Should revision always be allowed with factual consequences, or can a constraint become irrevocable after a disclosed production gate?
4. Is a longitudinal “observed tendency” view desirable if it frequently says there is no stable pattern yet?
5. Should studio identity and person-by-role identity both be explored, or should the first proof choose one?
6. Confirm that constraints, rule count, novelty, and austerity carry no intrinsic reward.
7. Does CRE continue, hold, or stop after Current Ops refresh?

Approval answers discovery questions only. It does not approve mechanics, vocabulary, formulas, identity labels, schema, implementation, or schedule.

## 12. Proof plan and falsification

Test with static production fixtures and paper/clickable preflight after separate authorization. Compare attention and understanding with the current unconstrained/manual flow:

- Can players distinguish Shape/Promise from the production constraint?
- Can they identify the exact resource/capability/schedule change and a real fallback?
- Do they sometimes rationally choose no constraint?
- Do stacked options expose redundancy rather than appear multiplicatively valuable?
- Can players explain a tendency statement by opening its credited evidence?
- Do they accept “insufficient or mixed evidence” as a legitimate identity result?
- Across repeated/no-event/duplicate-event cases, record setup time, repeated inputs, blocking prompts, dismissals, backtracking, missed material breaches, and retained understanding.

Falsify or redesign if players treat constraints as mandatory quality buffs, maximize rule count, cannot distinguish descriptive from enforced commitments, read identity as immutable talent, or find the preflight busier than the decision is valuable. Stop the identity layer if it merely repeats genre counts or creates stereotype labels. Do not add AMM to rescue an unclear high-level loop.

No game launch, media generation, algorithm training, schema work, or implementation is authorized by this proof plan.
