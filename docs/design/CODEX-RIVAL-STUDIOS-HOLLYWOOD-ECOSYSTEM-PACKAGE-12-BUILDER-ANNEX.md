### Archaeology hard findings

1. `GameState` is V14, correcting the V13 ecosystem scout.
2. `beginFounding()` globally replaces `state.concepts` with `correlateConceptCost(...)`; the trigger is opening the draft, not `foundStudio`.
3. `CompetingRelease` is only `{ marketPressure }`, worldgen emits no entries, and `competitionFactor` is exactly 1.0.
4. `televisionCompetition` initializes false and has no simulation consumer.
5. `FilmResult`, `TheatricalRun`, ledger rows, and contracts have no studio owner; `Contract` also has no contract ID.
6. `Standing` updates only the singleton player studio.
7. Awards, research, rank, technology progression, rivals, entry, failure, and Power Ranking do not exist.
8. Browser's era label is 1948 presentation truth; newer Unity's is 1920 presentation truth; neither is engine calendar authority.
9. Current production releases automatically when its countdown completes; there is no chosen release date.
10. Canonical projection V4 and living-lot projection V9 are whole player bundles, not an industry query protocol; `snapshotFor()` and `availableIntents()` each trigger a full-save digest.
11. V14's renewable original-screenplay minting removes the older scout's “30 films ever” terminal cap for the player. It remains a singleton player root with global `concept-orig-NNNN` IDs; rival supply and complete concept-ID-walker coverage do not exist.
12. `newGame()` is `beginFounding(generateWorld(seed))`; `beginFounding()` samples the whole talent pool with no rival-owner filter, so rival allocation must precede and atomically constrain every founding/hiring/assignment view.
13. The fixed population is 12 writers, 10 directors, 28 actors, and 10 craft; roster allocation and concurrent-project targets need an explicit role-feasibility/player-floor test.

### Industry film record


Never store generated headline text, “seen,” active tab, rank explanation prose, or debug scores.

### Frozen-leaf warning

Do not widen current `Studio`, `Standing`, `Production`, `TheatricalRun`, `FilmResult`, `Talent`, `Contract`, `FilmConcept`, `FilmParticipants`, `MarketState`/`CompetingRelease`, `FoundingState`, `TalentCareerEvent`, `EraConfig`, `CulturalForce`, or `SegmentId` in place. Every new root carrying a production/film ID must join `persistedProductionIds()`; every concept/screenplay-bearing root must join `persistedConceptIds()`. Those complete walkers—not a root-local taken set—are the allocation invariant.

- early game shows the actual cohort count without celebratory “#1 of 2” messaging;

### Transition event

A durable employer transition needs person ID, prior/new employer (nullable for free), effective week, legal reason, and contract/action reference. Every contract has its own stable ID. News derives from this fact. A roster is a query over current ownership, not a copied person array on each studio.

### Simultaneous offers

When live bidding arrives:

1. collect legal offers closing at one authoritative boundary;
2. evaluate person preferences and offer terms from one TypeScript function;
3. use a person/decision-specific derived RNG stream only for bounded preference uncertainty;
4. resolve ties by an explicit stable rule, never update order;
5. debit only the accepted employer and emit one transition;
6. make rejected offers observable only to their offeror unless a public-news rule exists.

### Contract expiry

The current player contract is active while `startWeek ≤ week < endWeekExclusive`; expiry removes it and enters the person into `freeAgents`. Preserve that boundary meaning when globalizing ownership. Do not turn expiry into an arbitrary same-week theft before the player/rival offer window is specified.

P12A must survive its own horizon without pretending four-year contracts last 120 years. At expiry, the incumbent rival may make one deterministic, affordable renewal under a versioned maintenance law. Failure makes the person free; a later rival decision boundary may make one deterministic, noncompetitive replacement offer. This is not a P12B bid window and produces no “player lost” narrative.

On every rival release, the shared person's credits, work history, genre experience, role-appropriate progression/Star Power, and additive career event update under the same authoritative law used for player credits. Validation must resolve the event against common industry film ownership, not only `player.releasedFilms`.

## O. Golden UX Journeys

### 1. Meet Hollywood

Start a 1920 world, enter the lot, then open Studio Charts from a global industry cue. See the player plus three persistent named rivals in separate Standing lanes. No Power arrow appears before authority exists.

### 2. Inspect a rival

Select Monarch. Its profile explains identity, three Standing values, public roster, recent films, and observed tendencies. It does not reveal cash, strategy weights, or unrevealed projects.

### 3. Rival development begins

Monarch commits a concept, writer, money, time, and a development slot. If not announced, it remains private; later history can still prove when it began without leaking it live.

### 4. Rival project packages honestly

Development completes. The studio can greenlight only if it has legal talent, cash, and capacity. A missing director produces wait/repackage/cancel—not an unchanged release timer.

### 5. Rival film releases

The film completes production/post, resolves one deterministic result, enters the recent Movie view and Rival Profile, and emits one typed release event.

### 6. Rival release has the P12A shared consequence

The lead is unsignable because Monarch employs her. Her twelve-week project commitment separately prevents illegal double-booking. Both facts use the same persistent person; no genre or box-office penalty is claimed in P12A.

### 7. Movie Charts without a magic score

Switch between Critics, Audience, Opening/Total, and run views. The same films reorder honestly by the selected public lane; rival contribution/ROI stays private unless a later law discloses its inputs.

### 8. Employer coherence

Open a person from a rival film. The profile says `Studio: Monarch Pictures`; selecting the employer returns to Monarch. Credits retain the same person ID after later moves.

### 9. Talent becomes free

At the exact contract boundary, one ownership transition makes the person available. The market and both profiles update atomically; a material/tracked event may enter the Pulse.

### 10. Rival signs talent

In P12B, legal offers close, one deterministic acceptance resolves, cost is debited, employer changes, and the previous studio's dependent projects react. No teleportation from a protected contract.

### 11. Player loses an opportunity

The player had a valid open offer or tracked person; another studio wins under the same rule. The notice explains the legal outcome without exposing the rival's private terms.

### 12. Quarterly Industry Pulse

Open a compact digest of material releases, talent moves, and studio events. Routine phase changes are absent. Every headline opens its source entity/facts.

### 13. Future Power snapshot

After at least two eligible quarters and a shipped formula, Studio Charts shows current/prior rank, movement, snapshot date, and typed drivers. Standing remains separate.

### 14. New studio enters

An authored deterministic window and population rule create a new identity/endowment/roster. It appears as `new`; it receives no fictional awards or filmography.

### 15. Rival distress and recovery

Later, weak receipts and obligations constrain a rival. Public status changes only at a real threshold. A subsequent successful project or restructuring can reverse it under the same state law.

### 16. Rival closure

A rival unable to recover settles/cancels projects, releases talent legally, exits active charts, and remains inspectable as a historical studio with films/credits intact.

### 17. Save, reload, reconnect

Save mid-rival-project, reload, and reconnect the client. Project timing, identity, employer, balance, events, and next decision match exactly; no release/news duplicates.

### 18. Fifty-/120-year replay

Run the same seed and player actions. Studio entry, films, talent transitions, closures, chart snapshots, and final history digest are identical. A different player action may change shared consequences without invalidating determinism.

### 19. Old save upgrade

Load a pre-P12 save. Existing player productions, scripts/workflows, runs, films, and contracts receive only derivable ownership; terms/payroll remain exact and free agents remain free. Rivals begin at the upgrade boundary. Earlier rival filmography/awards/ranks are absent or marked not recorded, and the player's unknown founding date is not invented.

### 20. Performance/history browsing

At 2040, open charts instantly, then page one rival's archive. The bridge sends bounded summaries/pages rather than every internal project and ledger row.

## P. Test Contract

### P12A hard gates

1. **Identity/allocation determinism.** Same seed creates identical IDs/names/brands/entrant order and role-feasible rosters; collision paths are bounded, one-employer exclusivity holds, and the authored per-role player founding floor remains available.
2. **Player live-state migration.** Existing productions, scripts/workflows, runs, results, and contracts gain only derivable player ownership without changing IDs, outcomes, terms, ordering, or payroll; free/founding states remain legal.
3. **No fictional rival backfill.** Migrated rival films/events/awards before `historyRecordedFromWeek` are impossible by validator/invariant, and unknown player founding date remains unknown.
4. **Complete identity walkers.** Every new production/film root joins `persistedProductionIds()` and every concept/screenplay root joins `persistedConceptIds()`; adversarial cross-root collisions fail or allocate the same deterministic next ID.
5. **Renewable rival screenplay source.** Three rivals can create legal, provenance-bearing concepts beyond the initial 30 and through week 6,240 without consuming the player `originalScreenplays` root or colliding.
6. **Anti-façade resource removal.** Remove required talent, cash, or capacity; release date/result must change or project must block/cancel. Restore and prove deterministic recovery.
7. **Capacity conservation.** Sum of active commitments never exceeds studio capacity; same person cannot occupy illegal commitments.
8. **Finance conservation.** Opening cash + persisted/summarized inflows − outflows = closing cash at every audit boundary. No implicit rescue/top-up.
9. **Lifecycle ordering.** No project skips development/package/production/post gates; terminal transition occurs once.
10. **Result determinism.** Same frozen inputs/version/RNG key produce identical critic/audience/business outcome.
11. **Contract identity/employer exclusivity.** Every contract has one ID/owner; one person has at most one exclusive employer; player and rival projections agree.
12. **Contract-horizon continuity.** The noncompetitive renewal/replacement law preserves legal rival staffing through 6,240 weeks while debiting real cash and never fabricating a player bid loss.
13. **Cross-studio career truth.** Rival release updates the same person's credits/career once, and the career event resolves through common film ownership after save/load.
14. **Chart authority.** TypeScript supplies cohort/rank/ties; clients render without recomputing a composite.
15. **Disclosure.** Private cash, weights, terms, unrevealed projects, exact hidden ability, and rival ROI inputs are absent from public DTOs/schema fixtures.
16. **Event truth/idempotence.** One material transition creates one typed event; prose is derived; reload/reconnect duplicates none.
17. **Save exactness.** export → import → export canonical bytes match; continuing from midpoint matches uninterrupted run.
18. **Bridge bounds and work.** Payload/page limits and stable cursors pass; digest/projection work occurs at most once per authoritative revision or an equivalent measured mitigation passes.
19. **P12A long horizon.** In a 6,240-week, three-rival, no-closure/no-entry fixture, every studio/person/film/contract/concept/event reference resolves and the final deterministic digest repeats.

P12E separately owns the closure/archive/entrant referential-integrity gate. It is not a P12A acceptance condition.

### Time budgets

- isolated rival subsystem at stress gate: ≤3 s over 6,240 weeks on the recorded CI/reference machine;
- incremental weekly rival step: median ≤0.5 ms, p95 ≤2 ms;
- no weekly operation scans all historical films, events, contracts, or transactions;
- quarterly ranking/pulse aggregation: ≤10 ms at stress fixture;
- rival profile first page projection: ≤10 ms from indexed state.

Full-save operations must be timed independently at the P12A/full/stress fixtures: canonical serialize, digest, strict validate/import, load-to-ready, and durable write. Provisional interaction gates are canonical serialize+digest p95 ≤100 ms at full target (≤200 ms stress), validate/import p95 ≤500 ms (≤1 s stress), and asynchronous persistence end-to-end p95 ≤250 ms at full target. Any synchronous main-thread storage step exceeding 50 ms is a ship blocker; change the storage/caching architecture rather than loosening history retention.

Record hardware, runtime version, commit, fixture seed, counts, warm-up, repetitions, median/p95/max, and allocation/heap evidence. A faster machine is not a complexity fix.

### Save budgets

- Current measurement anchor: `scripts/measure-v14-save-size.mts` records 715,193 bytes at week 520 with 114 player releases; tiered `studioEvents` use 30,473 bytes versus 202,925 unwindowed. This is current player evidence, not a rival estimate.
- active rival state ≤100 KB/studio uncompressed;
- average durable rival film ≤1.5 KB;
- typed event/annual-summary payload ≤1.5 MB at stress horizon;
- total incremental Package 12 save ≤8 MB at the full target and ≤10 MB at the stress horizon, uncompressed;
- no completed rival weekly run arrays and no weekly rank history;
- export/import remains deterministic and validates cross-references.

The current full player save is already projected to exceed browser localStorage over 120 years. Passing the incremental P12 budget does not waive the need for a durable storage solution.

### Projection budgets

- default Industry summary ≤128 KB;
- one Rival Profile detail/page ≤128 KB;
- at most 100 history rows/page;
- chart summaries include no private state or internal project arrays;
- update summary only on relevant authoritative revision/snapshot, not every render frame;
- reconnect fetches current bounded snapshot, then pages on demand;
- canonical full-save digest and industry projections are cached per authoritative revision (or equivalent measured mitigation); `snapshotFor()` and `availableIntents()` must not independently hash the same unchanged multi-megabyte save.

- `O(history)` work inside every rival/week loop;
