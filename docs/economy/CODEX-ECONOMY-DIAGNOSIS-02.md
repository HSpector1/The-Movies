## Verdict

No P0 authority failure was found. The two P1s have different causal shapes.

- **Wealth runaway:** favorable worlds repeatedly select packages with much larger absolute film contribution. Revenue scales far faster than payroll/overhead, and current capital surfaces do not absorb the resulting cash. Repeat production carries the surplus forward; it is not primarily a high-throughput exploit.
- **Week-208 wall:** studios that fail are already unable to fund renewals when the legal window opens. The synchronized founding cohort then expires together and converts a liquidity failure into complete role loss. The wall worsens operations, but removing it with one-time cash does not repair the underlying distress.

The smallest defensible future intervention class is therefore not one global tax or one renewal discount. It is:

1. a less one-dimensional forecast-choice frontier that retains a high-upside route;
2. rich-world, player-facing reinvestment or size pressure that does not touch poor studios; and
3. renewal work aimed at pre-window liquidity and founding-expiry phasing, tested together and through recurrence.

Those are experiment directions, not production tuning authorization.

### What makes money stop mattering

The successful loop is:

1. a favorable world makes high absolute-contribution packages available;
2. P5 chooses them repeatedly;
3. each release adds liquid cash much faster than payroll/overhead rises;
4. the cash keeps the next high-contribution package affordable; and
5. no current recurring obligation or capital ladder approaches the resulting scale.

For scale, the measured five-building estate is $4.38M and the largest tested Office step is $1.2M, against P5 runaway median cash of $215.68M and observed tails above $800M. Money ceases to constrain the package selector long before the five-year horizon in favorable worlds. Audit 01 already places the rich-tail onset at Week 104 for many P4/P5/P11 worlds and P5's median near the $60M runaway threshold at Week 208.

The midpoint selector proves that scoring is a genuine lever, but not a standalone solution:

| Selector | W260 median cash | Runaway | Ever distressed | Durable recovery at 103 among distressed | End negative |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Absolute profit, exponent 0 / P5 | $70.29M | 53.7% | 43.8% | 18.7% | 34.2% |
| Profit ÷ cost^0.5 | $20.36M | 43.4% | 50.4% | 10.7% | 42.3% |
| Profit ÷ cost, exponent 1 / P6 | $0.30M | 24.0% | 55.5% | 3.4% | 49.0% |

**Interpretation:** moving toward capital efficiency lowers runaway but worsens distress and recovery. The midpoint retains nonzero success, failure and outcome variance in this screen, yet it does not close the polarization. A future selector intervention must expose a meaningful risk/capital tradeoff, not simply replace P5 with P6.

## B. Week-208 wall causal model

### Exact law and timing

| Boundary | Current law |
| --- | --- |
| Week 0 | The synchronized experiment's founding contracts all start at 0 and end exclusively at 208. |
| Weeks 156/182 | Warnings are observable, but renewal actions are illegal. |
| Weeks 196–207 | The 12-week renewal window is legal. A renewal immediately pays its signing bonus and is rejected when current cash cannot cover it. |
| Week 207 → 208 | The full cohort receives its last payroll and employee overhead first. The tick then expires every contract whose end is at or before 208. Rejections themselves spend no cash and consume no RNG. |
| After 208 | Payroll falls, but missing actors/director/writers/craft can remove staffability. Base overhead remains. |

Accepted wider evidence remains authoritative: under C1 there are 1,400 intended owners, 735 unique rejected owners and 8,820 retries; 732/735 rejected owners have no affordable legal week. The 150-entry corpus has 70 full walls, one partial wall and 71 role-coverage losses. C2/C3/C4 ordering changes are almost inert, while C5/C6 move cadence but retain thousands of retries and 69/71 post-recurrence role-loss runs.

### Dominant causes

1. **Affordability at the legal boundary is necessary in this sample.** In the new 75-entry vacant corpus, all 32 positive all-obligation gaps are exactly the 32 baseline full walls. Those studios accept zero owners; 31 formally healthy studios produce zero walls. Development/casting has 0/25 walls, direct package 15/25 and scaled-two 17/25. Policy/roster splits are descriptive because pipeline and roster size also differ.
2. **Late timing contributes to a subset, but is not the majority explanation.** Of the 32 eventual walls, 11 could fund all quotes at illegal Week 156 and four at illegal Week 182; five could fund minimum role coverage at Week 182. By legal Week 196, all 32 are insolvent. Earlier-window action was not simulated and would change term timing, so these counts are exposure evidence, not proof of a fix.
3. **Ordering does not create the failure.** C3 role-first with no grant is final-state- and RNG-identical to C1 in all 75 entries. Half the all-obligation gap leaves 31/32 affected entries in a full wall and one partial wall.
4. **Synchronization creates the operational cliff.** Every failed founding contract disappears in one tick. Among affected entries, baseline role loss and zero roster are both 32/32 at Week 208/260.
5. **Roster construction changes exposure.** The seven-person direct and 13-person scaled cohorts fail; the eight-person development/casting policy does not. This is not a causal roster-size estimate because the operating policies and cash trajectories differ.

### Does distress cause the wall, or the wall cause distress?

Both directions exist, but they are not equal.

- **Before the wall:** every observed wall is already insolvent when legal renewal begins. The wall does not cause the initial cash failure.
- **At the wall:** simultaneous expiry turns that cash failure into all-role loss and staffability loss.
- **After the wall:** removing the cliff does not restore finances. In the 32 affected entries, an analysis grant equal to the exact Week-196 all-roster gap accepts every owner and eliminates all original walls/role loss, yet all 32 still end Week 260 negative. Median final cash is −$5.58M, and the cash delta net of the $4.01M median grant is −$4.25M. Median staffability blockers fall from 104 to zero, while affordability blockers rise from 12 to 64.
- **At recurrence:** the one-time full-gap cohort renews toward Week 404. By Week 428, all 32 affected studios again have zero active contracts and median cash −$18.51M. Across all 75 cells, current and full-grant arms both end with 63/75 zero rosters. The original-wall counter remains zero by definition; that must not be mistaken for durable success.

Thus the wall is an amplifier and an operational lockout, but persistent distress is upstream and survives its removal. In representative no-pipeline, negative-cash cases, the studio remains economically trapped under the tested operating policy: no positive production commitment is affordable and base overhead continues. The corpus does not prove every conceivable human recovery is impossible; cost-free public-talent creation remains legal.

### Preservation-gate result

No hypothetical production intervention passes all preservation gates on this branch. The frozen baseline still says to preserve:

- star cost/value tradeoffs and Fame saturation;
- horizon-dependent marketing choice;
- publicity cooldown, saturation, anti-spam and unused no-op behavior;
- the closed disengagement exploit;
- Office creative/revenue uplift;
- deterministic cash/RNG/save reconciliation; and
- nonzero success, failure, recovery and policy variation.

The selector arm directly checks only success/failure/variation. Shadow arms cannot check behavior because choices are frozen. Renewal grants check determinism and recurrence, not stars/marketing/publicity/Office. Recommendations are conditional on later full-gate reruns.

## D. Fixed-cost defect repair spec

### Reproduction and blast radius

The Annex witness proves two omissions, both caused by treating payroll + ordinary overhead as complete fixed operating cost:

| Surface | Reported | Complete current-law amount | Gap |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| One-week allocator/recap fixed cost | $210,000 | $213,500 | $3,500 |
| Current weekly burn | $15,000 | $18,500 | $3,500/week |
| Displayed runway | 1,267 weeks | 1,027 weeks | +240 weeks displayed (~23% high) |
| 14-week prospective cycle fixed cost | $210,000 | $259,000 | $49,000 |

The exact path is:

1. src/core/tick.ts correctly debits weeklyPlacementOperatingCost as facilityOpex.
2. financeTotals and periodSummary correctly include facilityOpex in their overhead bucket.
3. src/core/fixedCostAllocation.ts filters only payroll/overhead, so film allocation, idle burn, recap totalLedgerFixedCost and studioEconomicResult understate fixed operating cost.
4. Independently, weeklyBurn in src/core/economyView.ts returns payroll + ordinary overhead only. Runway, financeView, commitmentPreview post-runway, prospectiveCycleFixedCost, postSigningRunway and recap position inherit the optimistic basis.

**Blast radius:** engine cash, the ledger, saves, immediate canAfford legality, financeTotals, periodSummary and placement Opex displays are correct. Player-facing Dashboard/Assembly/package/offer/recap decision support is wrong whenever facilities operate. Facility-bearing policies that consult post-runway can also choose differently. Audit 01's direct P5/P6 corpus has no facilities and is unaffected.

**Severity: P2.** This is not a cash-conservation or production-law defect, but it is broader than recap-only attribution because player decisions consume the underreported burn/runway.

### Minimal later repair

1. Keep weeklyOverhead as the base/personnel component; add a named weeklyFacilityOpex wrapper over the authoritative placement calculator.
2. Make weeklyBurn equal payroll + ordinary overhead + operational facility Opex. Expose the facility component explicitly in FinanceView.
3. Let existing runway, commitment, cycle, signing and recap consumers inherit the corrected shared burn.
4. Expand ledgerFixedCostByWeek to payroll, overhead and facilityOpex. Preserve ledger sourcing, integer largest-remainder allocation and idle-burn reconciliation.
5. Do not change tick charges, canAfford, placement legality, save schema, film Contribution or financeTotals/periodSummary semantics.

Required regression coverage:

- no-facility byte/value control;
- one Annex and multiple facilities;
- construction completion, move and demolition timing;
- next-tick fixed outflow equals corrected weeklyBurn;
- immediate affordability remains unchanged while post-runway changes;
- all runway/cycle/offer/recap surfaces agree;
- occupied, shared and idle facility Opex allocation;
- exact per-film + idle = total fixed-operating ledger reconciliation;
- save round-trip/replay and no duplicate charge.

Existing D-12/D-17A tests that assert weeklyBurn or allocator total equals payroll + overhead encode the older incomplete invariant and must be updated deliberately, not merely appended around.

## E. Recommendation

### Strong candidate for future tuning experiments

1. **Selector risk/capital frontier:** retain a high-upside absolute-profit route, but expose a real cost/risk/runway tradeoff. The exponent screen proves leverage and harm on both sides; it does not nominate 0.5.
2. **Rich-world reinvestment or size pressure:** test player-chosen recurring capital demand and rich-only roster/facility/throughput liabilities. Do not tax poor worlds and do not begin by changing stars, publicity or Offices.
3. **Renewal 2×2 causal lab:** synchronized versus exogenously phased founding expiries crossed with current immediate bonus versus equal-total cash timing. Hold roster, nominal obligation, operating policy and RNG fixed; run beyond the next expiry.
4. **Pre-window survival/recovery purpose:** cheap production currently fails as a survival lane. Test whether stronger ordinary cash generation reduces both entry insolvency and wall exposure before changing quote/salary law.
5. **Integrate the fixed-cost read-model repair:** this is a truth fix, not balance tuning.

### Needs human playtest

- When does rich-world reinvestment feel like ambition rather than confiscation?
- Can players recognize absolute-profit tail risk and choose capital efficiency knowingly?
- Are renewal warnings, reserve needs and synchronized expiry legible and fair?
- Does preserving a roster while remaining cash-poor feel like recovery or prolonged failure?
- Does cheap production have a discoverable studio-fantasy purpose?
- Are rare blockbuster/collapse tails dramatic, or do they feel arbitrary?

### Probably leave alone

- Fame saturation and the current star salary/value tradeoff;
- marketing's horizon-dependent ordering;
- publicity anti-spam/cooldown and unused no-op behavior;
- Office creative uplift;
- immediate cash affordability, deterministic RNG and ledger reconciliation;
- production capacity until a wealthy managed-demand experiment shows it is causal.

### Rejected intervention

- blanket payroll/overhead increases;
- a literal automatic wealth or positive-margin tax;
- one measured estate purchase as the sole late-game sink;
- renewal reminders, retry order or in-window staggering as an economy cure;
- a one-time renewal grant/bailout as durable recovery;
- salary/quote cuts before the star-preservation gate;
- changing publicity, marketing endpoints, Fame or Offices to solve unrelated P1s.
