## Verdict

The current economy is deterministic and internally reconciling, but it is **not yet a healthy
long-run balance closure**. There is no P0 authority failure or catastrophic exploit. There are two
P1 balance failures: a sharply polarized cash economy in which favorable forecast-profit worlds
compound past meaningful prices by Years 2–5, and a harsh distress/Week-208 roster wall from which
most ordinary or cheap studios rarely meet the D-16 non-strict 103-week recovery criterion. The P5
runaway finding is P1 **within the direct-package current-law evidence class**; the managed corpus
confirms renewal fragility and undercapitalized scaling but has no P5-equivalent optimizer.

The result is not “one strategy always wins.” No normal player policy wins more than 50% of seeds at
any measured horizon. Instead, several incentives are locally degenerate:

- maximizing visible forecast **absolute profit** beats maximizing visible forecast ROI on 87.3%
  of paired five-year worlds;
- conserving cash by barely producing can look financially competitive despite negative movie ROI
  and terminal creative inactivity;
- extra Development & Casting capacity is usually inert because staffing, cash and pipeline demand
  bind first;
- Development Offices reliably improve every tested picture, but neither tier repays its actual
  capital and operating cost on one picture.

Several systems should explicitly be preserved: star-heavy spending is not automatic, minimum and
maximum marketing trade places with horizon, publicity spam is punished, the retired 100%-gross
disengagement exploit remains closed, and Office creative uplift is real rather than cosmetic.

## Direct answers

1. **What looks healthy?** Cash reconciles exactly in all 24,000 macro runs; unused publicity is an
   exact no-op; the bundled star-heavy policy buys better film outcomes without automatically buying victory; neither
   marketing endpoint dominates every horizon; aggressive publicity and cooldown spam lose badly;
   offices produce reliable creative/revenue uplift; premature large-roster expansion is punished.
2. **What looks suspicious?** The economy is bimodal, not smoothly risky. At Week 260 the P5
   forecast-profit policy has median cash `$70.29M` and p10 `-$10.32M`; P4 premium has median
   `$0.93M`, p90 `$433.20M`, and max `$1.322B`. The same rules generate both extreme top tails and
   long terminal decline. P9's high cash tournament share is substantially explained by not
   making films.
3. **What is demonstrably broken?** `allocateFixedCosts` and the Studio Run Recap omit paid
   `facilityOpex`. The one-week Annex witness reports `$210,000` while the complete fixed-operating
   ledger reports `$213,500`, an exact `$3,500` understatement. Cash simulation is unaffected; the
   current fixed-operating read-model/accounting attribution is incomplete. The allocator still
   satisfies its older D-17A payroll+overhead-only invariant; C2's later facility-burn truth is the
   unmet boundary. The Week-208 roster wall is also a
   demonstrated major balance residual, already accepted as open by D-17B rather than newly
   discovered here.
4. **Which strategies dominate?** None dominates the full normal-policy tournament above 50%.
   P5 locally dominates P6 (`+ $51.97M` paired median, 87.3% wins at Week 260). P11 adaptive beats
   P3 standard on 66.1% of paired seeds. P9 leads the normal cash tournament at Week 260 (29.6%),
   but does so with only 11 median releases and 182 median no-production weeks; that is an objective
   degeneracy, not healthy freelancer specialization.
5. **When does money become too easy or too punishing?** For favorable P4/P5/P11 worlds, by Week
   104: their p75 cash is already `$74.1M/$81.7M/$70.9M`, above the established 3x-opening-cash
   threshold; a Week-104 slice above `$60M` is sufficient to satisfy the max-over-run runaway
   classifier. P5's median reaches roughly that threshold at Week 208 (`$58.99M`) and exceeds
   it by Week 260. At the other end, P1's median is only `$0.11M` by Week 156; 46.8% are negative then,
   95.9% ever enter distress, and 0/959 distressed runs achieve the non-strict at103 criterion.
6. **Which systems have unexpectedly high or low ROI?** P5 package selection has unexpectedly high
   cash return;
   Q6 awareness maintenance is high but non-universal; stars are positive but costly rather than a
   no-brainer; bare-minimum and development-farm portfolios are negative; free capacity has near-zero
   value in most managed policies; Office II/III have certain per-picture benefit but negative
   one-picture cash payback. Exact values are below.
7. **What should eventually be tuned?** In order: the Week-208 renewal/recovery interaction; the
   cheap-film survival purpose; forecast-profit versus forecast-ROI selection incentives; cash sinks
   and size-scaling costs in rich worlds; the demand that makes purchased capacity useful; then Office
   portfolio payback and Q6 publicity value. These are measured targets, not proposed formula/value
   changes.
8. **What should not be changed?** Do not undo Fame saturation/star tradeoffs, marketing's interior
   choice shape, publicity cooldown/saturation/anti-spam, D-17A engagement persistence, exact cash
   ledger/RNG behavior, or Office creative uplift. Do not infer that Production Budget law itself
   needs a retune: this audit measures interacting packages, while D-13's isolated budget evidence
   remains the relevant authority. Do not delete capacity facilities because a free-slot sensitivity
   is not purchase ROI.
9. **What remains for human playtest?** Whether players can discover P5's advantage; whether hoarding
   cash while not making films feels like success; whether recovery choices feel legible and fair;
   when facilities feel worth building across a portfolio; whether Q6 feels like strategy or upkeep;
   whether repeated genre/concept concentration feels exploitative; and whether rare blockbuster or
   collapse tails feel dramatic rather than arbitrary.

## Strategy health and attribution

The normal-player cash tournament excludes the oracle, historical exploit, do-nothing diagnostic and
publicity variants. Its winner changes over time:

| Horizon | Leading cash win shares |
| --- | --- |
| Week 52 | P5 42.9%; P10 13.2%; P4 10.7% |
| Week 104 | P5 40.9%; P9 17.4%; P4 16.8% |
| Week 156 | P5 33.5%; P9 22.9%; P4 21.5% |
| Week 208 | P9 26.7%; P5 25.9%; P4 23.6% |
| Week 260 | P9 29.6%; P4 26.2%; P5 19.6%; P11 12.8% |

No global winner crosses 50%, but “winner changes” is not sufficient health when P9 wins through
stoppage. P5 is the strongest repeated observed cash-return action in the measured package surface. Its
paired disadvantage exemplar is `eta-macro-0789` (`-$6.78M` versus P6); its advantage exemplar is
`eta-macro-0899` (`+$684.06M`). That spread is why the 87.3% paired win rate and median effect are
more informative than either mean or one seed alone.

P16 is deliberately excluded from that tournament, so the idle comparison is a cash diagnostic and
does not re-certify D-17 G9's formal every-horizon waiting gate.

Raw-dollar two-way variance across the 13 normal policies shifts from seed/policy/interaction shares
of 63.9%/8.5%/27.6% at Week 52 to 32.4%/19.8%/47.8% at Week 260. Strategy matters more over time,
but world-policy interaction becomes the largest component. This explains why competent P5/P11
policies can have both extreme wealth and deeply negative p10 outcomes.

Genre concentration is observed but not certified as dominance. Nine direct-package policies use
exactly one genre on every seed; all player-policy median genre counts are one. Those policies reuse
the package/concept surface and do not model a human desire to diversify. A controlled legal
concentration-versus-diversification policy was not available, so this is a P3 harness/fantasy
question, not proof of an engine exploit.

The scaled-four policy requested 12/4/6/4 but the founding recruitment budget legally admitted only
11/4/6/3. It is intentionally aggressive and undercapitalized, so its result is a boundary, not a
recommended play style.

Managed “recovered” means only that a run went negative and later touched non-negative cash once; it
is not D-17's durable@103 definition. Renewal rejections are action attempts retried in each week of
the 12-week window, not unique failed contracts: 84 is seven contracts × 12 attempts, 156 is 13 × 12,
and 288 is 24 × 12. All founding contracts in these policies deliberately share a 208-week term, so
the result proves susceptibility to synchronized expiry, not that every human roster must fail then.

The +1/+2 arms grant **free** D&C capacity at the Annex/Hall opening weeks. They do not place a
building and charge no capex/opex. They answer “would a slot help if free?” only. For direct-package,
both arms are byte-identical to current on all 50 seeds. For scaled-two, free +1 changes mean cash by
only `+$21.5k` (95% mean CI `-$113.4k–+$156.4k`) and median releases by zero. For scaled-four, free +2
reliably removes refusals but worsens mean cash by `$1.431M`. Subtracting catalog payments yields
negative rough optimistic bounds for every policy median, but those bounds remain non-causal and
must not be reported as facility purchase ROI.

The observatory declines and rolls back a capacity-blocked intent; a “D&C rejection” is therefore
not a measurement of how a future player-visible queue would wait or resolve.
