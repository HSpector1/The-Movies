## 2. Updated P14 design — deliverable C

The package name, the three-part shape and the full ambition are unchanged: **Package 14 — Talent Market, Relationships & Career Lifecycle**, delivered as P14A → P14B → P14C, with P14D kept only as a parking label. What changes is the content of each part under the direction, and the firm rule that P14A proves the contested market without dragging P14B/C into the first checkpoint while leaving the seams they attach to.

### 2.1 P14A — Contested Talent Market, redefined

#### 2.1.1 The outcome P14A must produce

> A known professional approaches contract expiration. The player inspects that exact person from the profile, and from the lot when the person is on the player's roster. The player makes and reviews a proposal. One real rival makes a competing proposal under the same law. Known rival terms are shown where lawfully known; unknown terms say UNKNOWN. At the authoritative settlement boundary the person chooses deterministically and replayably, P10 settles the contract, P12 settles employer, exclusivity and interval truth, P11 observes the money, and P14 records the competitive case, the choice and its reasons. Exactly one outcome occurs, and the same `PersonId` survives whichever studio wins.

No agent stands between the player and the market. No promise, relationship, retirement or profession change is required to reach that outcome. Firing is integrated as a real roster/contract action because it already exists upstream; its tuning does not consume the checkpoint.

#### 2.1.2 Market eligibility (direction 4)

| Person state | Market status | Who may propose | Law |
|---|---|---|---|
| contracted, more than the renewal window remaining | `contracted_outside_window` — no case | nobody; no in-term approaches, no hidden tampering | accepted |
| contracted, inside the accepted renewal window (`renewalWindowOpen`, 12 weeks), no retirement announced | `renewal_window` — a case opens at the window's first week | the incumbent and any *entered* P12 rival, under the same law | P14A case law (§2.1.3) |
| contracted, retirement announced (P14C) | `retirement_announced` — no market case; an open case is `invalidated` with a typed reason at announcement | only the current employer, through the single one-year extension of §6.2; no proposal from any issuer may carry `endWeekExclusive` past the effective week | P14C (§6.2) |
| contract expired without settlement, or released early (§3), or a P14C transition committed | `free_agent` | any entered studio, immediately, under the **accepted instant-signing law**: the player's `signContract` (free agents listed first in the hiring market), the rival's `staff()` at its weekly decision. No case, no deadline, no person choice. The releasing studio is bound by the guard of §3.5 | accepted (P14A.1 Core); a free-agent *case* with a bounded horizon is Ready work |
| retirement effective week reached with an uncleared production seat (P14C) | `finishing_commitments` — off the market; no new assignments; the profession retirement is not finalized until the seat clears | nobody | P14C (§6.2) |
| retired from the profession (P14C) | `retired_or_ineligible` | nobody | P14C |

Eligibility reads `rivalEmployment` / `studioEmployerId` and the contract window, never the `employmentStatus` string (which says `unavailable` both for rival-employed and for off-rotation people). A reserved rival (`enteredWeek === null`) cannot propose. No case opens during the founding draft. A case subject is inspectable and proposable regardless of the 13-week hiring-market rotation sample: admission for a case subject is **interval algebra at the effective week** (no other P10 contract or P12 interval covers the proposal's `startWeek`), applied at proposal time and re-applied at settlement; the accepted current-week gates (`hiringMarketIds`, `staff()`'s `unavailable` set) continue to govern only non-case signings. That is a bounded generalization owned by P10 (a commit-from-draft primitive that accepts a future start) and P12 (a case-aware exclusion in `staff()` and in `enterRival`'s reservation), listed in §2.5.

**Why free agents stay instant-sign in the Core checkpoint.** The Owner's direction is that a legitimately free person may be pursued immediately under the normal market law, and the accepted law is first-come: the player acts before the tick, rivals inside it. A free-agent case with proposals and a bounded decision horizon (hypothesis: four weeks) would make free agency contestable too, but it changes a second accepted P10 behavior and adds a weekly attention stream per free agent; it is preserved as Ready work with that design, not built in Core.

#### 2.1.3 The case, without an intermediary

```text
person eligibility
  ├─ contracted_outside_window
  ├─ renewal_window
  ├─ retirement_announced
  ├─ finishing_commitments
  ├─ free_agent
  └─ retired_or_ineligible

market case
  discovered → proposals_open → decision_pending
            → settled | declined | expired | invalidated
```

`intermediary_contacted` and the vestigial `accepting_proposals` are gone; the August `expiring_to_market` and `unavailable_for_recorded_reason` states, which no table defined, are deleted. Discovery is authoritative and public: a case is `discovered` the week eligibility opens, derived from the accepted weekly scan of active employment at the expiry boundary (the same O(active roster) pass `finishHollywoodWeek` and `staff()` already perform), and every entered studio sees it at once. **The case's existence and its decision week become public by the disclosure rule of §2.1.5; at `592e926` a rival person's contract end is not public** (`bridge/people.ts` returns `contract: null` for rival-employed people), so this is a new, authored disclosure of one contract term, the end week, owned by the Industry projection. What stays private is the content of another studio's proposal.

**The decision week is derived on read from the live contract**, never copied: it is the subject's `endWeekExclusive` at the time of reading. **Invalidation triggers:** the subject is released early (§3; the person is a free agent now); retirement is announced (§6.2); the subject otherwise leaves eligibility. An invalidated case closes every proposal with a typed reason and settles nothing.

**The one accepted behavior P14A changes.** Today the incumbent's renewal is an immediate replacement of the running contract from the current week (`applyRenewContract`), and a solvent rival renews its own people on the first week of the window (`staff()`), so a rival's person never reaches a contested expiry and the player's person can be locked before any rival sees the case. Under P14A, for a person under a case, the incumbent's renewal becomes the incumbent's **proposal**: a draft whose `startWeek` is the old contract's `endWeekExclusive`, settled at the decision week with everyone else's. `renewContract` on a cased person is refused with a new typed refusal (`underMarketCase`) that redirects to the proposal path, and the accepted Renew confirmation gains a branch (§2.1.11). Outside a case the accepted immediate-renewal semantics stand unchanged. This is a SHARED GENERALIZATION with P10 (player) and P12 (rival maintenance: the renewal loop in `staff()` skips case subjects), and it is what makes an expiry contestable at all. An "exclusive negotiating period" for the incumbent before rivals may propose was considered and rejected: it narrows the Owner's direction that outside studios may compete for people entering the approved window, and it would let a diligent player nullify the contested market for every one of their own people.

#### 2.1.4 Proposals, the compensation lever, and what makes a rival propose

**The draft.** A proposal references a P10-priced draft, not a copy of a contract: it stores `(talentId, termWeeks, startWeek, premiumTier)` and a digest of the derived `ContractOffer`, and re-derives the six accepted terms through the shared pricing entry at review and at settlement; a digest mismatch invalidates the version. No P10 "draft entity" exists at `592e926` (`contractOffer` returns a transient value; the August `P10ContractDraftId` is absent machinery), which is why the reference is by inputs plus digest. Material terms are `termWeeks`, `startWeek`, `premiumTier` and, when P14B exists, the attached promise set; a change to any of them → new version; prior review token and submission invalid. The player submits, revises or withdraws; nobody accepts.

**The compensation lever (symmetric).** At `592e926` the player cannot set a salary: `signContract` / `renewContract` carry only `talentId` and `termWeeks`, and the annual salary is fixed at the person's ask. A contested market with no money lever is a contest the player can only lose on money, and the approved August design names pay as a lever in its product fantasy (§10) and its economy section (§16). P14A therefore adds **one bounded, typed premium tier on the draft, available to every issuer under the same law**: `annual = round(ask(term) × premium)`, `premium ∈ {1.00, 1.05, 1.10, 1.15, 1.20, 1.25}` (hypothesis), `signingBonus = round(0.18 × annual)` by the accepted law, `canAfford` re-asked on the bonus at submission and at settlement. This is a P10 contract-law change (Core, §2.5), priced by one studio-aware entry (`studioOffer(state, studioId, talentId, term, week, premiumTier)`, wrapping `offerForTalent`) that both the player's path and the exported P12 signing primitive call, so the floor of §3.5 and the premium live in one place and `offerForTalent` itself stays floor-free for the stateless Full-Custom preview. The rival's tier is **derived deterministically from its existing policy fields** (`reserveWeeks`, `negativeScale`, `affinities`) and the need below, with no persisted policy change in Core; a persisted policy v2 (explicit term preference and premium knobs) is Ready work under a validator version branch.

**What makes a rival propose (the trigger).** Rival maintenance today fills deficits against six fixed seats and never proposes. In P14A the rival evaluates, inside its existing weekly decision (`nextDecisionWeek`), a pure P12 policy function over a case description P14 hands it, and P14 creates and stores the proposal record (P14 owns proposals; P12 owns the policy evaluation). The rival proposes for a case subject when: (a) the subject is its own person in the window (the incumbent-rival proposal), or (b) the rival holds a same-role contract ending at or before the subject's effective week, or (c) it has a seat deficit at that role at the effective week. A policy-driven "upgrade" that would require the rival to release a current employee needs the Ready rival-termination path (§3.6) and is not in Core. **Consequence stated plainly:** in a live campaign the contests P14A.1 produces are (i) the player bidding on a rival's expiring person, which the accepted law forbids today and this generalization opens, and (ii) a rival bidding on the player's expiring person when the rival has an expiry or a vacancy at that role in that window. The P14A.1 fixture authors (ii) explicitly (a rival whose same-role person expires the same week), not a silent vacancy.

**Affordability.** The player's bonus passes the accepted `canAfford` legality gate (D-12 contract law); the rival's bonus passes its reserve rule (`operatingReserve`, P12 strategy). Neither is a P11 rule: P11 observes the money and owns no affordability law.

#### 2.1.5 What is lawfully known, and what says UNKNOWN

The accepted disclosure law hides every rival contract term ("Contract terms are kept private in Industry."; `contract: null` for rival-employed people). The Owner's "transparent competing terms" therefore needs an explicit disclosure rule authored with the proposal fact. This table is that rule; it is an **authored widening of the accepted P12 disclosure law** (UX-011 / SAF-014) owned by the Industry projection, with the serialized-envelope leak test extended to every P14A DTO.

| Fact | Player sees | Rival sees | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| that a case exists, its subject and its decision week (the subject's contract end week) | yes | yes | public at discovery (new disclosure of one contract term) |
| the person's public profile, credits, employer history, and the public preference descriptors the chooser reads (term preference and its basis, incumbency preference, reservation predicate) | yes | yes | P10/P12 public projection plus §2.1.7 |
| the person's ask for a chosen term | for the player's own draft | for its own draft | each studio prices its own draft; the ask function is shared law |
| that a competing proposal exists, from which studio, since which week | yes | yes | public from the week of submission (hypothesis) |
| the competing proposal's term length and effective week | yes | yes | public (hypothesis; a new disclosure of one term) |
| the competing proposal's premium tier, salary and bonus | **UNKNOWN** | **UNKNOWN** | private, before and after settlement. Because the ask is shared law and the term is public, UNKNOWN hides the premium tier and the bonus, not the ask itself; that is stated to the player, not concealed |
| the competing proposal's attached promises (P14B) | UNKNOWN | UNKNOWN | private |
| the person's ranking | no, until settlement; then the receipt's top reasons, which disclose **ordering only** ("their compensation ranked above yours"), never amounts | same | no hidden dice, no exposed formula |
| after settlement: who the person joined and on what term length | yes | yes | public, folded by P14A.2's Pulse projection into one `retained` or `moved` activity from the two P12 receipts (§2.1.8); salary stays private |

The comparison view always has a truthful column for the rival: known rows filled, unknown rows literally "UNKNOWN". No band, estimate or rumor is invented. With the intermediary removed, no lawful path to a rival's money exists in P14A; this narrows the August option B, which had let money become known through knowledge provenance, and it rests on direction 8 ("unknown rival facts remain explicitly unknown"). Post-settlement salary sits in `hollywood.employment[].terms` and must be leak-tested exactly as P12's envelope is.

#### 2.1.7 The person-choice rule

Deterministic, versioned, replayable, no RNG, not a single exposed formula, and never dependent on array order, Map/Set order, `PersonId` lexical order or `StudioIdentity.row` (P12's own staffing idiom of first-match in `state.talent` order is not a precedent for a contested choice).

**Reservation (absolute predicate, applied to every proposal before ranking).** A proposal clears reservation iff its annual salary ≥ the person's ask for that term (true by construction while the premium tier is ≥ 1.00; a below-ask tier is not offered in P14A), and, when P14B exists, the issuing studio is not `Distrusted` by this person and no `Nemeses`-tier relation of the person is on the issuer's roster. A single proposal (the P14C extension, a P14B waiver, a P14C transition choice) is accepted iff it clears; for the extension the person's reservation carries an authored retirement factor (hypothesis: ask × 1.10). Proposals that fail reservation, legality or affordability at freeze are dropped with typed reasons before ranking.

**Why not a lexicographic, compensation-first ranking (paper test).** Revision 2 ranked survivors lexicographically with compensation first. Paper-tested, that rule makes any higher premium tier dominate every later consideration: a rival at tier 1.05 with no opportunity, a Mixed trust record and no friends on its roster beats the incumbent at tier 1.00 with a qualifying lead promise, a Reliable record and the person's Close Friend on the lot, because the compensation key never ties. Money would be the only lever that matters, which contradicts the Owner's fantasy of a legible choice among pay, role, opportunity, trust and relationships. The rule below replaces it.

**Ranking: banded descriptors, pairwise wins, a public priority order (recommended).** Each surviving proposal is scored on a fixed list of typed descriptors, each reduced to a small **band** so that near-equal offers tie rather than differ by a dollar, and each band difference produces a typed reason:

| Descriptor | Bands (hypothesis) | P14A status |
|---|---|---|
| D1 compensation | `at ask` (tier 1.00), `above` (1.05–1.10), `well above` (1.15–1.25), read as annual ÷ ask for the offered term | live |
| D2 term against the person's public term preference (from `careerIdentity` and age band) | `match`, `near` (one catalogue step), `mismatch` | live |
| D3 opportunity | `qualifying promise attached` (a P14B promise whose class the person's public preference names), `none` | neutral in P14A |
| D4 trust in the issuer (per-pair record, aggregate fallback) | `Reliable`, `Mixed`, `Distrusted` (Distrusted already fails reservation) | neutral in P14A |
| D5 relationships at the issuer | `close ties here` (Close Friends / Inseparable / Partners on the roster), `none`, `enemies here` | neutral in P14A |
| D6 studio Standing by reference (player: P08 channels; rival: `RivalBusiness.standing`) | `higher`, `similar`, `lower` relative to the other issuers | live |
| D7 incumbency | `current employer`, `other` | live |

For every pair of proposals, count the descriptors on which each wins a band; the proposal with more pairwise wins is preferred; **dominance** (better-or-equal on every descriptor, better on at least one) always wins. With more than two proposals, a dominated proposal is removed first, then each survivor's score is the number of other survivors it beats pairwise (the Copeland count) and the highest score is preferred; the tie order below applies to equal scores. Ties in win count are broken by the person's **public priority order** over descriptors — an archetype derived from public facts and shown on the profile (hypothesis: capable-but-unproven and under-30 → opportunity, compensation, relationships, term, trust, Standing, incumbency; proven veterans → compensation, term, trust, relationships, incumbency, Standing, opportunity) — then by earliest submission week, then by incumbent-if-present, then decline-all. Reasons are the descriptors won ("they offered a qualifying lead role", "your record with her is Reliable, theirs Mixed", "their compensation band is above yours"), ordering-only. No RNG; no array, Map, `PersonId` or row order; the rule and its band edges are versioned and recorded on the receipt.

**Cases (NUMERICAL/CONTENT HYPOTHESIS).**
1. *Money alone does not win.* Incumbent: tier 1.00, term match, lead promise, Reliable, close ties, similar Standing. Rival: tier 1.10, term match, no promise, Mixed, none, similar Standing. Wins: incumbent D3, D4, D5, D7 (4); rival D1 (1). Incumbent retained; reasons: qualifying role, trust, friends here.
2. *Money wins when everything else ties.* Both at term match, no promises (P14A), neutral trust and relationships, similar Standing; rival tier 1.10 vs incumbent 1.00. Wins: rival D1 (1); incumbent D7 (1). Tie → the person's public priority order: a proven veteran ranks compensation before incumbency → rival wins ("their compensation band is above yours"); a capable-but-unproven person ranks opportunity first, but with no promises attached the next key is compensation → rival wins too. The incumbent must at least match the band to keep the person.
3. *Opportunity beats a modest premium for rising talent.* Rival: tier 1.05, lead promise attached (P14B), Reliable; incumbent: tier 1.00, no promise, Reliable, close ties. Wins: rival D1, D3 (2); incumbent D5, D7 (2). Tie → capable-but-unproven priority order puts opportunity first → rival wins ("they offered a qualifying lead role").
4. *A well-above premium still loses on pairwise wins.* Rival: tier 1.25, term mismatch, no promise, Mixed, enemies here; incumbent: 1.00, match, lead promise, Reliable, close ties. Neither dominates (the rival wins compensation); the incumbent wins 5 descriptors to 1; the money reason is listed against it, honestly ("their compensation band is well above yours").
5. *P14A before P14B.* With D3–D5 neutral, live descriptors are D1, D2, D6, D7: the person weighs compensation band, term fit, Standing and incumbency; a rival matching the band and the term needs higher Standing to beat the incumbent, which is the accepted P12 fact the player can see.

The person may rank every proposal below reservation and decline all, becoming a free agent. The Owner's "not trivially exposed as a single optimization formula" is met by bands (a dollar more buys nothing inside a band), pairwise wins (no single scalar), and the public archetype (which the player can read but not manipulate).

#### 2.1.8 Settlement

**Where it runs.** The decision phase is a new, terminal, fixed-order step of the weekly advance at the decision week W, after the tick has advanced (`market.tick = W`), after the P10 expiry step has removed the subject's contract and pushed the person into `freeAgents`, after scheduled rival entry, and after `finishHollywoodWeek` has closed the subject's interval and written its `expiry` receipt. It could not run earlier: before the advance `market.tick` is W−1 and the mirror would either no-op or write a renewal-shaped pair the validator rejects; before `finishHollywoodWeek` the `expiry` receipt the P14 receipt must reference does not exist. Because scheduled entry runs before this step, `enterRival`'s reservation set is widened to include open-case subjects (P12), so an arriving rival cannot take a subject in the same tick.

**What it does.** It freezes the submitted set; drops proposals that fail P10 legality (interval algebra at W), P12 (issuer entered, person not committed elsewhere), affordability (`canAfford` for the player's bonus; the reserve rule for the rival's) or, when P14B exists, promise re-feasibility (§4.3), each with a typed reason; applies the rule of §2.1.7 to the survivors; and commits the chosen proposal through the accepted owners: **P10** activates the new contract from a draft through one exported tick-side commit-from-draft primitive (contract with `startWeek = W`, `signingBonus` ledger row stamped W, `freeAgents` filtered, engagement flag), which is the same write set as `applySignContract`; for a rival winner the exported **P12** signing primitive appends the employment row with reason `replacement` (the closed union; a lawful hire from the free pool), the ordinal, the `signing` movement and the start receipt; for a player winner `recordPlayerEmployment` runs last and mirrors a `player-contract` start with `fromStudioId: null`, because the old row already ended by expiry. **P14** then writes the chooser receipt referencing the `expiry` receipt and the start receipt by `eventId`, and the case's terminal state. **P11** writes nothing: the bonus row is P10's, exactly as today. If the commit of the chosen proposal is refused by an owner, nothing is written for it and the case falls to the next valid proposal, or to free agency; a dropped proposal never cancels a valid one. Duplicate delivery consumes the same in-state idempotency key once. The P12 receipt union stays closed: an incumbent retention is therefore `expiry` + `player-contract` (or `expiry` + `replacement` for a rival), joined by the P14 receipt; a linked `from → to` receipt remains a Current Ops option (§7.4).

#### 2.1.9 Firing, integrated

The accepted `releaseTalent` action becomes P14A's early-termination action under §3: charge recalibrated, refusal extended to the busy set, confirmation disclosure widened, the anti-exploit guard applied, the release recorded as a P12 `termination` receipt exactly as today, the person a free agent immediately, and any entered studio (including rivals) free to sign them under §2.1.2. **A release invalidates the subject's open case:** the releasing studio's own proposal is withdrawn and every competing proposal is invalidated with a typed reason; the person is signable at once under the free-agent law. Rival early termination is symmetric in law and is Ready work rather than Core (§3.6).

#### 2.1.10 Bounded first checkpoint, restated

**P14A.1 — One Contested Expiry Core.** One immutable `PersonId`, two entered `StudioId`s (player and one rival present at week 0), one case, at most one current proposal per studio, the premium tier for both issuers, P10 draft references and version invalidation, the incumbent-renewal-as-proposal rule with its typed refusal, case-aware admission (P10 commit primitive, P12 `staff()` exclusion and `enterRival` reservation), the rival trigger, explicit UNKNOWN rival terms, one expiry-only atomic decision with typed ordering reasons, one P12 employer transition or free-agent result, the recalibrated firing action with its guard and busy-set refusal, the versioned termination rule for old saves, save/load/replay, duplicate-event refusal, honest old-save initialization — **and a thin player surface**, because the P13A package this one mirrors moved a thin native entry into Core for the same reason: the first product critique cannot be satisfied by a headless core. The thin surface is one named route, the **Core profile–offer–outcome route**: `Profile → Contract → Case block` on the accepted native contract sheet (state, decision week and date, competing-proposal rows with issuer / term / effective week / UNKNOWN, the person's public preference descriptors and priority order) → `Review & submit proposal` / `Revise` / `Withdraw` through the accepted quote-and-commit pattern of `bridge/contract.ts` → the settlement outcome line on the same sheet and in the accepted attention and Pulse projections, with its descriptor-won reasons. It is reached from the roster and the Industry roster the way the accepted contract sheet is reached today; it adds no new navigation surface. **P14A.2** adds the full Talent Market workspace (comparison, paged case and career pages, the widened Industry view enumeration, Pulse folding); **P14A.3** adds world navigation (the lot status glyph and world entry), keyboard/controller paths and Unity parity. The Owner can inspect, propose and read the outcome in Core through the profile route; the workspace and world navigation of §2.1.11 arrive with A.2/A.3.

**First-checkpoint pass sentence.** "The same person can receive one player and one rival proposal at an eligible expiry, see the rival's known terms and explicit unknowns on the Core profile route, be resolved exactly once by an atomic deterministic TypeScript decision through P10/P12 with readable descriptor reasons, retain the same identity and linked employer/career truth, be released early under the disclosed 26-week-capped charge without a fire-and-re-sign discount, and survive save/load/replay without a client choosing the winner."

#### 2.1.11 Information architecture without an intermediary (supersedes August §14, §15, §21 and journey 5)

**Profile → Contract (Core).** The accepted contract sheet plus a *case block* when a case is open: `Case open · decides 1934 · Week 12 (in 9 weeks)`; the person's public preferences (`Prefers longer terms · Would stay at equal terms`); a row per proposal: `Your Studio · 3 years · from Week 12 · $X/year (tier +10%) · submitted Week 3` and `Monarch Pictures · 4 years · from Week 12 · UNKNOWN · submitted Week 5`; the intents `Review & submit proposal`, `Revise`, `Withdraw`; and, after settlement, `Decided: joined Monarch Pictures · their compensation ranked above yours · they offered a longer term`. The accepted Renew confirmation gains a case branch: "Submits a 2-year proposal from Week 12 at $X/year (tier +5%); bonus $Y due at settlement if selected; Monarch Pictures has a competing proposal since Week 5; you may not be selected."

**Talent Market workspace (P14A.2).** `Attention` (deadlines, new competing proposals, revised terms, refusals) · `Cases` (Renewal window | Free agents | Settling | Closed) · candidate profile rail · offer comparison (employer / effective week / term / compensation tier / UNKNOWN rows / typed promises when P14B exists / person's reasons after settlement) · paged career and employer history · `Review & Submit`, `Revise`, `Withdraw`, with revalidation. No intermediary row, fee, route or "Approached" bucket.

### 3.2 The selected law, stated as code-shaped rules

```text
weekly        = round(annualSalary / 52)                          (unchanged)
remaining     = max(0, endWeekExclusive − week)                    (unchanged)
guaranteed    = weekly × remaining                                 (unchanged; base salary, bonus excluded)
charge        = weekly × min(remaining, 26)                        (NEW: replaces round(0.5 × guaranteed))
earned unpaid = 0 in the accepted economy (payroll is debited weekly; the bonus was paid at signing and is sunk)
effective end = the release week (person leaves the roster now, is a free agent now)
```

Equivalently: the studio pays every remaining week up to twenty-six of them, never more. The charge equals today's at exactly 52 weeks remaining (26 = ½ × 52); it is **higher** below 52 weeks (all remaining pay at ≤ 26 weeks; the 26-week cap, still above half, at 27–51 weeks) and **lower** above (capped instead of half of a growing number). It never rounds (integer × integer). "Base salary" is pinned to `guaranteedComp`'s definition, not to the D-17A "guaranteed obligation" that includes the bonus.

### 3.4 Effective end, refusal, disclosure and obligations

- **Effective end.** The release week, as today.
- **Refusal (recommendation R2).** The accepted refusal while a screenplay task is active is kept and extended to every busy-set seat: a director, cast member or craft lead seated on an active production (seats are reserved from greenlight through release) cannot be released until release. Today a released actor keeps shooting because nothing re-checks the contract inside the production; the extension is world-truth hygiene, and it is the same "finish current binding obligations" predicate retirement uses. A softer "notice now, effective at release" variant needs a pending-termination record and is Ready work. Release during the founding draft is refused.
- **Disclosure (direction 2), in the accepted confirmation's voice, two exact branches:** "Pays $X in early termination now — 26 weeks of the $Y still guaranteed through 1934 · Week 12 (cap applies: 88 weeks remain)" or "— all 19 remaining weeks of pay ($Y; fewer than 26 weeks remain, so no cap applies)". Then: remaining duration in weeks and date; remaining guaranteed compensation; the exact charge; that the charge is paid now and may take cash negative (release is not solvency-gated); effective employment end; "leaves the roster this week as a free agent; any studio may sign them at once; asks no less than $Z a year from this studio until 1934 · Week 12 (§3.5); their open market case closes and every competing proposal is invalidated; the release is published this week as a public employment transition; recorded credits and career history stay on the record"; and, when P14B exists, "1 active promise will be BROKEN". The sheet already carries `currentRemainingWeeks` and `guaranteedRemaining`, so both branches can be exact.
- **Earned unpaid obligations** are none in the accepted economy; the rule is stated so that a later P11 obligation kind is included automatically. **Unearned optional incentives** do not exist in the six-key contract; the rule excludes them by name so a later contract law cannot silently add them.
- **P11 booking.** Lump sum, written by P10's action under the existing `termination` kind and categorized as today ("Termination payments"; folded into Other Cash; never recurring cost, Upcoming or runway); the finance consequence envelope already shows the immediate cash change and the drop in guarantees. A charge paid over time would be a new finance root and an Owner gate; the package keeps lump sum.
- **Old saves (the versioned rule).** The validator recomputes every historical termination with the current formula at load, so the rule must be versioned or every save carrying a 50%-era termination stops loading. A campaign week cannot identify the era across saves, the ledger row is an exact-keyed frozen leaf that must not be widened, and a "boundary week accepts either" rule would admit the wrong formula for a new same-week transaction. The discriminator is therefore **an immutable migration receipt in the P14 root**: at the P14A migration, every player employment row ended by termination in the save (its exact `contractId`, `endedWeek` and the ledger amount actually paid) is enumerated once into `legacyTerminations`; the receipt is frozen at migration and nothing can join it later. The P12 `validateHollywood` generalization, through a validator policy parameter (the accepted idiom for widening a delegated validator), validates a terminated row whose `contractId` is in the receipt against the amount the receipt records (which is the historical `round(0.5 × guaranteed)`), and every other terminated row — including one ended in the migration week itself after the migration — against `weekly × min(remaining, 26)`. A fresh campaign carries an empty receipt; a Save As copy carries its original's. No ledger change, no week comparison, and no weakening of the exact-payment invariant.

### 3.5 Exploits examined, and the recommended guard

**E1 — fire, then immediately re-sign the same `PersonId` on cheaper terms.** Round-trip cost when at least 26 weeks remain: the charge (½ of the old annual) plus a fresh signing bonus (18% of the new annual). Saving: (old annual − new annual) × remaining years. With the new ask a fraction (1 − d) of the old, the round trip pays when `d > 0.68 / (Y + 0.18)`, `Y` = remaining years:

| Remaining | Break-even ask drop *d* (selected) | today's 50% | discarded 5% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 8–26 weeks | never (charge = full remaining pay) | 63–77% | 30–56% |
| 52 weeks | 57.6% | 57.6% | 19.5% |
| 104 weeks | 31.2% | 54.1% | 12.8% |
| 156 weeks | 21.4% | 52.8% | 10.4% |
| 200 weeks | 16.9% | 52.2% | 9.2% |

Worked case: STAR at $2,064,000 (weekly $39,692) with 200 weeks remaining, ask fallen 30% to $1,444,800 (weekly $27,785) → saving ($39,692 − $27,785) × 200 = $2,381,400; cost $1,031,992 + $260,064 = $1,292,056; **net gain $1,089,344**. Under today's rule the same round trip loses about $1.85M, so today's rule contains E1 by brute force and the selected cap opens it. In the accepted economy an in-term ask can fall only through fame loss (at most 4 points per released film, only on visible failures), because skills never decline and age never changes; reaching a 17–21% drop on a long deal needs three or more consecutive visible flops of a star early in the contract; the jitter is fixed per person and cannot move an in-term ask. The window is narrow today and **widens as soon as P14C aging exists** (the age factor alone moves an ask 15% from prime to the floor) or P13 salary scaling lets asks move. The 5% candidate opens it at 9–13% drops, inside two visible flops of a star (one for a mid-fame profile). Term switching alone never pays under the selected rule (a one-year 1.08× to four-year 0.90× switch is a 16.7% drop at ≤ 52 weeks remaining, where the break-even is 57.6%). The premium tier of §2.1.4 does not reopen E1: a re-sign at a tier ≥ 1.00 costs at least the floored ask.

**E2 — deliberate short-contract exploitation.** With ≤ 26 weeks remaining the charge equals remaining pay, so a late release saves only the accepted $1,500/week per-employee overhead and worsens liquidity (a lump today, possibly negative cash, against weekly pay); the break-even of E1 is "never" in that range, so fire-and-re-sign cannot pay on the charge alone. This holds without any renewal shortcut: under P14A the incumbent's in-window renewal for a cased person is a proposal settled at expiry (§2.1.3), not an immediate replacement, so the accepted "re-price the tail now" path Revision 2 leaned on no longer exists for a cased person. The lawful way to re-price at expiry is the incumbent's proposal at the ask (or a higher tier) settled at W, with no charge and with rivals free to compete. Between 27 and 51 weeks a release saves (remaining − 26) weeks of pay at the price of losing the person's work and opening the case to nobody (the person is a free agent): a genuine roster decision, not an exploit.

**E3 — signing-bonus interaction.** The bonus is sunk at signing and never refunded; a re-sign pays it again. It is a natural friction of 0.18 × annual on every round trip and is why the break-even never reaches zero, but it is weak on long terms (it lifts the 200-week break-even only from 13% to 17%). Not a sufficient guard. The contract-then-fire-as-freelancer pattern (a four-year MID deal, one eight-week picture, then release: bonus $85,050 + 8 weeks' pay $72,696 + cap $236,262 + overhead $12,000 = $406,008) stays ≈ 1.55× a $262,500 freelancer fee under the cap, and releasing on the first shooting day is refused under R2, so the freelancer premium survives.

**E4 — rival symmetry.** Rivals never terminate today. Under symmetric law a rival that releases early pays the same charge from its finite Cash under a new `termination` movement kind and is bound by the same guard. Cost check for a MID-level six-person team on the accepted `rivalWeeklyOperatingCost` (payroll $54,522 + overhead $24,000 + facility opex $23,500 = $102,022/week; reserve 12–20 weeks ≈ $1.2–2.0M): a MID release on a fresh four-year deal costs $236,262 ≈ 2.3 team-weeks (inside the reserve); a STAR release $928,798 ≈ 9.1 team-weeks (rich rivals only). Today's 50% rule would cost a MID release ≈ 9.3 team-weeks at 208 weeks remaining (rivals could essentially never release, mirroring the harness finding that the player never does); the 5% rule ≈ 0.9 week (rivals would churn). The cap is the one rule under which symmetric rival release is both affordable and non-trivial. Until the movement kind lands, the asymmetry is "rivals cannot fire", which favors the person, not the player.

**E5 — late-contract weirdness.** None: `weekly × min(remaining, 26)` is continuous at 26 weeks; there is no cliff, no incentive to time a release to a week boundary, and the renewal window interaction is the benign one in E2. One presentational oddity is fixed by the two copy branches of §3.4.

**E6 — the term-choice lean: "sign everyone for four years and fire freely."** For a person of constant value a four-year deal already costs 3.76 annual-equivalents over four years against 5.10 for four rolling one-year deals, because of the accepted length factor and one bonus instead of four; the cap adds to that lean. Against an *honest* term of known length the four-year-and-fire strategy still loses (MID: +18.6% if cut after one year, +10.6% after two, +9.6% after three), so it is not a dominant strategy against honest terms (the 5% candidate would have been: 4-year-and-fire cheaper than every honest shorter term). But against rolling one-year deals it wins from the second year, and for short holds the flat cap makes the 208-week deal cheaper than the 52-week deal for **every hold ≤ 38 weeks** (the one-year's 1.08× multiplies both its pay and its own 26-week charge, and its bonus is larger). In expectation, the break-even probability of keeping a hire beyond year one falls from **46% under today's rule to 15% under the cap** (model: "keep" = the full four years on the four-year deal, MID $1,975,146, against four rolling one-year deals, $2,676,272; "leave" = the four-year deal fired at week 52, $793,836 under the cap or $1,266,360 today, against one honest one-year deal, $669,068). This is not an exploit against the person (they receive 26 weeks of pay and become free agents), and its driver is the P10 length factor, which keeps paying the studio a discount for tail risk the cap no longer makes it bear. The package **does not retune the cap**; it records the consequence and the playtest signal (monotone four-year signing) and names the smallest levers if the Owner ever wants the term decision to stay live: flatten `CONTRACT_LENGTH_FACTOR` (a P10 tuning matter), the person's term preference in the chooser (§2.1.7), or, changing the selected law, a term-aware cap (`max(26, termWeeks/4)` weeks ⇒ 26/26/39/52 for 1/2/3/4-year contracts, which restores a 34% break-even and raises the 200-week fire-and-re-sign break-even from 16.9% to 29.3%). The last is listed for completeness only; it is not recommended and not asked.

**E7 — firing to escape a promise (P14B).** A release with an active promise makes the promise BROKEN immediately (studio-caused) and records the trust driver; the charge does not buy out a promise.

**The guard: a persistent salary expectation toward the releasing studio (recommended, R1).** For the terminated contract's original term, i.e. until its original `endWeekExclusive`, the person's ask toward the releasing `StudioId` is floored at the terminated contract's `annualSalary`: `ask = max(marketAsk, floor)`, applied inside the studio-aware pricing entry of §2.1.4 before any premium tier; where several terminated contracts of the same studio for the same person are still inside their original terms, the floor is the highest of their salaries. Consequences: E1's saving is zero by construction (new annual ≥ old annual), so the round trip always loses the charge plus a bonus at every remaining term; a genuine re-hire remains legal at the old price or better ("you guaranteed me $X"); a term reset (buying down 200 guaranteed weeks into a fresh one-year deal at the old rate) is a legitimate purchase, not an arbitrage; a person whose value rose is unaffected; rivals can still hire the person at the market ask, which is the honest cost of having released them; the rule is one sentence in the confirmation and one reason line in the offer options; it is symmetric for a rival that releases (the rival path prices through the same entry); it is deterministic; and it needs **no new persisted fact**.

**Termination evidence (corrected in Revision 3).** The memory is read from the accepted records that name a termination explicitly, never inferred from row shape: the P12 end receipt with reason `termination` keyed to the exact `contractId` (the validator groups receipts by `${contractId}:end`), and the P10 `termination` ledger row `applyReleaseTalent` writes unconditionally (week, `talentId`, amount). Revision 2's "no same-studio successor row starting at `endedWeek`" test is withdrawn: a release followed by a **separate** rehire command in the same week produces exactly such a successor (`recordPlayerEmployment` runs per command, so the release batch ends the row and appends the `termination` receipt, and the rehire batch appends a `player-contract` row starting that week), and the inference would have read a real termination as a renewal and lifted the floor for the very round trip the guard exists to stop. One accepted edge remains: a release and a rehire inside **one** `applyActions` batch are mirrored as a renewal pair with no termination receipt (`renewing` suppresses it), although the ledger row is still written; the package recommends closing it in the P12 mirror (the receipt is emitted whenever a `termination` ledger row for that person exists at that week) and, until then, the ledger row is the evidence of record. Every accepted client dispatches one action per command.

**Traces (paper).** *(a) Release → same-week rehire → later renewal.* Week 100: release from a 4-year contract at $2,064,000 ending week 260; charge 26 weeks; floor = $2,064,000 until week 260. Same week, separate command: rehire — the studio-aware entry prices `max(market, floor)`; if the market ask fell to $1,444,800 the draft is $2,064,000 (tier 1.00) and a fresh 18% bonus is paid; the new contract runs from week 100 for its own term. Week 250, inside the new contract's window: an ordinary renewal (no case, if no rival proposes) prices at `max(market, floor)` = $2,064,000 because week 250 < 260; from week 260 the floor lapses and pricing returns to market. The floor never extends because of the rehire: its horizon is the *original* contract's end. *(b) Release → rehire → release again.* The second release charges `weekly × min(remaining, 26)` on the **new** contract's weekly (≥ the floored annual ÷ 52) and writes a second termination receipt; the floor becomes the maximum over both unexpired memories, each until its own contract's original end. Two round trips cost two charges and two bonuses for no rate gain. *(c) Rival employment between two player employments.* The floor is keyed by (releasing `StudioId`, `PersonId`), not by whether the person is currently free: a rival signs the released person at the market ask (its entry has no floor for that pair); when that rival contract expires before the original end week, the player's re-sign is still floored; the rival's own later re-sign of its own released people is floored symmetrically by its own receipts. *(d) Save/load and Save As.* Both receipts and rows are in-state, so the floor survives save/load without a cache; a Save As copy carries the same receipts and diverges afterwards; an inactive record never advances. Releases before `hollywood.originWeek` have no interval row and no receipt, so no floor: NOT RECORDED, consistent with migration honesty. In P14B the same receipt also becomes a trust driver ("Terminated early by Studio X, 1931"), the successor to the original's Mood memory on rehire.

| Combinations | The floor alone closes every price motive; adding a cooldown removes only legitimate play; adding refusal drags in P14B. |

**Does a materially different gameplay alternative remain for the Owner?** No. The floor and the top-up produce the same round-trip economics; the cooldown and the refusal are strictly less permissive without closing the exploit. The guard is therefore IMPLEMENTATION RECOMMENDATION R1, not an Owner question.

### 4.1 What a promise is, in Project: Studio terms

A promise is a typed, versioned commitment from one studio (`StudioId`) to one person (`PersonId`), attached to a **proposal** — a market proposal (§2.1.4) or the P14C extension proposal — that names an objective predicate over authoritative facts the studio's own actions can satisfy, a qualifying window, and an outcome. It never rides an out-of-case immediate renewal, which has no proposal record and no acceptance step. It is never free text and never a salary term. It is evaluated only by events that can change its predicate and by its due week; it is never polled every week. The record is named `ProfessionalPromise` because `Promise` is already the accepted screenplay-promise type.

Every record carries: `promiseId` (P14-minted, in-state ordinal), `family`, `version`, `issuerStudioId`, `beneficiaryPersonId`, typed `predicate` parameters, `windowStartWeek`, `dueWeekExclusive`, the `feasibilityReceipt` (classification at offer time and at settlement freeze, inputs digest, rules version), bounded event-derived `progress`, `evidenceRefs` (production / film / assignment ids by reference), `outcome`, `outcomeWeek`, typed `outcomeCause`, and the `contractId` it rode in on.

### 4.3 The feasibility service

#### 4.3.2 The classification rule, by family

Let `X` be the promised count (1 for P4 and P5) and let the service enumerate the qualifying events it can schedule for this person inside the window under the inputs above, counting only paths the studio controls (commissioning, greenlighting, casting) and never a rival's action, a market outcome or an award. Each scheduled event carries an **expected first-take week** on the minimum lawful pipeline and a flag saying whether its path uses only projects and facilities that already exist. Two slack tests are used, because a count family and a singular family fail in different ways: a promise to begin filming three pictures needs spare events, while a promise about one named project cannot have a spare event by construction (its `N_max` is at most 1), so a spare-event buffer would make P5 never qualify.

- **Count families (P1, P2, P3).** `N_max` = the number of schedulable events. **REASONABLY ACHIEVABLE** iff `X ≤ N_max − buffer` (buffer hypothesis: one event, or 25% of `N_max` rounded up, whichever is larger) **and** the X earliest events all lie on existing-pipeline paths **and** the X-th event's expected first-take week leaves at least `slack` weeks before the due week (hypothesis: 8). **FRAGILE** iff `X ≤ N_max` but a condition above fails. **IMPOSSIBLE** iff `X > N_max`, the window lies outside the contract, or the retirement boundary or active promises already exhaust the seats.
- **Singular families (P4, P5).** There is one required event. **REASONABLY ACHIEVABLE** iff a qualifying path exists whose expected first-take week leaves at least `slack` weeks before the due week **and** the path uses only existing projects and facilities **and** the seat is not already committed (P5: the named project exists, is not produced, has not been greenlit with the seat filled by someone else, and the class-appropriate seat is open or not yet cast; P4: at least one genre-G project is already in the pipeline with an open seat). **FRAGILE** iff a path exists but the slack is short, or every path needs something not yet in the pipeline (a commission not yet made, a facility under construction, a seat currently held). **IMPOSSIBLE** iff no lawful path exists, the window lies outside the contract, (P5) the named project is produced or greenlit with the seat filled, or (P4) genre G is not producible in the window.

FRAGILE is not "offer it with a warning". FRAGILE candidates are shown as "not offerable: reason" with the exact bottleneck (capacity, time, an existing promise, a seat already filled) so the player can change the schedule and re-run the service. Under direction 6, pipeline-dependent FRAGILE (mere theoretical possibility) and IMPOSSIBLE are never offerable; the first slice offers **no** override for the buffer-zone case either (Q1 in §7.3 records that as the recommended first behavior, not as an approval).

#### 4.3.3 Worked examples under the accepted durations (NUMERICAL/CONTENT HYPOTHESIS), reproduced with the family tests

*Example A (count family P1).* A two-year renewal (104 weeks) for an actor at a founding-plant studio (two stages, two development-casting slots, two post slots). Pipeline today: one production in rehearsal on stage 7 (cast fixed at greenlight; the actor is not in it); one script ready (drama) with no production yet; one script drafting (due in 3 weeks). The actor can hold one seat at a time and a seat is reserved from greenlight through release, so each picture occupies the actor for at least 8 weeks; the qualifying event is the first take, the fifth week after greenlight on schedule. Schedulable events: the ready script greenlit now with the actor cast, taking the free stage 12 (first take ≈ +5, existing path); the drafting script (+3 draft, optional +1 casting, greenlight when the actor's first seat releases at ≈ +9, first take ≈ +14, existing path); then new commissions, each needing draft (≤ 6) + casting (1) + 5 weeks to the first take ≈ 12 weeks of pipeline, at most one seated at a time, so ≈ one event per 8–12 weeks over the remaining ≈ 85 weeks ≈ 7 more (commission paths). `N_max ≈ 9`; buffer = max(1, ⌈0.25 × 9⌉) = 3 → count test `X ≤ 6`; existing-path test: only the first two events are existing-path, so **X ≤ 2 is REASONABLY ACHIEVABLE** (slack: the second event at ≈ +14 leaves 90 weeks); **X = 3–9 is FRAGILE** (the third event needs a commission not yet made, even though the count test passes up to 6); **X ≥ 10 is IMPOSSIBLE**. Revision 2's "X ≤ 6" was the count test alone; the existing-path condition is what makes the offer conservative.

*Example B (count family P2).* Same actor; an active promise to another actor reserves the lead in the ready script, and the actor's own active P2 promise consumes the drafting script's lead. A new P2 (lead) with X = 2 inside 104 weeks: no existing-path events remain, every path needs a commission → **FRAGILE**. X = 1 with a 78-week window: the earliest event is a commission path → **FRAGILE** (it would pass the slack test — 78 − 12 = 66 ≥ 8 — but not the existing-path test); the honest presentation is "not offerable: needs a picture not yet commissioned".

*Example C (singular family P5).* The lead in an existing script in casting whose production is not yet greenlit and whose first take is expected in about six weeks; the person's contract starts next week; the window is the contract's first year. Singular test: a qualifying path exists (existing project), the lead seat is open, expected first take ≈ +6 leaves ≈ 46 weeks of slack ≥ 8 → **REASONABLY ACHIEVABLE** (no spare-event buffer applies to a singular family). If the project has been greenlit with the lead seat filled → **IMPOSSIBLE** (there is no recast verb, and cancelling and re-greenlighting is a studio action the service must not assume). If the project is produced → IMPOSSIBLE. If the project is still an uncommissioned concept → FRAGILE.

*Example D (count family P3, discipline law).* P3 with X = 1 for a person whose primary profession is Actor but whose directing discipline is capable under the accepted `careerIdentity`: the service counts director seats on the greenlight law (has-discipline), not on the profession label; with one existing ready script whose director seat is open and an expected first take at ≈ +5, X = 1 is **REASONABLY ACHIEVABLE** (count test: `N_max` counts every schedulable director seat over the term — here the two-year term admits several sequential director seats, so `N_max ≥ 2` and buffer = 1 leaves `X ≤ N_max − 1 ≥ 1`; existing path; slack). Had the term admitted only one director seat (`N_max = 1`), the count test would classify X = 1 as FRAGILE, exactly as the buffer intends. The primary-profession label affects only the accepted casting read-model pools and never excludes an otherwise legally qualified director here. Under P14C a completed Actor → Director transition changes the label; it does not change this eligibility.

*Example E (rival symmetry).* A rival runs one production at a time while drafting up to two scripts in parallel; its schedulable events over 104 weeks are bounded by ≈ 104 ÷ 9 ≈ 11 (eight ticks plus the skipped greenlight week) and by its policy; the same family tests classify its promises, with its two active scripts as the existing-path events.

### 4.5 Trust: professional memory, not a meter

Trust is a bounded, public, evidence-backed summary derived from material conduct events between one person and one studio, plus a studio-level aggregate every person in the market can see. Its inputs are exactly: promise outcomes (the studio's own and, for rivals, theirs); early termination by the studio (a P14A event); contracts run to `endWeekExclusive` without early termination; cancellation of a production the person was seated on after its first take. Nothing else feeds it. Each input is a typed driver with a weight and a recency horizon (hypothesis: drivers older than 260 weeks leave the current tier but stay in history).

Public descriptors (hypothesis): `Reliable`, `Mixed record`, `Distrusted`, with the top three drivers listed beside the label ("Kept 2 promises · Terminated early 1931 · Broke 1 promise 1933"). Consequences: the reservation predicate and the ranked descriptor in the P14A person-choice rule; refusal to accept a promise or a waiver from a Distrusted studio; the P14B trust driver that accompanies the firing guard. Trust is per (person, studio) with a studio-level public aggregate; it is never a hidden multiplier on money, quality or luck, and it lives in the P14 root, never on `Talent`. **Starting state at P14B migration:** termination and run-to-end drivers are derived from the accepted employment ledger from the P14A recording boundary onward (recorded facts, not reconstruction); receipts earlier than that boundary fall under Q3's labeled reconstruction question.

#### 5.4a Romance as a separate track (IMPLEMENTATION RECOMMENDATION, not an inferred Owner ruling)

Romance is **not** the automatic next rung above a close friendship. Deep platonic bonds are preserved as their own end state: Inseparable is a complete relationship with the strongest professional chemistry, and most Inseparable pairs stay platonic for a whole career. Romance runs on a second, separately evaluated track so that friendship depth never converts into romance by accumulation.

- **Eligibility (regardless of gender, direction 13).** Both people are adult professionals (every person in the accepted population is 18+); neither is currently a Partner of anyone; both are on the same friendship ladder at Friends or above (a working bond exists) — and nothing else about either person enters the test.
- **Development.** Romance interest is its own bounded value per eligible pair, fed by typed drivers that are not friendship drivers: repeated close collaboration in proximity seats (director–lead, co-leads) across more than one production, shared success, and, if Q2 is adopted, the public compatibility descriptor; it decays on its own horizon. Formation is deterministic: when the romance value crosses its authored threshold for an eligible pair, the Partners bond forms and a dated event is recorded with its drivers ("Partners since 1936 · three pictures together · two successes"). No RNG; no proximity-on-the-lot input; no social command.
- **Coexistence.** Partners is recorded beside the friendship tier, not instead of it: "Partners · Close Friends" and "Partners · Strained" are both legal states, and a breakup ends the romance bond (a recorded event, with a negative driver on the friendship track only when a conflict caused it) without erasing the friendship history.
- **Consequences.** Partners carries Inseparable's chemistry consequence and the shared-studio preference in the person-choice rule (D5), and nothing more in P14B; marriage, households and family stay outside P14 (direction 13).

This is labeled a recommendation because the Owner's direction selects romance and gender-neutral eligibility and excludes family systems; it does not select the two-track shape, which the package proposes so that "close/best-friend-style bonds" and "genuine romance" remain distinct fantasies as the original kept them.


**Compatibility prior.** The original used hidden personality to speed or slow growth. Project: Studio may use the accepted public `temperament` descriptor (derived from perceived persona) to scale driver gains by a bounded factor, disclosed in the drivers list ("Compatible temperaments"). If the Owner prefers no prior, every pair grows at the base rate. Recorded as a genuine product question (§7.3 Q2) because it changes whether any two people can become Inseparable through work alone.

## 6. Career lifecycle model (P14C) — deliverable G

**Direction it realizes:** rulings §3.4.1 items 9, 10, 11 and 14. **Class:** IMPLEMENTATION RECOMMENDATION; every age, horizon and count is a NUMERICAL/CONTENT HYPOTHESIS.

### 6.1 Aging

- **Age is derived from provenance and materialized, never incremented independently.** Each person carries birth provenance in the P14 root: `authored_exact_week` for cohort entrants and newly authored people; `legacy_age_anchor { ageAtMigration, migrationWeek }` for everyone who exists in a pre-P14C save with only the stored `age` (a continuous float for worldgen people, an integer for authored ones; the UI floors it today). At each person's birthday due week (an in-state bucket, not a scan) P14C computes the integer age from provenance and `market.tick` and **writes it into the accepted `Talent.age` leaf** — a value write inside the frozen exact-key shape, which `v8Number` accepts — and the validator cross-checks `Talent.age` against provenance. Consequence: every accepted reader of `talent.age` (`ageFactor` in the contract ask, `ageRunwayMult` in development, the profile formatter, and any P13 reader) ages for free, with no P10 reader change; the Owner's market-demand effect occurs through the accepted ask curve; and `ageRunwayMult` slows growth as it does today and never lowers a skill, ceiling or experience, so direction 9 holds.
- **Every persistent named professional ages:** actors, directors, writers and craft leads alike, rival employees under the same law, and any profession P13 adds (POST-P13 REFRESH REQUIRED). The two accepted P12 mint sites (`enterRival`, `staff()` deficit supply) route through the provenance-writing mint primitive of §6.5, and the P14 validator requires exactly one provenance record per `state.talent` id.
- **Apparent age is a seam, not a system.** `apparentAge` = actual age unless a later, separately owned system records a dated adjustment. P14C defines the field and its provenance; it never builds cosmetic surgery (the original's Nip & Tuck is corpus evidence, not P14 scope).
- **What age may affect (direction 9).** For performers, an apparent-age fit descriptor per genre that casting and the production result may consume by reference (SHARED GENERALIZATION, bounded, disclosed). The original's developer-reviewed ideal apparent ages (Action 30, Comedy 40, Horror 37, Romance 25, Sci-Fi 28; MEDIUM confidence, single-sourced) are the parity anchor; Project: Studio's genres are comedy, drama, crime, romance, horror and adventure, so the descriptor is authored per accepted genre with the original as guidance, not copied. Market demand and value flow through the accepted contract ask (`ageFactor`, a bell centered at 34 with a 0.85 floor), which is P10's to retune per profession. Retirement eligibility (§6.2). Directors' and writers' fit descriptors are flat by default: the Owner-supplied review states Directors were much less affected, and the corpus neither corroborates nor contradicts it, so this is direction rather than parity.
- **What age never does:** decrement a skill, a genre experience, a ceiling, a development rate or Star Power. The accepted development law only raises actuals toward ceilings; P14C adds no decay path.

### 6.2 Retirement

**Eligibility.** Profession-specific eligibility windows (hypothesis, anchored to the original's Star retirement at 70 and market-value peak at 55: performers eligible from 60 with a hard boundary at 70; directors and writers from 65 with a hard boundary at 75; craft 62/72). The window opens a visible planning fact on the profile; it forces nothing.

**Intent and announcement.** A deterministic, versioned intent rule evaluates only at age-boundary due weeks and considers age within the window, tenure, recent work (last qualifying event) and the hard boundary. When it fires, the person **announces** retirement with an effective week = max(announcement week + the announcement horizon, the end of the contract in force at announcement) (hypothesis: horizon 52 weeks). Announcement moves the person to the `retirement_announced` eligibility state (§2.1.2): any open market case is invalidated with a typed reason; no new case opens; **no proposal from any issuer, and no ordinary renewal, may carry `endWeekExclusive` past the effective week** (a P10 rule for `renewContract` and drafts, and the same cap in `staff()`'s renewal loop, P12), so the accepted immediate-replacement renewal cannot push retirement out by years and the chain the Owner forbids cannot form. Announcement is a public event on the profile, the roster, the calendar's commitments and the Talent Market's attention list.

**Obligations first, and the finishing-commitments state.** Between announcement and the effective week the person completes every binding obligation: seats on active productions (the accepted busy set reserves a seat from greenlight through release), active writing assignments, and the contract in force at announcement. New assignments whose on-schedule release (greenlight + 8 + 1 weeks) falls after the effective week are refused with a typed reason (the greenlight legality owner, using the same minimum-pipeline floor the feasibility service uses). If a production seat is still uncleared when the effective week arrives (a player picture held at the take gate or at an uncommitted release), the person enters the explicit **`finishing_commitments`** state instead of retiring or having retirement forced: the contract ends at its boundary as capped; the person remains seated and performs on that picture exactly as the accepted "obligation outlives the employment" law already allows a contract to expire mid-picture; **no new assignment, contract, proposal or case may attach** (the eligibility predicate of this section refuses them, so nothing can extend the state indefinitely except the player's own decision to keep that one picture unreleased, which harms nobody but that picture); and the profession retirement is **not finalized** — no `retiredFromProfession` event, no transition evaluation — until the last such production releases or is cancelled. Revision 2's eight-week forced settlement is withdrawn: it would have finalized a retirement while the person still performed the profession, which the selected obligations-first experience forbids.

**The one final extension.** In the last 12 weeks before the effective week (hypothesis: the accepted renewal window's width) the current employer, player or rival, receives **exactly one** opportunity to offer a **one-year extension at newly proposed compensation**: a P14A proposal draft (the §2.1.3 generalization: `termWeeks = 52`, `startWeek` = the effective week, the employer's chosen premium tier on the accepted ask curve — the package invents no other compensation), submitted through the proposal path as a one-issuer case variant (visible as discovered; proposable by exactly one `StudioId`; no competing studios). **The extension is the single exception to the term cap:** the cap forbids any draft ending past the effective week, and the extension is the one draft allowed to end at effective week + 52 exactly, no longer and no shorter. Its **start boundary** is the effective week itself: the contract in force ends there and the extension begins there, with no gap and no overlap, and no signing bonus is due until settlement. The person accepts iff the proposal clears the reservation of §2.1.7 with the retirement factor, with descriptor reasons (compensation band, trust, relationships at the studio). Acceptance moves the effective week to effective week + 52 and records `extensionUsed = true`; **one use**: after acceptance the term cap applies again to the new effective week with no further exception, so a chain cannot form. Declining, or no offer, leaves the effective week in place. **Overrun** during the extended year follows the obligations-first rule above: work already committed is not waived, no new work that cannot release before the new effective week may attach, and an uncleared seat at the new effective week enters `finishing_commitments` exactly as before. A free agent in the window has no employer and no offer. Rivals take the same decision under their policy at their cadence, with the same one-extension limit.

**Settlement.** At the effective week (or, from `finishing_commitments`, at the week the last uncleared production releases or is cancelled) one scheduled P14C candidate commits, all or none: P10 contract end and assignment clearance receipt; P11 obligation-or-none (no severance, no payment: the contract ended at its capped boundary or was extended to it); P12 roster / employer / exclusivity / interval close with its receipt (already closed at the capped boundary when the person was finishing); P14 `retiredFromProfession` event with alumni status. An uncleared guard the state machine does not model records a typed deferral and the next check; nothing partial is written.

**What retirement preserves:** `PersonId`, profile, films and credits, awards and honors, employer intervals, relationships and history, promise outcomes, career chronology. The person leaves active staffing and the market for that profession through a **P10-owned lifecycle eligibility predicate** that `signableUniverse`, `hiringMarketIds`, `freelancerMarketIds` and greenlight assignability consult and that P12's `staff()` and `enterRival` consult too (the accepted markets have no retirement exclusion today; the P14 root is the source of truth and the predicate reads it by reference); the body leaves the lot; the profile gains an alumni summary; every cross-view link still resolves; `state.talent` is never shortened (it is append-only and order-load-bearing).

### 6.3 Profession retirement versus industry retirement

Retirement is recorded **per profession**. A person retired from acting is an alumnus of acting; whether they are retired from the industry is a separate, later fact.

**Transition evaluation.** When a profession retirement commits, and again at bounded later due weeks while the person is below the industry retirement boundary (hypothesis: the profession's hard boundary plus 5 years), the deterministic transition rule evaluates the narrow catalogue:

| Transition | Eligibility inputs (all by reference; P10-owned where marked) | Disqualifiers |
|---|---|---|
| **Actor → Director** | credited acting depth (count of first-take events, lead count); the accepted `careerIdentity` for the directing discipline (P10: capable when perceived directing OVR ≥ the accepted capability minimum; proven when directing work history > 0); the public expected-potential band for directing (P10); recorded collaboration with directors (P14B evidence: mentorship labels, repeated director–lead work); age below the director hard boundary | not capable in directing; industry-retired; announced industry retirement |
| **Actor → Writer** | credited acting depth; `careerIdentity` and potential band for the writing discipline (P10); recorded collaboration with writers; age below the writer hard boundary | as above, for writing |

The accepted runtime already gives every person all four disciplines' skills, already allows cross-discipline assignment at greenlight, and already distinguishes proven from capable-but-unproven per discipline; there is no hidden "development focus" to consume, and none is invented. The rule is versioned and the receipt records its inputs. It is not automatic at a birthday, and nothing outside the catalogue is evaluated. When it fires, the person **chooses** under the reservation of §2.1.7 (they may decline and remain an alumnus); a chosen transition commits as: a P10 profession-change receipt rewriting `Talent.role` (P10 owns profession; no such action exists today and it is a P10 generalization); the P14C `careerTransition` event; free-agent eligibility in the new profession under the accepted instant-signing law (the person enters `state.freeAgents`); relationships and history untouched. **Why the role rewrite matters for each transition:** the accepted casting pools gate director / actor / craft seats on the primary role, so Actor → Director needs the rewrite to enter the director pool; the accepted script read model's writer list is has-discipline over contracted people, so an actor is already commissionable as a writer today — Actor → Writer still rewrites the role for profile, credit and rival-staffing truth (rival deficit filling and the candidate generator read the primary role), not for eligibility. The intended fantasy holds by construction: the legendary actor's credits, relationships, awards and former employers are the same career the new director carries.

**Industry retirement** is the final state: no active profession, no further transition evaluation, alumni for every profession held. It is reached by the hard boundary of the last profession, by declining every transition, or by exhausting the catalogue. The person still exists and is addressable.

### 6.5 Deterministic replenishment across the century

Unchanged from the August design in shape (P14 schedules bounded era-aware cohort requests; one-to-one receipts; hard maximum 32 per request; no emergency generation), with three refreshed facts: the August "P10 identity allocator / registration receipt" does not exist, so cohorts mint through **one exported primitive owned by P10/worldgen** that reuses the accepted isolated-seed idiom (`generateIndustryTalent`) with `uniqueIdentity` re-homed under it from P12, writes birth provenance, and appends to `state.talent`, with the cohort receipt in the P14 root; P12's two mint sites call the same primitive; cohorts author era-appropriate ages and birth provenance (today's mint draws age from N(38, 10) regardless of year); cohorts are keyed to the delivered P12 calendar and take their era context from P13 after acceptance (POST-P13 REFRESH REQUIRED). Appending to `state.talent` changes later hiring-market samples deterministically; that is accepted behavior, not a defect. Retirement never empties the market: the cohort schedule is sized against the accepted population and the retirement law, and its counts are tuning.

### 6.6 Symmetry, isolation and old saves

- The same aging, retirement, extension and transition law applies to rival employees; rivals decide extensions under their policy at their cadence, with the same one-extension limit and the same term cap.
- All lifecycle state lives inside `GameState`; every derived index is keyed by the identity of the immutable in-state object it is derived from; nothing is keyed by `PersonId`, seed or `worldId`. This matters beyond Save As: worldgen and authored `PersonId`s (`t-act-00`, `authored-0001`) are identical across **every** campaign regardless of seed, and up to 32 hydrated campaigns coexist in one process at library load; Save As additionally duplicates the world-keyed ids, the seed, `worldId`, `rngState` and every counter.
- Old saves: no person starts retired because an inferred boundary already passed; migration anchors `legacy_age_anchor`, opens the lifecycle recording boundary, and applies an explicit grandfathering rule (hypothesis: anyone already past a hard boundary at migration becomes eligible with the full announcement horizon from the migration week). No past retirement, extension, transition or phase event is invented, and the validator rejects any lifecycle row dated before the recording boundary and any anchor whose `migrationWeek` differs from it.
