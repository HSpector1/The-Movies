# Independent review 1344-B

**Verdict: REFINE**

The charter faithfully implements every clause of D-1329-1 and is exceptionally well cited — I independently verified essentially every file:line reference in §1-§4 against the code at HEAD 6b73e424 and found the underlying source facts accurate, including one non-trivial structural claim ("that throw is unreachable today") that required a multi-file trace to confirm. The core design (staffingBlocked/cashBlocked/economicRejection classification, shelve/retry/hold mechanics, promise guard, save precedent) is sound and correctly grounded in the actual `decide`/`chooseIndustryPackage`/`hollywoodValidation` code, not invented. However, three items are specific enough and consequential enough to the eventual RED staging (1344-C) that they should be resolved in the charter or its immediate follow-up before RED is written, plus several non-blocking precision notes.

## Blocking defects

**1. §3.4's retry mechanism does not account for `hotDevelopment(b)`'s closed active-only view (hollywoodTick.ts:46-55, :210).**
`hotDevelopment(b)` is defined as `{mode:'managed',projects:b.activeScriptOrdinals.map(i=>b.development.projects[i]!)}` (hollywoodTick.ts:47) — it only ever exposes ordinals already in `activeScriptOrdinals`. The existing greenlight loop that §3.4 says "runs" for a viable retry iterates `hotDevelopment(b).projects.filter(p=>p.status==='ready')` (hollywoodTick.ts:210). By §3.2's own design, a shelved screenplay's ordinal has *left* `activeScriptOrdinals`, so it is structurally invisible to `hotDevelopment(b)` and cannot be reached by reusing that loop as written. `storeHotDevelopment` (hollywoodTick.ts:49-55) likewise only ever reconciles `activeScriptOrdinals` from the "hot" subset it was handed — it cannot discover an ordinal that was outside that subset. §3.4's phrase "the ordinal re-enters activeScriptOrdinals and the existing greenlight runs" undersells this: the retry path needs a genuinely separate read (`b.development.projects[shelvedEntry.ordinal]` directly) and an explicit write-back that appends the retried ordinal, not a reuse of the existing `hotDevelopment`/`storeHotDevelopment` plumbing unchanged.
**Required change:** state explicitly in the charter (or in 1344-M/1344-C) that retry reads the shelved list's ordinals directly against `b.development.projects`, not through `hotDevelopment`, and that re-entry into `activeScriptOrdinals` is an explicit append, not an emergent property of `storeHotDevelopment`.

**2. Test 3's HEAD-equality claim is ambiguous and only achievable under one reading (1344-A §6 item 3, lines 147-148).**
"On a run where no screenplay reaches the threshold, state equals HEAD's state with `screenplayShelving` stripped." Per §3.1, *any* economic rejection (count=1, long before the threshold of 13) appends/increments an entry in `screenplayShelving.rejections`. On a real "no screenplay reaches threshold but some economic rejections occur" run (the natural way to test "below threshold"), `screenplayShelving` will *not* be the empty `[],[],0` state — it will contain nonzero counters. The claim is achievable only if "stripped" means "the field is excluded from the diff," not "the field equals its empty default." The charter's own §6 item 1 scenario ("a viable package greenlights... and never counts") is the one case where the field would stay literally empty; the broader "run where no screenplay reaches the threshold" framing in the same bullet is not obviously the same scenario.
**Required change:** the charter or 1344-C should say explicitly which reading governs, since only "field excluded from comparison" is generally realizable, and an implementer given the ambiguous text could write an unachievable (or accidentally too-narrow, always-zero-rejections) fixture.

**3. Test 11's "no studio shelves more than two screenplays in any 52 weeks" is not a property §3.1-§3.5 guarantee (1344-A §5 lines 131-136, §6 item 11 lines 168-170).**
`HOLLYWOOD_SHELVE_COMMISSION_HOLD_WEEKS`=13, `HOLLYWOOD_SHELVE_AFTER_REJECTIONS`=13, and `SCRIPT_DRAFT_WEEKS_MIN`=1 (`src/core/tuning.ts:966`, confirmed reused for rival commissioning at `hollywoodTick.ts:273`, `scriptDraftWeeks(...)` at `src/core/screenplay.ts:373-394`). §3.5's own stated bound is "the hold, plus draft time, plus the threshold" = 13+1+13 = **27 weeks minimum** per re-shelve cycle. A studio has two independent slots (`activeScriptOrdinals.length<=2`, `hollywoodValidation.ts:338`); after a hold ends, `decide` can commission a new screenplay in each of two consecutive weeks (the commission block at `hollywoodTick.ts:253-286` runs once per week, gated only by `activeScriptOrdinals.length<2`, not by how many were just commissioned). With minimum draft weeks, two staggered 27-week cycles can both complete comfortably inside a 52-week window, which arithmetically permits more than two shelvings for one studio in 52 weeks — nothing in §3.1-§3.5 structurally forbids it (cash depletion over time may prevent it in practice on the measured seed by converting later rejections to `cashBlocked`, but that is an empirical property of the one seed, not a law guarantee).
**Required change:** either derive and cite the actual worst-case bound from the tuning constants and justify why the seed's cash/staffing trajectory keeps it under two, or relabel this line as an outcome to be measured and calibrated after GREEN rather than asserted a priori. As written it risks being a test "pinned to an outcome the law does not guarantee" — the specific failure mode the review was asked to check for.

## Non-blocking notes

- **Facility blockage "unreachable today" — independently verified true, not just repeated.** I traced `scriptOccupiedFacilitySlots`/`screenplayOccupiedSlotKeys` (`src/core/occupancy.ts:667-683,444-453`) and confirmed a `ready` screenplay holds no reservation (only `drafting`/`rewriting` do, per the comment at `occupancy.ts:27-29` and `reservation:null` at `scriptDevelopment.ts:495`). Given the ≤2 active-ordinal bound and that a greenlight is only attempted from an already-`ready` (slot-free) project, the maximum simultaneous claims on the capacity-2 `development-casting` facility (`hollywood.ts:147`) is exactly 2 (one drafting screenplay + one just-greenlit production's Development-phase reservation), never 3. `addManagedProductionWorkflow`'s `QueueableCapacityRefusal` (`operations.ts:627-637`) is genuinely unreachable under the current law. Good, well-supported claim.

- **§3.6's `impossiblePath(..., 'the named script project is shelved')` — acceptable, structurally does not break existing promises.** Traced `opportunityAssessment` (`opportunityPromises.ts:259-278`) and `opportunityPhysicalImpossibility`'s only caller `targetSpecificImpossibility` (`promises.ts:896-903`), whose only caller is `breakPromisesOnCancel` (`promises.ts:1102-1132`) — gated on an actual production *cancellation*, structurally independent of shelving (a shelved screenplay was never greenlit). `paths()`/`opportunityAssessment` is otherwise used only at promise-authoring and substitution time (`promises.ts:586,1322,1366`), re-computed fresh each call, never cached. So the "impossible" label does not durably mark the screenplay and does not propagate into an existing open promise's outcome; it only affects whether a *new* promise or renegotiation is offered against this specific candidate this week. The reason string itself ("is shelved," present tense) does not claim permanence. This is acceptable as designed; no change required.

- **§3.3's promise guard is narrower than "existing obligations" in general — worth a one-line justification, not a required change.** The guard only defers shelving for an open `SPECIFIC_PROJECT` promise naming `projectOpportunity.scriptProjectId` (mirroring `hollywoodTick.ts:187`'s beneficiary-person guard pattern). A `genreOpportunity` promise that happens to rely on this screenplay as its only current path is not covered, and test 6 (§6 item 6) only exercises the `SPECIFIC_PROJECT` case. On inspection this is defensible — genre promises were never tied to a specific project, so shelving one candidate is the same category of event as any other status change already affecting `paths()`, and (per the point above) it cannot silently BREAK the promise; it can only affect authoring/substitution. Recommend the charter say this explicitly rather than leave it implicit, but it is not a defect.

- **Citation drift (three minor cases), all confirmed by direct read, none material:**
  - `promises.ts:406-407` cited for `unproducedScripts`' "digest." Lines 406-407 are person-skill/production-list digest entries; the actual script-project list entry is at `promises.ts:410-411` (`development?.projects.filter((p) => p.status !== 'produced' ...)`). Off by ~4 lines.
  - `bridge/industry.ts "about :136"` cited for "drops unlisted kinds." The actual catch-all `return []` for unmatched receipt kinds is at `bridge/industry.ts:133`; line 136 is a comment in an unrelated block about promise-outcome publishing. Off by 3 lines and a different topic.
  - `save.ts:10528` cited for "up-conversion adds a zero movement." Line 10528 is `const validated = validateSaveV40(save);`; the actual zero-movement assignment is `save.ts:10531` (`period.movements.termination = 0`). The paired `:10537-10551` citation for the down-conversion refusal is accurate.

- **Tuning rationale for `HOLLYWOOD_SHELVE_AFTER_REJECTIONS`=13 cites evidence that doesn't quite support "long enough."** §5 says 13 weeks is "Long enough for a staffing change to make a package viable; 75d70e18's restart came from such a change," but 1329-A's own measurement (`1329-A-c8-natural-chain-finding.md` §5) shows that restart took from week ~110 to week 208 — roughly 98 weeks, not 13. The rationale as stated somewhat overclaims what its own cited precedent shows. This is tempered by §5's own "hypothesis under the Owner's delegation" framing and the planned §7 natural-route verification, so it is not blocking, but the stated justification should either cite better support or be honest that 13 is chosen for chart-cadence alignment rather than because it matches the one measured staffing-driven recovery.

- **Reader-map completeness: two genuine `activeScriptOrdinals` consumers absent from §2/§3.6's enumeration, but independently confirmed benign.** `industryBusyTalentIds` (`hollywood.ts:92-104`) and `checkForeignRelevance`/cost-accounting (`promiseCapacityOwnerReplay.ts:813-839,2231-2266`) both iterate `business.activeScriptOrdinals` but gate their substantive effect on `status==='drafting'||'rewriting'` — a shelved screenplay is always `status==='ready'` throughout its shelved life, so removing its ordinal from `activeScriptOrdinals` does not change either function's output. Not a correctness gap, but the charter's stated "read-only reader map of src, bridge and ui/src" omitted these; recommend the production/verification step explicitly confirm they're unaffected rather than leave that implicit.

- **`hollywoodValidation.ts:225`'s `exact(b,[...])` closed field list will need `screenplayShelving` appended** or a Save43 business object will fail validation immediately (a self-catching, fail-loud omission risk, not silent).
