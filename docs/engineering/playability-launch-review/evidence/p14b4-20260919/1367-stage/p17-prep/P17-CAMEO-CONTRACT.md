## 3. Fame, performance, credits and the zero-cameo invariant

Three principals remain required. At most two **distinct** guests may be committed for a production. A guest cannot duplicate any credited writer/director/principal/craft person on that same film: no paying for a second label on the same contribution. Zero guests executes the existing numerical paths and produces no extra money/work/history rows; verify bit identity apart from the necessary new empty save roots/version.

**Publicity:** for each segment, rank principals plus guests by the fame available at that calculation boundary descending; ties use principal seat order, then committed guest order. Forecast locks the greenlight fame inputs; actual release reads contemporaneous fame through the same authoritative person source as its principals, then preserves the release result. Define the canonical pool `P(segment) = sum(f/(f+50) * 0.5^rank * segmentAffinity)`, where rank starts0. Compute it twice: all committed guests plus principals, and principals alone. Define `delta = (P_all - P_principals)/2`. The divisor is provisional normalization of the canonical pool's<2 bound, not a second rank-decay constant.

Use only this delta to modify the existing opening-star term: `newOpeningStar(segment) = clamp(oldOpeningStar + 100*delta, 0, 100)`. Replace that term once inside opening appeal. Keep the old linear `starDraw` unchanged. A high-fame off-segment guest can displace better-fit names in the canonical ordering and yield a negative delta; show that result honestly rather than silently clamping it to a bonus. Inserting a zero-fame last-ranked guest has zero publicity delta. The subtraction prevents existing principal fame being credited twice and preserves the no-guest result exactly.

For existing `starAttention`, add the segment-share-weighted same delta to its existing principal-only value and clamp once to[0,1]. Consume that result once through the ordinary awareness formula; do not append another Standing award. No cameo fame term changes legs, delivered expression, quality, franchise R/M/F, shared-market demand, or P16 valuation directly. Fees affect finance through their actual cost only.

**Performance:** add each completed guest's ordinary `castSlotExecution` at weight0.10 to the existing cast-execution numerator and denominator. For this bounded shape, evaluate its persona fit against the screenplay's existing support requirement using the support-fit primitive; this is a **new explicit cameo fit convention**, not a fourth principal slot or a claimed existing role requirement. Forecast uses perceived acting inputs; actual performance freezes the guest's actual-skill/persona performance inputs when their one week of work completes, so later development cannot rewrite that completed appearance. It uses the ordinary actual-skill calculation, not a fame or profession quality bonus. Guests never enter the cohesion centroid/`ROLE_WEIGHT`. Poor acting can therefore slightly reduce craft. Do not modify the weights of the three principals.

**Credits:** a completed one-week appearance earns one permanent `cameo` acting credit when that production releases. Add its actual work completion fact before release. Canceling an **unperformed booking** earns no acting work or release credit; canceling a film **after completed work** preserves that factual work and its earned fee, but an unreleased film grants no release credit or release-based career/fame award. Use the existing career-event pipeline once with a new provisional cameo visibility weight0.10, not a second fame award. Reconcile acted-work counters with that one completion/release path; do not increment once at booking and again at release. A cameo never fulfills a named principal/billing promise, becomes a major franchise association, or changes the person's primary profession. No separate finite “contract production credits” balance exists in inspected source; “consumes a production credit” means actual recorded work, not an invented contract quota.

Lock the new cameo contribution inputs alongside the ordinary greenlight forecast and retain that forecast permanently. Actual reception consumes only completed appearances. If a planned appearance is canceled or cannot be delivered, do not rewrite the original forecast or grant its publicity/performance/credit: its absence is an honest forecast-versus-delivery difference. Refresh the ordinary principal live/frozen fame source at the actual predecessor without changing its existing boundary; do not let later personal fame rewrite old booking fees or completed-work facts.

## 4. Booking, one-week work and exact fee law

### Quote and commitment

Booking is optional greenlight input, not an audition or extra casting-slate seat. Each entry names a real person and **one exact future work week**, chosen within the next nominal production span: `greenlightWeek < workWeek <= greenlightWeek + TUNING.PRODUCTION_TICKS`. This is a scheduling contract, not a promise that the production allocator will be ready on that date. A quote must state that readiness risk. No booking spans multiple weeks, and no automatic rescheduling extends the obligation.

Eligibility applies the same studio-aware employment/availability law for player and rivals: own lawful employee whose contract covers the booked week, or an actually available freelancer, never another studio's exclusive employee. The booking itself creates the freelancer's exact one-week obligation; subsequent employment cannot erase it. Announced retirement permits only work completing by its effective boundary; finishing/retired people take no new appearance. Check both the current profession and any recorded actor-retirement episode, so an actor who retired into writing cannot silently take a new acting job. The existing `assignmentRefusal` assumes a full `PRODUCTION_TICKS+1` company seat: use the same lifecycle authority with an explicit one-week obligation end, not that duration hard-coded for a cameo and not a parallel retirement rule. Existing production/writing/research commitments and accepted bookings for that week take precedence. The person may have other non-overlapping bookings, but no overlapping actual work. A proposed long production or research assignment must not silently consume a previously reserved guest week; use the same common availability/obligation resolver. Do not add guests to `productionCompanyTalentIds` for the whole film.

**Employment exit distinction:** stamp whether the booking was accepted as an own-employee appearance or as an independent freelance appearance. If an own employee's employment is lawfully terminated or transferred to another employer before this work completes, automatically cancel the unperformed booking with the standard25% retained charge/remainder refund, **in addition to** ordinary employment exit/claim accounting. Do not force them to work for their former employer or assume consent to a new freelance engagement. A new freelance appearance requires a fresh eligible quote/acceptance. By contrast, a booking independently accepted while freelance survives later employment as its already-consented one-week obligation; disclose it to the new employer and preserve it in availability/contract admission. Later employment does not silently seize that reserved week. Neither rule overrides the person's existing choice rights, lawful termination costs or P15/P16 estate disposition authority. Already completed work and earned appearance fees survive either kind of employment change.

The work is one actual week **inside the film's allocated shooting phase**, using that film's real stage/workflow capacity. `workWeek` names the interval `[workWeek, workWeek+1)`; completion is at its right boundary and must be no later than the effective retirement week. It needs no audition, casting-facility reservation or rehearsal of its own. Merely reaching the calendar week does not earn work. If the film cannot perform a shooting work unit that week, the guest has not performed; the booking is canceled under the rule below. A film cannot claim a completed appearance on a stalled/unallocated workflow. There is no new free filming unit or duplicate facility reservation.

At greenlight, reserve the guest's named week and commit the quote atomically with the main production. An unaccepted quote changes no state. The quote is stale if person/fame/acting quote inputs, employment, booking occupancy, retirement, week, studio money or the production/capability fact changed; refresh or refuse before any mutation. On refusal, preserve the existing campaign, every booking, the whole main production and all money.

### Provisional price

Use the existing salary-curve coefficients but the acting ability relevant to this one-week job:

`fee = round(0.35 * (SALARY_BASE + SALARY_SKILL_COEF*(perceivedActingOVR/100)^2 + SALARY_FAME_COEF*(fame/100)^2))`.

One final `Math.round`; no age/scarcity/term factor, Craft Services Annex discount, franchise/property premium or minimum-film-count discount. This is **a newly authored cameo fee**, not a call to existing `salaryCurve`, whose Scientist exception and primary-discipline pricing would conflict with this purpose. The proposed0.35 fraction follows the canonical starting point. At current coefficients, acting20/fame85 prices at162,575, before any future coefficient retune. Quote and receipt freeze the evaluated fee and rule version. It is strictly positive, including zero-fame people.

Charge the full fee once at booking as a direct production cost. Own employees receive this separate appearance fee while ordinary payroll remains unchanged; existing payroll is not a hidden free-cameo entitlement. Do not reduce their contract guarantee, shorten employment, subtract the fee from payroll, or create a franchise salary premium. For rivals, write the same economic movement to their own account/period; for the player, use the shared canonical ledger owner. Include it once in quote affordability/reserve, production committed cost, forecast ROI and eventual financial reconciliation.

### Cancellation and settlement

New provisional split:25% of the quoted fee is the nonrefundable reservation/cancellation charge, rounded once as `round(fee*0.25)`; the remaining exact `fee-retained` is refundable until completed work. The original full charge stays in history. A cancellation writes one linked refund, never deletes the charge or pays a second fee.

- Before completion, explicit cancellation, production cancellation, rights loss preventing the production, or the booked shooting week passing without actual guest work all close the booking once and refund the refundable remainder. Failed delivery retains only the declared reservation charge, with no performance/publicity/acting credit.
- Successful scheduled work closes the reservation, earns the whole already-paid fee, and records actual week/person/production once. No new payment fires on completion or release. Later film cancellation or studio failure preserves completed work and its earned fee.
- No free replacement or silent date move. A new guest/date is a fresh quote and fee subject to the same remaining two-guest production capacity. A canceled unperformed entry frees its slot; a completed appearance permanently consumes it. Preserve the canceled entry and all fee/refund history.
- P15 closure/P16 estate settlement owns the corporate consequence. Invoke their actual accepted disposition/refund/claim machinery instead of inventing a second estate ledger or claiming an unpaid refund executed. No rights transfer erases bookings or completed work. P18 guest work stays outside this film-only contract until its own explicit consumer exists.
