## 3. Part A: the binding-cash test (`rival-shelving/v2`)

### 3.1 The rule today and why it freezes the count

- **The rule.** 1344-A :57 defines `cashBlocked` as "at least one candidate was skipped by the cash gate and no
  candidate was viable", and only `economicRejection` increments the count (1344-A :61-62). `hollywoodTick.ts:236`
  implements exactly that (1357-F3 :9-11). No line asks whether a skipped package would have been viable
  (1357-R :93-94).
- **The effect** on `p13a-core-causal-01`, from the decide rows at 469a9547 (1357-R §2, :70-152):
  - Four frozen screenplays had weeks with all 54 packages affordable and all 54 unviable. The label flipped to
    `cashBlocked` when the dearest rung left reach (1357-R :120-122).
  - The counts froze below 13: r01 at 3 and 0, r02 at 12 and 8, r03 at 4 and 0 (:123-125).
  - The full index then blocked every commission and retry after weeks 262, 234 and 163 (:126-128;
    `hollywoodTick.ts:286-287`, `:301`). r01, r02 and r03 stopped with 3.4-7.1M above their reserves (1357-R :13).
  - 1344-V flag 4 records the same stall (1344-V :484-492).
- **The finding.** The code follows the charter; the gap is in the chartered law (1357-F3 :25-27).

### 3.2 The amended rule

For one evaluation of a ready screenplay with a full seatable team (`hollywoodTick.ts:225`) and no affordable viable
candidate:
- **`cashBlocked`** when at least one unaffordable candidate would pass the viability gate. Cash is then the binding
  constraint: more cash would buy a viable package.
- **`economicRejection`** otherwise. No candidate in the set, affordable or not, passes the gate.

The viability gate does not change (`hollywoodPolicy.ts:69-73`), and it reads no cash (§2). The amended label therefore
asks one cash-free question of each skipped candidate.

**What changes in code.**
- `searchIndustryPackages` gains one count, `unaffordableViable`: the unaffordable candidates that pass the viability
  test, each forecast with the same inputs, seed and key as an affordable one.
- It is computed for locked-screenplay searches only, because the commission search applies no viability gate
  (`hollywoodPolicy.ts:73`).
- `choice`, `affordable`, `unaffordable` and `viable` keep their meanings. `chooseIndustryPackage` returns what it
  returns today, and the landed counts contract holds (`tests/p14d1-rival-shelving.test.ts:206-253`).
- `evaluate()` labels a refusal from `unaffordableViable > 0` in place of `unaffordable > 0` (`hollywoodTick.ts:236`).

Nothing else in the law moves. The count rule (`:267`), the promise deferral (`:270-275`), shelving (`:277-281`), the
hold, the retry and the readers stay as 1344-A §3.2-§3.6 wrote them (1344-A :67-104).

**Cost.** A refusal's re-search forecasts up to 54 candidates where it forecast the affordable ones. Each draw is keyed
(`forecast.ts:407`), so no stream moves. 1363-V reports tick time, report only.

### 3.3 What counts as "otherwise hopeless"

- **The test.** A screenplay is hopeless in an evaluation when no candidate in its full set passes the viability gate
  at its current inputs.
- **The set.** For a locked screenplay it holds one shape × six billings × three negative scales × three marketing
  rungs, 54 candidates (`hollywoodPolicy.ts:49-61`; `tuning.ts:39`). The landed test derives 54 independently
  (`tests/p14d1-rival-shelving.test.ts:228-242`).
- **The inputs** are the week's: the seated team, the studio's standing, the market and the screenplay's assessment
  (`hollywoodTick.ts:226-232`).
- **Hopeless is a fact about one evaluation.** A later one can differ as the team, standing or market change. That is
  why the law counts consecutive rejections and keeps retrying shelved screenplays, as D-1329-1 requires: "Do not
  claim the screenplay is impossible forever" (1340-O :36).

### 3.4 A single unaffordable package

- **Unaffordability alone never decides the label.** The law scores a skipped package exactly as it scores an
  affordable one.
- **If the package would be viable,** the screenplay is not hopeless and cash binds. The evaluation is `cashBlocked`,
  and the count holds.
- **If it would not,** it adds nothing. The evaluation is an `economicRejection`, and the count advances. This is the
  1357-R case: the dearest rung left reach while every package failed (1357-R :120-122).
- **The residual case.** A viable package out of reach, at a rival with no income, still holds the count, and two such
  screenplays hold the index.
  - Part B catches that rival: a full index of cash-blocked screenplays closes its commission path by cash (§4.1), so
    it stops paying an idle team.
  - Whether a long true cash block should also count toward shelving is question P7.
  - 1363-V reports every `cashBlocked` evaluation with its `unaffordableViable` count and how long each block lasts.

### 3.5 Retries, `retryWeek` and the shelved list

- **The label.** A retry runs the same `evaluate()` (`hollywoodTick.ts:290`), so the amended label applies.
  - A hopeless due retry is an economic rejection, and its `retryWeek` moves 26 weeks out (`:296-297`; `tuning.ts:35`).
  - A retry whose viable package is out of reach changes nothing and is tried again at the next decision (1344-A :87).
- **Flag 1, retries that never succeed** (1344-V :467-474).
  - Today a cash-blocked retry stays due. As the lowest due ordinal (`find`, `:287`) it is retried at every decision,
    and every later due entry waits behind it.
  - r01's retries at weeks 250 and 262 came back cash-blocked and changed nothing (1344-V :139-142).
  - Under v2 a hopeless retry advances, and the next due entry gets its turn.
- **Flag 2, shelved lists that only grow** (1344-V :475-477).
  - v2 turns some `cashBlocked` evaluations into counted rejections and never the reverse. A screenplay therefore
    shelves in the same week or earlier, more screenplays shelve, and the lists grow faster.
  - The law keeps every shelved screenplay and keeps retrying it. Ending retries or pruning lists would contradict
    D-1329-1 (1340-O :36), so it is Owner question O5.
- **Limits that stay.**
  - At most one retry per decision, and only with no production and a free slot (`:286-287`; 1344-A :80-82).
  - `HOLLYWOOD_SHELVED_RETRY_WEEKS` stays 26 unless a tuning amendment under D-1329-1 moves it (§6.6).
- **Flag 4, the post-300 stall** (1344-V :484-492). v2 removes its first cause, and Part B handles a rival whose cash
  truly binds.

## 4. Part B: the scoped rival cost-cutting route (`rival-cost-cutting/v1`)

**Why.** 1357-R found that no rival verb cuts fixed cost (1357-R :153-228; levers L5-L8, :352-355):
- a full team is never surplus;
- every re-hire needs cash above the reserve;
- nothing removes a facility;
- new commitments ignore a frozen pipeline.

It also found that cost levers buy time and cannot save a rival alone (:334-336).

**What Part B does.** It gives a rival whose income has stopped, and cannot restart, a way to stop paying for staff it
cannot use and to stop taking on new commitments. It uses the existing termination law and adds no money kind.

### 4.1 Entry: income has stopped and cannot restart

At the end of `decide()` (`hollywoodTick.ts:201-336`) in week W, a rival not already cutting costs starts cutting costs
(`costCutting.since = W`) when all three conditions hold:
1. **No income in prospect.** It has no production and no theatrical run. Its runs are its only inflow
   (`hollywoodTick.ts:414-417`; `hollywoodValidation.ts:295`).
2. **Nothing started or in development.** This decision greenlit nothing and commissioned nothing, and no screenplay is
   drafting or rewriting.
3. **Cash closes the commission path.** One of these holds:
   - cash is below the reserve, which the commission rule refuses on its own (`:301`);
   - with cash at or above the reserve, the commission step stopped at the package search with no affordable
     candidate (`:324-327`);
   - or at a writer or team slot that `staff()` left unfilled this week at its reserve check (`:118`, `:159`;
     `staff()` runs first, `:362`);
   - or the index is full (`:301`) and every indexed screenplay met a cash refusal this week: `cashBlocked` under
     Part A, or `staffingBlocked` with a slot that `staff()` left unfilled for cash.

A hold week (`:302`) or a busy writer is not a cash refusal, so neither starts cost-cutting.

**Why this test.**
- **Cash only falls.** With no production and no run, cash falls every week by at least the floor (§4.4). A rival
  that cannot fund new development this week cannot fund it later without an outside inflow, and none exists before
  P15B's loans.
- **Economic rejection alone does not start it.** While the rival can still commission, a new screenplay is a new
  draw.
- **The low-market seed.** On `p15a1-w2-market-01` every package fails viability (1355-C4 :209-216). Its rivals keep
  shelving and commissioning until cash closes the commission path, and Part B starts then.
- **No waiting period.** The condition cannot clear without an inflow, so a persistence count would only spend fixed
  cost. The false-positive control, (e) in §6.3, checks the trigger on every measured route.

### 4.2 While cutting costs

**No new commitments.**
- `staff()` renews nobody (`:102-127`) and fills no slot (`:140-174`). The slot loop retains nobody, so every own
  employee becomes surplus.
- Scientist demand is zero (`:363`; `rivalResearch.ts:113-123`).
- **The P14 market.**
  - The rival trigger proposes nothing (`talentMarket.ts:1395`, `:698-717`).
  - In the market pass of the entry week, before any settlement, the rival's current proposals are withdrawn with the
    existing verb (`talentMarket.ts:485-491`).
- **Research and technology.**
  - No research plan is admitted (`hollywoodTick.ts:367`; `rivalResearch.ts:130-153`).
  - No technology is adopted or bought (`:361`; `technologyRival.ts:19-25`).
  - No research project begins and no new seat is assigned (`rivalResearch.ts:232-255`).
  - Active projects run on under the existing law (`rivalResearch.ts:258-262`), because no rival pause verb exists
    (question P3).

**Releases: REDUCE_OBLIGATIONS under R3's law.**
- **Who is surplus.** While cutting costs, the surplus filter (`:183-184`) also takes Scientists who hold no
  unreleased seat.
- **R3's checks decide each release, unchanged:**
  - no production, writing or unreleased research seat (`:180-182`, `:187`);
  - more than `HIRING_TERMINATION_CAP_WEEKS` left (`:187`; `tuning.ts:415`);
  - no open promise from this studio to the person (`:188`);
  - cash after the charge at or above the reserve without the person (`:190-191`).
- **What a release writes.**
  - The charge is `terminationCost` (`employment.ts:207-210`), booked as `termination` (`:195`).
  - The receipt is an `employment` receipt with reason `termination` (`:196`).
  - The person becomes a free agent at the end of the week (`:464-472`).
- **One added policy check, the payback rule.** Release a person only if charge × weekly operating cost ≤ cash × weekly
  saving, where the saving is the salary plus `OVERHEAD_PER_EMPLOYEE` (`tuning.ts:486`).
  - Take cash C, weekly cost w, charge X and saving Δ. Cash reaches zero after (C − X)/(w − Δ) weeks in place of C/w.
    The first is no sooner exactly when X·w ≤ C·Δ.
  - R3's reserve check alone allows releases that bring that week a little forward when runway is short (worked
    example in the notes). Question P2 asks whether to keep the rule.
- **Order.** Releases run in ascending employment ordinal, and each recomputes cash and cost (`:185`, `:190-194`).

**Unchanged.**
- Facilities and their opex stay.
- `decide()` still runs. Without a full team its evaluations refuse for staffing (`:225`) and leave counts as they
  are.
- Shelved screenplays stay shelved, and promises settle under their owners.

### 4.3 What the route respects

| Owner's word | How Part B respects it |
|---|---|
| Existing employment costs | Every release pays `terminationCost`. No contract ends early without its charge. A contract with 26 weeks or less runs to expiry (`:187`), since releasing it would save nothing. Payroll and overhead keep reconciling with employment intervals (`hollywoodValidation.ts:312-315`) |
| Binding work | Nobody seated on a production, writing a screenplay or holding an unreleased research seat is released (`:180-182`, `:187`). No production is cancelled, because Part B starts only with none. Active research runs on. A person holding an open promise from the studio stays, and the promise settles under its existing owner (`:188`; 1340-O :40) |
| Facilities | Unchanged, with their opex (§4.7) |
| History | Nothing is removed: receipts, films, employment rows, screenplays and shelving entries stay. Each release appends its receipt (`:196`) |
| Accounting | No new money kind. Every movement goes through `moveRivalMoney` (`hollywood.ts:41-52`) as `termination`, and the validator's existing reconciliations hold (`hollywoodValidation.ts:251-259`, `:294`, `:295`) |

### 4.4 What the route never does

- **No subsidy.**
  - It creates no inflow: `studioRevenue` stays the only positive rival kind (`hollywoodValidation.ts:295`).
  - It never touches the player.
- **No replacement and no minimum count.** It adds no studio and keeps none alive (ruling 4, approved text :59-60).
- **No guaranteed survival.**
  - A rival cutting costs keeps paying overhead and facility opex, 41,500-46,500 a week with no staff
    (1357-R :205-210).
  - It fails when its cash runs out, under P15B's law once that integrates.
  - 1357-R §7 already gives the arithmetic: cost levers move the date, and "Any floor with no income ends below zero"
    (1357-R :334-336).
- **No hastened failure.**
  - R3's reserve check keeps cash above the post-release reserve, so no release makes a week low for P15B by itself.
    P15B counts a week as low below four weeks of cover (1352-A :66, :152); the reserve is 12-20 weeks.
  - The payback rule keeps the zero-cash week from moving earlier (§4.2). Control (g) in §6.3 measures it.

### 4.5 Exit

- **A greenlight ends cost-cutting** (`since = null`) in the same decision.
  - A rival keeps whoever R3 could not release. If it still holds a full team and a viable package becomes
    affordable, it films, and normal staffing resumes.
- **Nothing else ends it in v1.** Before P15B's loans no inflow exists (§4.1), so this rule is exact.
- **Loans later.** Whether a P15B loan should reopen hiring is Wave 3's rival-selection question (P1).

### 4.6 How it fits P15B

- **Owners.** P15 owns "distress, closure and the disposal handoff" (ruling 3, approved text :55). Part B is P14 rival
  policy, the bounded management policy that sits beside the shared laws (`tuning.ts:28`). It writes no stage, notice,
  loan, closure or P15 root, and it reads none.

### 4.7 Facilities stay out of v1

## 5. Save and state

**Part A needs no saved state and no save step.**
- It changes which evaluations increment an existing count. The count, the shelved list, the hold and the
  `screenplayShelved` receipt keep their shapes.
- The validator checks their bounds and receipts and replays no outcome (`hollywoodValidation.ts:344-367`,
  `:536-543`; the receipt's count need only be an integer of at least 1, `:540`). A count written under v1 and one
  written under v2 are equally valid.
- So no stored value needs an era version: 1357-A :145-148 binds only values that a validator reconciles against a
  formula.
- A loaded save keeps its counts. A count frozen at 12 under v1 shelves at its first v2 economic rejection.

**Part B needs one field and one save step.**
- **Why.** The state must persist across weeks, because `staff()` runs before `decide()` (`hollywoodTick.ts:362`,
  `:369`) and must refuse to re-hire after a release.
  - Without it, a release followed by a re-hire could repeat every week, paying a charge and a bonus each time.
  - No existing field can carry the state. The business keys are exact (`hollywoodValidation.ts:228`), and a receipt
    cannot tell a cost-cutting release from an ordinary R3 one.
- **Shape.** `RivalBusiness.costCutting: { version: 1; since: number | null }`.
- **Step.**
  - Save46 is the next free step after Save45. 1361-F ruling 17 gave P15B "the next free step after Save45"

  - P15B Wave 2 then takes Save47. Its genuine Save(N−1) fixture becomes a Save46 capture, minted at this step's last
    writer (1357-A :333-335).
- **Up.** `convertV45ToV46` adds `{version: 1, since: null}` to every business. The law applies from the next tick, and
  nothing is backfilled.
- **Down.** `convertV46ToV45` strips the field when every `since` is null. It refuses by name otherwise, because a set
  `since` is real state (the Save43 pattern, 1344-A :123-125).
- **Frozen chain.** A `rivalCostCutting` era flag threads to `validateHollywood`, as Save41's and Save43's flags did
  (`hollywoodValidation.ts:62-72`).
- **Validation at era 46.**
  - Exact keys and version 1.
  - `since` is null, or an integer from the studio's entry week to T.
