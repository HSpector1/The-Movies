# Independent review 1352-B

**Verdict: REFINE**

The charter is well-sourced (its own citations to W0, 1350-A, 1122-A and the P11→P12 contract check out against the text I read directly) and correctly closes the three forbidden shortcuts it explicitly names. But it has concrete, fixable gaps that must be resolved *before* Wave 1 RED tests are authored — an ambiguous state-machine spec produces an ambiguous test suite — and one gap (the cover/runway question) is a citation-without-engagement against a controlling cross-package contract clause, not a routine delegated detail.

## Blocking defects

**1. `distressWeeks` update rule is underspecified and, as literally written, breaks the charter's own closure-timing claim.** — DEVIATES / PARTIAL
Charter 1352-A §4.2 lines 74–78 lists "the counters update first" as three formulas (`lowRun`, `negativeRun`, `clearRun`, each `X ? prev+1 : 0`) plus a fourth, purely descriptive line: "`distressWeeks` counts evaluated weeks since entering distress, including the entry week" (line 78). No formula is given. On the entry week itself (the week the `warning→distress` transition fires, table row line 85), `prev.stage` is still `'warning'`, not `'distress'` — so the natural reading of "counters update first" (`distressWeeks = prev.stage==='distress' ? prev.distressWeeks+1 : 0`) yields `distressWeeks=0` on the entry week, not `1`. That contradicts "including the entry week" and, worked through the charter's own worked example (line 100–101: "4 low weeks, then warning, then negative through week 8, then 26 distress weeks... 33 consecutive negative weeks"), produces closure at week 34, not the claimed week 33, unless `distressWeeks` is set to `1` as part of *firing* the transition rather than as part of the generic per-week counter update. The same ambiguity applies to the relapse rule at line 89 ("`distressWeeks` restarts").
*Fix:* state explicitly that `distressWeeks` is set by the transition-commit step, not the fact-only counter step — e.g. "on any week a transition into `distress` fires (from `warning` or from `recovery`), `distressWeeks := 1` for that week; on any week the studio remains in `distress` without transitioning, `distressWeeks := prev.distressWeeks + 1`." Add this as an explicit fifth formula alongside the other three, and add a RED leaf to item 1 (`condition-transition-table`) pinning the exact value of `distressWeeks` on the entry week and the week immediately after.

**2. The relapse trigger (`recovery→distress`) is single-week-sensitive, unlike the initial entry trigger, and the charter never states or justifies this asymmetry.** — PARTIAL
Table row line 89: `recovery | negative | distress`. The condition column is the bare fact `negative` (this week's cash<0), not a sustained-run condition like every other multi-week row (`negativeRun ≥ 8`, `clearRun ≥ 4`, `lowRun ≥ 4`, `clearRun ≥ 13`). RED item 5 (line 173) confirms this reading: "a negative week in recovery returns to distress." Compare this to the charter's own §4.2 property (line 98): "One large week that cash absorbs (one bad film) produces no distress" — that property is only proven for the `stable/warning→distress` path (verified below); it does *not* hold for a studio already in `recovery`, where a single bad week immediately relapses it into `distress` and restarts the 26-week/33-week clock. This is the exact "one bad film" shortcut the charter says (line 98, and implicitly review Q3's forbidden-shortcuts list) is closed — reintroduced at the recovery boundary. It may be a deliberate design choice ("once already known-troubled, react fast in both directions" — `distress→recovery` is symmetric, also single-week at line 87), but the charter states no such rationale, and a single-week relapse that restarts a 26-week distress clock is in tension with ruling 3's "meaningful recovery opportunities."
*Fix:* add an explicit property sentence in §4.2 stating and justifying the relapse asymmetry, and add a dedicated RED leaf (not just folded into item 5) that pins this behavior as intentional so a future reader doesn't read it as a bug.

**3. Loan principal formula has an unaddressed zero-fixed-cost hole.** — MISSING (edge case)
§4.4 line 132: `max = floor(LOAN_MAX_FIXED_COST_WEEKS × weeklyFixedCost / LOAN_AMOUNT_STEP) × LOAN_AMOUNT_STEP`; principal must be "a multiple of `LOAN_AMOUNT_STEP` in `[LOAN_AMOUNT_STEP, max]`" (line 131). If `weeklyFixedCost = 0`, `max = 0`, and the range `[1000, 0]` is empty — no valid principal exists, so a studio eligible for a loan (in `warning`/`distress`, not founding) cannot actually take one, precisely in the scenario of low/no ongoing burn with negative cash. I traced this is reachable for the *player*: `economyEngagedEver` (`employment.ts:69-70`) is a persisted, monotonic flag set only by a signing action (`actions.ts:2578`, `:2607`); a fresh world starts with it `false` (`worldgen.ts:756`); overhead and facility opex both gate on `engaged && founding===null` (per W0 §1 rows 7.5/7.6). If founding can complete with zero signings (unverified from what I read — needs a source check I don't have evidence for either way), a founded-but-never-engaged player studio has `weeklyPayroll=weeklyOverhead=weeklyFacilityOperatingCost=0`, i.e. `weeklyFixedCost=0`, and the loan becomes mathematically impossible even though the studio is nominally eligible. For rivals this cannot happen — floor burn is always ≥38,500/week (W0 §2) since rivals can't shed facilities — so this is a player-only hole. RED item 11 ("refusal of zero... negatives, non-multiples and amounts above max") only covers a *requested* zero, not the case where `max` itself computes to zero.
*Fix:* either (a) define a floor independent of `weeklyFixedCost` (e.g. a minimum absolute principal when `O` could legitimately be 0), or (b) explicitly document and test that a zero-fixed-cost studio simply has no loan route (and confirm via source whether that state is even reachable — I could not verify this from the files I have access to, so it is NOT VERIFIED, not refuted). Either way this needs an explicit RED leaf; right now it is silent.

**4. The "cover" measure is cited against, but never reconciled with, the P11→P12 contract's runway prohibition.** — NOT VERIFIED / possible DEVIATES
`docs/engineering/P11-TO-P12-AND-P12-FUTURE-CONSUMER-CONTRACT.md:346`: "Negative cash alone is not bankruptcy, and **current-pace runway is not a new P15B distress threshold**." The charter cites this exact line as authority (1352-A line 6: ":185-195 and :346") but the word "runway" never appears anywhere in the charter's own text. §4.1 (lines 64–66) defines `Cover c = cash / O` (weeks of cash against fixed obligations + loan installment) and `low = c < CORPORATE_WARN_COVER_WEEKS`; this is used to gate the `stable→warning` transition (line 84, `lowRun ≥ 4`). This is *not* the codebase's existing D-12.16 "runway" formula (`economyView.ts:121-137`, which nets active-run revenue: `weeks = cash/(burn−rev)`) — it is instead the *exact* formula already used for 1350-A's financial-strength band (`weeks = floor(cash/weeklyFixedCost)`, 1350-A line 90). That distinction matters: 1350-A's band is explicitly pure *display*, never a mechanical gate ("The band ... never feeds points, rank, row order or any sort key," 1350-A line 97) — that is plausibly *why* reusing that formula for the band didn't trip the P11→P12 prohibition. The charter now reuses the identical formula as an actual mechanical threshold that gates the `warning` stage (and downstream: loan eligibility, remedy notices, the path toward `distress`/`closed`). Whether "distress threshold" in the P11→P12 clause means only the literal `distress`-stage entry (which, as I traced, uses `negativeRun ≥ 8` alone, no cover/runway term at all — so *that* specific gate is clean) or the broader failure-track concept (in which case gating `warning` on sustained low runway is exactly what's forbidden) is genuinely arguable, but the charter must make the argument, not skip past the clause it cites.
*Fix:* add an explicit paragraph in §4.1 or §4.2 quoting `:346`, and either (a) argue why a *sustained* (4-week), obligation-normalized "cover" gating only the advisory `warning` stage is distinct from a "current-pace runway... distress threshold," or (b) redesign the `warning` gate to not depend on this ratio. Add a RED leaf pinning whichever position is taken. Absent that, this is not a routine implementation detail delegated by ruling 3 — it's compliance with a separate controlling document, and if no convincing distinction can be written, it becomes a real Owner question rather than something the parent can quietly resolve.

## Non-blocking notes

- **Q1 (loans/bailout).** MET WITH EVIDENCE on the core question: the loan is explicit, interest-bearing (flat 12%/52wk, provably an integer since principal is a multiple of 1,000 — §4.4 lines 134–138), one law for both studios, cannot itself prevent a closure ("the loan cannot stop a closure that later cash does not prevent," line 145–146), and is fully documented in this charter (satisfying "no undocumented debt product"). "Stable studios cannot borrow" (line 130) is well-reasoned and citation-backed (P15:847 parks growth financing in P16+) — restricting loans to `warning`/`distress` reads as "recovery opportunity," consistent with ruling 3's framing, not a reduction of it. No bailout concern found in the mechanics themselves.
- **Q2 (state machine, remainder).** No unreachable rows found among the 8 table rows. The apparent "ambiguous ordering" within a FROM-state's multiple rows (e.g. `warning`'s two rows, `distress`'s two rows) is not actually ambiguous in practice: `negative` always implies `low` given the `Cover`/`low` definitions (verified algebraically for both the `O>0` and `O=0` branches), which makes the paired conditions in every row mutually exclusive. The charter never states this invariant explicitly as the reason the stated "checked in this order" note is safe — recommend adding it as a one-line lemma in §4.2 so a future reader doesn't have to re-derive it. I independently verified "distress needs ≥4 weeks of warning" (lines 96–97) and "one bad film produces no distress" (line 98, on the stable/warning path) are both provably correct as stated, given the `lowRun ≥ negativeRun` invariant.
- **Q3 (forbidden shortcuts, remainder).** "cash<0 alone as warning trigger" and "instant reset" are both cleanly closed (verified). "One bad film/one week as distress" is closed on the forward path but reopened at the `recovery→distress` boundary — see Blocking Defect 2, which is the same underlying gap as this question's third forbidden shortcut.
- **Q4 (remedy symmetry).** "No studio flag" and the shared fixed-cost definition (§2, line 39) are both MET WITH EVIDENCE and match 1350-A exactly. The "two legitimate routes" property (§4.3 lines 122–124, RED item 9) is honestly scoped for Wave 4 (where it matters — closure only *settles* in Wave 4, gated after Wave 3 unifies termination eligibility per §4.3 line 119 and the wave gate table §3), so the interim Wave 2–3 asymmetry (W0 §4/§5: rival termination needs reserve + no open promise + surplus non-Scientist + >26 weeks, vs. the player's ungated termination) does not have live consequences before it's fixed. Recommend one addition to RED item 9: a rival-realistic capability input (`terminableContracts=0` despite "having staff") to confirm the function correctly reports fewer than 2 routes rather than silently over-counting, since Wave 1's pure-law test can construct that input today even though Wave 2/3 don't yet wire it live.
- **Q5 (rounding).** Installment math is sound and exact given the current TUNING values (`floor(total/term)+remainder` sums exactly to `total`; minimum `total=1120` at `term=52` always gives `floor≥21`, no zero-installment risk under current constants — this is implicitly dependent on those constants staying above a rough threshold, a light note for future re-tuning, not a defect today).
- **Q6 (§7 deferrals).** Deferring dormancy is reasonable but should be characterized more precisely: ruling 4 declines the late-entry pack, and the charter's own `recovery` stage already fills the role the package's `dormant` state served — recommend stating dormancy is largely *superseded* by the redesigned state machine rather than merely "deferred," to avoid leaving a phantom open item over Wave 4. On the annex D.5/R participant-manifest right-sizing: ANNEX R frames this as a P15B-wide *stop condition* (W0 §0, §9), not a Wave-4-scoped detail; the charter's proposal to satisfy its *purpose* (exact-once, atomicity, idempotency, no partial state) via a single-reducer/full-state-validator design is reasonable, and Waves 1–3 genuinely don't depend on its resolution (closure settles only in Wave 4). But deferring the *final* adequacy judgment to "Wave 4 review" risks three waves of work before anyone checks whether the right-sizing argument holds. Recommend committing now to raising this as an explicit, early Owner-reviewable item before Wave 4 implementation begins, not after.
- **Q7 (TUNING plausibility).** Internally consistent (8 sustain weeks > 6-week theatrical run; 26 distress weeks matches the 26-week loan horizon). Appropriately gated behind the unexecuted §6 measurement probe — NOT VERIFIED pending that run, which I cannot execute (read-only, no shell/execution tools). Worth having §6's probe specifically check two mismatches I found: (a) the `warning` cover threshold (4 weeks) is well below the 1350-A band's "Strained" threshold (13 weeks) — a chronically-Strained studio never mechanically warns; and (b) rival `reserveWeeks` (12–20, `hollywoodStartingData.ts:12-36`) already halts rival growth actions well before the `warning` cover threshold (4) would ever fire, meaning `warning` may fire long after a rival's growth has already visibly stalled. Neither is necessarily wrong, but both are exactly the kind of miscalibration §6 exists to catch.
- **Q8.** Concrete RED additions needed beyond the current 14: (1) an explicit `distressWeeks` entry/relapse-value leaf under item 1; (2) a dedicated relapse-asymmetry leaf (not folded into item 5); (3) a `weeklyFixedCost=0 → max=0` leaf under item 11; (4) a leaf or accompanying charter text distinguishing "cover" from "current-pace runway" against `P11-TO-P12...CONTRACT.md:346`; (5) a rival-shaped capability input added to item 9.
