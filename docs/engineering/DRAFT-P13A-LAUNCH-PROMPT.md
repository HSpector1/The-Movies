## P13A — one synchronized-sound transition, reached from the lot

*This is a draft for Current Ops to review, not an execution order. Nothing below authorizes coding until Current Ops issues one.*

**Build this.** A studio walks to a Research Laboratory on its own lot, assigns a named Scientist, funds a synchronized-sound project, and watches a real estimate move. A second studio does nothing and buys the same technology when it is commercially released. A third buys it outright once it is released, through a real job with real weeks, because the accepted catalogue requires non-zero capex and at least one build week and cannot express an instant grant. All three end with a soundstage that can record synchronized dialogue, and the game can say truthfully how each of them got there and what it cost.

**Start at the lot, not at a spreadsheet.** The first thing a reviewer can play must be entered through an actual workspace interaction: select the Laboratory, see who works there, choose a project, approve a budget, and come back later to a changed estimate. The existing design sequences the world route last, at P13A.3. Move a thin native entry forward into the first playable slice instead. The three future product critiques inspect real screens and real tasks, so a headless core cannot satisfy the first of them. Correctness work does not move; only the order in which a person can reach it.

**What counts as done.** A production that could not record dialogue can now record it, on one exact stage, because one exact installation completed. A badge does not count. A generic quality bonus does not count. A timeline that advances without changing what a production may do does not count. One rival reaches the same capability under the same law, by a route the player can see, and the player can lose the lead.

**What must keep working.** The silent route stays fully lawful; sound is an option, never a tax. A film already in first actual filming keeps the technology it started with, and a film that has not started filming may use the new one. A studio that simply buys the commercial product needs no Laboratory, no Scientist and no research budget: those requirements bind the development route only. Old equipment keeps working after a public standard exists.

**Who owns what.** P09 owns the physical job, its progress, its reservations and its completion. P13 owns eligibility, research, adoption and the capability consequence. P11 owns cash, ledgers and when a charge starts. One physical work owner and one commit, with no duplicated progress, no duplicated reservation, no duplicated charge and no second grant of the same capability. P12 owns studio and rival identity; reference its IDs rather than opening a second registry. When installation cancellation arrives, it follows option B: pay completed work and necessary restoration, recover genuinely refundable unused commitments.

**Build the foundations first, and treat them as engineering.** The Laboratory body with seats, the named research employee with payroll, per-project seat assignment, the project budget with its usable-spend ceiling, the acoustic instrument module, the invention provenance record and installation jobs that respect the dependency chain all have to exist before a first sound film is legal. The Owner has already selected that this department exists. What remains is to build it, so none of it returns as a product question.

**Necessary sound work only.** Convert what synchronized sound actually requires: acoustic treatment on one stage, the recording package, compatible capture and sound-capable Post. My Buildings, ordinary office renovation, broad upgrade families and slot tuning are not launch prerequisites and must not become them.

**The first demonstration is not the finished experience.** Two selected capabilities sit in the first tier rather than after it: the per-project budget ceiling, and retained verified work across pause and cancel, which Owner direction makes unconditional even though a first sound film need never cancel anything. Staffing to laboratory capacity, cooperation and splitting across laboratories, plan queues, gap-aware conversion, forecast precision, option-B installation cancellation and decomposed inventor pricing come next, by name, with reasons, not quietly dropped. A parallel-research proof needs two genuinely useful briefs, never an empty second queue, so it waits on a real second consumer.

**Review points.** Three product critiques: an early playable slice, a completed causal core, then integrated ready scope. Critics inspect actual screens and tasks. Separate correctness checks cover state, costs, scheduling and migration, and they do not substitute for each other. One implementation builder and one native-input owner. No routine Owner checkpoint waits.

**Numbers are not settled.** Prices, the five-times scale, office durations and the two held recommendations stay provisional. Use fixture values, label them, and change none of them into tuning by shipping them.

**Stop at the stop line.** P13A ends when research, waiting and purchase reach truthful outcomes, one rival consequence is visible, sound gates one exact production decision, and the lot shows the change. It does not continue because a seam now exists.
