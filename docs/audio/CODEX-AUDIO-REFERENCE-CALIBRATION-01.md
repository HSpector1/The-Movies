## 1. How to read this report

Every material statement is tagged by evidence class:

- **SOURCE-DERIVED FINDING** — stated or directly observable in a cited official, developer, archival, or contemporary professional source.
- **CURRENT-PILOT FINDING** — measured from the immutable Project: Studio pilot evidence.
- **TECHNICAL INFERENCE** — a bounded conclusion from measurements or multiple sources; it is not direct testimony.
- **ARTISTIC INTERPRETATION** — a creative reading offered for evaluation, not history.
- **PROJECT: STUDIO RECOMMENDATION** — the proposed direction after weighing the evidence.
- **UNKNOWN / DEFERRED** — evidence is insufficient; no confident reconstruction is claimed.

The aim is functional, era, and design-grammar calibration. It is not permission to reproduce a melody, chord sequence, arrangement, performance, recording, character, script, or named creator's style.

## 2. Decision summary

1. **SOURCE-DERIVED FINDING:** *The Movies* contained roughly ninety originally composed pieces available in film post-production. Lionhead associate producer Brynley Gibson says the team later put its music on the lot so it would be heard more often and developed that presentation into a changing DJ station; exact cue identity, master reuse, and routing are undocumented. [Shacknews developer interview](https://www.shacknews.com/article/39083/the-movies-interview) **ARTISTIC INTERPRETATION:** The resulting combination of era color and conspicuous Hollywood genre shorthand is evident in the contemporary soundtrack review, but it is synthesis—not Gibson's stated design formula. [Soundtrack catalogue/review](https://www.soundtrack.net/album/the-movies/)
2. **PROJECT: STUDIO RECOMMENDATION:** Preserve that conceptual unity—one musical world spanning the studio and filmmaking—but give lot playback quieter arrangements, longer development, weaker hooks, and more rests than film-facing or milestone music.
3. **SOURCE-DERIVED FINDING:** The English manual verifies seven DJ identities, not their exact chronology. Only three unnamed period/personality examples are developer-verified. The complete name-to-decade mapping circulating in the community remains low-confidence.
4. **PROJECT: STUDIO RECOMMENDATION:** Use overlapping broadcaster practices rather than one caricature per decade. Presenter performance must establish period before any bandwidth, noise, or EQ treatment.
5. **TECHNICAL INFERENCE:** Successful period games use several different strategies—licensed masters, newly composed period language, sparse adaptive score, diegetic radio, or mixtures. No single comparator validates one universal density or authenticity target.
6. **CURRENT-PILOT FINDING:** Signal analysis can test rough pulse, density, spectral profile, sectioning, and stability, but it cannot decide instrumentation, historical authenticity, protected-reference resemblance, annoyance, or musical worth. Those remain the Owner's blind listening decisions.

### 3.4 The central tension: plausible period versus Hollywood homage

**SOURCE-DERIVED FINDING:** Silent-film cue sheets were practical systems for matching mood, tempo, and duration to scenes; Library of Congress scholarship also notes that such compiled accompaniments could become monotonous and sometimes joined material awkwardly. This is both historical evidence and a warning against treating isolated period gestures as a full management score. [Library of Congress cue-sheet project](https://blogs.loc.gov/music/2025/04/by-the-people-transcribing-cue-sheets-for-silent-film/), [silent-film scores collection](https://www.loc.gov/collections/silent-film-scores-and-arrangements/about-this-collection/)

**ARTISTIC INTERPRETATION:** *The Movies* feels coherent not because every cue is a museum reconstruction, but because it treats cinema history as a fluent repertoire of moods, genres, ensembles, and production colors. Its quick tonal pivots support the fantasy of running a dream factory.

**PROJECT: STUDIO RECOMMENDATION:** Use a three-layer test:

1. **Era anchor:** Does the instrumentation, pulse, harmony, arrangement, and production contain several defensible features of its time?
2. **Studio lens:** Does it add flexible, lightly theatrical motion suitable for the business of making movies?
3. **Management restraint:** Can it coexist with ambience, PA, radio, and repeated planning without behaving like a trailer or film climax?

A track need not be a documentary reconstruction. It must not depend on one cliché, famous-theme proximity, or anachronism that reads as accidental. Film homage may flavor the arrangement; it should not erase the broader era or dominate the lot.

## 4. Calibration anchors for the two pilot epochs

### 4.1 `acoustic_electrical_1920_1932`

**SOURCE-DERIVED FINDING:** The interval contains several simultaneous practices: silent-film accompaniment; Black-led jazz and blues; dance orchestras; stride piano; and smaller ensembles. Commercial electrical examples existed by 1920; Columbia and Victor implemented and introduced electrical recording at scale in 1925, after which acoustic recording rapidly declined. Smithsonian material emphasizes jazz's plurality, Black roots, call-and-response, syncopation, ensemble development, and increasing solo prominence. It is not simply “ragtime.” [Smithsonian jazz overview](https://music.si.edu/story/jazz), [Library of Congress recording timeline](https://www.loc.gov/programs/national-recording-preservation-plan/tools-and-resources/historical-background/timeline/)

**LISTEN FOR:**

- acoustic players that seem to answer one another rather than a uniform synthetic block;
- period-plausible rhythmic lift, swing/syncopation, stride or blues vocabulary without turning into slapstick;
- limited but clear melodic gestures that can recede during management play;
- a recording perspective that suggests a room/ensemble without fake shellac noise doing all the historical work;
- sectional contrast that remains coherent over two minutes;
- studio optimism, craft, or wit that is not a comedy cue.

**RED FLAGS:** only detuned honky-tonk piano; relentless cartoon chase rhythm; modern sub-bass or drum-machine precision; one large swing-band cliché standing in for 1920–32; pseudo-“old” noise masking modern harmony/arrangement; foreground soloing that never releases; a familiar cinematic melody or exact silent-film trope.

### 4.2 `sampled_digital_1987_1999`

**SOURCE-DERIVED FINDING:** This interval is also plural. Affordable samplers/drum machines, analogue and digital editing, hip-hop, R&B, alternative/post-rock, house, and techno developed alongside many other styles. Hip-hop and R&B are distinct Black musical lineages even where 1990s production practices overlap. Smithsonian accounts locate hip-hop in Black and Latino youth culture in the Bronx and in Black, Puerto Rican/Latino, Jamaican, and other Caribbean exchange—not in a device preset. [Smithsonian “Hip-Hop Comes to the Smithsonian”](https://americanhistory.si.edu/press/releases/hip-hop-comes-smithsonian), [Smithsonian “The sazón in hip-hop”](https://americanhistory.si.edu/explore/stories/sazon-hip-hop), [NMAAHC Black Music Month](https://nmaahc.si.edu/explore/stories/celebrating-black-music-month)

**ARTISTIC INTERPRETATION:** `DFG-01` is a rights-constrained, wholly generated hybrid lane; “sample-shaped” describes a compositional abstraction without sourced recordings, not a historical reconstruction of either hip-hop or R&B. `DFG-03` likewise combines two distinct local traditions—Chicago house and Detroit techno—for a bounded pilot question, not a claim to authenticate both.

**LISTEN FOR:**

- deliberate sample-shaped or edited form without any recognizable borrowed recording;
- stable but not exhausting grooves; space around drums/bass/synth/band textures;
- period-specific analogue/digital coexistence—including increasingly mature late-1990s digital editing and production—rather than present-day maximal processing;
- human or machine repetition varied enough for long management sessions;
- an independent musical identity across hip-hop/R&B, alternative/post-rock, and house/techno families;
- a playful studio-management angle without becoming television pastiche.

**RED FLAGS:** generic “retro synthwave”; current festival build/drop grammar; a copied break, hook, voice, or famous-recording texture; all-era gated drums; constant distorted-guitar wall; club intensity that never relaxes; token scratches or vinyl noise used as authenticity; faux-vocal/mouth artifacts; one genre standing in for 1987–99.

## 5. Owner listening rubric

The first pass should remain intuitive. The Owner need not determine historical perfection or identify instruments. The optional calibration fields answer distinct questions:

| Field | What it means | What it does **not** mean |
|---|---|---|
| `movies_spirit_1_5` | Does this have the playful, flexible, Hollywood-management grammar that made *The Movies* legible? | Does it copy or resemble a particular Daniel Pemberton cue? |

The primary Owner questions remain: does it sound broken; does it sound good; what era does it suggest; and would it be welcome during studio management?

## 6. Imitation and rights guardrail

**PROJECT: STUDIO RECOMMENDATION:** A favorable reference match means functional/era/design-grammar similarity only. Never request or select for:

- a copied melody, bass line, riff, hook, chord sequence, cadence, or orchestration;
- note-for-note reconstruction of a *The Movies* cue or any comparator score;
- a distinctive protected recording, sample, or production fingerprint;
- imitation “in the style of” a named composer, artist, song, soundtrack, franchise, or performer;
- historical voice cloning or impersonation.

The U.S. Copyright Office distinguishes musical compositions from sound recordings and explains that derivative-work protection does not authorize use of pre-existing protected material. This report is a procurement/design safeguard, not legal advice. [Copyright Office music exhibit](https://www.copyright.gov/history/copyright-exhibit/artifacts/), [Circular 14](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ14.pdf)
