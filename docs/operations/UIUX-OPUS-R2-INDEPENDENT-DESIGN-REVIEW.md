## 6. Independently inspect R2 and diagnose the visual gap

Assess **two separate axes**:
- **Usability and game awareness:** can the player understand, locate, act, recover and keep track of the studio?
- **Visual craft and game identity:** does the actual composition, typography, asset treatment and component execution meet the intended quality level?

A working prototype can pass the first partially and still fail the second. A polished picture can fail both in use. Do not collapse these into one comforting score.

Investigate, without assuming, whether the unfinished impression comes from: primitive or mismatched schematic art; repeated silhouette portraits; flat generic panel/card styling; bulky corner controls; inconsistent radius/border/shadow treatment; weak type hierarchy; tiny secondary text; excessive ID/debug labels; generic glyph icons; poor spacing rhythm; competing color accents; empty areas versus overcrowded ones; or lack of visual connection between a selected entity and its lot context.

R2 explicitly uses a schematic SVG, system fonts, fictional identities and selectively scaled chrome. Those choices are not automatically defects. Separate **placeholder-world/portrait quality** from **the quality of the actual UI**, then state which placeholders prevent a finished-art verdict. Do not demand a new game renderer to fix a mock background. Do not dismiss poor UI craft merely by calling the whole package a prototype: high-fidelity direction was requested.
