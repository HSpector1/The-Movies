# CHECKER — one bounded independent pass (Sonnet), verbatim record and disposition

Scope: one read-only Sonnet agent, told to be as willing to reject the proposal as R2,
given the package, the renders and the prototype (which it re-ran itself in a fresh
headless Chromium with network blocked). It made no edits. Its record is reproduced
verbatim below; my disposition follows. No second checker or fleet was used.

## Verbatim record

> **Method:** Read 00-INDEX/FINDINGS/DESIGN/WALKTHROUGH/HANDOFF-TO-CODEX; viewed CMP-01/02/03/05, A01-A03 annotated, K1-K4, X-A/X-B, V-long-names, V-waiting, V-compact-rails, S-overview-1280-200, C01. Independently re-measured the live prototype (fresh headless Chromium, network blocked, `index.html?screen=overview|person`) rather than trusting the doc's numbers.
>
> **1. Are the R2 findings fair?** Mostly yes, and better-grounded than typical design review prose — they cite counted facts (radii, shadow, font-size) rather than adjectives. Confirmed independently: R2's HUD/rail text does bottom out at 10-11px and the material really is nested pale-blue panels. One finding is slightly overstated: V-04 says R2 has "no display face"; the crop (`A01` box 8) shows R2 does bold/upsize names — the real gap is a *distinct* display face, not hierarchy per se, so the finding's framing is a hair harsher than the evidence strictly requires. Nothing in FINDINGS.md reads as manufactured to flatter the Owner — the doc explicitly separates usability (mostly PASS) from visual craft (FAIL), which is a harder position to defend than blanket condemnation, and it credits R2 for real things (exact-identity handling U-03, Back/offset restoration U-10, Build catalogue U-18). I could not find anything genuinely good in R2 that the proposal failed to preserve; every R2 strength I can find is already in the KEEP list.
>
> **2. Is the comparison honest?** Same fixture data (`living-studio.js`), same 1440×900 canvas, same isolated-Chromium method for both — yes. `CMP-01`'s middle column genuinely reuses R2's own lot SVG with only the UI swapped (verified: buildings/positions match R2's original 1:1), so the "UI-only" claim holds. The mild dishonesty-adjacent issue: the four hero "key screens" (K1–K4) that anchor the recommendation in 00-INDEX all run on the flattering **target-lot proof**, not R2's schematic lot — the fair, apples-to-apples board is one click deeper. That's disclosed, not hidden, but it is a presentation choice that makes the recommendation look more finished at first glance than the isolated-UI comparison supports.
>
> **3. Visual craft, as an art director.** Real improvement over R2, not transformational. Composition/hierarchy: clean — HUD recedes, cards have one clear focal row, counts are legible (`K1`). Color: a genuine palette (cream/brass/greenlight/signal) replaces R2's monochrome pale-blue; measured and confirmed **3 radii (50%, 4px, 8px) and 2 shadows** system-wide. Icons (`C01` §4): one consistent filled two-tone set, a real improvement over R2's Unicode glyphs. Portraits: 12 **structurally distinct** SVGs (independently confirmed — 12/12 unique markup, not just recolors), but the art style itself is a generic flat "SaaS avatar-generator" look (round head, simplified hair blob) that clashes tonally with the Deco/brass ambition elsewhere — this is the weakest link and it's honestly flagged as an unresolved art dependency, not hidden. Does it read as a game rather than a dashboard? Better, but only partly: strip away the cream tint and it is still two scrolling list-panels either side of a map, i.e., the same information-architecture grammar as a Notion/Trello directory — the design changes the skin, not the underlying "two rails of rows" shape. Card-stack fidelity to the original is real in Exploration A (cream index cards) but is diluted in the recommendation by B's translucent shelf, which reintroduces a light panel-behind-panel structure R2 was faulted for (smaller-scale, better-executed, but the same species of container). I would **not reject** this on craft, but I would not wave it through untouched either — see changes below.
>
> **4. A vs B vs Recommended — reasoned or rigged?** Reasoned, and unusually honest: the doc self-criticizes A ("world leaks between cards," "close to monotone," confirmed visually) and self-criticizes B ("generic 2018–2025 sim," "status colour lost," confirmed — every chip really is amber-on-dark). The recommendation takes A's vocabulary and B's shelf/density for stated, checkable reasons. The one gap: no independent third exploration was actually rendered and blind-compared — the hybrid was reasoned into existence, not tested against a genuine alternative. I'd have built at minimum a variant with A's shelf omitted entirely to test whether the container is needed at all; on the current evidence I might have leaned further from any panel-behind-panel toward the original's true "cards float, no shelf" purity.
>
> **5. Constraints.** 1280×720@200% holds up — HUD, rails and lot all remain legible, no clipped text observed. Long names truncate cleanly with ellipsis in both rails; no overflow or overlap. No contrast failures observed at render scale; computed contrast pairs in `C01` §1 look defensible for a game UI, though the sheet's own caveat is appropriate.
>
> **6. Concrete defects.** (a) A small white/pale parallelogram floats unattached near the road edge by Production Post in `K1`/`K2`/`K3`/`V-waiting` — reads as an unfinished asset. (b) Elliptical grass-color blobs under some trees look like stray shadow artifacts rather than intentional ground texture. (c) Portrait/avatar style is visually inconsistent with the Deco/brass direction elsewhere. (d) The translucent shelf reintroduces a panel-on-panel structure in miniature — not a bug, but a fidelity-to-critique tension worth naming. No misalignment, clipping, or broken layout found in any of the 15+ renders inspected.
>
> **7. Verdict.** **APPROVE WITH CHANGES**: (1) replace/redesign the placeholder portraits before any of this ships as "final" direction; (2) resolve the two lot artifacts noted in #6; (3) get the Owner's ruling on the two open product questions (rail default, lot art target) before implementation starts; (4) commission one more exploration that drops the translucent shelf entirely to test whether the shelf is load-bearing or just familiar. One-line fact-check: the review's own numeric claims (**3 radii, 2 shadows, 0 reading text <12px, 12/12 distinct portraits**) all **check out** against independent re-measurement of the live prototype.
