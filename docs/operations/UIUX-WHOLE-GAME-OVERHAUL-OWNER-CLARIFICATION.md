## 2. What full coverage means

The following matrix is Future Ops' recommended operational interpretation of the Owner's full-game direction. The exact screen inventory must be completed against the then-accepted build; these rows are required coverage domains, not claims that every listed interaction already exists or is defective. Do not implement missing gameplay merely to fill a UI row.

| Domain | Full-upgrade target | Required coverage beyond a happy-path screenshot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Studio home / HUD / tracking | Coherent time/cash/campaign identity, people discovery, selected R3 picture cards, studio selection/locating, attention and navigation. The studio remains operable, not wallpaper. | Early and busy studios; long/duplicate names; offscreen people; exact worksite/assignment links; filtering, search, scrolling and live list updates; active productions versus history. |
| Complete movie-making journey | Development/scripts, casting, budget and greenlight review, scheduling/production, worksite/blocker diagnosis, Post, release, results and history use one connected interaction grammar. | Available, occupied, waiting, actionable, missing and stale states; meaningful cost reviews; preserved script/cast draft; exact film/company/facility excursions and return. No invented progress, dates, remedies or production rules. |
| People / casting / contracts / assignments | Readable and recognizable portraits/dossiers, role/ability/availability/cost shown in decision context, useful candidate comparison, clear employment-versus-assignment boundaries. | Exact identity, missing location, busy/unavailable candidate, same-name people, draft changes, lawful existing offer/contract review and refusal. Future P14 law is not imported. |
| Buildings / lot tools / delivered Laboratory | Understandable catalogue, place/inspect flow, real footprint/quote, operational versus under-construction state, and clear delivered research/installation interactions. | Invalid placement, cancellation before commit versus work already submitted, occupancy, unsupported remedies, research ceiling versus actual spend and recurring costs. New P13B mechanics remain P13B's assignment. |
| Finance / Industry / records / outcomes | Legible comparisons and summaries, periods and estimates explained, meaningful drill-downs, consistent history/attribution and exact return. | Actual versus forecast, conditional versus guaranteed, unknown versus zero, public rival information versus private facts. | Menu / campaigns / settings / help | Clear entry, resume/new/load/save/Save As/switch/quit and existing settings/help routes. | Shared controls / navigation / feedback | Predictable primary/secondary/destructive actions, selection, focus, Back/Escape/Cancel, context retention, scrolling, typing, loading/pending and truthful results. | Mouse/trackpad and supported keyboard/controller paths, modality, camera interaction, no click-through/double-submit, long waits, failure recovery, focus/scroll restoration and stale-state refusal. Device support must be scoped, not inferred. |

Interface sound/motion refinements may be recommended where useful, but require scoped ownership and must not hide latency or replace visual meaning. |

All delivered player-facing surfaces must receive an explicit disposition: **redesign**, **refine**, or **retain because demonstrated to meet the shared standard**. Record exact screen/state, existing owner/source, visual target, behavior, acceptance task and remaining gap in the existing companion. 'Not in the four hero mockups' or 'not one of the eight audit findings' is not a reason to omit a surface.

Full upgrade does not mean changing every working button for novelty. Preserve successful interactions and correct command owners, but evaluate them against the common standard. A screen retained unchanged needs a justified, evidenced disposition; checking only that it still opens is not an experience review.

## 3. Design completion and implementation are linked, not competing tasks

Continue the existing plan and design ownership. Current Ops coordinates the implementation order; Future Ops conveys product/design decisions; the existing designer completes the required views and shared conventions. Do not open a competing mockup/research branch or instruct every upstream planner to redesign its screens.

The selected R3 main-screen design is the foundation, not a complete eight-family specification. Use the retained R2 workflows and Opus research selectively, then complete the remaining People/Casting/Build/Finance/Industry/campaign/result/settings presentations in the same family. Include full labels, states, navigation, cost/reason placement, resizing and real data-owner requirements. Do not copy obsolete art or rules simply because they remain in an old prototype.

Produce enough rendered/annotated coverage for every materially different remaining layout and its risky states before that layout is implemented. Use shared component/state sheets for repeated cases; no need for a large new gallery of near-duplicates. Keep editable designs, exact source pins and an evidence-linked coverage matrix together. Missing production data or incompatible native behavior is a named dependency, not an invented visual success.

Implementation may proceed in finite increments under Current Ops, starting with the selected studio view and a complete real person/production interaction. Shared components can be built once and reused. The first increment does not close the whole upgrade. Do not require a replacement UI framework or full engine rewrite as a prerequisite; bounded structural UI/navigation changes may be necessary and should be explicitly owned and reviewed rather than forbidden by a cosmetic-only interpretation.

Interface art is in scope where it determines this experience: final portrait treatment, coherent icons, stage art and font availability need actual placement and deliverables. A representative portrait proof can establish the standard before population-wide work. Do not silently declare procedural placeholder faces final. General terrain/building/animation reconstruction of the entire world is a separate art/renderer scope, not implied by this UI instruction.

## 5. Acceptance for the whole upgrade

Retain three product reviews: early selected experience, completed shared interaction/visual system, integrated whole-game result. Correctness and persistence review remain separate from design critique. Tests passing do not prove usability; an attractive prototype does not prove native controls or finances.

Whole-upgrade completion requires:

- Every delivered surface/state in the reconciled inventory is dispositioned and backed by its required design/native evidence; no silent legacy island with incompatible controls or styling.
- The player can enter/resume a disposable campaign, understand the studio, work through the actual film journey, inspect/manage people and facilities, understand finance/Industry/results, and save/leave/relaunch without a narrated guide to hidden controls.
- Common input, selection, focus, Back/Cancel, list context, text sizing, enabled/disabled explanations, commitment review, pending/refusal and recovery behaviors work consistently across destinations, not merely on the main screen.
- Both ordinary and crowded states, normal waits, blocked tasks, missing/stale records, slow/refused commands and naming/overwrite scenarios have been exercised on the exact native build and permitted fixtures. Compare against the approved visuals at appropriate scale.
- Portrait/icon/font and remaining-screen treatment outcomes are declared honestly; unresolved material visual gaps prevent claims of finished art. No unsupported worldwide accessibility/device certification is inferred.
- Existing production, finance, employment, disclosure, time and campaign laws remain correct. No lost progress, duplicate charges, speculative success or inactive-campaign leakage is accepted for fewer clicks.
- Howard judges the integrated game materially better to look at and operate. A single card choice or an early checkpoint is not that final acceptance.
